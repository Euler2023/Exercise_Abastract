# Exercise F54: Transitivity of Algebraic Extensions

## Problem Statement

> [!question] Exercise 3.10
> A field extension $K/F$ is an algebraic extension if every element of $K$ is algebraic over $F$. Let $K/F$ and $L/K$ be algebraic field extensions. Prove that $L/F$ is an algebraic extension.

## Hints

> [!hint]- Hint 1
> A polynomial for one element of $L$ uses only finitely many coefficients from $K$.

## Solution

> [!success]- Solution
> Let $\alpha\in L$. Since $L/K$ is algebraic, $\alpha$ satisfies a nonzero polynomial
>
> $$
> c_nx^n+\cdots+c_0\in K[x].
> $$
>
> Only the finitely many coefficients $c_0,\ldots,c_n$ occur. Each is algebraic over $F$, so the field
>
> $$
> E=F(c_0,\ldots,c_n)
> $$
>
> is a finite extension of $F$. The element $\alpha$ is algebraic over $E$, hence $E(\alpha)/E$ is finite. Therefore
>
> $$
> [E(\alpha):F]=[E(\alpha):E][E:F]<\infty.
> $$
>
> Thus $\alpha$ is algebraic over $F$. Since $\alpha$ was arbitrary, $L/F$ is algebraic.

## Notes

- **Routing:** Field Theory is primary because this is the transitivity theorem for algebraic extensions.
- **Method boundary:** Neither $K/F$ nor $L/K$ is assumed finite; finiteness is used only for the subextension generated by the finitely many coefficients involved with one element.
- **Source status:** [S1, Ch. 15, §15.3, Ex. 3.10, printed p. 473, PDF p. 485]. The proof is independent.
