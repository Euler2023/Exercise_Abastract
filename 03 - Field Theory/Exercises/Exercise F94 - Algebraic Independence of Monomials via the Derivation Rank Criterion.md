# Exercise F94: Algebraic Independence of Monomials via the Derivation Rank Criterion

## Problem Statement

> [!question]
> Let $k$ be a field of characteristic 0, and let $z_1, \ldots, z_r$ be algebraically independent over $k$. Let $(e_{ij})$, $i = 1, \ldots, m$ and $j = 1, \ldots, r$ be a matrix of integers with $r \ge m$, and assume that this matrix has rank $m$. Let
> $$
> w_i = z_1^{e_{i1}} \cdots z_r^{e_{ir}} \quad \text{for } i = 1, \ldots, m.
> $$
> Show that $w_1, \ldots, w_m$ are algebraically independent over $k$. [Hint: Consider the $K$-homomorphism mapping the $K$-space of derivations of $K/k$ into $K^{(r)}$ given by
> $$
> D \mapsto (Dz_1/z_1, \ldots, Dz_r/z_r),
> $$
> and derive a linear condition for those $D$ vanishing on $k(w_1, \ldots, w_m)$.]

## Hints

> [!hint]- Hint 1: The derivation space of a rational function field
> Let $K = k(z_1, \ldots, z_r)$. What is the $K$-dimension of $\operatorname{Der}_k(K, K)$? Show that the map $D \mapsto (Dz_1/z_1, \ldots, Dz_r/z_r)$ is a vector space isomorphism from $\operatorname{Der}_k(K, K)$ onto $K^r$.

> [!hint]- Hint 2: Logarithmic derivatives of Laurent monomials
> For $w_i = z_1^{e_{i1}} \cdots z_r^{e_{ir}}$, compute $D(w_i)/w_i$ in terms of $Dz_j/z_j$. How is the condition $D(w_i) = 0$ expressed as a system of linear equations in the coordinates of $D$?

> [!hint]- Hint 3: Derivation dimension and transcendence degree
> Recall from Lang Chapter VIII §5 that for any intermediate field $F = k(w_1, \ldots, w_m)$, the space of derivations $\operatorname{Der}_F(K, K)$ has dimension $\operatorname{tr.deg}(K/F) = r - \operatorname{tr.deg}(F/k)$ in characteristic 0. What does the rank $m$ of the matrix $(e_{ij})$ imply about $\operatorname{tr.deg}(F/k)$?

## Solution

> [!success]-
> ### Step 1: The space of derivations of $K/k$
> 
> Let $K = k(z_1, \ldots, z_r)$ be the rational function field in $r$ variables over $k$.
> Since $z_1, \ldots, z_r$ are algebraically independent over $k$, the space $\operatorname{Der}_k(K, K)$ of $k$-derivations of $K$ is an $r$-dimensional $K$-vector space with basis given by the partial derivations:
> $$
> \left\{ \frac{\partial}{\partial z_1}, \ldots, \frac{\partial}{\partial z_r} \right\}.
> $$
> 
> Consider the map suggested in the hint:
> $$
> \psi: \operatorname{Der}_k(K, K) \to K^r, \quad D \mapsto \left( \frac{D(z_1)}{z_1}, \ldots, \frac{D(z_r)}{z_r} \right).
> $$
> - $\psi$ is clearly $K$-linear.
> - If $\psi(D) = 0$, then $D(z_j) / z_j = 0$, so $D(z_j) = 0$ for all $j = 1, \ldots, r$. Since $\{z_j\}$ generates $K$ over $k$, $D = 0$. Thus $\ker \psi = \{0\}$.
> - Given any vector $(c_1, \ldots, c_r) \in K^r$, the derivation
>   $$
>   D = \sum_{j=1}^r c_j z_j \frac{\partial}{\partial z_j}
>   $$
>   satisfies $D(z_j) = c_j z_j$, so $D(z_j) / z_j = c_j$.
> - Therefore, $\psi$ is an **isomorphism of $K$-vector spaces**: $\operatorname{Der}_k(K, K) \cong K^r$.
> 
> ### Step 2: Linear condition for derivations vanishing on $w_i$
> 
> For each $i \in \{1, \ldots, m\}$, the element $w_i$ is a Laurent monomial:
> $$
> w_i = \prod_{j=1}^r z_j^{e_{ij}}.
> $$
> Applying a derivation $D \in \operatorname{Der}_k(K, K)$ and dividing by $w_i$ yields the logarithmic derivative formula:
> $$
> \frac{D(w_i)}{w_i} = \sum_{j=1}^r e_{ij} \frac{D(z_j)}{z_j}.
> $$
> Under the isomorphism $\psi$, writing $c_j = D(z_j) / z_j$, we have:
> $$
> \frac{D(w_i)}{w_i} = \sum_{j=1}^r e_{ij} c_j.
> $$
> Since $w_i \neq 0$, the condition $D(w_i) = 0$ is equivalent to:
> $$
> \sum_{j=1}^r e_{ij} c_j = 0.
> $$
> 
> Let $E = (e_{ij}) \in M_{m \times r}(\mathbb Z)$ be the matrix of exponents.
> Then a derivation $D$ vanishes on all elements $w_1, \ldots, w_m$ if and only if the coordinate vector $\mathbf{c} = (c_1, \ldots, c_r)^T \in K^r$ satisfies the homogeneous linear system:
> $$
> E \mathbf{c} = \mathbf{0}.
> $$
> 
> ### Step 3: Dimension of the kernel subspace
> 
> By assumption, the integer matrix $E$ has rank $m$.
> Because $\operatorname{char} k = 0$, the prime field of $k$ is $\mathbb Q$.
> The rank of an integer matrix over $\mathbb Z$, $\mathbb Q$, and $K$ is identical:
> $$
> \operatorname{rank}_K(E) = \operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb Q}(E) = m.
> $$
> 
> By the rank-nullity theorem for the linear map $E: K^r \to K^m$:
> $$
> \dim_K \ker(E) = r - \operatorname{rank}_K(E) = r - m.
> $$
> 
> Now, let $F = k(w_1, \ldots, w_m) \subset K$ be the subfield generated by $w_1, \ldots, w_m$.
> An element $D \in \operatorname{Der}_k(K, K)$ vanishes on $F$ if and only if $D(w_i) = 0$ for all $i = 1, \ldots, m$.
> Thus:
> $$
> \operatorname{Der}_F(K, K) = \{D \in \operatorname{Der}_k(K, K) : D(w_1) = \cdots = D(w_m) = 0\} \cong \ker(E).
> $$
> Consequently,
> $$
> \dim_K \operatorname{Der}_F(K, K) = r - m.
> $$
> 
> ### Step 4: Connecting derivation dimension to transcendence degree
> 
> In characteristic 0, every field extension is separable.
> By Lang Chapter VIII, §5, Proposition 5.5 (printed p. 372, PDF p. 387):
> The extension $K/F$ is finitely generated (by the $z_j$) and separably generated because the characteristic is zero. Thus the dimension of its space of derivations is its transcendence degree:
> $$
> \dim_K \operatorname{Der}_F(K, K) = \operatorname{tr.deg}(K/F).
> $$
> 
> By Step 3, this dimension is $r - m$:
> $$
> \operatorname{tr.deg}(K/F) = r - m.
> $$
> On the other hand, by additivity of transcendence degree in the tower $k \subset F \subset K$ (Exercise F89):
> $$
> \operatorname{tr.deg}(K/k) = \operatorname{tr.deg}(K/F) + \operatorname{tr.deg}(F/k).
> $$
> Since $K = k(z_1, \ldots, z_r)$ with $\{z_j\}$ algebraically independent, $\operatorname{tr.deg}(K/k) = r$.
> Substituting $\operatorname{tr.deg}(K/F) = r - m$:
> $$
> r = (r - m) + \operatorname{tr.deg}(F/k) \implies \operatorname{tr.deg}(F/k) = m.
> $$
> 
> ### Step 5: Conclusion
> 
> The field $F = k(w_1, \ldots, w_m)$ is generated over $k$ by the $m$ elements $w_1, \ldots, w_m$.
> A field extension generated by $m$ elements has transcendence degree $m$ if and only if those $m$ generators are **algebraically independent over $k$**.
> (Indeed, if there were any nonzero polynomial $P \in k[W_1, \ldots, W_m]$ with $P(w_1, \ldots, w_m) = 0$, then $\operatorname{tr.deg}(F/k) \le m - 1 < m$, a contradiction).
> 
> Therefore, $w_1, \ldots, w_m$ are algebraically independent over $k$.

## Notes

- **Source and proof status**: [S2, Ch. VIII, Exercise 9, printed p. 375, PDF p. 390]. The original subscript in $z_r$ is retained; it is not an identified source error. In the hint, $K$ means $k(z_1,\ldots,z_r)$.
- **Imported result**: Lang VIII, Proposition 5.5, printed p. 372, PDF p. 387, gives the dimension of derivations for a finitely generated separably generated extension. The dimension comparison above is independently derived from that result.
- **Characteristic in this proof**: Characteristic zero keeps the integer matrix rank unchanged in $K$ and permits the derivation argument. The conclusion also has a proof in arbitrary characteristic: if $a,b\in\mathbb N^m$ are distinct, full row rank over $\mathbb Q$ implies $aE\ne bE$. Thus distinct monomials $w^a$ become distinct Laurent monomials in $z$, which are linearly independent over any field. A polynomial relation among the $w_i$ is therefore impossible. This does not assert that the derivation proof works unchanged in characteristic $p$.
