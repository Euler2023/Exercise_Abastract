# Exercise F38: Finite Generation and Algebraic Integers

## Problem Statement

> [!question] Miscellaneous Problem M.8
> **(a)** Let $\alpha$ be a complex number, and let $\mathbb Z[\alpha]$ be the subring of $\mathbb C$ generated by $\alpha$. Prove that $\alpha$ is an algebraic integer if and only if $\mathbb Z[\alpha]$ is a finitely generated abelian group.
>
> **(b)** Prove that if $\alpha$ and $\beta$ are algebraic integers, then the subring $\mathbb Z[\alpha,\beta]$ of $\mathbb C$ that they generate is a finitely generated abelian group.
>
> **(c)** Prove that the algebraic integers form a subring of $\mathbb C$.

## Hints

> [!hint]- Hint 1
> For the converse in (a), represent multiplication by $\alpha$ on the finite free abelian group $\mathbb Z[\alpha]$ and apply Cayley–Hamilton.

## Solution

> [!success]- Solution
> **(a)** If $\alpha$ satisfies a monic polynomial of degree $n$, every higher power reduces to an integer combination of $1,\alpha,\ldots,\alpha^{n-1}$, so $\mathbb Z[\alpha]$ is finitely generated.
>
> Conversely, a finitely generated subgroup of the additive group of $\mathbb C$ is torsion-free and hence free abelian. Multiplication by $\alpha$ is an integer endomorphism of $\mathbb Z[\alpha]$. Its characteristic polynomial is monic with integer coefficients, and Cayley–Hamilton applied to $1$ shows that $\alpha$ satisfies it. Thus $\alpha$ is integral.
>
> **(b)** Suppose $\alpha$ and $\beta$ satisfy monic equations of degrees $m$ and $n$. Reducing powers separately shows that $\mathbb Z[\alpha,\beta]$ is generated as an abelian group by
>
> $$
> \alpha^i\beta^j,
> \qquad
> 0\le i<m,quad0\le j<n.
> $$
>
> **(c)** If $\alpha,\beta$ are algebraic integers, the finite abelian group from (b) is stable under multiplication by $\alpha+\beta$, $\alpha-\beta$, and $\alpha\beta$. Applying the same characteristic-polynomial argument to each multiplication endomorphism proves that all three numbers are algebraic integers. Since $0,1$ are integral, the algebraic integers form a subring of $\mathbb C$.
