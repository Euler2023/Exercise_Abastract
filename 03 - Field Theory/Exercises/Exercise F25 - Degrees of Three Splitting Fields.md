# Exercise F25: Degrees of Three Splitting Fields

## Problem Statement

> [!question] Exercise 3.2
> Determine over $\mathbb Q$ the degrees of the splitting fields of (a) $x^3-2$, (b) $x^4-1$, (c) $x^4+1$.

## Hints

> [!hint]- Hint 1
> Write down all roots and identify the smallest field containing them.

## Solution

> [!success]- Solution
> (a) With $\alpha=\sqrt[3]2$ and $\omega=e^{2\pi i/3}$, the splitting field is $\mathbb Q(\alpha,\omega)$. Eisenstein gives degree $3$ for $\alpha$, and the real field $\mathbb Q(\alpha)$ does not contain $\omega$, so the degree is $6$.
>
> (b) $x^4-1$ has roots $\pm1,\pm i$; its splitting field is $\mathbb Q(i)$, of degree $2$.
>
> (c) Its roots are primitive eighth roots of unity, so the splitting field is $\mathbb Q(\zeta_8)=\mathbb Q(i,\sqrt2)$, of degree $\varphi(8)=4$.
