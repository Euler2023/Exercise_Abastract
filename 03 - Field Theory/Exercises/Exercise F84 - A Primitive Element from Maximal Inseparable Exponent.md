# Exercise F84: A Primitive Element from Maximal Inseparable Exponent

## Problem Statement

> [!question] Lang, Chapter V, Exercise 25
> Let $E$ be a finite extension of $k$ and let $p^r=[E:k]_i$. We assume that the characteristic is $p>0$. Assume that there is no exponent $p^s$ with $s<r$ such that $E^{p^s}k$ is separable over $k$ (i.e., such that $\alpha^{p^s}$ is separable over $k$ for each $\alpha$ in $E$). Show that $E$ can be generated by one element over $k$.
>
> [Hint: Assume first that $E$ is purely inseparable.]

## Hints

> [!hint]- Hint 1
> Let $S$ be the maximal separable subfield. Find $\alpha$ whose purely inseparable degree over $S$ is $p^r$.

> [!hint]- Hint 2
> Write $S=k(\beta)$ and choose $c\in k$ so that $(\alpha+c\beta)^{p^r}$ generates $S$.

## Solution

> [!success]- Solution and proof status
> Let $S$ be the subfield of elements of $E$ separable over $k$. Lang's decomposition theorem says $S/k$ is finite separable, $E/S$ is purely inseparable, and
> $$
> [E:S]=[E:k]_i=p^r.
> $$
> If $r=0$, then $E=S$ and the primitive element theorem applies. Suppose $r>0$. The hypothesis at $s=r-1$ gives $\alpha\in E$ with $\alpha^{p^{r-1}}\notin S$. Since $E/S$ is purely inseparable of degree $p^r$, every element has $p^r$th power in $S$: its simple degree is a power of $p$ dividing $p^r$. Consequently
> $$
> [S(\alpha):S]=p^r,\qquad E=S(\alpha),\qquad a=\alpha^{p^r}\in S.
> $$
> This already proves the purely inseparable case $S=k$.
>
> For the general case choose $\beta$ with $S=k(\beta)$, using separability. The base field $k$ is infinite here: otherwise the finite field $E$ would be separable over $k$, contrary to $r>0$. Let $\sigma_1,\ldots,\sigma_m$ be the distinct $k$-embeddings of $S$ into an algebraic closure, where $m=[S:k]$. The values $\sigma_i(\beta)$ are distinct, as are their $p^r$th powers.
>
> For each pair $i\ne j$, the equation
> $$
> \sigma_i(a)+c^{p^r}\sigma_i(\beta)^{p^r}
> =\sigma_j(a)+c^{p^r}\sigma_j(\beta)^{p^r}
> $$
> excludes at most one $c\in k$, since Frobenius is injective. Choose $c$ outside these finitely many exclusions, and set
> $$
> \theta=\alpha+c\beta,\qquad z=\theta^{p^r}=a+c^{p^r}\beta^{p^r}\in S.
> $$
> All $m$ images $\sigma_i(z)$ are distinct, so the minimal polynomial of $z$ over $k$ has at least $m$ roots. As $z\in S$, its degree is at most $m$. Thus $k(z)=S$.
>
> Now $S\subseteq k(\theta)$ because $z=\theta^{p^r}$, and then $\alpha=\theta-c\beta\in k(\theta)$. Hence
> $$
> E=S(\alpha)=k(\theta).
> $$
