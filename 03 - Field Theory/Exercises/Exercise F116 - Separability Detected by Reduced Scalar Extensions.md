# Exercise F116: Separability Detected by Reduced Scalar Extensions

## Problem Statement

> [!question] Lang XVI.3
> Let $E$ be a finite extension of a field $k$. Show that $E$ is separable over $k$ if and only if $E\otimes_k L$ has no nilpotent elements for all extensions $L$ of $k$, and also when $L=k^a$.

## Hints

> [!hint]- Hint 1
> Here $k^a$ denotes an algebraic closure. A finite separable extension has a primitive element, so Exercise 1 applies.

> [!hint]- Hint 2
> If an element $\alpha\in E$ is inseparable, its minimal polynomial has repeated roots in $k^a$. The product of its distinct linear factors gives a nonzero nilpotent in $k(\alpha)\otimes_k k^a$. Show that this algebra injects into $E\otimes_k k^a$.

## Solution

> [!success]- Independent derivation of the three equivalent conditions
> As usual, “has no nilpotent elements” means no **nonzero** nilpotent elements, or equivalently that the algebra is reduced. We prove the equivalence of:
>
> (i) $E/k$ is separable;
>
> (ii) $E\otimes_k L$ is reduced for every field extension $L/k$;
>
> (iii) $E\otimes_k k^a$ is reduced.
>
> **(i) implies (ii).** By the primitive element theorem for finite separable extensions, $E=k(\alpha)$ for some $\alpha$ with separable minimal polynomial. [[03 - Field Theory/Exercises/Exercise F114 - Separable Tensor Products as Products of Fields|Exercise F114]] gives a finite product decomposition of $E\otimes_k L$ into fields for every $L/k$. If a tuple in a product of fields has a power equal to zero, each coordinate has a power equal to zero in a field and hence is zero. Thus the product is reduced.
>
> **(ii) implies (iii).** Take $L=k^a$.
>
> **(iii) implies (i).** Suppose instead that $E/k$ is not separable. Then there is $\alpha\in E$ whose irreducible minimal polynomial $f$ is not separable. Over $K=k^a$ it factors as
> $$
> f(X)=\prod_{j=1}^s(X-\beta_j)^{m_j},
> $$
> where the $\beta_j$ are distinct, the positive integers $m_j$ sum to $\deg f$, and at least one $m_j>1$. Put
> $$
> q(X)=\prod_{j=1}^s(X-\beta_j),\qquad N=\max_j m_j.
> $$
> We have $\deg q<\deg f$, so the class $[q]$ in $K[X]/(f)$ is nonzero. On the other hand, every exponent in $q^N$ is at least the corresponding exponent in $f$, so $f\mid q^N$ and $[q]^N=0$. By [[03 - Field Theory/Exercises/Exercise F115 - Base Change of a Simple Algebraic Extension|Exercise F115]], this is a nonzero nilpotent in $k(\alpha)\otimes_k K$.
>
> The inclusion $k(\alpha)\hookrightarrow E$ remains injective after tensoring with $K$. For an explicit justification, choose a $k$-vector-space complement $V$ to $k(\alpha)$ in $E$, and let $\pi:E\to k(\alpha)$ be its linear projection. The induced map $\pi\otimes\operatorname{id}_K$ is a left inverse to the induced inclusion, proving injectivity. The inclusion is also an algebra homomorphism, so it carries the exhibited nilpotent to a nonzero nilpotent in $E\otimes_k K$. This contradicts (iii).
>
> Therefore (iii) implies separability, completing the equivalence.

## Notes

- Source: [S2, Ch. XVI, Exercise 3, printed p. 637, PDF p. 652], checked on the original page image. The final superscript is $a$: an algebraic closure suffices as a single test extension.
- Named proof input: the primitive element theorem for finite separable extensions, checked in [S2, Ch. V, Theorem 4.6 and proof, printed pp. 243-244, PDF pp. 258-259]. The tensor decomposition, construction of the nilpotent, and preservation of this nonzero element are proved here or in Exercises F114–F115. No assertion that an arbitrary finite extension has a primitive element is used.
- The reverse implication only needs the simple subextension generated by one inseparable element. The $k$-linear projection used to prove injectivity need not be a homomorphism of fields.
