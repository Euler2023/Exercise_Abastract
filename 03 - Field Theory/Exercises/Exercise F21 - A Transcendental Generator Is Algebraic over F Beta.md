# Exercise F21: A Transcendental Generator Is Algebraic over $F(\beta)$

## Problem Statement

> [!question] Exercise
> Let $K=F(\alpha)$ be a field extension generated by a transcendental element $\alpha$, and let $\beta$ be an element of $K$ that is not in $F$. Prove that $\alpha$ is algebraic over $F(\beta)$.

## Hints

> [!hint]- Hint
> Write $\beta=p(\alpha)/q(\alpha)$ and move all terms to one side.

## Solution

> [!success]- Solution
> Since $K=F(\alpha)$ is a rational function field, write
> $$
> \beta=\frac{p(\alpha)}{q(\alpha)}
> $$
> with $p,q\in F[x]$ and $q\ne0$. Then $\alpha$ is a root of
> $$
> h(x)=p(x)-\beta q(x)\in F(\beta)[x].
> $$
> This polynomial is nonzero. Indeed, if all its coefficients vanished, a nonzero coefficient of $q$ would express $\beta$ as a quotient of two elements of $F$, contradicting $\beta\notin F$. Therefore $\alpha$ satisfies a nonzero polynomial over $F(\beta)$ and is algebraic over that field.

## Notes

This independent proof is the one-variable form of the fact that a nonconstant rational map of curves induces a finite extension of function fields.
