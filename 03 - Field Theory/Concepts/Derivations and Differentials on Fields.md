# Derivations and Differentials on Fields

## Definition

> [!info] Derivation of a ring or field
> Let $A$ be a commutative ring and $B$ an $A$-algebra. A map $D: B \to B$ is an **$A$-derivation** (or derivation over $A$) if:
> 1. $D$ is $A$-linear: $D(x + y) = D(x) + D(y)$ and $D(a x) = a D(x)$ for all $a \in A$, $x, y \in B$.
> 2. $D$ satisfies the **Leibniz rule**:
>    $$
>    D(xy) = x D(y) + y D(x) \quad \text{for all } x, y \in B.
>    $$
> The set of all $A$-derivations of $B$ into itself forms a $B$-module, denoted $\operatorname{Der}_A(B, B)$ or $\operatorname{Der}_A(B)$.

> [!info] Kähler Differentials
> For a field extension $K/k$, the **module of Kähler differentials** $\Omega_{K/k}^1$ is the $K$-vector space generated by symbols $dx$ for $x \in K$, subject to the relations:
> 1. $d(x + y) = dx + dy$,
> 2. $d(xy) = x\, dy + y\, dx$,
> 3. $da = 0$ for all $a \in k$.
> The canonical universal derivation $d: K \to \Omega_{K/k}^1$ satisfies $\operatorname{Hom}_K(\Omega_{K/k}^1, K) \cong \operatorname{Der}_k(K, K)$.

## Intuition and Duality

Derivations formalize algebraic differentiation without limits or topology.
For an element $y \in K^\times$, its **logarithmic derivative** is $x = \frac{Dy}{y}$. The Leibniz rule gives:
$$
\frac{D(yz)}{yz} = \frac{Dy}{y} + \frac{Dz}{z},
$$
which mirrors the classic calculus identity $(\log(yz))' = (\log y)' + (\log z)'$.

The differential $d\log P = \frac{dP}{P}$ gives a group homomorphism from the multiplicative group $K^\times$ into the additive group of differentials $\Omega_{K/k}^1$:
$$
d\log(PQ) = d\log P + d\log Q.
$$

## Key Properties

1. **Rational function fields**: If $K=k(t_1,\ldots,t_r)$ with the $t_i$ algebraically independent, prescribed values $D(t_i)=w_i\in K$ determine a unique $k$-derivation. On polynomials use $D(f)=\sum_i(\partial f/\partial t_i)w_i$ and extend to fractions by the quotient rule. Hence the partial derivations form a $K$-basis of $\operatorname{Der}_k(K,K)$, and $dt_1,\ldots,dt_r$ form a basis of $\Omega_{K/k}^1$.
2. **Separable algebraic extension of a derivation**: If $L/K$ is separable algebraic, a derivation $D:K\to K$ extends uniquely to $L$. For an element $\alpha\in L$ with minimal polynomial $f(T)\in K[T]$, the extension must satisfy
   $$
   D(\alpha)=-\frac{f^D(\alpha)}{f'(\alpha)},
   $$
   where $f^D$ means that $D$ is applied to the coefficients. The denominator is nonzero by separability. This formula defines the extension on a simple subextension; uniqueness makes the definitions on finite subextensions compatible. If $D$ vanishes on a subfield $k\subseteq K$, the extension does too.
3. **Dimension and separating bases under finite-generation hypotheses**: Suppose $K/k$ is finitely generated and separably generated, of transcendence degree $r$. Then
   $$
   \dim_K\operatorname{Der}_k(K,K)=\dim_K\Omega_{K/k}^1=r.
   $$
   Elements $t_1,\ldots,t_r\in K$ form a separating transcendence basis if and only if $dt_1,\ldots,dt_r$ form a basis of $\Omega_{K/k}^1$. This is Lang VIII, Proposition 5.5, printed p. 372, PDF p. 387. If, in addition, $L/K$ is separable algebraic, extension of the $r$ basis derivations gives
   $$
   \operatorname{Der}_k(L,L)\cong L\otimes_K\operatorname{Der}_k(K,K),
   \qquad \dim_L\Omega_{L/k}^1=r=\operatorname{tr.deg}(L/k).
   $$
   The hypotheses on $K/k$ are essential to this dimension conclusion; separability of $L/K$ alone is insufficient.
4. **Inseparability and the specified base**: If $\operatorname{char}K=p>0$ and $L=K(\alpha)$ with $\alpha^p=a\in K\setminus K^p$, then $\Omega_{L/K}^1=L\,d\alpha$ has dimension $1$, although $\operatorname{tr.deg}(L/K)=0$. The relation $\alpha^p-a=0$ contributes no differential relation over $K$, so $d\alpha$ is nonzero; equivalently the $K$-derivation sending $\alpha$ to $1$ is well defined. The base field matters: for $k=\mathbb F_p$, $K=k(t^p)$, $L=k(t)$, the extension $L/K$ is inseparable but $\dim_L\Omega_{L/k}^1=\operatorname{tr.deg}(L/k)=1$.
5. **Operator identity**: If $y\ne0$ and $x=Dy/y$, multiplication operators satisfy
   $$
   D+x=y^{-1}\circ D\circ y,
   \qquad (D+x)^n(1)=y^{-1}D^n(y)\quad(n\ge0).
   $$
   This follows from $D(yf)=yD(f)+fD(y)$ and cancellation of the adjacent multiplication operators under composition. These operators are linear over the constant field $\ker D$, not generally over $K$.

## Examples

> [!example] Rational functions and logarithmic differentials
> For $K=k(z_1,\ldots,z_r)$ with the $z_i$ algebraically independent,
> $$
> dP=\sum_{j=1}^r\frac{\partial P}{\partial z_j}\,dz_j.
> $$
> For $P\ne0$ this gives $d\log P=P^{-1}dP$. The product rule proves $d\log(PQ)=d\log P+d\log Q$ for $P,Q\in K^\times$. No actual logarithm in $K$ is needed.

> [!example] Algebraic elements over the constant field
> Suppose $\operatorname{char}k=0$, $K/k$ is an extension, and $D$ is a **$k$-derivation** of $K$. If $\alpha\in K$ is algebraic over $k$ with minimal polynomial $f$, then differentiating $f(\alpha)=0$ gives $f'(\alpha)D(\alpha)=0$. Characteristic zero makes $f$ separable, so $D(\alpha)=0$. The requirement $D|_k=0$ cannot be dropped.

> [!example] Why separability of the upper step is not enough
> Let $k=\mathbb F_p(s)$ and $K=L=k(s^{1/p})$. The identity extension $L/K$ is separable algebraic, but $\dim_L\Omega_{L/k}^1=1$ while $\operatorname{tr.deg}(L/k)=0$. It is the extension over the field appearing in the differential subscript that controls the dimension comparison.

## Source and Proof Status

- Derivations and their extensions are developed in [S2, Ch. VIII, §5, printed pp. 369–370, PDF pp. 384–385]. The finite-dimensional dual-space description of differentials and the separating-basis criterion occur in Proposition 5.5 [printed pp. 371–372, PDF pp. 386–387].
- The presentation of Kähler differentials by generators and relations is given here explicitly. In the finitely generated setting used by the exercises, its dual is the derivation space: a linear functional is uniquely specified by $dx\mapsto D(x)$, and the defining relations are exactly the derivation rules. This identifies it with Lang's finite-dimensional dual-space construction.
- The operator identity and the counterexamples clarifying the required base field and finiteness hypotheses are independent derivations. The converse logarithmic-derivative construction is proved in [[03 - Field Theory/Exercises/Exercise F93 - Logarithmic Derivatives and Derivation Kernel Filtrations|Exercise F93]].
