# Exercise MF24: Conditional Summation and the Weight Two Eisenstein Anomaly

## Problem Statement

> [!question] Original Exercise 1.2.8
> (a) Verify the Fourier expansion of $G_2$.
>
> (b) Suppose that $G_2$ satisfies the identity in (1.4) for two particular matrices $\gamma_1,\gamma_2\in SL_2(\mathbb Z)$. Show that $G_2$ then satisfies the identity for the product $\gamma_1\gamma_2$ and the inverse $\gamma_1^{-1}$ as well. Thus to establish (1.4) it suffices to prove the identity for a set of generators for $SL_2(\mathbb Z)$. (Hints for this exercise are at the end of the book.)
>
> (c) Recall that $SL_2(\mathbb Z)$ is generated by $\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\0&1\end{pmatrix}$ and $\begin{pmatrix}0&-1\\1&0\end{pmatrix}$. The Fourier expansion for $G_2$ shows that it satisfies (1.4) for $\gamma=\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\0&1\end{pmatrix}$. For the other generator, show that
> $$
> (G_2[\begin{pmatrix}0&-1\\1&0\end{pmatrix}]_2)(\tau)
> =\tau^{-2}G_2(-1/\tau)
> =\sum_{d\in\mathbb Z}\sum_{c\in\mathbb Z'_d}\frac1{(c\tau+d)^2}
> =2\zeta(2)+\sum_{d\in\mathbb Z}\sum_{c\ne0}\frac1{(c\tau+d)^2},
> $$
> which differs from $G_2(\tau)=2\zeta(2)+\sum_{c\ne0}\sum_{d\in\mathbb Z}(c\tau+d)^{-2}$ in the reversed order of summation. Next use partial fractions to show that
> $$
> \sum_{c\ne0}\sum_{d\in\mathbb Z}\frac1{(c\tau+d)(c\tau+d+1)}=0.
> $$
> Subtract this from $G_2$ to get
> $$
> G_2(\tau)=2\zeta(2)+\sum_{c\ne0,d\in\mathbb Z}\frac1{(c\tau+d)^2(c\tau+d+1)},
> $$
> where now the sum is absolutely convergent by Exercise 1.1.4 and the limit comparison test, and thus it can have its order of summation reversed. Doing so and separating the convergence terms back out shows that
> $$
> G_2(\tau)=\tau^{-2}G_2(-1/\tau)-\sum_{d\in\mathbb Z}\sum_{c\ne0}\frac1{(c\tau+d)(c\tau+d+1)}.
> $$
> The error term is
> $$
> -\lim_{N\to\infty}\sum_{d=-N}^{N-1}\sum_{c\ne0}\left(\frac1{c\tau+d}-\frac1{c\tau+d+1}\right).
> $$
> Reverse the order and the inner sum telescopes. Manipulate the result into an expression including a sum for $\pi\cot(\pi N/\tau)$ per the first equality of (1.1). Finally, use the other side of (1.1) to take the limit.
>
> (d) Show that
> $$
> \frac{\pi}{j(\gamma,\tau)^2\operatorname{Im}(\gamma\tau)}
> =\frac{\pi}{\operatorname{Im}\tau}-\frac{2\pi ic}{c\tau+d}
> \quad\text{for }\gamma=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\c&d\end{pmatrix}\in SL_2(\mathbb Z),
> $$
> and therefore the function $G_2(\tau)-\pi/\operatorname{Im}\tau$ is weight-$2$ invariant under $SL_2(\mathbb Z)$.
>
> (e) Show that $G_{2,N}\in M_2(\Gamma_0(N))$.
>
> (f) Compute $G_2(i)$ and $G_2(\mu_3)$ where $\mu_3=e^{2\pi i/3}$.
>
> **Definitions and referenced formulas** (printed p. 18 / PDF p. 33). The summation convention is
> $$
> G_2(\tau)=\sum_{c\in\mathbb Z}\left(\sum_{d\in\mathbb Z'_c}(c\tau+d)^{-2}\right),
> \qquad \mathbb Z'_c=\begin{cases}\mathbb Z\setminus\{0\},&c=0,\\\mathbb Z,&c\ne0.\end{cases}
> $$
> The inner sum is taken first. With $q=e^{2\pi i\tau}$ and $\sigma(n)=\sum_{d\mid n,\ d>0}d$, the target expansion and transformation are
> $$
> G_2(\tau)=2\zeta(2)-8\pi^2\sum_{n\ge1}\sigma(n)q^n,
> \qquad (G_2[\gamma]_2)(\tau)=G_2(\tau)-\frac{2\pi ic}{c\tau+d}.\tag{1.4}
> $$
> Here $j(\gamma,\tau)=c\tau+d$ is the automorphy factor and $G_{2,N}(\tau)=G_2(\tau)-NG_2(N\tau)$.

## Hints

> [!hint]- Hint 1
> Apply the weight-two instance of the one-dimensional Eisenstein summation formula to each nonzero $c$, and only then sum over $c$.

> [!hint]- Hint 2
> For the inversion, subtract the telescoping term before changing the order. The new summand has cubic, rather than quadratic, decay in the lattice coordinates.

> [!hint]- Hint 3
> The remaining finite-cutoff error is $-2\pi\tau^{-1}\cot(\pi M/\tau)+2/M$. Since $\operatorname{Im}(1/\tau)<0$, the cotangent tends to $i$.

> [!hint]- Hint 4
> For (e), conjugate $\gamma\in\Gamma_0(N)$ to $\begin{pmatrix}a&Nb\\c/N&d\end{pmatrix}$. For (f), apply the nonholomorphic completion at points fixed by nontrivial weight-two rotations.

## Solution

> [!success]- Solution
> **Proof scope:** Independent solution
>
> **(a): the ordered series.** The weight-two one-dimensional formula from (1.2), derived in Exercise 1.1.6, says for $\operatorname{Im}z>0$ that
> $$
> \sum_{d\in\mathbb Z}(z+d)^{-2}=-4\pi^2\sum_{m\ge1}m e^{2\pi imz}.
> $$
> The inner sums for $c$ and $-c$ agree after replacing $d$ by $-d$. The $c=0$ term is $2\zeta(2)$. Hence
> $$
> G_2(\tau)=2\zeta(2)-8\pi^2\sum_{c,m\ge1}m q^{cm}
> =2\zeta(2)-8\pi^2\sum_{n\ge1}\sigma(n)q^n.
> $$
> The double series is absolutely and locally uniformly convergent: for $|q|\le\rho<1$,
> $$
> \sum_{c,m\ge1}m\rho^{cm}
> =\sum_{m\ge1}\frac{m\rho^m}{1-\rho^m}
> \le\frac1{1-\rho}\sum_{m\ge1}m\rho^m<\infty.
> $$
> This justifies the last rearrangement and proves that the ordered series is holomorphic.
>
> **(b): propagation of the anomaly.** Set $A_\gamma(\tau)=2\pi ic/j(\gamma,\tau)$ for the bottom-left entry $c$ of $\gamma$. The automorphy-factor identity
> $$
> j(\gamma\gamma',\tau)=j(\gamma,\gamma'\tau)j(\gamma',\tau)
> $$
> and logarithmic differentiation give
> $$
> A_{\gamma\gamma'}=A_\gamma[\gamma']_2+A_{\gamma'}.
> $$
> Indeed $(\gamma'\tau)'=j(\gamma',\tau)^{-2}$ and $\partial_\tau\log j(\gamma,\tau)=c/j(\gamma,\tau)$, so this identity is an equality of rational functions and does not depend on choosing global logarithms. If $G_2[\gamma]_2=G_2-A_\gamma$ and the same holds for $\gamma'$, then
> $$
> G_2[\gamma\gamma']_2
> =(G_2-A_\gamma)[\gamma']_2
> =G_2-A_{\gamma'}-A_\gamma[\gamma']_2
> =G_2-A_{\gamma\gamma'}.
> $$
> For inverses, $0=A_I=A_\gamma[\gamma^{-1}]_2+A_{\gamma^{-1}}$; applying $[\gamma^{-1}]_2$ to the identity for $\gamma$ gives the required formula. Thus generators suffice.
>
> **(c): inversion and the summation-order error.** Put $u=c\tau+d$. For each fixed $c\ne0$ the series $\sum_d1/(u(u+1))$ is absolutely convergent, and its partial fractions telescope to zero. The identity
> $$
> \frac1{u^2}-\frac1{u(u+1)}=\frac1{u^2(u+1)}
> $$
> therefore gives the source's corrected summand. For fixed $\tau\in\mathcal H$, the real-linear map $(c,d)\mapsto c\tau+d$ is invertible, so $|c\tau+d|\ge C_\tau\max(|c|,|d|)$. Outside finitely many pairs, $|u+1|\ge|u|/2$. The corrected summand is consequently bounded by a constant times $\max(|c|,|d|)^{-3}$. There are $8n$ integer pairs with maximum norm $n$, so its absolute sum is dominated by $\sum_{n\ge1}8n/n^3<\infty$.
>
> We may now interchange the corrected sums. Splitting them back for each fixed $d$ gives
> $$
> G_2(\tau)=\tau^{-2}G_2(-1/\tau)-\lim_{M\to\infty}U_M,
> \quad U_M=\sum_{d=-M}^{M-1}\sum_{c\ne0}\left(\frac1{c\tau+d}-\frac1{c\tau+d+1}\right).
> $$
> Here the reversed quadratic sum is exactly $\tau^{-2}G_2(-1/\tau)$ after relabeling the original inner and outer indices; its row sums converge by (a). For finite $M$, the inner series defining $U_M$ has quadratic decay in $c$, so finite summation and that series commute. Telescoping in $d$ yields
> $$
> U_M=\sum_{c\ne0}\left(\frac1{c\tau-M}-\frac1{c\tau+M}\right).
> $$
> The partial-fraction formula $\sum_{c\in\mathbb Z}^{\rm sym}(c+z)^{-1}=\pi\cot(\pi z)$, which is (1.1), gives
> $$
> U_M=-\frac{2\pi}{\tau}\cot\left(\frac{\pi M}{\tau}\right)+\frac2M.
> $$
> The term $2/M$ removes the $c=0$ contribution $-2/M$. Since $\operatorname{Im}(M/\tau)<0$, the identity
> $$
> \cot z=i\frac{e^{2iz}+1}{e^{2iz}-1}
> $$
> shows that the cotangent tends to $i$. Thus $U_M\to-2\pi i/\tau$ and
> $$
> \tau^{-2}G_2(-1/\tau)=G_2(\tau)-\frac{2\pi i}{\tau}.
> $$
> The translation identity is immediate from (a). Part (b) now proves (1.4) for all $SL_2(\mathbb Z)$.
>
> **(d): completion.** Write $y=\operatorname{Im}\tau$ and $j=c\tau+d$. The imaginary-part formula gives
> $$
> \frac{\pi}{j^2\operatorname{Im}(\gamma\tau)}
> =\frac{\pi\bar j}{yj}
> =\frac{\pi}{y}-\frac{\pi(j-\bar j)}{yj}
> =\frac{\pi}{y}-\frac{2\pi ic}{j}.
> $$
> Subtracting this from (1.4) proves that $G_2^*(\tau)=G_2(\tau)-\pi/y$ is weight-two invariant. It is a real-analytic completion, not a holomorphic modular form.
>
> **(e): level $N$.** For $\gamma=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\c&d\end{pmatrix}\in\Gamma_0(N)$, put $\gamma_N=\begin{pmatrix}a&Nb\\c/N&d\end{pmatrix}\in SL_2(\mathbb Z)$. Then $N\gamma\tau=\gamma_N(N\tau)$, with the same automorphy factor $c\tau+d$. The two anomalies in $G_2(\gamma\tau)-NG_2(N\gamma\tau)$ cancel, proving weight-two invariance of $G_{2,N}$. Its Fourier coefficients are
> $$
> -8\pi^2\bigl(\sigma(n)-N\sigma(n/N)\bigr),
> $$
> with $\sigma(n/N)=0$ unless $N\mid n$. Since $\sigma(n)\le n(n+1)/2$, these have polynomial growth. The series is holomorphic on $\mathcal H$ and at $\infty$, so Proposition 1.2.4, proved independently in Exercise 1.2.6, supplies holomorphy at all cusps. Thus $G_{2,N}\in M_2(\Gamma_0(N))$, including $G_{2,1}=0$.
>
> **(f): fixed points.** The matrix $S=\begin{pmatrix}0&-1\\1&0\end{pmatrix}$ fixes $i$ and has automorphy factor $i$. Weight-two invariance of $G_2^*$ gives $G_2^*(i)=i^2G_2^*(i)$, so $G_2^*(i)=0$ and $G_2(i)=\pi$.
>
> The matrix $ST=\begin{pmatrix}0&-1\\1&1\end{pmatrix}$ fixes $\rho=\mu_3$ and has factor $\rho+1$, whose square is $\rho\ne1$. The same argument gives $G_2^*(\rho)=0$, and hence
> $$
> G_2(\mu_3)=\frac{\pi}{\operatorname{Im}\mu_3}=\frac{2\pi}{\sqrt3}.
> $$
