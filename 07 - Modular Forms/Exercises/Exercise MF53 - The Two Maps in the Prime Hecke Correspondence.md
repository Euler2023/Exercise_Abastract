# Exercise MF53: The Two Maps in the Prime Hecke Correspondence

## Problem Statement

> [!question] Original Exercise 5.2.10
> Specializing the general double coset operator to the data for $T_p$, i.e., $\Gamma_1=\Gamma_2=\Gamma_1(N)$ and $\alpha=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&p\end{pmatrix}$, so that $\Gamma_3=\Gamma_1^0(N,p)$, the corresponding modular curve $X_3$ is denoted $X_1^0(N,p)$. Show that in the configuration (5.1) the maps $\pi_2$ and $\pi_1\circ\alpha$ from $X_1^0(N,p)$ to $X_1(N)$ are
> $$
> \pi_2(\Gamma_1^0(N,p)\tau)=\Gamma_1(N)\tau,
> \qquad(\pi_1\circ\alpha)(\Gamma_1^0(N,p)\tau)=\Gamma_1(N)\tau/p.
> $$
> The moduli space versions of these maps were given in (1.11) and derived in Exercise 1.5.6. Show that their descriptions and the commutative diagram give another derivation of (5.6).
>
> **Printed diagram, printed p. 179 / PDF p. 193, transcribed.**
> $$
> \begin{array}{ccccc}
> S_1(N)&\longleftarrow&S_1^0(N,p)&\longrightarrow&S_1(N)\\
> \psi_1\downarrow&&\psi_1^0\downarrow&&\downarrow\psi_1\\
> X_1(N)&\underset{\pi_2}{\longleftarrow}&X_1^0(N,p)&\underset{\pi_1\circ\alpha}{\longrightarrow}&X_1(N).
> \end{array}
> $$
> Here $S_1^0(N,p)$ consists of $[E,C,Q]$ with $C$ of order $p$, $Q$ of order $N$, and $C\cap\langle Q\rangle=0$. Its maps are $[E,C,Q]\mapsto[E,Q]$ and $[E,C,Q]\mapsto[E/C,Q+C]$. Formula (5.6) is $T_p[E,Q]=\sum_C[E/C,Q+C]$ over these admissible $C$.

## Hints

> [!hint]- Hint 1
> The map $\alpha$ on the upper half-plane divides its argument by $p$.

> [!hint]- Hint 2
> The standard triple over $\tau$ is $[E_\tau,\langle\tau/p+\Lambda_\tau\rangle,1/N+\Lambda_\tau]$. Its quotient lattice is $\Lambda_{\tau/p}$.

## Solution

> [!success]- Solution
> **Proof scope:** Independent derivation.
>
> The inclusion $\Gamma_3\subseteq\Gamma_2=\Gamma_1(N)$ makes $\pi_2$ the forgetting map on orbit classes, so $\pi_2(\Gamma_3\tau)=\Gamma_1(N)\tau$. The intermediate isomorphism takes $\Gamma_3\tau$ to $\Gamma_3'\alpha\tau$, and projection to $X_1(N)$ then gives $\Gamma_1(N)\alpha\tau=\Gamma_1(N)(\tau/p)$. Conjugation $\alpha\Gamma_3\alpha^{-1}\subseteq\Gamma_1(N)$ ensures the second formula is well defined.
>
> In the marked-torus dictionary, $\Gamma_3\tau$ corresponds to
> $$
> [E_\tau,C_0,Q],\qquad
> C_0=\langle\tau/p+\Lambda_\tau\rangle,\quad Q=1/N+\Lambda_\tau.
> $$
> Forgetting $C_0$ leaves the same marked torus and hence the class $\Gamma_1(N)\tau$. The full inverse image of $C_0$ is
> $$
> \Lambda_\tau+\mathbb Z\tau/p=(\tau/p)\mathbb Z+\mathbb Z=\Lambda_{\tau/p}.
> $$
> Therefore quotienting by $C_0$ gives $[E_{\tau/p},1/N+\Lambda_{\tau/p}]$, corresponding to $\Gamma_1(N)(\tau/p)$. This proves both sides of the square commute, including the marked point, rather than only the underlying unmarked curves.
>
> For a general $[E,Q]$, the fiber of the forgetting map consists of the admissible cyclic subgroups $C\subset E$ of order $p$, retaining coincidences of enhanced isomorphism classes with the correspondence multiplicities. The complete representative calculation is Exercise 5.2.8: there are the $p$ groups generated by $(\tau+j)/p$ in a lattice presentation, and additionally $\langle1/p\rangle$ if $p\nmid N$. The latter meets $\langle Q\rangle$ nontrivially when $p\mid N$ and is then omitted.
>
> The double coset operation first takes the inverse-image multiset under $\pi_2$ and then applies $\pi_1\circ\alpha$ to each term. By the two commuting squares just proved, the same process in moduli language first lists these $[E,C,Q]$ and then replaces each by $[E/C,Q+C]$. Thus its resulting divisor is exactly
> $$
> T_p[E,Q]=\sum_{\substack{C\subset E,\ |C|=p\\C\cap\langle Q\rangle=0}}[E/C,Q+C],
> $$
> which is (5.6). Repetitions must remain; the calculation at $[i]$ in Exercise 5.2.9 exhibits why.
