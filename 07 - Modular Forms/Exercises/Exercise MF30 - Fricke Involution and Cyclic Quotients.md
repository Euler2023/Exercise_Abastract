# Exercise MF30: Fricke Involution and Cyclic Quotients

## Problem Statement

> [!question] Original Exercise 1.5.4
> Let $w_N=\begin{pmatrix}0&-1\\N&0\end{pmatrix}\in GL_2^+(\mathbb Q)$. Show that $w_N$ normalizes the group $\Gamma_0(N)$ and so gives an automorphism $\Gamma_0(N)\tau\mapsto\Gamma_0(N)w_N(\tau)$ of the modular curve $Y_0(N)$. Show that this automorphism is an involution (meaning it has order $2$) and describe the corresponding automorphism of the moduli space $S_0(N)$. (A hint for this exercise is at the end of the book.)

> [!warning] Source issue and scope
> The instruction “has order $2$” needs the boundary condition $N>1$. At $N=1$ the induced automorphism of $Y_0(1)$ is the identity, of order $1$. The displayed original statement is preserved; the universally valid conclusion is $W_N^2=1$, with exact order $2$ for $N>1$.

## Hints

> [!hint]- Hint 1
> Compute $w_N\gamma w_N^{-1}$ for $\gamma\in\Gamma_0(N)$ and $w_N^2$. Scalar matrices induce the identity on $\mathcal H$.

> [!hint]- Hint 2
> For $E_\tau$ and $C=\langle1/N\rangle$, the quotient lattice is $\tau\mathbb Z+(1/N)\mathbb Z$. Multiply $\Lambda_{-1/(N\tau)}$ by $\tau$ to obtain this lattice.

## Solution

> [!success]- Solution
> **Proof scope:** Independent solution
>
> For $\gamma=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\Nc&d\end{pmatrix}$,
> $$
> w_N\gamma w_N^{-1}=\begin{pmatrix}d&-c\\-Nb&a\end{pmatrix}\in\Gamma_0(N).
> $$
> Conjugation by $w_N^{-1}$ gives the reverse containment, so $w_N$ normalizes the group. The fractional transformation is $\tau\mapsto-1/(N\tau)$ and $w_N^2=-NI$, so the induced automorphism has square equal to the identity.
>
> Write $\pi:E\to E/C$ for the quotient isogeny. The moduli description is
> $$
> W_N:[E,C]\longmapsto[E/C,\pi(E[N])]=[E/C,E[N]/C].
> $$
> The second group is cyclic of order $N$: normalize $E=E_\tau$, $C=\langle1/N\rangle$ and put $L=\tau\mathbb Z+(1/N)\mathbb Z$. Then $E/C=\mathbb C/L$, and $E[N]/C$ is generated by $\tau/N+L$. Multiplication by $\tau$ identifies $E_{-1/(N\tau)}$ with $\mathbb C/L$ and sends its canonical marked cyclic group $\langle1/N\rangle$ to $\langle\tau/N+L\rangle$, proving the asserted formula.
>
> A second application enlarges $L$ by $\tau/N$ to $(1/N)\Lambda_\tau$. The resulting curve is identified with $E_\tau$ by multiplication by $N$. Under this map the second marked subgroup is again $C$: it is generated before scaling by $1/N^2+(1/N)\Lambda_\tau$, which becomes $1/N+\Lambda_\tau$. Thus the formula itself also verifies $W_N^2=1$.
>
> For completeness it has exact order $2$ when $N>1$. If it fixed every orbit, take $\tau=iy$ with $y^2$ irrational. Some $\gamma=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\c&d\end{pmatrix}\in\Gamma_0(N)$ would send $iy$ to $i/(Ny)$. Taking imaginary parts gives $(N-c^2)y^2=d^2$. Irrationality forces $N=c^2$ and $d=0$. Since $N\mid c$, this implies $N=N^2t^2$ for an integer $t$, impossible for $N>1$. For $N=1$, $w_1\in SL_2(\mathbb Z)$, so the map is the identity.
