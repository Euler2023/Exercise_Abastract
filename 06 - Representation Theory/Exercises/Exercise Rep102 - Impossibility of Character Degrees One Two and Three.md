# Exercise Rep102: Impossibility of Character Degrees One Two and Three

## Problem Statement

> [!question] Miscellaneous Exercise M.2
> What can be said about a group that has exactly three irreducible characters, of dimensions $1$, $2$, and $3$, respectively?

## Hints

> [!hint]- Hint 1
> Use both the degree-square formula and the meaning of having only one linear character.

## Solution

> [!success]- Solution
> The degree-square formula would give
>
> $$
> |G|=1^2+2^2+3^2=14.
> $$
>
> Having only one one-dimensional character means $G^{\mathrm{ab}}$ is trivial, so $G$ would be perfect. But every group of order $14$ has a normal Sylow $7$-subgroup and a quotient of order $2$, hence a nontrivial linear character. This is a contradiction. Therefore no finite group has exactly those three irreducible character degrees. $\square$
