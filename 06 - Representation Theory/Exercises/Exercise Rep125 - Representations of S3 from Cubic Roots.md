# Exercise Rep125: Representations of S3 from Cubic Roots

## Problem Statement

> [!question] Lang XVIII.1 — The group $S_3$
> Let $S_3$ be the symmetric group on $3$ elements,
>
> (a) Show that there are three conjugacy classes.
>
> (b) There are two characters of dimension $1$, on $S_3/A_3$.
>
> (c) Let $d_i$ ($i=1,2,3$) be the dimensions of the irreducible characters. Since $\sum d_i^2=6$, the third irreducible character has dimension $2$. Show that the third representation can be realized by considering a cubic equation $X^3+aX+b=0$, whose Galois group is $S_3$ over a field $k$. Let $V$ be the $k$-vector space generated by the roots. Show that this space is $2$-dimensional and gives the desired representation, which remains irreducible after tensoring with $k^a$.
>
> (d) Let $G=S_3$. Write down an idempotent for each one of the simple components of $\mathbb C[G]$. What is the multiplicity of each irreducible representation of $G$ in the regular representation on $\mathbb C[G]$?

> [!info] Inherited hypothesis and coefficient fields
> Lang's standing convention is $\operatorname{char}k\nmid\lvert G\rvert$ [Ch. XVIII, printed p. 667, PDF p. 682], so in (c) it excludes characteristics $2,3$. The character enumeration and group-algebra idempotents below are over $\mathbb C$. The root-space construction is over $k$, and $k^a$ denotes an algebraic closure. Its absolute irreducibility actually only needs $\operatorname{char}k\ne3$; this sharper observation does not remove the source's standing convention.

## Hints

> [!hint]- Hint 1: Start with the permutation module
> The action on three letters has character equal to the number of fixed letters. Remove the constant line. Relate the resulting two-dimensional module to the map $k^3\to V$ sending the standard basis vectors to the three roots.

> [!hint]- Hint 2: Use differences and central projections
> A nonzero invariant subspace of the sum-zero permutation module contains a difference $e_i-e_j$, obtained by subtracting a transposed vector. For (d), use $e_\chi=(\chi(1)/6)\sum_{g\in S_3}\chi(g^{-1})g$.

## Solution

> [!success]- Independent derivation of the representations and idempotents
> **(a).** Conjugation relabels cycles, so cycle type is invariant. All transpositions are conjugate, and the two $3$-cycles are conjugate. Thus the three classes are $\{1\}$, $\{(12),(13),(23)\}$, and $\{(123),(132)\}$, of sizes $1,3,2$.
>
> **(b).** In a one-dimensional complex representation, all transpositions have the same scalar $\varepsilon$, because they are conjugate. Their squares are $1$, so $\varepsilon=\pm1$. Since transpositions generate $S_3$, there are at most two such representations. Both occur: the trivial character $1$ and the sign character $\mathrm{sgn}$. They factor through $S_3/A_3\simeq C_2$.
>
> **(c), complex character.** The permutation character on $\mathbb C^3$ has values $3,1,0$ on the three classes. The constant line is trivial, and the sum-zero complement
>
> $$
> W_k=\{(x_1,x_2,x_3)\in k^3:x_1+x_2+x_3=0\}
> $$
>
> has character $\chi= (2,0,-1)$ when $k=\mathbb C$. Its squared character norm is $(4+3\cdot0+2\cdot1)/6=1$, so it is irreducible. The character-class theorem gives three irreducible characters, and their squared degrees sum to $6$, so $1,\mathrm{sgn},\chi$ are all of them.
>
> **Root realization and absolute irreducibility.** We give an argument valid over any field of characteristic different from $3$. Let $L$ be the splitting field, with distinct roots $\alpha_1,\alpha_2,\alpha_3$ permuted by the full group $S_3$. Their sum is zero. The equivariant map
>
> $$
> T:k^3\longrightarrow V\subseteq L,
> \qquad (x_i)\longmapsto\sum_i x_i\alpha_i
> $$
>
> kills the constant line $D=k(1,1,1)$. Since $3$ is invertible, $k^3=D\oplus W_k$, so the restriction $T|_{W_k}$ is onto $V$.
>
> To prove $W_k$ simple, let $0\ne U\subseteq W_k$ be invariant. A nonzero vector $u\in U$ cannot have all coordinates equal: otherwise its coordinate sum would be $3c=0$, giving $u=0$. Choose $i,j$ with $u_i\ne u_j$. Then
>
> $$
> u-(ij)u=(u_i-u_j)(e_i-e_j)\in U.
> $$
>
> Thus $e_i-e_j\in U$, and its permutations span $W_k$. Hence $U=W_k$. This proof works after every field extension of $k$, so $W_k$ is absolutely irreducible.
>
> The map $T$ has nonzero image, because the three distinct roots are not all zero. Thus $T|_{W_k}$ is a nonzero map from a simple module; its kernel is zero. Consequently $V\simeq W_k$, $\dim_kV=2$, and $V\otimes_k k^a\simeq W_{k^a}$ is irreducible. This identifies the root action with the standard representation just described over $\mathbb C$.
>
> **(d).** Put $c=(123)$, $c^2=(132)$, and $T_2=(12)+(13)+(23)$ in the complex group algebra. The central idempotents are
>
> $$
> e_{1}=\frac{1}{6}(1+T_2+c+c^2),\qquad
> e_{\mathrm{sgn}}=\frac{1}{6}(1-T_2+c+c^2),
> $$
>
> $$
> e_{\chi}=1-e_1-e_{\mathrm{sgn}}
> =\frac{1}{3}(2\cdot1-c-c^2).
> $$
>
> The first two averaging operators project onto the trivial and sign summands, respectively: multiplying a group element into the sums gives the corresponding scalar, and summing the sign values gives zero for their product. Thus they are orthogonal central idempotents. Their complement is also a central idempotent. Equivalently, all three follow from the displayed character-idempotent formula, which acts as the identity on the corresponding irreducible and as zero on every other irreducible. Since the three irreducibles exhaust the list, these are the identities of the three simple components.
>
> The regular character is $6$ at the identity and $0$ elsewhere, so its inner product with an irreducible character $\eta$ is $\eta(1)$. Hence the multiplicities are respectively $1,1,2$, and the dimensions of the three components are $1,1,4$.

## Notes

- **Source and proof status:** All four parts were checked at [S2, Ch. XVIII, Ex. 1, printed p. 722, PDF p. 737]. The inherited characteristic convention was checked at printed p. 667, PDF p. 682. The independent solution uses the proved character results [S2, Ch. XVIII, Corollary 5.13, Proposition 5.14, Theorems 5.15 and 5.17, printed pp. 683–685, PDF pp. 698–700]: regular multiplicities, central character projections, the class-function basis, and the norm-one criterion.
- **Characteristic boundary:** Without the inherited hypothesis, (c) can fail. Over $k=\mathbb F_3(t)$, $X^3-tX-1$ is irreducible: a rational root would lie in $\mathbb F_3[t]$ by the monic polynomial rational-root argument, and comparison of degrees rules out every nonconstant polynomial root, while substitution rules out constant roots. If $\alpha$ is a root and $s^2=t$, its roots are $\alpha,\alpha+s,\alpha-s$. The extensions generated by $\alpha$ and by $s$ have coprime degrees $3$ and $2$, so their compositum is the degree-$6$ splitting field with Galois group $S_3$. The root span is $k\alpha\oplus ks$, but $ks$ is a proper invariant line. Thus the claimed irreducibility requires a characteristic condition.
- **Scalar-extension boundary:** $V\otimes_k k^a$ is the abstract base extension of the root representation. It is not identified with the one-dimensional $k^a$-span of numerical roots inside $k^a$.
