# Exercise Rep57: Failure of Complete Reducibility in Characteristic p

## Problem Statement

> [!question] Exercise 3.5
> Let $x$ be a generator of a cyclic group $G$ of order $p$. Sending
>
> $$
> x\longmapsto\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\0&1\end{pmatrix}
> $$
>
> defines a matrix representation $G\to GL_2(\mathbb F_p)$. Prove that this representation is not the direct sum of irreducible representations.

## Hints

> [!hint]- Hint 1
> The line spanned by the first basis vector is invariant.

> [!hint]- Hint 2
> Show that it has no invariant complementary line.

## Solution

> [!success]- Solution
> Put $U=\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\0&1\end{pmatrix}$. In characteristic $p$,
>
> $$
> U^p=(I+N)^p=I+N^p=I,
> $$
>
> where $N^2=0$, so this is indeed a representation of $C_p$. The line $L=\mathbb F_pe_1$ is invariant. If an invariant complementary line existed, it would be generated by $v=ae_1+be_2$ with $b\ne0$, and invariance would give $Uv=\lambda v$. But $U$ has only the eigenvalue $1$, so $Uv=v$, which forces $b=0$, a contradiction.
>
> Thus the invariant subrepresentation $L$ has no invariant complement, and the representation cannot be a direct sum of irreducibles. Equivalently, every one-dimensional $\mathbb F_p$-representation of $C_p$ is trivial because $t^p-1=(t-1)^p$, whereas $U\ne I$. $\square$
