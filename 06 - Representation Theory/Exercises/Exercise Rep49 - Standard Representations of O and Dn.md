# Exercise Rep49: Standard Representations of O and Dn

## Problem Statement

> [!question] Exercise 1.2
> **(a)** Choose a suitable basis for $\mathbb R^3$ and write the standard representation of the octahedral group $O$ explicitly.
> **(b)** Do the same for the dihedral group $D_n$.

## Hints

> [!hint]- Hint 1
> Use coordinate axes through opposite faces of a cube for $O$.

> [!hint]- Hint 2
> Generate $D_n$ by one rotation and one reflection.

## Solution

> [!success]- Solution
> **(a)** Place a cube at the origin with faces perpendicular to the coordinate axes. Every orientation-preserving symmetry permutes the three coordinate axes and changes an even-compatible set of signs. Thus the standard representation identifies $O$ with the determinant-one signed permutation matrices. For example it is generated by
>
> $$
> A=\begin{pmatrix}0&-1&0\\1&0&0\\0&0&1\end{pmatrix},
> \qquad
> B=\begin{pmatrix}0&0&1\\1&0&0\\0&1&0\end{pmatrix},
> $$
>
> representing a quarter-turn about the $z$-axis and a third-turn about the diagonal $(1,1,1)$.
>
> **(b)** With the polygon in the Euclidean plane and $\theta=2\pi/n$, take
>
> $$
> R=\begin{pmatrix}\cos\theta&-\sin\theta\\ \sin\theta&\cos\theta\end{pmatrix},
> \qquad
> S=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&-1\end{pmatrix}.
> $$
>
> Then $R^n=S^2=I$ and $SRS=R^{-1}$, so $r\mapsto R$, $s\mapsto S$ is the standard two-dimensional representation of $D_n$. $\square$
