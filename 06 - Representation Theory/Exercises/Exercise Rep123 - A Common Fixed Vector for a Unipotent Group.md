# Exercise Rep123: A Common Fixed Vector for a Unipotent Group

## Problem Statement

> [!question] Lang XVII.8 — Kolchin
> Let $E$ be a finite-dimensional vector space over a field $k$. Let $G$ be a subgroup of $GL(E)$ such that every element $A\in G$ is of type $I+N$ where $N$ is nilpotent. Assume $E\ne0$. Show that there exists an element $v\in E$, $v\ne0$ such that $Av=v$ for all $A\in G$.
>
> **Printed hint.** First reduce the question to the case when $k$ is algebraically closed by showing that the problem amounts to solving linear equations. Secondly, reduce it to the case when $E$ is a simple $k[G]$-module. Combining Burnside's theorem with the fact that $\operatorname{tr}(A)=\operatorname{tr}(I)$ for all $A\in G$, show that if $A_0\in G$, $A_0=I+N$, then $\operatorname{tr}(NX)=0$ for all $X\in\operatorname{End}_k(E)$, and hence that $N=0$, $A_0=I$.

## Hints

> [!hint]- Hint 1: Use finite-dimensionality twice
> The rows of all matrices $A-I$, although indexed by a possibly infinite group, span a finite-dimensional space of linear equations over $k$. After extending scalars, choose a nonzero invariant subspace of smallest dimension.

> [!hint]- Hint 2: Pair a nilpotent difference with the whole matrix algebra
> On that simple invariant subspace, Burnside's theorem says that the acting group spans the full endomorphism algebra. For fixed $A_0$, calculate $\operatorname{tr}((A_0-I)A)$ using $A_0A\in G$. Test the resulting trace identity against matrix units.

## Solution

> [!success]- Independent derivation using the source's Burnside theorem
> Write $d=\dim_k E>0$. The proof uses the following named source theorem, with its hypotheses unchanged: if $K$ is algebraically closed, $W$ is a nonzero finite-dimensional $K$-space, and a multiplicative submonoid $H\subseteq GL(W)$ has no invariant subspaces other than $0,W$, then
> $$
> K[H]=\operatorname{span}_K H=\operatorname{End}_K(W).
> $$
> Here $K[H]$ denotes the image algebra generated by the operators, rather than the abstract monoid algebra. Lang proves this in Chapter XVII, §3, Corollaries 3.3–3.4, printed pp. 648–649, PDF pp. 663–664, using the density theorem. No characteristic-zero hypothesis occurs in this result.
>
> **1. Scalar extension and descent of the fixed-vector condition.** Choose a $k$-basis of $E$. For each $A\in G$, the condition $(A-I)v=0$ is a homogeneous system with coefficients in $k$. Let $L\subseteq k^d$ be the span of all rows of all matrices $A-I$. It has a finite basis $\ell_1,\ldots,\ell_r$ chosen from this collection of rows, with $0\le r\le d$. A vector is fixed by every $A$ exactly when it is killed by these $r$ row equations: every other row is their $k$-linear combination.
>
> Let $K$ be an algebraic closure of $k$. The matrix with rows $\ell_1,\ldots,\ell_r$ has the same rank $r$ over $K$ as over $k$. Indeed, its nonzero minors remain nonzero under the field inclusion, and its zero minors remain zero. Consequently its kernel is nonzero over $K$ if and only if $r<d$, which is also precisely the condition for a nonzero kernel over $k$. The same finite row equations describe the common fixed vectors after scalar extension.
>
> Thus it suffices to find a nonzero common fixed vector in $E_K=K\otimes_k E$. Each extended operator is still unipotent: if $(A-I)^s=0$ on $E$, the same identity holds on $E_K$.
>
> **2. Pass to a simple invariant subspace.** Among the nonzero $G$-invariant $K$-subspaces of $E_K$, choose one, $W$, of smallest dimension. Such a subspace exists because $E_K$ itself is one and the dimensions are positive integers at most $d$. It is simple under the action by its minimality. Each restriction $A|_W$ is invertible: both $A$ and $A^{-1}$ preserve $W$. Let $H\subseteq GL(W)$ be the image of $G$ on $W$.
>
> Every $h\in H$ is unipotent, because $h-I_W$ is the restriction of a nilpotent operator. If $N$ is any nilpotent operator on a finite-dimensional space, a basis adapted to the filtration $0\subseteq\ker N\subseteq\ker N^2\subseteq\cdots$ gives a strictly triangular matrix for $N$: it sends each filtration step into the preceding one. Hence $\operatorname{tr}(N)=0$ in every characteristic. In particular,
> $$
> \operatorname{tr}(h)=\operatorname{tr}(I_W)=(\dim_K W)\cdot1_K
> \qquad(h\in H).
> $$
>
> **3. Burnside and the trace pairing force the action on $W$ to be trivial.** Apply the quoted theorem to $H$ acting on $W$. Its assumptions hold because $K$ is algebraically closed and $W$ is nonzero, finite-dimensional, and simple. Thus $H$ spans $\operatorname{End}_K(W)$.
>
> Fix $h_0\in H$ and put $N=h_0-I_W$. Since $h_0h\in H$ for every $h\in H$,
> $$
> \operatorname{tr}(Nh)
> =\operatorname{tr}(h_0h)-\operatorname{tr}(h)
> =\operatorname{tr}(I_W)-\operatorname{tr}(I_W)=0.
> $$
> Linearity and the spanning assertion imply $\operatorname{tr}(NX)=0$ for every $X\in\operatorname{End}_K(W)$. Choose a basis of $W$, write the matrix of $N$ as $(n_{ij})$, and take $X=E_{ji}$. The matrix-unit calculation gives
> $$
> 0=\operatorname{tr}(NE_{ji})=n_{ij}.
> $$
> All entries of $N$ vanish. Therefore $h_0=I_W$, and since $h_0$ was arbitrary, every element of $H$ is the identity. Any nonzero vector of $W$ is consequently fixed by the whole original group on $E_K$.
>
> **4. Return to $k$.** The nonzero common fixed vector on $E_K$ means the finite row system in step 1 has rank $r<d$. Its kernel over $k$ therefore has dimension $d-r>0$. Choose a nonzero vector $v$ in that kernel. Every row of every $A-I$ is in $L$, so $(A-I)v=0$ for all $A\in G$. This is the required common fixed vector in $E$.

## Notes

- **Source and proof status:** The complete problem and printed hint were visually checked at [S2, Ch. XVII, Exercise 8, printed p. 661, PDF p. 676]. Burnside's theorem and its proof were separately checked at [S2, Ch. XVII, §3, Corollaries 3.3–3.4, printed pp. 648–649, PDF pp. 663–664]. Burnside is a proved theorem imported from the source; the scalar descent, simple-subspace reduction, and trace argument above are independently expanded solutions of this exercise.
- **Arbitrary characteristic:** The argument never divides by $\dim W$. Even when that dimension is zero as an element of $K$, subtracting the two traces and testing against matrix units remain valid.
- **No finiteness condition on $G$:** Neither finite generation nor finite order of the group is assumed. Finite-dimensionality of $E$ reduces the common fixed-vector equations to a finite system; it does not reduce the group to a finite group.
- **Subspace, not just a quotient:** A fixed vector in a quotient need not lift to a fixed vector. The proof chooses a simple invariant subspace, so its fixed vector is already a vector of $E_K$.
- **Notation:** Lang's $k[G]$ in this argument is the subalgebra of endomorphisms generated by the representation. It is the image of the abstract group algebra under its action, and the action map need not be injective.
