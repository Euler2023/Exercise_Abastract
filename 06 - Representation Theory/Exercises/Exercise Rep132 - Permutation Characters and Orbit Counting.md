# Exercise Rep132: Permutation Characters and Orbit Counting

## Problem Statement

> [!question] Lang XVIII.8
> Let $G$ be a finite group operating on a finite set $S$. Let $\mathbb C[S]$ be the vector space generated by $S$ over $\mathbb C$. Let $\psi$ be the character of the corresponding representation of $G$ on $\mathbb C[S]$.
>
> (a) Let $\sigma\in G$. Show that $\psi(\sigma)=$ number of fixed points of $\sigma$ in $S$.
>
> (b) Show that $\langle\psi,1_G\rangle_G$ is the number of $G$-orbits in $S$.

## Hints

> [!hint]- Hint 1: Read the diagonal of a permutation matrix
> The basis vector $e_s$ contributes $1$ to the trace exactly when $\sigma s=s$.

> [!hint]- Hint 2: Count the same pairs in two orders
> Count $\{(g,s)\in G\times S:gs=s\}$ first by $g$, then by $s$. On an orbit $O$, each stabilizer has order $\lvert G\rvert/\lvert O\rvert$.

## Solution

> [!success]- Independent trace and double-counting proof
> **(a).** By definition, $\mathbb C[S]$ has the basis $\{e_s:s\in S\}$ and $\sigma e_s=e_{\sigma s}$. The matrix entry on the diagonal at $s$ is $1$ if $\sigma s=s$, and $0$ otherwise. Therefore
>
> $$
> \psi(\sigma)=\operatorname{tr}(\sigma\mid\mathbb C[S])
> =\lvert\{s\in S:\sigma s=s\}\rvert.
> $$
>
> **(b).** The trivial character is identically $1$, so the character inner product and (a) give
>
> $$
> \langle\psi,1_G\rangle_G
> =\frac1{\lvert G\rvert}\sum_{g\in G}\lvert\{s:gs=s\}\rvert
> =\frac1{\lvert G\rvert}\sum_{s\in S}\lvert G_s\rvert.
> $$
>
> The second equality counts pairs $(g,s)$ satisfying $gs=s$ in the two orders. For an orbit $O$, the map $G/G_s\to O$, $gG_s\mapsto gs$, is a bijection. Thus $\lvert G_s\rvert=\lvert G\rvert/\lvert O\rvert$ for $s\in O$, and
>
> $$
> \sum_{s\in O}\lvert G_s\rvert=\lvert O\rvert\frac{\lvert G\rvert}{\lvert O\rvert}=\lvert G\rvert.
> $$
>
> Every orbit therefore contributes $1$ to the inner product, proving (b). If $S$ is empty, both sides are $0$.
>
> Equivalently, the invariant vectors are precisely the vectors whose coefficients are constant on each orbit. The orbit sums $\sum_{s\in O}e_s$ form a basis of $\mathbb C[S]^G$, making the same orbit count the multiplicity of the trivial representation.
