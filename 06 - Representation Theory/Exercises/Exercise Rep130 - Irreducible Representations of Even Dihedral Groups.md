# Exercise Rep130: Irreducible Representations of Even Dihedral Groups

## Problem Statement

> [!question] Lang XVIII.6 — Dihedral group $D_{2n}$
> There is a group of order $2n$ ($n$ even integer $\ge2$) generated by two elements $\sigma,\tau$ such that
>
> $$
> \sigma^n=1,\quad\tau^2=1,\quad\text{and}\quad\tau\sigma\tau^{-1}=\sigma^{-1}.
> $$
>
> It is called the dihedral group.
>
> (a) Show that there are four representations of dimension $1$, obtained by the four possible values $\pm1$ for $\sigma$ and $\tau$.
>
> (b) Let $C_n$ be the cyclic subgroup of $D_{2n}$ generated by $\sigma$. For each integer $r=0,\ldots,n-1$ let $\psi_r$ be the character of $C_n$ such that
>
> $$
> \psi_r(\sigma)=\zeta^r\qquad(\zeta=\text{primitive }n\text{-th root of unity}).
> $$
>
> Let $\chi_r$ be the induced character. Show that $\chi_r=\chi_{n-r}$.
>
> (c) Show that for $0<r<n/2$ the induced character $\chi_r$ is simple, of dimension $2$, and that one gets thereby $(n/2-1)$ distinct characters of dimension $2$.
>
> (d) Prove that the simple characters of (a) and (c) give all simple characters of $D_{2n}$.

## Hints

> [!hint]- Hint 1: Use two cosets of the rotation subgroup
> On the induced representation, choose vectors corresponding to $1$ and $\tau$. The rotation is diagonal and the reflection interchanges the two basis vectors.

> [!hint]- Hint 2: Distinguish pairs of eigenvalues
> The two eigenvalues of $\sigma$ are $\zeta^r,\zeta^{-r}$. They coincide only when $r=0$ or $r=n/2$. Finish with the sum of squared degrees.

## Solution

> [!success]- Independent construction and completeness proof
> We work over $\mathbb C$ and read subscripts $r$ modulo $n$.
>
> **(a).** In a one-dimensional representation, put $\sigma\mapsto a$ and $\tau\mapsto b$. Commutativity of scalars and the conjugation relation give $a=a^{-1}$, so $a=\pm1$; also $b^2=1$, hence $b=\pm1$. Conversely, since $n$ is even, each pair $(a,b)\in\{\pm1\}^2$ satisfies all the defining relations. The four representations are distinct and exhaust the one-dimensional representations.
>
> **(b).** Let $u$ span the $C_n$-module of character $\psi_r$. In the induced module use the basis $1\otimes u,\tau\otimes u$. Since $\sigma\tau=\tau\sigma^{-1}$, the action is
>
> $$
> \rho_r(\sigma)=
> \begin{pmatrix}\zeta^r&0\\0&\zeta^{-r}\end{pmatrix},
> \qquad
> \rho_r(\tau)=
> \begin{pmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix}.
> $$
>
> Consequently
>
> $$
> \chi_r(\sigma^m)=\zeta^{rm}+\zeta^{-rm},\qquad
> \chi_r(\tau\sigma^m)=0.
> $$
>
> These formulas are unchanged on replacing $r$ by $n-r$, proving the claimed equality. For $r=0$, the notation $\chi_n$ means $\chi_0$.
>
> **(c).** If $0<r<n/2$, then $\zeta^r\ne\zeta^{-r}$. Any invariant line would have to be one of the two eigenspaces of $\sigma$, but $\tau$ interchanges these lines. Thus $\rho_r$ is irreducible. If $\rho_r\simeq\rho_s$, restricting to $C_n$ forces equality of multisets
>
> $$
> \{\zeta^r,\zeta^{-r}\}=\{\zeta^s,\zeta^{-s}\}.
> $$
>
> Hence $s\equiv r$ or $s\equiv-r\pmod n$. For $0<r,s<n/2$, only $s=r$ is possible. This gives $n/2-1$ distinct irreducibles of degree $2$.
>
> At the excluded endpoints $r=0,n/2$, the rotation acts as $I$ or $-I$, and the two eigenlines of $\tau$ split $\rho_r$ into the corresponding two one-dimensional representations of (a).
>
> **(d).** For a finite group over $\mathbb C$, the sum of squared degrees over all irreducibles is the group order. The pairwise distinct representations already constructed contribute
>
> $$
> 4\cdot1^2+\left(\frac n2-1\right)2^2=2n=\lvert D_{2n}\rvert.
> $$
>
> Since any additional irreducible would contribute a positive square, there are no others. When $n=2$, the degree-two range is empty and $D_4\simeq C_2\times C_2$ has exactly the four linear representations, so the boundary case is included.
