# Exercise R280: Dimension and Catenarity of Affine Domains over the Integers

## Problem Statement

> [!question]
> Let $A = \mathbf{Z}[x_1, \ldots, x_n]$ be a finitely generated entire ring over $\mathbf{Z}$. Show that every maximal chain of prime ideals as in Exercise 18 must have $m = r + 1$. Here, $r = \text{transcendence degree of } \mathbf{Q}(x_1, \ldots, x_n) \text{ over } \mathbf{Q}$.

> [!warning] Source hypothesis
> The printed expression $\mathbf Q(x_1,\ldots,x_n)$ presupposes $\mathbf Z\hookrightarrow A$, so the intended hypothesis is $\operatorname{char}A=0$. For a domain of positive characteristic, that expression is undefined; the corresponding field-base result is Exercise 18.

## Hints

> [!hint]- Hint 1
> For a maximal ideal $M$ of $A$, show that the field $A/M$, being a finitely generated ring over $\mathbf Z$, must be finite. To rule out characteristic $0$, use Zariski's lemma over $\mathbf Q$ and clear the finitely many denominators of monic equations for ring generators.

> [!hint]- Hint 2
> Use the external dimension formula from [[02 - Ring Theory/Concepts/Krull Dimension and Catenarity|Krull Dimension and Catenarity]] for $\mathbf Z\subset A$ and a maximal ideal $M$ lying above $(p)$:
> $$
> \operatorname{ht}(M)=\operatorname{ht}(p)+r-\operatorname{tr.deg}_{\mathbf F_p}(A/M).
> $$
> Then use catenarity only for the fixed interval $(0)\subset M$.

## Solution

> [!success]- Solution
> ### Approach and proof inputs
> We take $\operatorname{char}A=0$ as specified above and write $F=\operatorname{Frac}(A)$, with $\operatorname{tr.deg}_{\mathbf Q}F=r$. The proof uses the two external dimension-theory inputs stated in [[02 - Ring Theory/Concepts/Krull Dimension and Catenarity#External dimension-theory inputs|External dimension-theory inputs]], from [Stacks, Tag 00NM](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/00NM) and [Tag 02IJ](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/02IJ). The remaining residue-field and chain arguments are supplied below.
>
> ### Step 1: Fields finitely generated over $\mathbf Z$ are finite
> Let $E=\mathbf Z[e_1,\ldots,e_s]$ be a field. Suppose first that its characteristic is $0$. Since $E$ contains $\mathbf Q$, we also have $E=\mathbf Q[e_1,\ldots,e_s]$. Zariski's lemma [S2, Ch. IX, Corollary 1.2, printed p. 379, PDF p. 394] implies that $E/\mathbf Q$ is finite.
>
> Choose a nonzero integer $d$ such that all coefficients of the monic minimal polynomials of the $e_i$ lie in $B=\mathbf Z[1/d]$. Then
> $$
> E=B[e_1,\ldots,e_s]
> $$
> is integral over $B$. If a field is integral over a subdomain $B$, then $B$ is a field: for $0\ne b\in B$, an equation
> $$
> (b^{-1})^N+c_{N-1}(b^{-1})^{N-1}+\cdots+c_0=0,\qquad c_i\in B,
> $$
> multiplied by $b^{N-1}$ expresses $b^{-1}$ as an element of $B$. But $\mathbf Z[1/d]$ is not a field; a prime number not dividing $d$ is not invertible there. This is a contradiction.
>
> Therefore $\operatorname{char}E=p>0$. Now $E=\mathbf F_p[e_1,\ldots,e_s]$ is finite-dimensional over $\mathbf F_p$ by Zariski's lemma, hence is a finite field $\mathbf F_{p^f}$ for some $f\ge1$.
>
> Apply this to $E=A/M$ for any maximal ideal $M$ of $A$. We obtain
> $$
> M\cap\mathbf Z=(p),\qquad \operatorname{tr.deg}_{\mathbf F_p}(A/M)=0.
> $$
>
> ### Step 2: Compute the height of each maximal ideal
> The ring $\mathbf Z$ is Noetherian and Cohen–Macaulay. Indeed its localization at $(0)$ is the field $\mathbf Q$, and at $(p)$ it is a one-dimensional local domain whose maximal ideal is generated by the non-zero-divisor $p$. Its local depth therefore equals its local dimension.
>
> The first external input makes $\mathbf Z$ universally catenary, and consequently its finite-type algebra $A$ is catenary. The second applies because $\mathbf Z\subset A$ is an inclusion of domains of finite type over this Noetherian universally catenary base. For the maximal ideal $M$ above $(p)$ it yields
> $$
> \operatorname{ht}(M)
> =\operatorname{ht}_{\mathbf Z}(p)+\operatorname{tr.deg}_{\mathbf Q}F
> -\operatorname{tr.deg}_{\mathbf F_p}(A/M)
> =1+r.
> $$
>
> ### Step 3: Recover the length of the prescribed chain
> In a maximal chain as in Exercise 18, $P_1=M$ is maximal, and every prime-to-prime inclusion from $(0)$ to $M$ is saturated. Catenarity makes the length of this chain equal to $\operatorname{ht}(M)$, because the endpoints are fixed and this height is finite. Thus
> $$
> m=\operatorname{ht}(M)=r+1.
> $$
> This argument applies separately to every maximal ideal. In particular, since every prime is contained in a maximal ideal, it also proves $\dim A=r+1$.

## Notes

- **Independent derivation with external inputs:** The source is [S2, Ch. IX, Exercise 19, printed p. 412, PDF p. 427]. Universal catenarity and the dimension formula are imported from the precise sources named above; their proofs are not reproduced here. Finiteness of the residue fields and the application of those theorems are proved in the solution.
- **Catenarity is not equicodimensionality:** A catenary ring need not have all maximal ideals of the same height. Here the separate height calculation establishes that fact for this particular class of $\mathbf Z$-algebras.
- **Residue fields:** The residue field at a maximal ideal is some $\mathbf F_{p^f}$, not necessarily $\mathbf F_p$.
