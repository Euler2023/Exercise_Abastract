# Exercise R341: Two Generators for Every Dedekind Ideal

## Problem Statement

> [!question] Neukirch I.3.6
> Every ideal of a Dedekind domain can be generated by two elements.
>
> **Original hint:** Use exercise 5.

## Hints

> [!hint]- Hint 1: Fix the first generator
> Treat the zero ideal separately. For a nonzero ideal $I$, choose any $0\ne a\in I$ and look at the image of $I$ in $R/(a)$.

> [!hint]- Hint 2: Use the corrected quotient theorem
> Exercise I.3.5 proves, after correcting its printed terminology, that $R/(a)$ is a principal ideal ring. Thus $I/(a)$ has a single generator; lift that generator to an element $b\in I$.

> [!hint]- Hint 3: Lift generation back to the original ideal
> For $x\in I$, generation modulo $(a)$ means that $x-rb\in(a)$ for some $r\in R$. Rewrite this as an expression for $x$ in the ideal $(a,b)$.

## Solution

> [!success]- Independent derivation
> Let $R$ be a Dedekind domain and $I\subseteq R$ an integral ideal. If $I=(0)$, choose $a=b=0$, so $I=(a,b)$. Assume now that $I\ne(0)$.
>
> Choose any $0\ne a\in I$. Since $(a)\subseteq I$, the quotient $I/(a)$ is an ideal of the quotient ring $R/(a)$. The corrected result of [[02 - Ring Theory/Exercises/Exercise R340 - Quotients of Dedekind Domains Are Principal Ideal Rings|Exercise I.3.5]] says that this quotient ring is a principal ideal ring. Therefore there is an element $\overline b\in I/(a)$ with
> $$
> I/(a)=(\overline b).
> $$
> Choose a lift $b\in I$ of $\overline b$.
>
> For each $x\in I$, there is $\overline r\in R/(a)$ such that $\overline x=\overline r\,\overline b$. Lift $\overline r$ to $r\in R$. Then $x-rb\in(a)$, so there is $s\in R$ with
> $$
> x=sa+rb.
> $$
> This proves $I\subseteq(a,b)$. Conversely, $a,b\in I$ and $I$ is an ideal, so $(a,b)\subseteq I$. Hence
> $$
> I=(a,b).
> $$
> If $a$ is a unit, then $I=R=(a,0)$; this also fits the argument since $R/(a)$ is the zero ring. If $I=(a)$ already, one can take $b=0$. Thus “two elements” means at most two, with zero or redundant generators permitted. In fact, the proof shows that any chosen nonzero element of a nonzero ideal can be used as the first generator.

## Notes

- **Source status:** The problem and its one-sentence hint were visually checked at [S4, Ch. I, §3, Exercise 6, printed p. 23, PDF p. 42]. The book points to Exercise 5 without giving a full proof of Exercise 6. The lifted-generation proof above is independent. No source figure is needed.
- **Inputs and source issue:** The proof uses the corrected principal-ideal-ring statement of [[02 - Ring Theory/Exercises/Exercise R340 - Quotients of Dedekind Domains Are Principal Ideal Rings|Exercise R340]], whose full proof is included in the vault. Its printed “principal ideal domain” assertion is false; this argument uses only principal generation of quotient ideals. The ideal-correspondence and quotient constructions are the imported prerequisites from the linked concepts.
- **Scope:** “Ideal” means an integral ideal $I\subseteq R$, including the zero and unit ideals. The argument makes no assumption that $I$ is already principal. It proves a two-generator bound, not that two nonzero or distinct generators are necessary.
- **Routing:** The proof chooses an ideal element, passes to a quotient, and lifts a principal quotient ideal. These are primarily ring-theoretic operations; finite module generation is a related viewpoint.
