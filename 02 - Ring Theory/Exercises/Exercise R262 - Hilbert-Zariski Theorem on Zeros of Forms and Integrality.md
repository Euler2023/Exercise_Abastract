# Exercise R262: Hilbert-Zariski Theorem on Zeros of Forms and Integrality

## Problem Statement

> [!question]
> (Hilbert-Zariski) Let $k$ be a field and let $V$ be a homogeneous variety with generic point $(x)$ over $k$. Let $\mathfrak{A}$ be the algebraic set of zeros in $k^a$ of a homogeneous ideal in $k[X]$ generated by forms $f_1, \ldots, f_r$ in $k[X]$. Prove that $V \cap \mathfrak{A}$ has only the trivial zero if and only if each $x_i$ is integral over the ring $k[f(x)] = k[f_1(x), \ldots, f_r(x)]$. (Compare with Theorem 3.7 of Chapter VII.)

## Hints

> [!hint]- Hint 1
> A point $\xi \in (k^a)^n$ lies in $V \cap \mathfrak{A}$ if and only if $\xi$ is a zero of the homogeneous prime ideal $\mathfrak{p} = I(V)$ and also satisfies $f_j(\xi) = 0$ for all $j = 1, \ldots, r$.
>
> Use the homogeneous Hilbert Nullstellensatz: the only common zero is the trivial zero $(0, \ldots, 0)$ if and only if the radical of the ideal $\mathfrak{p} + (f_1, \ldots, f_r)$ contains the irrelevant maximal ideal $\mathfrak{m} = (X_1, \ldots, X_n)$.

> [!hint]- Hint 2
> In the coordinate ring $R = k[x] = k[X]/\mathfrak{p}$, the condition $\mathfrak{m} \subseteq \sqrt{\mathfrak{p} + (f_1, \ldots, f_r)}$ means that for each variable $X_i$, some power $x_i^N$ lies in the ideal $\mathfrak{b} = (f_1(x), \ldots, f_r(x)) R$.
>
> Show that $R/\mathfrak{b}$ is finite-dimensional over $k$. Use induction on degree to lift a finite homogeneous spanning set to generators of $R$ as a module over $k[f(x)]$. A ring that is finite as a module over a subring is integral over that subring.

## Solution

> [!success]- Solution
> We establish the equivalence in both directions.
>
> ### Preliminaries and Setup
>
> Let $k[X] = k[X_1, \ldots, X_n]$ be the polynomial ring in $n$ variables, graded by standard degree.
> Since $V$ is a homogeneous variety over $k$ with generic point $(x) = (x_1, \ldots, x_n)$, its vanishing ideal $\mathfrak{p} = I(V) \subset k[X]$ is a homogeneous prime ideal.
> The homogeneous coordinate ring of $V$ is the graded domain
> $$
> R = k[x] = k[X_1, \ldots, X_n]/\mathfrak{p}.
> $$
> Let $\mathfrak{a} = (f_1, \ldots, f_r) \subseteq k[X]$ be the homogeneous ideal generated by the forms $f_1, \ldots, f_r$. The algebraic set $\mathfrak{A} \subset (k^a)^n$ is the zero locus $V(\mathfrak{a})$.
> The intersection $V \cap \mathfrak{A}$ is the zero locus of the sum ideal $\mathfrak{p} + \mathfrak{a} = \mathfrak{p} + (f_1, \ldots, f_r)$ in $(k^a)^n$.
> Let $\mathfrak{m} = (X_1, \ldots, X_n)$ be the irrelevant ideal of $k[X]$.
>
> ### Direction 1: $V \cap \mathfrak{A} = \{0\} \implies x_i$ is integral over $k[f(x)]$
>
> Suppose $V \cap \mathfrak{A}$ has only the trivial zero $(0, \ldots, 0)$.
> By Hilbert's Nullstellensatz (Chapter IX, §1, Theorem 1.5 [S2, printed p. 380, PDF p. 395]), an algebraic set defined by a homogeneous ideal has no non-trivial zeros in $(k^a)^n$ if and only if the radical of that ideal contains the irrelevant ideal $\mathfrak{m}$.
> Therefore,
> $$
> \mathfrak{m} \subseteq \sqrt{\mathfrak{p} + (f_1, \ldots, f_r)}.
> $$
> This means that for each $i \in \{1, \ldots, n\}$, there exists an integer $N_i \ge 1$ such that
> $$
> X_i^{N_i} \in \mathfrak{p} + (f_1, \ldots, f_r).
> $$
> Taking images under the canonical projection $k[X] \to R = k[x]$, the ideal $\mathfrak{p}$ maps to $0$, and we obtain:
> $$
> x_i^{N_i} \in (f_1(x), \ldots, f_r(x)) R = \sum_{j=1}^r f_j(x) R.
> $$
> Setting $N = \max_i N_i$, every monomial of degree $n(N-1) + 1$ in $x_1, \ldots, x_n$ lies in $\sum_{j=1}^r f_j(x) R$.
>
> Let $B = k[f(x)] = k[f_1(x), \ldots, f_r(x)] \subseteq R$.
> Since each $f_j(X)$ is a form of positive degree $d_j \ge 1$, each $f_j(x)$ is a homogeneous element of $R$ of positive degree $d_j \ge 1$.
> Let $R_+ = \bigoplus_{d \ge 1} R_d$ be the maximal homogeneous ideal of $R$.
> The condition $x_i^{N_i} \in \sum_{j=1}^r f_j(x) R$ implies that the ideal $J = (f_1(x), \ldots, f_r(x)) R$ satisfies $\sqrt{J} = R_+$.
>
> Put $D=n(N-1)+1$, and let $M$ be the $B$-submodule generated by the finitely many monomials in $x_1,\ldots,x_n$ of degree less than $D$. We prove $R=M$ by induction on homogeneous degree. The assertion holds in degrees below $D$. If $h\in R_d$ with $d\ge D$, then $h\in J$ because every degree-$d$ monomial lies in $J$. Taking degree $d$ in a representation of $h$ gives
> $$
> h=\sum_{j=1}^r f_j(x)h_j,\qquad h_j\in R_{d-d_j},
> $$
> where $R_e=0$ for $e<0$. Since $d_j\ge1$, every nonzero $h_j$ has smaller degree than $h$, so the induction hypothesis puts $h_j$ in $M$. Thus $h\in M$. Every element of $R$ is a finite sum of homogeneous elements, proving that $R$ is a finitely generated $B$-module.
> Since $R$ is a finitely generated $B$-module and $B = k[f(x)]$, every element of $R$, and in particular each generator $x_i$, is integral over $B = k[f(x)]$.
>
> ### Direction 2: $x_i$ integral over $k[f(x)] \implies V \cap \mathfrak{A} = \{0\}$
>
> Conversely, suppose each $x_i$ is integral over $B = k[f(x)]$.
> Then for each $i \in \{1, \ldots, n\}$, $x_i$ satisfies a monic polynomial relation with coefficients in $B$:
> $$
> x_i^{m_i} + b_{i, m_i - 1} x_i^{m_i - 1} + \cdots + b_{i, 0} = 0, \qquad b_{i, l} \in k[f_1(x), \ldots, f_r(x)].
> $$
> The subring $B$ is graded because its generators $f_j(x)$ are homogeneous. Write $(b_{i,l})_d$ for the degree-$d$ component of $b_{i,l}$, and take degree $m_i$ in the displayed relation. This gives a new monic relation
> $$
> x_i^{m_i}+\sum_{l=0}^{m_i-1}c_{i,l}x_i^l=0,
> \qquad c_{i,l}=(b_{i,l})_{m_i-l}\in B_{m_i-l}.
> $$
> Since $m_i-l>0$, each $c_{i,l}$ is a $k$-linear combination of positive-degree monomials in the $f_j(x)$, and hence has an expression in these generators with zero constant term. This conclusion concerns the new coefficients $c_{i,l}$; the original coefficients $b_{i,l}$ need not have zero constant term.
>
> Let $\xi = (\xi_1, \ldots, \xi_n) \in (k^a)^n$ be a point in $V \cap \mathfrak{A}$.
> Since $\xi \in V$, the evaluation homomorphism $\operatorname{ev}_\xi: k[x] \to k^a$ given by $x_i \mapsto \xi_i$ is well-defined.
> Since $\xi \in \mathfrak{A}$, we have $f_j(\xi) = 0$ for all $j = 1, \ldots, r$.
> Evaluating the new homogeneous relation at $\xi$, each coefficient $c_{i,l}$ evaluates to $0$:
> $$
> \operatorname{ev}_\xi(c_{i,l})=0\quad\text{for all }l<m_i.
> $$
> Therefore, evaluating the monic relation at $\xi$ yields:
> $$
> \xi_i^{m_i} + 0 + \cdots + 0 = 0 \implies \xi_i^{m_i} = 0 \implies \xi_i = 0.
> $$
> This holds for all $i \in \{1, \ldots, n\}$, so $\xi = (0, \ldots, 0)$.
> Thus $V \cap \mathfrak{A}$ has only the trivial zero, completing the proof.

## Notes

- **Independent derivation:** The solution uses Hilbert's Nullstellensatz, an explicit induction on degree, and the finite-module criterion for integrality. The exercise statement is checked at [S2, Ch. IX, Exercise 1, printed p. 410, PDF p. 425].
- **Source comparison:** Lang's reference to Chapter VII, Theorem 3.7 concerns the General Integrality Criterion [S2, printed p. 352, PDF p. 367]. The degree induction here is an independent argument, rather than a quotation of a graded version of that theorem.
- **Geometric meaning:** Geometrically, $V \cap \mathfrak{A} = \{0\}$ means that the projective subvarieties $V_{\mathbb{P}}$ and $\mathfrak{A}_{\mathbb{P}}$ in $\mathbb{P}^{n-1}$ do not intersect: $V_{\mathbb{P}} \cap \mathfrak{A}_{\mathbb{P}} = \varnothing$. The theorem shows that this geometric disjointness is equivalent to the morphism $V \to \mathbb{A}^r$ defined by $(f_1, \ldots, f_r)$ being a finite morphism.
