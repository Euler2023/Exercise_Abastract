# Exercise R260: Total Ramification and Prime Powers in Prime Cyclotomic Rings

## Problem Statement

> [!question] Lang, Chapter VII, Exercise 11
> Let $p$ be a prime and $\zeta$ a primitive $p$-th root of unity. Show that there is a principal ideal $J$ in $\mathbb Z[\zeta]$ such that $J^{p-1} = (p)$ (the principal ideal generated by $p$).

## Hints

> [!hint]- Hint 1: Consider the element $1-\zeta$
> Let $J=(1-\zeta)$ be the principal ideal generated by $1-\zeta$ in the ring $\mathbb Z[\zeta]$.

> [!hint]- Hint 2: Factor the prime $p$ via the cyclotomic polynomial
> The $p$-th cyclotomic polynomial is $\Phi_p(X) = X^{p-1} + X^{p-2} + \cdots + X + 1 = \prod_{j=1}^{p-1} (X - \zeta^j)$. Evaluate both sides at $X=1$.

> [!hint]- Hint 3: Relate the factors using cyclotomic units
> From Exercise 10(a), show that for each $j\in\{1,\ldots,p-1\}$, the ratio $(1-\zeta^j)/(1-\zeta)$ is a unit in $\mathbb Z[\zeta]$. What does this imply about the principal ideals $(1-\zeta^j)$ and $(1-\zeta)$?

## Solution

> [!success]- Complete Derivation
> Let $p$ be a prime number and let $\zeta$ be a primitive $p$-th root of unity in $\mathbb C$.
> 
> Let $J$ be the principal ideal of $\mathbb Z[\zeta]$ generated by the element $1 - \zeta$:
> $$
> J = (1 - \zeta) = (1 - \zeta)\mathbb Z[\zeta].
> $$
> 
> ### Step 1: The factorization of $p$ in $\mathbb Z[\zeta]$
> The minimal polynomial of $\zeta$ over $\mathbb Q$ is the $p$-th cyclotomic polynomial:
> $$
> \Phi_p(X) = X^{p-1} + X^{p-2} + \cdots + X + 1.
> $$
> 
> Over $\mathbb C$, the roots of $\Phi_p(X)$ are the $p-1$ distinct primitive $p$-th roots of unity, namely $\zeta, \zeta^2, \ldots, \zeta^{p-1}$.
> Thus $\Phi_p(X)$ splits completely as:
> $$
> \Phi_p(X) = \prod_{j=1}^{p-1} (X - \zeta^j).
> $$
> 
> Evaluating both expressions at $X = 1$:
> $$
> \Phi_p(1) = 1^{p-1} + 1^{p-2} + \cdots + 1 + 1 = p.
> $$
> 
> Therefore, we obtain the fundamental factorization of $p$ in $\mathbb Z[\zeta]$:
> $$
> p = \prod_{j=1}^{p-1} (1 - \zeta^j).
> $$
> 
> ### Step 2: Invariance of ideals under cyclotomic units
> For each integer $j \in \{1, 2, \ldots, p-1\}$, we have $\gcd(j, p) = 1$.
> 
> Applying Exercise 10(a):
> 1. The element:
>    $$
>    u_j = \frac{1 - \zeta^j}{1 - \zeta} = 1 + \zeta + \zeta^2 + \cdots + \zeta^{j-1}
>    $$
>    is an algebraic integer belonging to $\mathbb Z[\zeta]$.
> 
> 2. Since $\gcd(j, p) = 1$, there exists an integer $k \in \{1, \ldots, p-1\}$ such that $j k \equiv 1 \pmod p$.
>    Then $\zeta = (\zeta^j)^k$, and consequently:
>    $$
>    u_j^{-1} = \frac{1 - \zeta}{1 - \zeta^j} = \frac{1 - (\zeta^j)^k}{1 - \zeta^j} = 1 + \zeta^j + (\zeta^j)^2 + \cdots + (\zeta^j)^{k-1} \in \mathbb Z[\zeta].
>    $$
> 
> Thus $u_j$ is an invertible element of the ring $\mathbb Z[\zeta]$, i.e.,
> $$
> u_j \in \mathbb Z[\zeta]^\times.
> $$
> 
> This implies that the element $1 - \zeta^j$ differs from $1 - \zeta$ only by a unit factor:
> $$
> 1 - \zeta^j = u_j (1 - \zeta),\qquad u_j \in \mathbb Z[\zeta]^\times.
> $$
> 
> Therefore, the principal ideals generated by these elements are identical:
> $$
> (1 - \zeta^j) = (1 - \zeta) = J\qquad\text{for every }j = 1, 2, \ldots, p-1.
> $$
> 
> ### Step 3: Deduction of $J^{p-1} = (p)$
> Consider the principal ideal $(p) = p\mathbb Z[\zeta]$ generated by $p$.
> Using the factorization of $p$ from Step 1:
> $$
> (p) = \left(\prod_{j=1}^{p-1} (1 - \zeta^j)\right).
> $$
> 
> In any commutative ring, the ideal generated by a product of elements equals the product of the ideals generated by those elements:
> $$
> (p) = \prod_{j=1}^{p-1} (1 - \zeta^j).
> $$
> 
> Substituting the equality $(1 - \zeta^j) = J$ for each factor:
> $$
> (p) = \prod_{j=1}^{p-1} J = J^{p-1}.
> $$
> 
> This establishes that $J = (1 - \zeta)$ is a principal ideal in $\mathbb Z[\zeta]$ satisfying:
> $$
> J^{p-1} = (p).
> $$

## Notes

- **Total Ramification of $p$:** The degree of the cyclotomic field extension is $[\mathbb Q(\zeta):\mathbb Q] = \varphi(p) = p-1$. The ideal decomposition $(p) = J^{p-1}$ proves that the rational prime $p$ is **totally ramified** in $\mathbb Q(\zeta)$, with ramification index $e = p-1$ and residue degree $f = 1$.
- **Primality of $J$:** The quotient ring $\mathbb Z[\zeta]/J = \mathbb Z[\zeta]/(1-\zeta)$ is canonically isomorphic to $\mathbb Z/p\mathbb Z = \mathbb F_p$. Hence $J$ is a maximal (and prime) ideal of $\mathbb Z[\zeta]$.
- **Eisenstein at $Y = X-1$:** Substituting $X = Y + 1$ into $\Phi_p(X)$ produces the Eisenstein polynomial $Y^{p-1} + \binom{p}{1} Y^{p-2} + \cdots + p$ with respect to the prime $p$. This provides an independent verification that $1-\zeta$ generates a prime ideal lying above $p$.
- **Independent Derivation:** The solution derives the ideal equality $(p)=J^{p-1}$ directly from the evaluation $\Phi_p(1)=p$ and the cyclotomic unit ratio established in Exercise R259.
