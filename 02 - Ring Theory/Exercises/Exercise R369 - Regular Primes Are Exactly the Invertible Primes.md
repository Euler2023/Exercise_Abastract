# Exercise R369: Regular Primes Are Exactly the Invertible Primes

## Problem Statement

> [!question] Neukirch I.12.5
> In a one-dimensional noetherian integral domain the regular prime ideals $\ne0$ are precisely the invertible prime ideals.

## Hints

> [!hint]- Hint 1: Localize an invertible ideal
> In a local ring a finite sum of products equal to $1$ has a unit product among its summands. Use this to make an invertible ideal principal locally.

> [!hint]- Hint 2: Use dimension one
> For distinct nonzero primes $\mathfrak p,\mathfrak q$, maximality gives an element of $\mathfrak p\setminus\mathfrak q$, so $\mathfrak pA_{\mathfrak q}=A_{\mathfrak q}$.

> [!hint]- Hint 3: Construct the inverse globally
> Let $J=\{x\in\operatorname{Frac}(A):x\mathfrak p\subseteq A\}$. Finite generation allows common-denominator clearing to show $J_{\mathfrak m}=(\mathfrak pA_{\mathfrak m})^{-1}$.

## Solution

> [!success]- Independent derivation
>
> Let $A$ be the stated domain, $F=\operatorname{Frac}(A)$, and $\mathfrak p\ne0$ a prime. Dimension one makes $\mathfrak p$ maximal. A regular prime here means that the local ring $A_{\mathfrak p}$ is a discrete valuation ring; equivalently its maximal ideal is principal in this one-dimensional Noetherian local domain. This is the source's regular/nonsingular convention, made explicit in §13.
>
> We first justify the local equivalence without an intersection theorem. If a Noetherian local domain $R$ has nonzero maximal ideal $(\pi)$, every nonzero nonunit can be divided by $\pi$. This process cannot continue indefinitely for a fixed $x\ne0$. Otherwise all $x/\pi^k$ lie in $R$ and their principal ideals form an ascending chain. At a stationary step, $x/\pi^{k+1}=r x/\pi^k$ for some $r\in R$, which after cancellation gives $1=r\pi$, a contradiction. Thus each nonzero element is $\pi^k u$ with $u$ a unit. Such an exponent is unique since $\pi$ is a nonunit. Every nonzero ideal is then generated by an element of least exponent, giving a discrete valuation ring. The converse is part of the definition of a DVR.
>
> ### 1. Invertible implies regular
> Suppose $\mathfrak pJ=A$ for a fractional ideal $J$. Localize at $\mathfrak p$ and write $1=\sum_i a_i b_i$ with $a_i\in\mathfrak pA_{\mathfrak p}$ and $b_i\in J_{\mathfrak p}$. At least one product $a_i b_i$ is a unit, since otherwise the sum would be in the maximal ideal. For that pair and every $x\in\mathfrak pA_{\mathfrak p}$,
> $$
> \frac{x}{a_i}=\frac{x b_i}{a_i b_i}\in A_{\mathfrak p}.
> $$
> Thus $\mathfrak pA_{\mathfrak p}=a_i A_{\mathfrak p}$. Localization preserves the Noetherian domain property, and its nonzero maximal ideal is principal. The preceding local argument shows $A_{\mathfrak p}$ is a DVR, so $\mathfrak p$ is regular.
>
> ### 2. Regular implies invertible
> If $\mathfrak p$ is regular, its localization at itself is principal. At any other maximal ideal $\mathfrak q$, take $a\in\mathfrak p\setminus\mathfrak q$; its image is a unit, hence $\mathfrak pA_{\mathfrak q}=A_{\mathfrak q}$. Therefore $\mathfrak p$ is principal at every maximal localization.
>
> Define
> $$
> J=\{x\in F:x\mathfrak p\subseteq A\}.
> $$
> This is a fractional ideal: it contains $A$, and for $0\ne a\in\mathfrak p$ it lies inside $a^{-1}A$, whose submodules are finitely generated because $A$ is Noetherian. For any maximal $\mathfrak m$, finite generation of $\mathfrak p=(a_1,\ldots,a_t)$ proves
> $$
> J_{\mathfrak m}=\{x\in F:x\mathfrak pA_{\mathfrak m}\subseteq A_{\mathfrak m}\}.
> $$
> One inclusion follows by localizing. For the other, clear the finitely many denominators of $xa_i\in A_{\mathfrak m}$: there is $s\notin\mathfrak m$ such that $sxa_i\in A$ for all $i$, so $sx\in J$ and $x\in J_{\mathfrak m}$.
>
> Write $\mathfrak pA_{\mathfrak m}=c_{\mathfrak m}A_{\mathfrak m}$ with $c_{\mathfrak m}\ne0$. The equality just proved gives $J_{\mathfrak m}=c_{\mathfrak m}^{-1}A_{\mathfrak m}$ and
> $$
> (\mathfrak pJ)_{\mathfrak m}=A_{\mathfrak m}
> \quad\text{for every maximal }\mathfrak m.
> $$
> If $\mathfrak pJ$ were a proper ideal, it would lie in some maximal ideal $\mathfrak m$, and localization would put it inside the proper maximal ideal $\mathfrak mA_{\mathfrak m}$, a contradiction. Hence $\mathfrak pJ=A$, proving invertibility.
