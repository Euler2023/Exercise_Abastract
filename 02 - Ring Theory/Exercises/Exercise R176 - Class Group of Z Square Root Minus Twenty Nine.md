# Exercise R176: Class Group of Z[sqrt(-29)]

## Problem Statement

> [!question] Exercise 8.3
> Let $R=\mathbb Z[\delta]$, with $\delta=\sqrt{-29}$. In each case, compute the norm, explain what conclusions one can draw about ideals in $R$ from the norm computation, and determine the class group of $R$:
>
> $$
> N(1+\delta),\quad N(4+\delta),\quad N(5+\delta),\quad N(9+2\delta),\quad N(11+2\delta).
> $$

## Hints

> [!hint]- Hint 1
> The class group is generated by prime ideals above $2$, $3$, and $5$. Choose $P=(2,1+\delta)$, $Q=(3,1+\delta)$, and $S=(5,1+\delta)$.

> [!hint]- Hint 2
> Factor the first three principal ideals with the chosen orientations. To prove the order of $[Q]$ is exactly six, rule out generators of norms $3$, $9$, and $27$.

## Solution

> [!success]- Solution
> For $\alpha=a+b\delta$,
>
> $$
> N(\alpha)=a^2+29b^2.
> $$
>
> The requested norms are
>
> $$
> \begin{aligned}
> N(1+\delta)&=30=2\cdot3\cdot5,\\
> N(4+\delta)&=45=3^2\cdot5,\\
> N(5+\delta)&=54=2\cdot3^3,\\
> N(9+2\delta)&=197,\\
> N(11+2\delta)&=237=3\cdot79.
> \end{aligned}
> $$
>
> Let
>
> $$
> P=(2,1+\delta),\qquad Q=(3,1+\delta),\qquad S=(5,1+\delta),
> $$
>
> and put $p=[P]$, $q=[Q]$, $s=[S]$. Testing the elements modulo these ideals and their conjugates gives
>
> $$
> (1+\delta)=PQS,
> $$
>
> $$
> (4+\delta)=Q^2\overline S,
> $$
>
> and
>
> $$
> (5+\delta)=P\overline Q^{,3}.
> $$
>
> Consequently
>
> $$
> pqs=1,\qquad q^2s^{-1}=1,\qquad pq^{-3}=1.
> $$
>
> Thus $s=q^2$, $p=q^3$, and $q^6=1$. The ideal $Q$ is not principal because $a^2+29b^2=3$ has no solution. If $q^2=1$, then $Q^2$ would be generated by an element of norm $9$; the only elements of norm $9$ are $\pm3$, and $(3)=Q\overline Q\ne Q^2$. If $q^3=1$, an element of norm $27$ would exist, but $a^2+29b^2=27$ has no solution. Hence $q$ has order exactly six, and
>
> $$
> \boxed{\operatorname{Cl}(R)\cong\mathbb Z/6\mathbb Z.}
> $$
>
> The prime norm $197$ shows that $(9+2\delta)$ itself is a principal prime ideal above $197$, so that prime ideal has trivial class. Finally, let
>
> $$
> U=(79,\delta-34).
> $$
>
> Since $34^2\equiv-29\pmod{79}$ and $11+2\cdot34\equiv0\pmod{79}$,
>
> $$
> (11+2\delta)=QU.
> $$
>
> Therefore $[U]=q^{-1}$.
