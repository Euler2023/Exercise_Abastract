# Exercise R269: Stability of Powers of Graded Pieces and Well-Behaved Degrees

## Problem Statement

> [!question]
> **Shared setup for Exercises 5–9.** Let $R=k[x]=k[X]/\mathfrak{p}$, where $\mathfrak{p}$ is a homogeneous prime ideal. Thus $(x)$ is a homogeneous generic point of a $k$-variety. Let $K=k(x)=\operatorname{Frac}(R)$ and let $I$ be the integral closure of $R$ in $K$. Assume, as in the source, that $K/k$ is a regular extension. Write $R_d$ and $I_d$ for the homogeneous degree-$d$ pieces, with their gradings established for $I$ in Exercise 6. In Exercise 7, $I=\sum_{i=1}^sRz_i$ with homogeneous $z_i$ of degrees $d_i$.
>
> Define an integer $m \geqq 1$ to be **well behaved** if $I_m^q = I_{qm}$ for all integers $q \geqq 1$. If $R = I$, then all $m$ are well behaved. In Exercise 7, suppose $m \geqq \max d_i$. Show that $m$ is well behaved.

## Hints

> [!hint]- Hint 1
> For any $m \ge 1$ and $q \ge 1$, the inclusion $I_m^q \subseteq I_{qm}$ holds automatically because multiplication in a graded ring adds degrees: $\deg(u_1 \cdots u_q) = q m$ for $u_j \in I_m$.
> 
> Therefore, proving that $m$ is well behaved reduces to proving the reverse inclusion: $I_{qm} \subseteq I_m^q$.

> [!hint]- Hint 2
> Recall the formula from Exercise 7: $I_n = \sum_{i=1}^s R_{n - d_i} z_i$, where $d_i = \deg(z_i)$.
> 
> Note that $R = k[x_1, \ldots, x_n]$ is generated by elements of degree $1$ ($R_1$), which means $R_a R_b = R_{a+b}$ and $R_k = R_1^k$ for all $k \ge 0$.
> 
> If $m \ge \max_i d_i$, then $m - d_i \ge 0$. Decompose $qm - d_i = (q-1)m + (m - d_i)$.

## Solution

> [!success]- Solution
> Let $R = k[x_1, \ldots, x_n]$ be the coordinate ring of the homogeneous affine variety in the shared setup.
> The ring $R$ is standard graded over $k$, meaning $R_0 = k$ and $R$ is generated as a $k$-algebra by elements of degree $1$:
> $$
> R = k[R_1] \implies R_a R_b = R_{a+b} \quad \text{for all } a, b \ge 0.
> $$
> In particular, $R_{qm} = R_m^q$ for all $m, q \ge 1$.
>
> Let $I = \bigoplus_{n=0}^\infty I_n$ be the integral closure of $R$ in $k(x)$.
> By Exercise 7, $I = R z_1 + \cdots + R z_s$ with each $z_i$ homogeneous of degree $d_i \ge 0$.
> Consequently, for any integer $n \ge 0$, the $n$-th graded piece is
> $$
> I_n = \sum_{i=1}^s R_{n - d_i} z_i,
> $$
> with the convention that $R_j = \{0\}$ when $j < 0$.
>
> An integer $m \ge 1$ is defined to be **well behaved** if
> $$
> I_m^q = I_{qm} \quad \text{for all integers } q \ge 1.
> $$
>
> ### Case 1: When $R = I$
>
> If $R = I$, then $I_n = R_n$ for all $n$.
> Since $R$ is generated by $R_1$, we have
> $$
> I_m^q = R_m^q = (R_1^m)^q = R_1^{qm} = R_{qm} = I_{qm}.
> $$
> Thus, every integer $m \ge 1$ is well behaved when $R = I$.
>
> ### Case 2: General Case with $m \ge \max_{1 \le i \le s} d_i$
>
> Let $m\ge1$ and $m \ge \max_{1 \le i \le s} d_i$. We show that $I_m^q = I_{qm}$ for all $q \ge 1$.
>
> 1. **Inclusion $I_m^q \subseteq I_{qm}$:**
>    This holds for any graded algebra:
>    The product of $q$ homogeneous elements of degree $m$ has degree $m + \cdots + m = qm$.
>    Since $I$ is a subring of $k(x)$, the $k$-subspace $I_m^q$ generated by all products $u_1 \cdots u_q$ (with $u_j \in I_m$) is contained in the homogeneous subspace of degree $qm$, which is $I_{qm}$.
>    Hence $I_m^q \subseteq I_{qm}$ for all $q \ge 1$.
>
> 2. **Inclusion $I_{qm} \subseteq I_m^q$:**
>    By the graded generation formula of Exercise 7, any element of $I_{qm}$ is a finite sum of elements of the form
>    $$
>    w = r z_i, \qquad \text{where } r \in R_{qm - d_i} \text{ and } 1 \le i \le s.
>    $$
>    Since $m \ge \max_j d_j$, we have $m - d_i \ge 0$.
>    We can partition the degree $qm - d_i$ as follows:
>    $$
>    qm - d_i = (q - 1) m + (m - d_i).
>    $$
>    Since both $(q - 1)m \ge 0$ and $m - d_i \ge 0$, and since $R$ is generated in degree $1$, the multiplication of graded pieces of $R$ satisfies:
>    $$
>    R_{qm - d_i} = R_{(q - 1)m + (m - d_i)} = R_{(q - 1)m} \cdot R_{m - d_i} = R_m^{q - 1} \cdot R_{m - d_i}.
>    $$
>    Therefore, any element $r \in R_{qm - d_i}$ can be written as a finite sum of products
>    $$
>    r = \sum_\alpha u_\alpha v_\alpha, \qquad u_\alpha \in R_m^{q - 1}, \quad v_\alpha \in R_{m - d_i}.
>    $$
>    Multiplying by $z_i$, we obtain:
>    $$
>    w = r z_i = \sum_\alpha u_\alpha (v_\alpha z_i).
>    $$
>    Now observe the two factors:
>    - $u_\alpha \in R_m^{q - 1} \subseteq I_m^{q - 1}$, because $R_m \subseteq I_m$.
>    - $v_\alpha z_i \in R_{m - d_i} z_i \subseteq I_m$, because $\deg(v_\alpha) + \deg(z_i) = (m - d_i) + d_i = m$.
>    
>    Therefore, each term $u_\alpha (v_\alpha z_i)$ belongs to $I_m^{q - 1} \cdot I_m = I_m^q$.
>    Since $w$ is a linear combination of such terms, we have $w \in I_m^q$.
>    
>    Because $I_{qm}$ is spanned by elements of the form $w = r z_i$, we conclude that
>    $$
>    I_{qm} \subseteq I_m^q.
>    $$
>
> Combining both inclusions yields $I_m^q = I_{qm}$ for all $q \ge 1$.
> Hence, $m$ is well behaved.

## Notes

- **Independent derivation and source scope:** The shared setup and Exercise 8 are checked at [S2, Ch. IX, printed p. 410, PDF p. 425]. The derivation relies on the factorization of degree $qm-d_i$ into $(q-1)m$ and $m-d_i$, using the generation of $R$ in degree $1$. Products of graded pieces mean the $k$-linear span of products, not just the set of individual products.
- **Connection to Veronese embeddings:** In algebraic geometry, a well-behaved integer $m$ corresponds to a degree for which the $m$-th Veronese subring $I^{(m)} = \bigoplus_{q \ge 0} I_{qm}$ is generated in its first non-trivial degree $I_m$. This ensures that the projective scheme $\operatorname{Proj}(I)$ is projectively normal under the $m$-th Veronese embedding.
