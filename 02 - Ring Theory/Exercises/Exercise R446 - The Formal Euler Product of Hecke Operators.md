# Exercise R446: The Formal Euler Product of Hecke Operators

## Problem Statement

> [!question] Original Exercise 5.3.2
> Show that definitions (5.10) and (5.11) imply relation (5.12).
>
> **Referenced formulas, printed p. 179 / PDF p. 193.** With $T_1=I$,
> $$
> T_{p^r}=T_pT_{p^{r-1}}-p^{k-1}\langle p\rangle T_{p^{r-2}}\quad(r\geq2),
> \qquad T_n=\prod_iT_{p_i^{r_i}}\quad\left(n=\prod_ip_i^{r_i}\right).
> $$
> Relation (5.12) is
> $$
> g(s)=\sum_{n\geq1}T_nn^{-s}
> =\prod_p(1-T_pp^{-s}+\langle p\rangle p^{k-1-2s})^{-1}.
> $$

## Hints

> [!hint]- Hint 1
> Multiply the prime-power generating function by its proposed quadratic denominator.

> [!hint]- Hint 2
> Every coefficient of positive degree vanishes by the recurrence. Unique prime factorization then assembles the local factors.

## Solution

> [!success]- Solution
> **Proof scope:** Independent derivation.
>
> Work in the commutative algebra generated by the commuting Hecke operators. For a fixed prime $p$, put
> $$
> A_p(X)=\sum_{r\geq0}T_{p^r}X^r,\qquad C_p=p^{k-1}\langle p\rangle.
> $$
> The constant coefficient of $(I-T_pX+C_pX^2)A_p(X)$ is $I$. Its coefficient of $X$ is $T_p-T_p=0$. For $r\geq2$, the coefficient of $X^r$ is
> $$
> T_{p^r}-T_pT_{p^{r-1}}+C_pT_{p^{r-2}}=0
> $$
> by (5.10). A formal power series with constant coefficient $I$ has a unique inverse, determined recursively by coefficients. Hence
> $$
> A_p(X)=(I-T_pX+C_pX^2)^{-1}.
> $$
> Now introduce the formal monomials $n^{-s}$, with multiplication $m^{-s}n^{-s}=(mn)^{-s}$. In the product $\prod_pA_p(p^{-s})$, the coefficient of $n^{-s}$ receives a contribution from precisely the prime powers in the unique factorization $n=\prod_ip_i^{r_i}$. It is $\prod_iT_{p_i^{r_i}}=T_n$ by (5.11). Thus
> $$
> \sum_{n\geq1}T_nn^{-s}=\prod_pA_p(p^{-s})
> =\prod_p(I-T_pp^{-s}+\langle p\rangle p^{k-1-2s})^{-1}.
> $$
> For each fixed coefficient only finitely many primes occur, so the formal infinite product is well defined. This proves (5.12) coefficientwise. Evaluating it as an analytic operator-valued series requires convergence bounds beyond the two algebraic definitions.
