# Exercise R289: Surjectivity of a Finite Local Homomorphism by Nakayama

## Problem Statement

> [!question] Lang, Chapter X, Exercise 10
> Let $A,B$ be local rings with maximal ideals $\mathfrak m_A,\mathfrak m_B$, respectively. Let $f:A\to B$ be a homomorphism. We say that $f$ is **local** if $f^{-1}(\mathfrak m_B)=\mathfrak m_A$. Suppose this is the case. Assume $A,B$ Noetherian, and assume that:
>
> 1. $A/\mathfrak m_A\to B/\mathfrak m_B$ is an isomorphism,
> 2. $\mathfrak m_A\to\mathfrak m_B/\mathfrak m_B^2$ is surjective;
> 3. $B$ is a finite $A$-module, via $f$.
>
> Prove that $f$ is surjective. [*Hint:* Apply Nakayama twice.]

## Hints

> [!hint]- Hint 1
> First use Nakayama over $B$ on the quotient of $\mathfrak m_B$ by the ideal $f(\mathfrak m_A)B$.

> [!hint]- Hint 2
> Once $\mathfrak m_B=f(\mathfrak m_A)B$, use the residue-field hypothesis to show that the finite $A$-module $B/f(A)$ equals $\mathfrak m_A(B/f(A))$. Apply Nakayama over $A$.

## Solution

> [!success]- Independently derived solution
> We use the local form of Nakayama's lemma: if $(R,\mathfrak m)$ is a commutative local ring, $E$ is a finitely generated $R$-module, and $E=\mathfrak mE$, then $E=0$. This is Lang's Lemma 4.1 specialized to a local ring [S2, Ch. X, §4, printed pp. 424-425, PDF pp. 439-440].
>
> **First application, over $B$.** Let
>
> $$
> I=f(\mathfrak m_A)B.
> $$
>
> Locality of $f$ gives $I\subseteq\mathfrak m_B$. The surjectivity in condition 2 says that each element of $\mathfrak m_B$ differs from some element of $f(\mathfrak m_A)$ by an element of $\mathfrak m_B^2$. In particular,
>
> $$
> \mathfrak m_B=I+\mathfrak m_B^2.
> $$
>
> The $B$-module $E=\mathfrak m_B/I$ is finitely generated, because $B$ is Noetherian and $\mathfrak m_B$ is an ideal. The last equality gives
>
> $$
> E=\mathfrak m_BE.
> $$
>
> Nakayama over $B$ yields $E=0$, hence
>
> $$
> \mathfrak m_B=f(\mathfrak m_A)B.
> $$
>
> **Second application, over $A$.** The surjective residue-field map in condition 1 implies that every $b\in B$ has the same residue class as some $f(a)$, so
>
> $$
> B=f(A)+\mathfrak m_B
> =f(A)+f(\mathfrak m_A)B.
> $$
>
> Regard $B$ as an $A$-module via $f$. Its additive subgroup $f(A)$ is an $A$-submodule, since $f(a)f(a')=f(aa')$. Consequently
>
> $$
> C=B/f(A)
> $$
>
> is an $A$-module, finitely generated by condition 3. Taking the quotient of the preceding equality by $f(A)$ gives $C=\mathfrak m_AC$. Nakayama over $A$ now gives $C=0$. Thus $B=f(A)$ and $f$ is surjective.

## Notes

- **Source status:** The complete statement, all three hypotheses, and the printed hint were visually checked at [S2, Ch. X, Ex. 10, printed p. 444, PDF p. 459]. The two applications are independently worked out. The imported Nakayama statement and its source proof were checked at printed pp. 424-425 / PDF pp. 439-440.
- **Different coefficient rings:** The first application is to a finite $B$-module; the second is to a finite $A$-module. Finite generation in the second step is exactly where hypothesis 3 is needed.
- **Quotient convention:** $B/f(A)$ is an additive module quotient, not generally a quotient ring, since $f(A)$ need not be an ideal of $B$.
- **Hypothesis boundary:** The proof only needs $\mathfrak m_B$ to be finitely generated for the first step; it does not separately use the Noetherian property of $A$. The original stronger hypotheses are preserved in the problem statement.
