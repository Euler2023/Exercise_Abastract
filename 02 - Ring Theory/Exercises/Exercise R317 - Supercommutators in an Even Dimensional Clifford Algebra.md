# Exercise R317: Supercommutators in an Even Dimensional Clifford Algebra

## Problem Statement

> [!question] Lang XIX.17
> Suppose that $k=\mathbb R$, so $E$ is a real vector space, which we now assume of even dimension $2m$. We also assume that $g$ is non-degenerate. We omit the index $g$ since the symmetric form is now fixed, and we write $C^+,C^-$ for the spaces of degree $0$ and $1$ respectively in the $\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z$-grading. For elements $x,y$ in $C^+$ or $C^-$, define their supercommutator to be
>
> $$
> \{x,y\}=xy-(-1)^{(\deg x)(\deg y)}yx.
> $$
>
> Show that $F_{2m-1}$ is generated by supercommutators.

## Hints

> [!hint]- Hint 1
> Choose an orthogonal basis $e_1,\ldots,e_{2m}$ with $e_j^2=c_j\ne0$. For a proper subset $I$, choose $j\notin I$.

> [!hint]- Hint 2
> Compute $\{e_j,e_je_I\}$. To prove equality of spans, show that every supercommutator has zero coefficient on the top monomial $e_1\cdots e_{2m}$.

## Solution

> [!success]- Independent derivation
> Here “generated” is understood as real linear span. Set $n=2m$. First suppose $n>0$. A nondegenerate real symmetric form has an orthogonal basis with $g(e_j,e_j)=c_j\in\{1,-1\}$. One can construct it inductively: some $v$ has $g(v,v)\ne0$, since otherwise polarization would give $g=0$; split off $\mathbb Rv$, whose orthogonal complement is again nondegenerate, and rescale each resulting vector.
>
> The Clifford relations are $e_j^2=c_j$ and $e_ie_j=-e_je_i$ for $i\ne j$. By the ordered-basis argument of R316, $F_{n-1}$ has basis the monomials $e_I$ for proper subsets $I\subsetneq\{1,\ldots,n\}$.
>
> Choose $j\notin I$, and put $r=\lvert I\rvert$. The two homogeneous elements $e_j$ and $e_je_I$ have parities $1$ and $r+1$ modulo $2$. Also $e_je_Ie_j=(-1)^r c_je_I$. Hence
>
> $$
> \begin{aligned}
> \{e_j,e_je_I\}
> &=c_je_I-(-1)^{r+1}e_je_Ie_j\\
> &=c_je_I-(-1)^{r+1+r}c_je_I
> =2c_je_I.
> \end{aligned}
> $$
>
> Since $2c_j$ is invertible, every basis vector of $F_{n-1}$ is a scalar multiple of a supercommutator.
>
> For the reverse inclusion, bilinearity reduces the question to $\{e_I,e_J\}$. Products of orthogonal Clifford basis monomials are nonzero scalar multiples of $e_{I\triangle J}$. A term of degree $n$ can occur only if $I\triangle J$ is the full index set, which means $I,J$ are disjoint complementary subsets. In that case
>
> $$
> e_Je_I=(-1)^{\lvert I\rvert\lvert J\rvert}e_Ie_J,
> $$
>
> so their supercommutator is zero. Every other such bracket has degree at most $n-1$. Therefore the linear span of all supercommutators equals $F_{n-1}$.
>
> If $m=0$, use $F_{-1}=0$. The algebra is $\mathbb R$ in even degree, all supercommutators vanish, and the assertion remains true with that convention.

## Notes

The statement was checked at [S2, Ch. XIX, Exercise 17, printed p. 757, PDF p. 772]. The proof is independent and spells out the span interpretation: for positive dimension, $1=\{e_j,e_j\}/(2c_j)$ is a supercommutator multiple, so the ideal generated by all supercommutators would instead be the whole algebra. Nondegeneracy is used to make every $c_j$ invertible. The same span argument actually works in odd positive dimension as well.
