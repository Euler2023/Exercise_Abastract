# Exercise R224: The p-Adic Integers as an Inverse Limit

## Problem Statement

> [!question] Exercise 17
> **(a)** Let $n$ range over the positive integers and let $p$ be a prime number. Show that the abelian groups
>
> $$
> A_n=\mathbb Z/p^n\mathbb Z
> $$
>
> form a projective system under the canonical homomorphisms for $n\ge m$. Let $\mathbb Z_p$ be its inverse limit. Show that $\mathbb Z_p$ maps surjectively onto each $\mathbb Z/p^n\mathbb Z$; that $\mathbb Z_p$ has no divisors of zero and has a unique maximal ideal generated by $p$. Show that $\mathbb Z_p$ is factorial, with only one prime, namely $p$ itself.
>
> **(b)** Next consider all ideals of $\mathbb Z$ as forming a directed system by divisibility. Prove that
>
> $$
> \varprojlim_{(a)}\mathbb Z/(a)
> =
> \prod_p\mathbb Z_p,
> $$
>
> where the limit is taken over all ideals $(a)$ and the product is taken over all primes $p$.

> [!info] Indexing convention
> In part (b), as in the nonzero-ideal convention used in this ideal-theoretic inverse system, $(a)$ ranges over nonzero ideals of $\mathbb Z$. Including $(0)$ would add the quotient $\mathbb Z/(0)$ and change the stated inverse limit.

## Hints

> [!hint]- Hint 1
> A compatible family in $\mathbb Z_p$ has a unique base-$p$ expansion
>
> $$
> x=a_0+a_1p+a_2p^2+\cdots,
> \qquad
> 0\le a_i<p.
> $$

> [!hint]- Hint 2
> A nonzero $p$-adic integer has a first nonzero digit. Factor off the corresponding power of $p$ and show that the remaining factor is a unit.

> [!hint]- Hint 3
> For (b), apply the Chinese remainder theorem to each nonzero integer
>
> $$
> a=\prod_p p^{v_p(a)}
> $$
>
> and then check compatibility as $(a)$ varies.

## Solution

> [!success]- Solution
> **(a) The inverse system and its projections.** For $n\ge m$, reduction modulo $p^m$ gives a surjective ring homomorphism
>
> $$
> \rho_{nm}:\mathbb Z/p^n\mathbb Z
> \longrightarrow
> \mathbb Z/p^m\mathbb Z.
> $$
>
> These maps satisfy $\rho_{nn}=\operatorname{id}$ and $\rho_{m\ell}\rho_{nm}=\rho_{n\ell}$, so they form an inverse system. Its limit is
>
> $$
> \mathbb Z_p
> =
> \left\{(x_n)\in\prod_{n\ge1}\mathbb Z/p^n\mathbb Z:
> x_{n+1}\equiv x_n\pmod{p^n}\right\}.
> $$
>
> Given a class $c\in\mathbb Z/p^n\mathbb Z$, choose an integer representative $c$. The family of its reductions modulo every $p^m$ is compatible and has $n$-th coordinate $c$. Hence every projection
>
> $$
> \mathbb Z_p\longrightarrow\mathbb Z/p^n\mathbb Z
> $$
>
> is surjective.
>
> **Digits, units, and zero divisors.** Compatibility determines unique digits $a_i\in\{0,\ldots,p-1\}$ such that
>
> $$
> x_n\equiv\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}a_ip^i\pmod{p^n}.
> $$
>
> Explicitly, $a_0$ is determined by $x_1$, and once $a_0,\ldots,a_{n-1}$ are known, the lift from $x_n$ to $x_{n+1}$ determines the unique next digit $a_n$.
>
> If $x\ne0$, let $r$ be the least index for which $a_r\ne0$. Shifting the digit sequence gives
>
> $$
> x=p^r u,
> $$
>
> where the first digit of $u$ is $a_r\ne0$. Such a $u$ is a unit: every coordinate $u_n$ is invertible modulo $p^n$, and the inverses are compatible. Conversely, an element with zero first digit is divisible by $p$ and cannot be a unit. Thus
>
> $$
> \mathbb Z_p^\times
> =
> \{x:x\bmod p\ne0\},
> \qquad
> \mathbb Z_p\setminus\mathbb Z_p^\times=p\mathbb Z_p.
> $$
>
> If $x=p^ru$ and $y=p^sv$ are nonzero with $u,v$ units, then
>
> $$
> xy=p^{r+s}uv\ne0.
> $$
>
> Here $uv$ is a unit and $p^{r+s}$ is nonzero because its coordinate modulo $p^n$ is nonzero for every $n>r+s$. Hence the displayed product cannot vanish.
>
> Hence $\mathbb Z_p$ is an integral domain.
>
> The reduction map $\mathbb Z_p\to\mathbb F_p$ has kernel $p\mathbb Z_p$, so $p\mathbb Z_p$ is maximal. Every nonunit lies in it, and therefore it is the unique maximal ideal.
>
> Finally, every nonzero nonunit has a factorization
>
> $$
> x=u p^r
> $$
>
> with $u$ a unit and $r\ge1$. The integer $r$ is the position of the first nonzero digit and is therefore unique. Thus $\mathbb Z_p$ is a unique factorization domain, and, up to associates, its only prime element is $p$.
>
> **(b) Decomposition over all primes.** Let the inverse limit range over nonzero ideals $(a)$, equivalently over positive integers $a$, ordered by divisibility. For
>
> $$
> a=\prod_{p\mid a}p^{v_p(a)},
> $$
>
> the Chinese remainder theorem gives
>
> $$
> \mathbb Z/a\mathbb Z
> \cong
> \prod_{p\mid a}\mathbb Z/p^{v_p(a)}\mathbb Z.
> $$
>
> A compatible family $(x_a)_a$ determines, by restricting to $a=p^n$, one element of $\mathbb Z_p$ for every prime $p$. This defines a ring homomorphism
>
> $$
> \Phi:
> \varprojlim_{(a)\ne(0)}\mathbb Z/(a)
> \longrightarrow
> \prod_p\mathbb Z_p.
> $$
>
> If every $p$-primary coordinate of a family is zero, then the Chinese remainder decomposition shows that $x_a=0$ for every $a$, so $\Phi$ is injective.
>
> Conversely, take a family $(y_p)_p\in\prod_p\mathbb Z_p$. For each positive integer $a$, define $x_a\in\mathbb Z/a\mathbb Z$ by requiring, for the finitely many primes dividing $a$,
>
> $$
> x_a\equiv y_p
> \pmod{p^{v_p(a)}}.
> $$
>
> The Chinese remainder theorem gives a unique $x_a$, and these residues are compatible whenever one modulus divides another. Thus $(x_a)_a$ lies in the inverse limit and maps to $(y_p)_p$. Therefore $\Phi$ is surjective and
>
> $$
> \varprojlim_{(a)\ne(0)}\mathbb Z/(a)
> \cong
> \prod_p\mathbb Z_p.
> $$

## Notes

- **Terminology:** Lang's “projective system” is an inverse system, and “factorial” means a unique factorization domain.
- **Index boundary:** The inverse limit in (b) is indexed by nonzero ideals. This convention is made explicit rather than silently allowing the zero ideal.
- **Routing rationale:** Ring multiplication, units, maximal ideals, prime elements, and the Chinese remainder theorem provide the primary computational toolkit.
- **Source status:** Part (a) was checked on printed p. 169/PDF p. 184 and part (b), including the inverse-limit arrow and full direct product, on printed p. 170/PDF p. 185: [S2, Ch. III, Ex. 17]. The proofs are independent.
