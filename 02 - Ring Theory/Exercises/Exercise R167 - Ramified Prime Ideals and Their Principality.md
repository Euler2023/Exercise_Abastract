# Exercise R167: Ramified Prime Ideals and Their Principality

## Problem Statement

> [!question] Exercise 6.5
> Assume that $d$ is congruent $2$ or $3$ modulo $4$.
>
> **(a)** Prove that a prime integer $p$ ramifies in $R$ if and only if $p=2$ or $p$ divides $d$.
>
> **(b)** Let $p$ be an integer prime that ramifies, and say that $(p)=P^2$. Find an explicit lattice basis for $P$. In which cases is $P$ a principal ideal?

## Hints

> [!hint]- Hint 1
> Ramification is equivalent to $x^2-d$ having a repeated root modulo $p$.

> [!hint]- Hint 2
> For principality, an ideal of index $p$ must be generated by an element of norm $p$.

## Solution

> [!success]- Solution
> Here $R=\mathbb Z[\delta]$ with $\delta^2=d$.
>
> **(a)** For an odd prime $p$, ramification means that $x^2-d$ has a repeated root modulo $p$. Its discriminant is $4d$, so this occurs exactly when $p\mid d$. For $p=2$, if $d$ is even then $x^2-d\equiv x^2$, while if $d$ is odd then $x^2-d\equiv x^2+1=(x+1)^2$. In either case the root is repeated. Hence
>
> $$
> \boxed{p\text{ ramifies}\iff p=2\text{ or }p\mid d.}
> $$
>
> **(b)** The ramified prime ideal has the following lattice bases:
>
> $$
> P=
> \begin{cases}
> (p,\delta),&p\text{ odd and }p\mid d,\\
> (2,\delta),&p=2\text{ and }d\equiv2\pmod4,\\
> (2,1+\delta),&p=2\text{ and }d\equiv3\pmod4.
> \end{cases}
> $$
>
> In each case the displayed lattice has index $p$, its quotient is $\mathbb F_p$, and direct multiplication gives $P^2=(p)$. When $p$ is odd, the square is generated by $p^2,p\delta,d$; square-freeness of $d$ gives $\gcd(p^2,d)=p$. For $p=2$ with even $d$, the same argument uses $\gcd(4,d)=2$. For $p=2$ with odd $d$, the generators include $4$, $2+2\delta$, and $(1+\delta)^2$; subtracting $2(1+\delta)$ from the last gives $d-1$, and $\gcd(4,d-1)=2$ because $d\equiv3\pmod4$. Thus each square is exactly $(p)$.
>
> The ideal $P$ is principal exactly when there is an element of norm $p$. Since
>
> $$
> N(a+b\delta)=a^2-db^2=a^2+|d|b^2,
> $$
>
> the equation $N(a+b\delta)=p$ has the following possibilities:
>
> - If $p$ is odd and divides the square-free integer $d$, it has a solution exactly when $d=-p$, in which case $P=(\delta)$.
> - If $p=2$ and $d\equiv2\pmod4$, it has a solution exactly when $d=-2$, again with $P=(\delta)$.
> - If $p=2$ and $d\equiv3\pmod4$, it has a solution exactly when $d=-1$, with $P=(1+\delta)$ up to association.
>
> Therefore
>
> $$
> \boxed{P\text{ is principal exactly for }d=-p
> \text{ (odd }p),\text{ or }(p,d)=(2,-2),(2,-1).}
> $$
