# Exercise R437: Hecke Eigenvalue Rings and Redundant Character Values

## Problem Statement

> [!question] Original Exercise 6.5.2
> Let $f\in S_2(\Gamma_1(N))$ be a normalized eigenform. Thus $f\in S_2(N,\chi)$ for some Dirichlet character $\chi:(\mathbb Z/N\mathbb Z)^*\longrightarrow\mathbb C^*$ and $\lambda_f(\langle d\rangle)=\chi(d)$ for all $d\in(\mathbb Z/N\mathbb Z)^*$. Show that there is a ring and $\mathbb Z$-module isomorphism $\mathbb T_{\mathbb Z}/I_f\simeq\mathbb Z[\{a_n(f),\chi(d)\}]$. Show that adjoining the $\chi(d)$ values is redundant, making (6.12) in the text correct. (A hint for this exercise is at the end of the book.)

## Hints

> [!hint]- Hint 1
> Apply the first isomorphism theorem to the eigenvalue homomorphism on the integral Hecke algebra.

> [!hint]- Hint 2
> For a prime $p\nmid N$, use $a_p^2-a_{p^2}=p\chi(p)$. Products show $m\chi(m)$ lies in the Fourier-coefficient ring whenever $\gcd(m,N)=1$.

> [!hint]- Hint 3
> For a positive representative $d$ prime to $N$, the integers $d$ and $d+N$ are coprime and have the same character value. Use Bezout to remove their integer factors.

## Solution

> [!success]- Solution
> **Proof scope:** Independent derivation.
>
> The eigenvalue map $\lambda_f:\mathbb T_{\mathbb Z}\to\mathbb C$ is a unital ring homomorphism, since $(TT')f=\lambda_f(T)\lambda_f(T')f$ and eigenvalues add for sums. Its kernel is $I_f$. The integral Hecke algebra is generated by the $T_n$ and diamond operators; on a normalized eigenform their eigenvalues are $a_n(f)$ and $\chi(d)$. Therefore its image is exactly $\mathbb Z[\{a_n(f),\chi(d)\}]$. The first isomorphism theorem gives the required ring isomorphism, which is also $\mathbb Z$-linear.
>
> Put $B=\mathbb Z[\{a_n(f):n\ge1\}]$. For each prime $p\nmid N$, the weight-two Hecke relation $T_p^2=T_{p^2}+p\langle p\rangle$ gives
> $$
> p\chi(p)=a_p(f)^2-a_{p^2}(f)\in B.
> $$
> If $m>0$ is prime to $N$, factor it into primes and use multiplicativity of $\chi$:
> $$
> m\chi(m)=\prod_{p\mid m}\bigl(p\chi(p)\bigr)^{v_p(m)}\in B.
> $$
> Now choose a positive representative $d$ of any unit residue class. Both $d$ and $d+N$ are prime to $N$, their greatest common divisor is $\gcd(d,N)=1$, and their character values are equal. Thus $d\chi(d)$ and $(d+N)\chi(d)$ lie in $B$. Choose integers $u,v$ with $ud+v(d+N)=1$. Then
> $$
> \chi(d)=u\,d\chi(d)+v(d+N)\chi(d+N)\in B.
> $$
> Every diamond eigenvalue is already in $B$, proving
> $$
> \mathbb T_{\mathbb Z}/I_f\simeq\mathbb Z[\{a_n(f)\}].
> $$
> No existence theorem for primes in arithmetic progressions is required by this argument.
