# Exercise R99: A Parametric Plane-Curve Kernel Is Principal

## Problem Statement

> [!question] Exercise 9.13
> Let $\varphi:\mathbb C[x,y]\to\mathbb C[t]$ fix $\mathbb C$ and send $x\mapsto x(t)$, $y\mapsto y(t)$, where $x(t),y(t)$ are not both constant. Prove that $\ker\varphi$ is a principal ideal.

## Hints

> [!hint]- Hint 1
> First obtain a nonzero polynomial relation by a dimension count, then choose an irreducible factor in the prime kernel.

> [!hint]- Hint 2
> If another kernel element were coprime to that factor, the two curves would have only finitely many common points.

## Solution

> [!success]- Solution
> Put $d=\max\{\deg x(t),\deg y(t)\}$. For $0\le i,j\le N$, the $(N+1)^2$ polynomials $x(t)^iy(t)^j$ all have degree at most $2Nd$, so they lie in a vector space of dimension $2Nd+1$. For sufficiently large $N$,
>
> $$
> (N+1)^2>2Nd+1.
> $$
>
> They are therefore linearly dependent over $\mathbb C$, and the resulting dependence is a nonzero polynomial relation. Thus $K=\ker\varphi\ne0$. Since the target $\mathbb C[t]$ is a domain, $K$ is prime. Choose $0\ne h\in K$ and an irreducible factor $f$ of $h$. Primality gives $f\in K$.
>
> For any $g\in K$, suppose $f\nmid g$. Then $f$ and $g$ have no common nonconstant factor, so Artin's finite-intersection theorem says $V(f,g)$ is finite. But every point $(x(t),y(t))$ lies in this intersection. At least one coordinate polynomial is nonconstant and hence takes infinitely many values on $\mathbb C$, so the parametrized image is infinite. This contradiction shows $f\mid g$. Hence $K=(f)$.
