# Exercise R388: Discriminants of Primitive Elements and Common Index Divisors

## Problem Statement

> [!question] Neukirch III.2.1
> Let $d(\alpha)=d(1,\alpha,\ldots,\alpha^{n-1})$, for an element $\alpha\in\mathcal O$ such that $L=K(\alpha)$. Show that $\mathfrak d_{L|K}$ is generated by all discriminants of elements $d(\alpha)$ if $\mathfrak o$ is a complete discrete valuation ring and the residue field extension $\lambda|\kappa$ is separable. In other words, $\mathfrak d_{L|K}$ equals the gcd of all discriminants of individual elements. This fails to hold in general. Counterexample: $K=\mathbb Q$, $L=\mathbb Q(\alpha)$, $\alpha^3-\alpha^2-2\alpha-8=0$. (See [60], chap. III, §25, p. 443. The untranslatable German catch phrase for this phenomenon is: there are “außerwesentliche Diskriminantenteiler”.)

## Hints

> [!hint]- Hint 1
> Over a complete discrete valuation ring with separable residue extension, the integral closure has the form $\mathfrak o[\beta]$. Thus at least one power basis is an integral basis.

> [!hint]- Hint 2
> In the counterexample, put $\beta=(\alpha^2+\alpha)/2$. Compute the discriminant of $1,\alpha,\beta$, then check that every element of $\mathcal O_L/2\mathcal O_L$ satisfies $z^2=z$.

> [!hint]- Hint 3
> The three-dimensional algebra $\mathbb F_2^3$ cannot be generated by one element as an $\mathbb F_2$-algebra: every element satisfies $X^2-X$. Consequently every integral power basis has even index.

## Solution

> [!success]- Solution
> **Proof scope:** Independent derivation
>
> Here $\mathfrak d$ is the discriminant ideal in the base ring, whereas $\mathfrak D$ is the different in the extension ring. These must be distinguished. Put $n=[L:K]$.
>
> **1. The complete local case.** The proved generation lemma [Jürgen Neukirch, Algebraic Number Theory, English ed., 1999, Ch. II, §10, (10.4), printed p. 178, PDF p. 197] gives $\mathcal O=\mathfrak o[\beta]$ under the complete discrete valuation and separable residue-extension hypotheses. Hence $1,\beta,\ldots,\beta^{n-1}$ is an $\mathfrak o$-basis and $\mathfrak d_{L|K}=(d(\beta))$.
>
> The power basis of any other integral primitive element $\gamma$ has a change-of-basis matrix $A\in M_n(\mathfrak o)$ relative to this integral basis. Transforming the Gram matrix of the trace pairing gives
> $$
> d(\gamma)=\det(A)^2d(\beta)\in(d(\beta)).
> $$
> Thus the ideal generated by all $d(\gamma)$ contains the generator $d(\beta)$ and is contained in $(d(\beta))$, so it equals $\mathfrak d_{L|K}$.
>
> **2. An integral basis for the printed counterexample.** Put $f(X)=X^3-X^2-2X-8$. Modulo $3$, it is nonzero at $0,1,2$, so $f$ is irreducible. The cubic discriminant formula gives
> $$
> \operatorname{disc}(f)=-2012=-4\cdot503.
> $$
> Put $\beta=(\alpha^2+\alpha)/2$. Substitution using $f(\alpha)=0$ gives
> $$
> \alpha^2=2\beta-\alpha,\quad
> \alpha\beta=2\beta+4,\quad
> \beta^2=3\beta+2\alpha+6,\quad
> \beta^3-3\beta^2-10\beta-8=0.
> $$
> The last equation proves that $\beta$ is integral, and the first three show that $B=\mathbb Z+\mathbb Z\alpha+\mathbb Z\beta$ is a subring of the field. The change-of-basis determinant is $1/2$, so
> $$
> \operatorname{disc}(B)=\tfrac14\operatorname{disc}(1,\alpha,\alpha^2)=-503.
> $$
> The number $503$ is prime: check the primes $2,3,5,7,11,13,17,19$ below $\sqrt{503}<23$. The square of the index of $B$ in the full ring of integers divides $503$, so that index is $1$. Hence $B=\mathcal O_L$ and $d_L=-503$.
>
> **3. Every integral primitive power basis has even index.** In $B/2B$, the multiplication relations become
> $$
> \bar\alpha^2=\bar\alpha,\qquad \bar\beta^2=\bar\beta,\qquad \bar\alpha\bar\beta=0.
> $$
> Evaluation at $(\bar\alpha,\bar\beta)\mapsto(0,0),(1,0),(0,1)$ gives $B/2B\simeq\mathbb F_2^3$: the matrix of these three evaluations on the basis $1,\bar\alpha,\bar\beta$ is invertible. Every $z$ satisfies $z^2=z$, so $\mathbb F_2[z]$ has dimension at most $2$ and cannot equal the three-dimensional algebra $B/2B$.
>
> If an integral primitive element $\theta$ satisfied $2\nmid[B:\mathbb Z[\theta]]$, multiplication by $2$ on the finite quotient $B/\mathbb Z[\theta]$ would be invertible, giving $B=\mathbb Z[\theta]+2B$. Then $B/2B$ would be generated by $\bar\theta$, a contradiction. Every such index is therefore even, and
> $$
> d(\theta)=[B:\mathbb Z[\theta]]^2d_L
> $$
> is divisible by $4\cdot503$. The discriminant of $\theta=\alpha$ is exactly $-4\cdot503$. Thus the gcd ideal of all element discriminants is $(2012)$, whereas the field discriminant ideal is $(503)$; they are different.
