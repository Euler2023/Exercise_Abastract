# Exercise R282: A Uniform Power in the Primary Ideal Product Criterion

## Problem Statement

> [!question] Lang, Chapter X, Exercise 3
> Let $\mathfrak q$ be a primary ideal. Let $\mathfrak a,\mathfrak b$ be ideals, and assume $\mathfrak a\mathfrak b\subset\mathfrak q$. Assume that $\mathfrak b$ is finitely generated. Show that $\mathfrak a\subset\mathfrak q$ or there exists some positive integer $n$ such that $\mathfrak b^n\subset\mathfrak q$.

## Hints

> [!hint]- Hint 1
> If $\mathfrak a\not\subseteq\mathfrak q$, fix $a\in\mathfrak a\setminus\mathfrak q$ and apply primaryness separately to the finitely many generators of $\mathfrak b$.

> [!hint]- Hint 2
> If $\mathfrak b=(b_1,\ldots,b_t)$ and $b_i^{e_i}\in\mathfrak q$, use
>
> $$
> n=1+\sum_{i=1}^t(e_i-1).
> $$
>
> Every monomial of total degree $n$ in the $b_i$ contains one of the powers $b_i^{e_i}$.

## Solution

> [!success]- Independently derived solution
> If $\mathfrak a\subseteq\mathfrak q$, there is nothing further to prove. Otherwise choose $a\in\mathfrak a\setminus\mathfrak q$ and a finite generating set
>
> $$
> \mathfrak b=(b_1,\ldots,b_t).
> $$
>
> If $\mathfrak b=0$, the second alternative holds with $n=1$. For each generator in the remaining case, $ab_i\in\mathfrak a\mathfrak b\subseteq\mathfrak q$. Since $\mathfrak q$ is primary and $a\notin\mathfrak q$, there exists $e_i\ge1$ such that $b_i^{e_i}\in\mathfrak q$.
>
> Set $n=1+\sum_{i=1}^t(e_i-1)$. The ideal $\mathfrak b^n$ is generated by the products
>
> $$
> b_1^{d_1}\cdots b_t^{d_t},
> \qquad d_i\ge0,\qquad \sum_i d_i=n.
> $$
>
> For each such product, some $d_i\ge e_i$; otherwise its total degree would be at most $\sum_i(e_i-1)=n-1$. The product therefore belongs to the ideal generated by $b_i^{e_i}$ and hence belongs to $\mathfrak q$. All generators of $\mathfrak b^n$ lie in $\mathfrak q$, proving $\mathfrak b^n\subseteq\mathfrak q$.

## Notes

- **Source status:** The statement was visually checked at [S2, Ch. X, Ex. 3, printed p. 443, PDF p. 458]. The proof is an independent finite-generator argument.
- **Definition used:** Primaryness means $ab\in\mathfrak q$ and $a\notin\mathfrak q$ imply $b^e\in\mathfrak q$ for some $e\ge1$; this is Lang's ideal criterion [S2, Ch. X, §3, printed p. 421, PDF p. 436].
- **Role of finite generation:** Individual generators initially have different exponents. The displayed bound produces one power of the whole ideal. No Noetherian assumption on $A$ is needed.
