# Exercise R263: Common Zeros of Homogeneous Forms in Excess Variables

## Problem Statement

> [!question]
> Let $f_1, \ldots, f_r$ be forms in $n$ variables and suppose $n > r$. Prove that these forms have a non-trivial common zero.

## Hints

> [!hint]- Hint 1
> Consider the forms $f_1, \ldots, f_r$ in $k[X_1, \ldots, X_n]$ over a field $k$, with common zeros evaluated in an algebraic closure $k^a$.
>
> What is the dimension of the affine space $\mathbb{A}^n(k^a)$, and what does Krull's Hauptidealsatz (or the affine dimension theorem) say about the codimension of the zero locus of an ideal generated by $r$ elements?

> [!hint]- Hint 2
> The zero locus $V(f_1, \ldots, f_r) \subset \mathbb{A}^n(k^a)$ is an algebraic cone containing the origin $(0, \ldots, 0)$.
>
> If the origin were the only common zero, the projective zero locus in $\mathbb{P}^{n-1}(k^a)$ would be empty. What is the minimum possible dimension of the intersection of $r$ hypersurfaces in $\mathbb{P}^{n-1}(k^a)$?

## Solution

> [!success]- Solution
> Let $k$ be a field containing the coefficients of the forms $f_1, \ldots, f_r \in k[X_1, \ldots, X_n]$, and let $k^a$ be an algebraic closure of $k$.
> We seek a point $(\xi_1, \ldots, \xi_n) \in (k^a)^n \setminus \{(0, \ldots, 0)\}$ such that $f_j(\xi_1, \ldots, \xi_n) = 0$ for all $j = 1, \ldots, r$.
>
> We present two standard proofs: one via projective intersection dimension, and one via Exercise 1 (Hilbert-Zariski integrality).
>
> ### Method 1: Projective Dimension and Krull's Principal Ideal Theorem
>
> Let $\mathfrak{a} = (f_1, \ldots, f_r) \subseteq k[X_1, \ldots, X_n]$ be the homogeneous ideal generated by the $r$ forms, and let
> $$
> Z = V(\mathfrak{a}) = \{x \in (k^a)^n : f_1(x) = \cdots = f_r(x) = 0\} \subset \mathbb{A}^n(k^a).
> $$
> Since each $f_j$ is a homogeneous polynomial of positive degree $d_j \ge 1$, we have $f_j(0, \ldots, 0) = 0$ for all $j$. Thus $0 \in Z$, so $Z$ is non-empty.
>
> In the affine coordinate ring $k[X_1, \ldots, X_n]$, the Krull dimension is $\dim(k[X_1, \ldots, X_n]) = n$.
> By Krull's Generalized Principal Ideal Theorem (or the codimension bound for complete intersections), every minimal prime ideal $\mathfrak{p}$ over the ideal $\mathfrak{a} = (f_1, \ldots, f_r)$ has height bounded by the number of generators:
> $$
> \operatorname{ht}(\mathfrak{p}) \le r.
> $$
> Because $k[X_1, \ldots, X_n]$ is an affine domain over a field, the dimension formula holds for every prime ideal:
> $$
> \dim(k[X]/\mathfrak{p}) = n - \operatorname{ht}(\mathfrak{p}) \ge n - r.
> $$
> Since $n > r$, we have $n - r \ge 1$.
> Therefore, every irreducible component of $Z$ has dimension at least $n - r \ge 1$.
>
> If $Z$ consisted solely of the single point $\{0\}$, then the origin would be an isolated point of $Z$, forming an irreducible component of dimension $0$.
> But this contradicts the fact that every irreducible component of $Z$ has dimension $\ge 1$.
> Thus $Z$ must contain points other than the origin, which means $f_1, \ldots, f_r$ have a non-trivial common zero in $(k^a)^n$.
>
> Alternatively, in the projective space $\mathbb{P}^{n-1}(k^a)$, the projective dimension of $\mathbb{P}^{n-1}$ is $n - 1$.
> Cutting by each hypersurface $V_{\mathbb{P}}(f_j)$ reduces the dimension of each component by at most $1$.
> The intersection $V_{\mathbb{P}}(f_1, \ldots, f_r) = \bigcap_{j=1}^r V_{\mathbb{P}}(f_j)$ therefore has dimension at least
> $$
> (n - 1) - r \ge 0.
> $$
> A projective algebraic set over an algebraically closed field of dimension $\ge 0$ is necessarily non-empty.
> Any point $[\xi_1 : \cdots : \xi_n] \in V_{\mathbb{P}}(f_1, \ldots, f_r)$ provides a non-trivial common zero $(\xi_1, \ldots, \xi_n) \neq (0, \ldots, 0)$.
>
> ### Method 2: Via Exercise 1 (Hilbert-Zariski Integrality)
>
> We apply Exercise 1 to the full affine space $V = \mathbb{A}^n$, whose generic point is $(X) = (X_1, \ldots, X_n)$ with coordinate ring $k[X] = k[X_1, \ldots, X_n]$.
> Suppose for contradiction that $V(f_1, \ldots, f_r)$ has only the trivial zero $\{0\}$.
> By Exercise 1, this holds if and only if each coordinate variable $X_i$ is integral over the subring
> $$
> B = k[f_1(X), \ldots, f_r(X)] \subseteq k[X_1, \ldots, X_n].
> $$
> If each $X_i$ is integral over $B$, then the extension of fraction fields
> $$
> k(f_1(X), \ldots, f_r(X)) \subseteq k(X_1, \ldots, X_n)
> $$
> is an algebraic extension.
> Consequently, the transcendence degrees over $k$ must be equal:
> $$
> \operatorname{tr.deg}_k k(X_1, \ldots, X_n) = \operatorname{tr.deg}_k k(f_1(X), \ldots, f_r(X)).
> $$
> The left-hand side is $n$, because $X_1, \ldots, X_n$ are algebraically independent indeterminates over $k$.
> The right-hand side is at most $r$, because the field is generated by the $r$ elements $f_1(X), \ldots, f_r(X)$:
> $$
> \operatorname{tr.deg}_k k(f_1(X), \ldots, f_r(X)) \le r.
> $$
> Hence we obtain $n \le r$.
> But this contradicts the hypothesis $n > r$.
> Therefore, $f_1, \ldots, f_r$ cannot have only the trivial zero, so a non-trivial common zero exists.

## Notes

- **Independent derivation:** Both the dimension-theoretic proof and the transcendence-degree/integrality reduction are independently supplied.
- **Hypotheses:** The result holds over any ground field $k$, with the common zero found in an algebraic closure $k^a$. If $k$ itself is algebraically closed, the common zero has coordinates in $k$.
