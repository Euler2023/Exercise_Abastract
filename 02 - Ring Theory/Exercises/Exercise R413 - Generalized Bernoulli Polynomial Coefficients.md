# Exercise R413: Generalized Bernoulli Polynomial Coefficients

## Problem Statement

> [!question] Neukirch VII.2.1
> Let $F_\chi(t,x)=\sum_{a=1}^m\chi(a)\dfrac{te^{(a+x)t}}{e^{mt}-1}$. The Bernoulli polynomials $B_{k,\chi}(x)$ associated to the Dirichlet character $\chi$ are defined by
> $$
> F_\chi(t,x)=\sum_{k=0}^\infty B_{k,\chi}(x)\frac{t^k}{k!}.
> $$
> Thus $B_{k,\chi}(0)=B_{k,\chi}$. Show that
> $$
> B_{k,\chi}(x)=\sum_{i=0}^k\binom{k}{i}B_{i,\chi}x^{k-i}.
> $$

## Hints

> [!hint]- Hint 1
> Factor $e^{xt}$ out of the finite sum defining $F_\chi(t,x)$.

> [!hint]- Hint 2
> Compare the $t^k$ coefficient in $F_\chi(t,0)e^{xt}$.

## Solution

> [!success]- Solution
> **Proof scope:** Independent derivation
>
> Work over the characteristic-zero field $\mathbb Q(\chi)$ generated by the character values. Since $(e^{mt}-1)/t$ has nonzero constant term $m$, every summand defining $F_\chi$ is a formal power series. We have exactly
> $$
> F_\chi(t,x)=F_\chi(t,0)e^{xt}
> =\left(\sum_{i\ge0}B_{i,\chi}\frac{t^i}{i!}\right)
> \left(\sum_{j\ge0}x^j\frac{t^j}{j!}\right).
> $$
> The coefficient of $t^k$ is therefore
> $$
> \frac{B_{k,\chi}(x)}{k!}=\sum_{i=0}^k\frac{B_{i,\chi}x^{k-i}}{i!(k-i)!}.
> $$
> Multiplying by $k!$ gives the claim for all $k\ge0$. For instance,
> $$
> B_{1,\chi}(x)=B_{1,\chi}+B_{0,\chi}x,
> \qquad B_{0,\chi}=\frac1m\sum_{a=1}^m\chi(a).
> $$
> For a nontrivial character the sum is zero: choose a unit $u$ with $\chi(u)\ne1$ and permute the residues by $a\mapsto ua$. Hence $B_{1,\chi}(x)$ is constant in that case. This is consistent with the general formula rather than a failure of its polynomial degree bound.
