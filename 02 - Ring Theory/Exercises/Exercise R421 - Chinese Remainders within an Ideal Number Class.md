# Exercise R421: Chinese Remainders within an Ideal Number Class

## Problem Statement

> [!question] Neukirch VII.7.4
> A system of finitely many congruences with pairwise relatively prime moduli is simultaneously solvable if every congruence is solvable individually in such a way that the individual solutions are equivalent (with respect to $\sim$).

## Hints

> [!hint]- Hint 1
> Use ordinary CRT idempotents $e_i$ for the ideals generated by the moduli.

> [!hint]- Hint 2
> If the chosen solutions $u_i$ belong to one $K$-multiple, the sum $x=\sum e_i u_i$ is defined and satisfies $x\equiv u_i$ at every modulus.

## Solution

> [!success]- Solution
> **Proof scope:** Independent derivation
>
> The source constructs a multiplicative group $\widehat K^*$ with a surjective ideal map $a\mapsto(a)$ and exact sequence
> $$
> 1\longrightarrow\mathfrak o^*\longrightarrow\widehat K^*\longrightarrow J\longrightarrow1.
> $$
> Its integral ideal numbers are $\widehat{\mathfrak o}=\{a:(a)\subseteq\mathfrak o\}$, and $a\sim b$ means $a/b\in K^*$. This multiplicative set is not generally a ring. Sums below are formed only among elements in a common $K$-multiple, where ordinary addition is defined.
>
> Let the pairwise coprime integral modulus ideals be $\mathfrak m_i=(m_i)$. By ordinary CRT choose $e_i\in\mathfrak o$ with
> $$
> e_i\equiv1\pmod{\mathfrak m_i},\qquad e_i\equiv0\pmod{\mathfrak m_j}\quad(j\ne i).
> $$
> Let $u_i$ be integral individual solutions with $u_i\sim u_j$ for every $i,j$. Choose $t=u_1$, so $u_i=t\alpha_i$ with $\alpha_i\in K^*$, and set
> $$
> x=\sum_i e_i u_i=t\sum_i e_i\alpha_i.
> $$
> Every $\alpha_i$ lies in the fractional ideal $(t)^{-1}$ because $u_i$ is integral. Their integral linear combination lies there also; hence $x$ is integral if nonzero. For each $j$,
> $$
> x-u_j=\sum_i(e_i-\delta_{ij})u_i.
> $$
> Every coefficient lies in $\mathfrak m_j$, so the quotient of this sum by $m_j$ is integral; all summands are in a common $K$-multiple. Thus $x\equiv u_j\pmod{m_j}$, provided $x\ne0$. If $x=0$, take any ordinary nonzero $h\in\prod_i\mathfrak m_i$ and replace $x$ by $x+nht$ with an integer $n$ chosen to make it nonzero. This is still integral, remains in the class of $t$, and preserves every congruence.
>
> For a system $a_jx\equiv b_j\pmod{m_j}$ with integral coefficients, the stronger congruence $x\equiv u_j\pmod{m_j}$ implies
> $$
> a_jx-b_j=a_j(x-u_j)+(a_ju_j-b_j)
> $$
> is divisible by $m_j$. Equivalence to $b_j$ also holds because $x\sim u_j$. More generally the same substitution preserves polynomial congruences whose expressions are defined within these classes. This proves the asserted sufficiency. The common-class condition is necessary for this construction: one actual simultaneous solution is itself an individual solution of each congruence, so such choices can always be made when a simultaneous solution exists.
>
> For ordinary integers and moduli $3,5$, take $e_1=10,e_2=6$. Residues $1,2$ give $x=10+12=22$, which is $1$ modulo $3$ and $2$ modulo $5$. The ideal-number proof performs this same calculation inside a fixed fractional-ideal lattice.
