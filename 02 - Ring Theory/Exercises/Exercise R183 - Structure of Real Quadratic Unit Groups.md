# Exercise R183: Structure of Real Quadratic Unit Groups

## Problem Statement

> [!question] Exercise 9.4
> Let $R$ be the ring of integers in a real quadratic number field. What structures are possible for the group of units in $R$?

## Hints

> [!hint]- Hint 1
> The first-quadrant units form an infinite cyclic group, and the only roots of unity in a real field are $\pm1$.

## Solution

> [!success]- Solution
> Let $U_0$ be the first-quadrant subgroup and let $\varepsilon>1$ generate it. Every unit has norm $1$ or $-1$, and its signs under the two real embeddings determine its quadrant.
>
> The torsion subgroup consists exactly of $\{\pm1\}\cong C_2$: a real root of unity is $1$ or $-1$. The quotient by this torsion is generated by a fundamental unit. Hence, as an abstract group, the only possibility is
>
> $$
> \boxed{R^\times\cong C_2\times\mathbb Z.}
> $$
>
> There are two geometric norm patterns. If a unit of norm $-1$ exists, then units occur in all four quadrants, and the square of a least such positive unit generates $U_0$. If no norm-$-1$ unit exists, all units lie in the first and third quadrants and are $\pm\varepsilon^n$ with $N(\varepsilon)=1$. These patterns do not change the abstract group structure.
