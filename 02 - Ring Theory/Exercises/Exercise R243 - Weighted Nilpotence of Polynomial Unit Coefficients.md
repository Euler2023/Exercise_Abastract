# Exercise R243: Weighted Nilpotence of Polynomial Unit Coefficients

## Problem Statement

> [!question] Lang, Chapter IV, Exercise 20
> Let $A$ be a commutative ring and $t$ a variable. Let $f(t)=\sum_{i=0}^m a_it^i$ and $g(t)=\sum_{i=0}^n b_it^i$ with $a_0=b_0=1$. If $f(t)g(t)=1$, show that there is an integer $N=(m+n)(m+n-1)$ such that any monomial, printed as
> $$
> a_1^{r_1}\cdots a_n^{r_n},\qquad \sum_j jr_j>N,
> $$
> is zero. [Hint: replace the coefficients by variables. Use Exercise 19(b) to show that a monomial of weight $>N$ lies in the ideal $I$ generated by
> $$
> c_k=\sum_{i=0}^k a_ib_{k-i},
> $$
> with $a_0=b_0=1$. These are the elementary symmetric functions of $m+n$ variables $(X,Y)$.] The source also points to Cartier's Bourbaki talk, 1982-1983.

> [!warning] Source issue / scope clarification
> The source indexes $f$ through $a_m$ but ends the displayed monomial at $a_n$. The solution states the intended assertion for $a_1,\ldots,a_m$, with coefficients outside their specified ranges set to zero when forming $c_k$.

## Hints

> [!hint]- Hint 1
> First work in the universal coefficient ring, then map its coefficients to $A$.

> [!hint]- Hint 2
> Use IV.19(a) in all $m+n$ root variables, and IV.19(d) to contract the ideal back to the coefficient ring.

## Solution

> [!success]- Solution
> ### Approach
> First work in the universal coefficient ring, then map its coefficients to $A$.
>
> ### Proof
> Use the intended coefficient range $a_1,\ldots,a_m$. Let
> $$
> C=\mathbb Z[A_1,\ldots,A_m,B_1,\ldots,B_n].
> $$
> Put $A_0=B_0=1$ and set other out-of-range coefficients to zero. Define $c_k=\sum_i A_iB_{k-i}$ for $1\le k\le m+n$, and $I=(c_1,\ldots,c_{m+n})$.
>
> Embed $C$ into $D=\mathbb Z[X_1,\ldots,X_m,Y_1,\ldots,Y_n]$ by identifying $A_i$ with the $i$-th elementary symmetric polynomial in $X$, and $B_j$ with the $j$-th one in $Y$. This is injective by IV.19(b)-(c). Multiplication of $\prod_i(1+X_it)$ and $\prod_j(1+Y_jt)$ shows that $c_k$ becomes the $k$-th elementary symmetric polynomial in all $m+n$ variables.
>
> A monomial $M=\prod_{i=1}^m A_i^{r_i}$ of weight $w=\sum_i ir_i$ becomes a homogeneous polynomial of degree $w$ in $D$. If $w>(m+n)(m+n-1)$, IV.19(a) puts it in $ID$. By the contraction identity IV.19(d), $ID\cap C=I$, so already $M\in I$ in the universal coefficient ring.
>
> Now specialize $A_i\mapsto a_i$, $B_j\mapsto b_j$ in the given ring $A$. The hypothesis $fg=1$ says exactly that every $c_k$ maps to zero. Thus every element of $I$, in particular $M$, maps to zero. This proves the required nilpotence bound for arbitrary commutative $A$, even with zero divisors. If one list of variables is empty the same argument uses an empty symmetric-polynomial block. If $m=n=0$, there is no monomial of positive weight to consider.
