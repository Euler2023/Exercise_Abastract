# Exercise R468: Cubic Reciprocity Characters for One Two and Three

## Problem Statement

> [!question] Original Exercise 4.11.6
> (a) Describe the Cubic Reciprocity character $\chi$ for $d=1$.
>
> (b) For $d=2$, determine the conditions on $a,b\pmod2$ making $a+b\mu_3$ invertible modulo $2A$, and similarly modulo three. Show $G=(A/6A)^\times$ has order18; the six units form a cyclic subgroup $H$ of order six; and $G/H$ is generated by $g=1+3\mu_3$. Explain why $\chi$ descends and, up to complex conjugation, is $\chi(H)=1$, $\chi(gH)=\zeta_3$, $\chi(g^2H)=\zeta_3^2$. Here $\zeta_3$ distinguishes the codomain root from $\mu_3\in A$.
>
> (c) Describe the Cubic Reciprocity character for $d=3$.
>
> The weak Cubic Reciprocity input uses $N=3\prod_{p\mid d}p$ and supplies a cubic character on $(A/NA)^\times$, trivial on units and rational primes away from $N$, with $\chi(\pi)=1$ for norm-$p$ primes $\pi$ precisely when $d$ is a cube modulo $p$.

## Hints

> [!hint]- Hint 1
> Modulo two the ring is $\mathbb F_4$; modulo three an element is a unit precisely when $a+b\ne0$.

> [!hint]- Hint 2
> For $d=3$, use modulus nine and also quotient by the rational unit residues. That leaves a cyclic quotient of order three.

## Solution

> [!success]- Solution
> **Proof scope:** Independent derivation.
>
> Put $\rho=\mu_3$ and fix $\zeta_3=e^{2\pi i/3}$ in the target. All characters below are extended by zero on nonunits of the indicated modulus.
>
> **(a)** The integer one is a cube modulo every prime. Thus the character is trivial on the entire unit residue group $(A/3A)^\times$: every nonzero residue there is represented by an Eisenstein unit, and the character is required to be trivial on those units. It is the principal character modulo $3A$ (its restriction has conductor one). Extending by zero at the prime above three accounts for the excluded bad prime in the cubic counting series.
>
> **(b)** Modulo two, $X^2+X+1$ is irreducible, so $A/2A\cong\mathbb F_4$ and $a+b\rho$ is invertible exactly when $(a,b)\not\equiv(0,0)\pmod2$. Modulo three, $X^2+X+1=(X-1)^2$; an element is invertible exactly when $a+b\not\equiv0\pmod3$. There are respectively three and six units, so the Chinese remainder theorem gives $|G|=18$. The six roots of unity $\{\pm1,\pm\rho,\pm\rho^2\}$ remain distinct modulo three (write $\rho=1+\epsilon$, $\epsilon^2=0$), hence also modulo six. They form the cyclic subgroup $H$ of order six.
>
> For $g=1+3\rho$, direct multiplication gives $g^3=1-18\rho\equiv1\pmod6$. Also $g\equiv1\pmod3$ but $g\equiv1+\rho\ne1\pmod2$. Since only the unit1 reduces to1 modulo three, $g\notin H$. Hence its coset generates the order-three quotient $G/H$. The reciprocity character is trivial on $H$, so it descends. It cannot be the trivial character: $|g|^2=7$ and two is not a cube modulo seven, whose nonzero cubes are $\pm1$. Thus $\chi(g)$ is a primitive cube root, proving the two conjugate possibilities in the question.
>
> **(c)** Here $N=9$. Let $G_9=(A/9A)^\times$, $H$ the image of Eisenstein units, and $R$ the image of rational units $(\mathbb Z/9\mathbb Z)^\times$. The unit condition $a+b\ne0\pmod3$ gives $|G_9|=81-27=54$. Both $H$ and $R$ have order six and intersect in $\{\pm1\}$: a nonreal unit has a nonzero $\rho$ coefficient modulo nine. Therefore $|HR|=18$ and $G_9/(HR)$ has order three.
>
> The same $g=1+3\rho$ has $g^3\equiv1\pmod9$ and does not lie in $HR$. Indeed $g\equiv1\pmod3$; a product of an Eisenstein unit and a rational unit can have that scalar residue only if its Eisenstein unit is $\pm1$, since the other four unit residues have nonzero nilpotent coefficient. Such a product is rational modulo nine, whereas $g$ has $\rho$ coefficient three. Thus $gHR$ generates the quotient.
>
> Cubic Reciprocity requires triviality on $H$ and on rational primes away from three, hence on $R$ by multiplicativity (every rational integer coprime to three factors into those primes). It consequently factors through this quotient. Since $|g|^2=7$ and three is not a cube modulo seven, it is nontrivial. Its complete description, up to conjugation, is therefore
> $$
> \chi(HR)=1,\qquad\chi(gHR)=\zeta_3,\qquad\chi(g^2HR)=\zeta_3^2.
> $$
> Its ideal conductor is $9A$. To verify minimality, write $\alpha=1+2\rho$, so $9A=\alpha^4A$ up to a unit, and $1+3\alpha=4+6\rho\equiv4g^2\pmod{9A}$. Hence $\chi(1+3\alpha)=\zeta_3^2\ne1$, although $1+3\alpha\equiv1\pmod{\alpha^3A}$. It cannot factor through the largest proper divisor $\alpha^3A$, and therefore through no proper ideal divisor of $9A$. The two choices are complex conjugates and give the same trivial/nontrivial cube-residue test. This quotient description specifies every unit residue and the zero extension specifies every other element.
