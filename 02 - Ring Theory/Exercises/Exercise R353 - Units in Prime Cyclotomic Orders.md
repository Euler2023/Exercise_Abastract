# Exercise R353: Units in Prime Cyclotomic Orders

## Problem Statement

> [!question] Neukirch I.7.4
> Let $\zeta$ be a primitive $p$-th root of unity, $p$ an odd prime number. Show that
> $$
> \mathbb Z[\zeta]^\times=(\zeta)\mathbb Z[\zeta+\zeta^{-1}]^\times.
> $$
> Show that
> $$
> \mathbb Z[\zeta]^\times
> =\{\pm\zeta^k(1+\zeta)^n\mid0\le k<5,\ n\in\mathbb Z\},
> $$
> if $p=5$.

In the first displayed formula, the source's $(\zeta)$ means the cyclic subgroup $\langle\zeta\rangle$ of units, not the principal ideal generated by $\zeta$.

## Hints

> [!hint]- Hint 1: Compare a unit with its complex conjugate
> If $u\in\mathbb Z[\zeta]^\times$, then $u/\overline u$ belongs to the same ring and every conjugate has absolute value $1$. Prove directly that any such element is $\pm\zeta^k$ by summing the squared absolute values of its conjugates.

> [!hint]- Hint 2: Eliminate the minus sign and take a phase square root
> Modulo $(1-\zeta)$, the ring is $\mathbb F_p$, conjugation is the identity, and $u/\overline u\equiv1$. Since $p$ is odd, this eliminates $-\zeta^k$. Now solve $2j\equiv k\pmod p$ and check that $\zeta^{-j}u$ is real.

> [!hint]- Hint 3: Identify the real subring and specialize to five
> Show $\mathbb Z[\zeta]\cap\mathbb R=\mathbb Z[\zeta+\zeta^{-1}]$ using symmetric integer coefficients. For $p=5$, put $t=\zeta+\zeta^{-1}$ and $\eta=1+t$. Then $\eta^2-\eta-1=0$ and $1+\zeta=-\zeta^3\eta$.

## Solution

> [!success]- Independent derivation, including the unit decomposition
> Write $R=\mathbb Z[\zeta]$ and $R^+=\mathbb Z[\zeta+\zeta^{-1}]$. The polynomial $\Phi_p(T)=1+T+\cdots+T^{p-1}$ is irreducible over $\mathbb Q$: $\Phi_p(T+1)=((T+1)^p-1)/T$ is Eisenstein at $p$. Thus $[\mathbb Q(\zeta):\mathbb Q]=p-1$, its embeddings send $\zeta$ to $\zeta^j$ for $1\le j\le p-1$, and the only rational relation among $1,\zeta,\ldots,\zeta^{p-1}$ is a multiple of their sum.
>
> **1. An elementary absolute-value lemma.** Suppose $w\in R$ and all its conjugates have absolute value $1$. Write
> $$
> w=\sum_{i=0}^{p-1}a_i\zeta^i,\qquad a_i\in\mathbb Z.
> $$
> Summing a finite geometric progression gives $\sum_{j=1}^{p-1}\zeta^{j(i-k)}=p-1$ for $i=k$ and $-1$ otherwise. Hence
> $$
> \begin{aligned}
> p-1
> &=\sum_{j=1}^{p-1}\left|\sum_i a_i\zeta^{ij}\right|^2\\
> &=p\sum_i a_i^2-\left(\sum_i a_i\right)^2
> =\sum_{i<k}(a_i-a_k)^2.
> \end{aligned}
> $$
> Subtracting the minimum of the $a_i$ from every coefficient leaves $w$ unchanged because $\sum_i\zeta^i=0$. We may assume that all coefficients are nonnegative and at least one is zero. Not all are zero since $w\ne0$. If exactly $q$ coefficients are positive, then $1\le q\le p-1$, and the pairs consisting of a positive coefficient and a zero coefficient contribute at least $q(p-q)$ to the last sum. But
> $$
> q(p-q)-(p-1)=(q-1)(p-q-1)\ge0.
> $$
> Equality of the whole sum with $p-1$ forces $q=1$ or $q=p-1$, and every positive coefficient to be $1$. In the first case $w=\zeta^i$; in the second $w=\sum_{k\ne i}\zeta^k=-\zeta^i$. We have proved the needed lemma without assuming a classification of roots of unity in the field.
>
> **2. Remove the complex phase from an arbitrary unit.** Let $u\in R^\times$. Its conjugate and its inverse are in $R$, so $w=u/\overline u\in R$. Every embedding commutes with complex conjugation, giving $|\sigma(w)|=1$. Step 1 implies
> $$
> u/\overline u=\pm\zeta^k.
> $$
> The quotient ring is
> $$
> R/(1-\zeta)
> \cong\mathbb Z[T]/(\Phi_p(T),T-1)
> \cong\mathbb Z/p\mathbb Z.
> $$
> Conjugation induces the identity on this quotient, while the residue of a unit is nonzero. Therefore $u/\overline u\equiv1$. Both $\zeta^k$ and $-\zeta^k$ reduce to $1$ and $-1$, respectively, and those are distinct because $p$ is odd. Thus the sign is positive.
>
> Choose $j$ with $2j\equiv k\pmod p$ and put $v=\zeta^{-j}u$. Then
> $$
> \frac v{\overline v}=\zeta^{-2j}\frac u{\overline u}=1.
> $$
> Hence $v$ is a real unit of $R$.
>
> **3. Identify the fixed subring integrally.** Let $v=\sum_{i=0}^{p-1}a_i\zeta^i\in R$ be real. The equality $v=\overline v$ gives a relation with coefficients $a_i-a_{-i}$, indices taken modulo $p$. These coefficients must all be equal, since the relation is a multiple of $\Phi_p$; their coefficient at index $0$ is zero, so they are all zero. Hence $a_i=a_{-i}$, and
> $$
> v=a_0+\sum_{i=1}^{(p-1)/2}a_i(\zeta^i+\zeta^{-i}).
> $$
> Each summand is an integer polynomial in $t=\zeta+\zeta^{-1}$: set $P_0=2$, $P_1=t$, and $P_{i+1}=tP_i-P_{i-1}$. This proves $R\cap\mathbb R=R^+$. The inverse of a real unit of $R$ is real and also lies in $R$, so it belongs to $R^+$ as well. Thus $v\in(R^+)^\times$.
>
> We have shown $u=\zeta^jv$ with $v\in(R^+)^\times$. Conversely, each such product is a unit of $R$. Therefore
> $$
> R^\times=\langle\zeta\rangle(R^+)^\times.
> $$
>
> **4. Determine the real units when $p=5$.** The relation $1+\zeta+\cdots+\zeta^4=0$ gives $t^2+t-1=0$. Put $\eta=1+t$; then $\eta^2-\eta-1=0$, and
> $$
> R^+=\mathbb Z[\eta]
> =\mathbb Z\left[\frac{1+\sqrt5}{2}\right].
> $$
> Indeed $\eta$ is one of $(1\pm\sqrt5)/2$, and either root generates this same ring. To check its units explicitly, put $\varphi=(1+\sqrt5)/2>1$. It has norm $-1$ and $\varphi+\varphi^{-1}=\sqrt5$. A unit $v=(a+b\sqrt5)/2$ with $1<v<\varphi$ would satisfy
> $$
> 0<b=\frac{v-\overline v}{\sqrt5}
> \le\frac{v+v^{-1}}{\sqrt5}<1,
> $$
> impossible for integer $b$. Dividing positive units by powers of $\varphi$, and then allowing a minus sign, gives $(R^+)^\times=\{\pm\varphi^n:n\in\mathbb Z\}$. Since $\eta$ is either $\varphi$ or $-\varphi^{-1}$, this is also $\{\pm\eta^n:n\in\mathbb Z\}$.
>
> Finally,
> $$
> -\zeta^3\eta
> =-(\zeta^2+\zeta^3+\zeta^4)=1+\zeta.
> $$
> This identity proves that $1+\zeta$ is a unit and permits replacing every power of $\eta$ by a power of $1+\zeta$, absorbing a sign and a power of $\zeta$. Combining with Step 3 proves
> $$
> \mathbb Z[\zeta]^\times
> =\{\pm\zeta^k(1+\zeta)^n:0\le k<5,\ n\in\mathbb Z\}.
> $$

## Notes

- **Source status:** Both tasks and the full $p=5$ expression were checked at [S4, Ch. I, §7, Exercise 4, printed p. 44, PDF p. 63]. There is no printed hint. No source figure is needed.
- **Proof status and inputs:** The proof is an independent derivation. Its external elementary inputs are Eisenstein's irreducibility criterion, the complex embeddings of a field generated by a root of an irreducible polynomial, and the quadratic integer-ring description proved in R333. The coefficient-square identity, phase elimination modulo $1-\zeta$, fixed-subring identity, real unit calculation, and $p=5$ generator change are proved above.
- **Proof boundary:** The unit decomposition is not merely quoted as a CM-field unit theorem. In particular, a real element of $R$ is shown to lie in the real integer subring without division by $2$. The argument does not assume that every root of unity in the field has already been classified, nor does it need a theorem identifying $R$ with the full integer ring.
- **Routing:** Ring Theory is primary because the target is an explicit unit group, established by conjugation, integral coefficients, and reduction in a quotient ring. Cyclotomic embeddings are a cross-topic prerequisite.
