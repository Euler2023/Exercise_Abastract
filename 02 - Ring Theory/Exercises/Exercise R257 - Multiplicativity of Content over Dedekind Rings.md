# Exercise R257: Multiplicativity of Content over Dedekind Rings

## Problem Statement

> [!question] Lang, Chapter VII, Exercise 8
> Using prime ideals instead of prime numbers for a Dedekind ring $A$, define the notion of content as in the Gauss lemma, and prove that if $f(X), g(X) \in A[X]$ are polynomials of degree $>0$ with coefficients in $A$, then
> $$
> \operatorname{cont}(fg) = \operatorname{cont}(f)\operatorname{cont}(g).
> $$
> Also if $K$ is the quotient field of $A$, prove the same statement for $f,g\in K[X]$.

## Hints

> [!hint]- Hint 1: Define content as an ideal of $A$
> For $f(X)=\sum_{i=0}^m a_i X^i \in A[X]$, define $\operatorname{cont}(f)$ as the ideal generated by its coefficients: $\operatorname{cont}(f)=(a_0,a_1,\ldots,a_m)\subseteq A$.

> [!hint]- Hint 2: Reduce to local discrete valuations
> In a Dedekind ring, ideals factor uniquely into prime ideals: $\mathfrak a = \prod_{\mathfrak p} \mathfrak p^{v_{\mathfrak p}(\mathfrak a)}$. What is the localization $A_{\mathfrak p}$ at a nonzero prime ideal? Use the DVR valuation to show $v_{\mathfrak p}(fg) = v_{\mathfrak p}(f) + v_{\mathfrak p}(g)$.

> [!hint]- Hint 3: Alternatively, inspect reductions modulo $\mathfrak p$
> Since $A/\mathfrak p$ is a field (hence an integral domain), $(A/\mathfrak p)[X]$ has no zero divisors. If the reductions $\bar{f}_1,\bar{g}_1$ are non-zero modulo $\mathfrak p$, show that their product cannot vanish modulo $\mathfrak p$.

> [!hint]- Hint 4: Clear denominators for $K[X]$
> For $f\in K[X]$, define $\operatorname{cont}(f)$ as the fractional ideal $\sum A c_i$. Choose a common denominator $d\in A\setminus\{0\}$ such that $df\in A[X]$, and apply the group structure of fractional ideals.

## Solution

> [!success]- Complete Derivation
> Let $A$ be a Dedekind ring with quotient field $K$.
> 
> ### Part 1: Content in $A[X]$ and Multiplicativity
> 
> #### Definition (Content of a Polynomial over $A$)
> For a non-zero polynomial $f(X) = \sum_{i=0}^m a_i X^i \in A[X]$, its **content** is defined as the ideal of $A$ generated by its coefficients:
> $$
> \operatorname{cont}(f) = (a_0, a_1, \ldots, a_m) = A a_0 + A a_1 + \cdots + A a_m \subseteq A.
> $$
> 
> Since $A$ is a Dedekind ring, every nonzero ideal factors uniquely into a product of nonzero prime ideals:
> $$
> \operatorname{cont}(f) = \prod_{\mathfrak p} \mathfrak p^{v_{\mathfrak p}(f)},
> $$
> where the product runs over all non-zero prime (hence maximal) ideals $\mathfrak p$ of $A$, and:
> $$
> v_{\mathfrak p}(f) = \min_{0\le i\le m} v_{\mathfrak p}(a_i)
> $$
> is the $\mathfrak p$-adic valuation of $f$, which is zero for all but finitely many $\mathfrak p$.
> 
> #### Proof of $\operatorname{cont}(fg) = \operatorname{cont}(f)\operatorname{cont}(g)$
> Let $f(X), g(X)\in A[X]$ be non-zero polynomials. By unique factorization of ideals into prime ideals, to prove the ideal identity $\operatorname{cont}(fg)=\operatorname{cont}(f)\operatorname{cont}(g)$, it suffices to prove that for every nonzero prime ideal $\mathfrak p$ of $A$:
> $$
> v_{\mathfrak p}(fg) = v_{\mathfrak p}(f) + v_{\mathfrak p}(g).
> $$
> 
> Fix a nonzero prime ideal $\mathfrak p$ of $A$. The localization $A_{\mathfrak p}$ is a discrete valuation ring (DVR) with discrete valuation $v_{\mathfrak p}$ and maximal ideal $\mathfrak m = \mathfrak p A_{\mathfrak p}$.
> 
> Let $\pi\in A_{\mathfrak p}$ be a uniformizing element (so $v_{\mathfrak p}(\pi)=1$).
> Write:
> $$
> f(X) = \pi^{v_{\mathfrak p}(f)} f_1(X),\qquad g(X) = \pi^{v_{\mathfrak p}(g)} g_1(X),
> $$
> where $f_1, g_1 \in A_{\mathfrak p}[X]$ are polynomials whose coefficients all belong to $A_{\mathfrak p}$, and at least one coefficient of $f_1$ (resp. $g_1$) has valuation 0, i.e., is a unit in $A_{\mathfrak p}$.
> 
> Consider the canonical reduction homomorphism modulo $\mathfrak m$:
> $$
> \rho: A_{\mathfrak p}[X] \longrightarrow (A_{\mathfrak p}/\mathfrak m)[X] = (A/\mathfrak p)[X].
> $$
> 
> - Since $v_{\mathfrak p}(f_1)=0$, the reduction $\bar{f}_1 = \rho(f_1)$ is a non-zero polynomial in $(A/\mathfrak p)[X]$.
> - Since $v_{\mathfrak p}(g_1)=0$, the reduction $\bar{g}_1 = \rho(g_1)$ is a non-zero polynomial in $(A/\mathfrak p)[X]$.
> 
> Because $\mathfrak p$ is a maximal ideal in the Dedekind ring $A$, the residue ring $A/\mathfrak p$ is a field.
> Consequently, the polynomial ring $(A/\mathfrak p)[X]$ is an integral domain!
> 
> Since $\bar{f}_1 \ne 0$ and $\bar{g}_1 \ne 0$ in $(A/\mathfrak p)[X]$, their product is non-zero:
> $$
> \rho(f_1 g_1) = \bar{f}_1 \bar{g}_1 \ne 0 \in (A/\mathfrak p)[X].
> $$
> 
> This means that at least one coefficient of $f_1 g_1$ does not belong to the maximal ideal $\mathfrak m$, and therefore has valuation 0:
> $$
> v_{\mathfrak p}(f_1 g_1) = 0.
> $$
> 
> Now, compute the product polynomial $f(X)g(X)$:
> $$
> fg = \left(\pi^{v_{\mathfrak p}(f)} f_1\right)\left(\pi^{v_{\mathfrak p}(g)} g_1\right) = \pi^{v_{\mathfrak p}(f) + v_{\mathfrak p}(g)} (f_1 g_1).
> $$
> 
> Taking the $\mathfrak p$-adic valuation:
> $$
> v_{\mathfrak p}(fg) = v_{\mathfrak p}(f) + v_{\mathfrak p}(g) + v_{\mathfrak p}(f_1 g_1) = v_{\mathfrak p}(f) + v_{\mathfrak p}(g) + 0 = v_{\mathfrak p}(f) + v_{\mathfrak p}(g).
> $$
> 
> Because this holds for all prime ideals $\mathfrak p$:
> $$
> \operatorname{cont}(fg) = \prod_{\mathfrak p} \mathfrak p^{v_{\mathfrak p}(fg)} = \prod_{\mathfrak p} \mathfrak p^{v_{\mathfrak p}(f) + v_{\mathfrak p}(g)} = \left(\prod_{\mathfrak p} \mathfrak p^{v_{\mathfrak p}(f)}\right)\left(\prod_{\mathfrak p} \mathfrak p^{v_{\mathfrak p}(g)}\right) = \operatorname{cont}(f)\operatorname{cont}(g).
> $$
> 
> ---
> 
> ### Part 2: Content in $K[X]$ and Multiplicativity
> 
> #### Definition (Content of a Polynomial over $K$)
> For a non-zero polynomial $f(X) = \sum_{i=0}^m c_i X^i \in K[X]$, its **content** $\operatorname{cont}(f)$ is defined as the fractional ideal of $A$ in $K$ generated by its coefficients:
> $$
> \operatorname{cont}(f) = \sum_{i=0}^m A c_i \subset K.
> $$
> 
> Since there are only finitely many coefficients $c_i\in K$, we can choose a common non-zero denominator $d\in A\setminus\{0\}$ such that $d c_i \in A$ for all $i=0,\ldots,m$.
> 
> Then:
> $$
> f_0(X) = d f(X) \in A[X],
> $$
> and by definition of fractional ideal scaling:
> $$
> \operatorname{cont}(f) = d^{-1} \operatorname{cont}(f_0).
> $$
> 
> #### Proof of Multiplicativity in $K[X]$
> Let $f(X), g(X)\in K[X]$ be non-zero polynomials.
> 
> Choose elements $d, e \in A\setminus\{0\}$ such that:
> $$
> f_0(X) = d f(X) \in A[X],\qquad g_0(X) = e g(X) \in A[X].
> $$
> 
> Their product is:
> $$
> f_0(X) g_0(X) = (de) f(X)g(X) \in A[X].
> $$
> 
> Applying the multiplicativity result for $A[X]$ from Part 1 to $f_0$ and $g_0$:
> $$
> \operatorname{cont}(f_0 g_0) = \operatorname{cont}(f_0)\operatorname{cont}(g_0).
> $$
> 
> Expressing each term via the content in $K[X]$:
> - $\operatorname{cont}(f_0 g_0) = \operatorname{cont}((de) fg) = (de) \operatorname{cont}(fg)$;
> - $\operatorname{cont}(f_0) = d \operatorname{cont}(f)$;
> - $\operatorname{cont}(g_0) = e \operatorname{cont}(g)$.
> 
> Substituting these identities gives:
> $$
> (de) \operatorname{cont}(fg) = (d \operatorname{cont}(f))(e \operatorname{cont}(g)) = (de) \operatorname{cont}(f)\operatorname{cont}(g).
> $$
> 
> By Exercise 7, the non-zero fractional ideals of $A$ form an abelian group under multiplication.
> Multiplying both sides by the inverse of the principal fractional ideal $(de) = de A$:
> $$
> \operatorname{cont}(fg) = \operatorname{cont}(f)\operatorname{cont}(g).
> $$
> 
> This completes the proof for all polynomials $f,g\in K[X]$.

## Notes

- **Classical Gauss's Lemma as a Special Case:** When $A=\mathbb Z$ and $K=\mathbb Q$, every ideal is principal. Writing $\operatorname{cont}(f) = c(f)\mathbb Z$ with $c(f)>0$ recovers the classical Gauss's lemma $c(fg)=c(f)c(g)$ and the irreducibility transfer between $\mathbb Z[X]$ and $\mathbb Q[X]$.
- **Primitive Polynomials:** A polynomial $f\in A[X]$ is called **primitive** if $\operatorname{cont}(f)=A$. The multiplicativity theorem implies that the product of primitive polynomials over any Dedekind ring is primitive.
- **Independent Derivation:** The proof combines localization at maximal ideals with the group structure of fractional ideals established in Exercise R256.
