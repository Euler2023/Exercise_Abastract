# Exercise R432: Congruence Roots and Explicit Elliptic Points

## Problem Statement

> [!question] Original Exercise 3.7.6
> (a) Show that the number of solutions to $x^2-x+1\equiv0\pmod N$ is given by the formula for $\varepsilon_3(\Gamma_0(N))$ in Corollary3.7.2. (A hint for this exercise is at the end of the book.)
>
> (b) Show that the orbits $\Gamma_0(N)\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\n&1\end{pmatrix}(\mu_3)$ for $n=0,\ldots,N-1$ such that $n^2-n+1\equiv0\pmod N$ are distinct in $X_0(N)$.
>
> (c) Confirm formula(3.14).
>
> (d) Similarly show that the period-two elliptic points of $\Gamma_0(N)$ are given by formula(3.15).
>
> The formulas are $\Gamma_0(N)(n+\mu_3)/(n^2-n+1)$ for roots of $n^2-n+1$, and $\Gamma_0(N)(n+i)/(n^2+1)$ for roots of $n^2+1$, each with $n$ taken modulo $N$. The count in(a) is zero if $9\mid N$, and otherwise $\prod_{p\mid N}(1+(\frac{-3}{p}))$, with factors $2,0,1$ for $p\equiv1\pmod3$, $p\equiv2\pmod3$, $p=3$.

## Hints

> [!hint]- Hint 1
> For $p\ne3$, the polynomial roots correspond to nontrivial cube roots of unity after negation.

> [!hint]- Hint 2
> Use bottom-row cosets and $C=ST$, which fixes $\mu_3$, to prove distinctness and exhaustiveness simultaneously.

## Solution

> [!success]- Solution
> **Proof scope:** Independent derivation.
>
> **(a)** For $p\ne2,3$, a root $x$ gives $-x$ a nontrivial cube root of unity, since $x^3+1=(x+1)(x^2-x+1)$ and $x=-1$ is excluded unless $p=3$. Thus there are two roots for $p\equiv1\pmod3$ and none for $p\equiv2\pmod3$, by cyclicity of $\mathbb F_p^\times$. Modulo two the polynomial has no root. At every root for $p\ne3$, the derivative $2x-1$ is nonzero, so the lifting calculation $F(x+tp^e)/p^e\equiv F(x)/p^e+tF'(x)\pmod p$ gives a unique lift at every stage. Modulo three the single root is two; substituting $x=2+3t$ gives $F(x)\equiv3\pmod9$, so there is no lift to nine. The Chinese remainder theorem gives the required product and obstruction.
>
> **(b,c)** Put $\alpha_n=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\n&1\end{pmatrix}$ and $C=ST=\begin{pmatrix}0&-1\\1&1\end{pmatrix}$. The effective stabilizer of $\mu_3$ is generated by $C$. As in the bottom-row criterion proved in3.7.5, left cosets of $\Gamma_0(N)$ correspond to primitive rows modulo $N$ up to unit multiples. Right multiplication by $C$ takes $(c,d)$ to $(d,d-c)$. If this is $u(c,d)$, then $d=uc$, so primitivity implies $c,d$ are units. Normalize the row to $(n,1)$; its fixed condition is
> $$
> (1,1-n)=(1-n)(n,1)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad n^2-n+1\equiv0\pmod N.
> $$
> Here $1-n$ is a unit because $n(1-n)=1$. Thus the fixed cosets are exactly $\Gamma_0(N)\alpha_n$ for these roots. They are distinct: proportional rows $(n,1)$ and $(m,1)$ have proportionality factor one, hence $n=m$ modulo $N$. A point over $\mu_3$ has period three precisely when its coset is fixed; each fixed coset is already a singleton under the stabilizer, so no further identifications occur. This proves(b) and exhaustiveness. Finally
> $$
> \alpha_n\mu_3=\frac{\mu_3}{n\mu_3+1}=\frac{n+\mu_3}{n^2-n+1},
> $$
> proving(3.14).
>
> **(d)** Right multiplication by $S$ takes $(c,d)$ to $(d,-c)$. Its fixed primitive rows normalize to $(n,1)$ with $n^2+1\equiv0\pmod N$, by the same argument, and are distinct. Moreover $\alpha_n i=(n+i)/(n^2+1)$. This proves(3.15) and all of its orbit assertions.
