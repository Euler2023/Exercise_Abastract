# Exercise R182: Euclidean Algorithm and the Ideal (sqrt(2))

## Problem Statement

> [!question] Exercise 9.3
> **(a)** Prove that the size function $\sigma(\alpha)=|N(\alpha)|$ makes the ring $\mathbb Z[\sqrt2]$ into a Euclidean domain, and that this ring has unique factorization.
>
> **(b)** Make a sketch showing the principal ideal $(\sqrt2)$ of $R=\mathbb Z[\sqrt2]$, in the embedding depicted in Figure 13.9.6.

## Hints

> [!hint]- Hint 1
> Write a quotient as $r+s\sqrt2$ and round $r$ and $s$ independently to the nearest integers.

> [!hint]- Hint 2
> An element of $(\sqrt2)$ has the form $2b+a\sqrt2$, so its rational coefficient is even.

## Solution

> [!success]- Solution
> **(a)** Let $\alpha,\beta\in R$ with $\beta\ne0$, and write
>
> $$
> \frac{\alpha}{\beta}=r+s\sqrt2
> $$
>
> with $r,s\in\mathbb Q$. Choose $m,n\in\mathbb Z$ so that $|r-m|\le1/2$ and $|s-n|\le1/2$, and put $q=m+n\sqrt2$. Then
>
> $$
> \left|N\left(\frac{\alpha}{\beta}-q\right)\right|
> =\left|(r-m)^2-2(s-n)^2\right|
> \le\frac12<1.
> $$
>
> With $\rho=\alpha-\beta q$, multiplicativity of the norm yields
>
> $$
> |N(\rho)|<|N(\beta)|.
> $$
>
> Thus $\sigma(\gamma)=|N(\gamma)|$ is a Euclidean function. Every Euclidean domain is a principal ideal domain, and every principal ideal domain is a unique factorization domain, so $R$ has unique factorization.
>
> **(b)** Under the two-real-embedding map,
>
> $$
> c+d\sqrt2\longmapsto(c+d\sqrt2,c-d\sqrt2).
> $$
>
> Since
>
> $$
> \sqrt2(a+b\sqrt2)=2b+a\sqrt2,
> $$
>
> the ideal consists exactly of those lattice points whose rational coefficient is even. It is the index-two sublattice generated by the embedded vectors $(2,2)$ and $(\sqrt2,-\sqrt2)$, shown in blue below.
