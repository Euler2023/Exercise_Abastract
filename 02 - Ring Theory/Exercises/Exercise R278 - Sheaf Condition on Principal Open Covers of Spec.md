# Exercise R278: Sheaf Condition on Principal Open Covers of Spec

## Problem Statement

> [!question]
> Let $U_i = \operatorname{spec}(A_{f_i})$ be a finite family of open subsets of $\operatorname{spec}(A)$ covering $\operatorname{spec}(A)$. For each $i$, let $a_i / f_i \in A_{f_i}$. Assume that as functions on $U_i \cap U_j$ we have $a_i / f_i = a_j / f_j$ for all pairs $i, j$. Show that there exists a unique element $a \in A$ such that $a = a_i / f_i$ in $A_{f_i}$ for all $i$.

> [!warning] Source ambiguity: equality of functions
> In the printed statement, “as functions” must be understood here as equality of the canonical images in $A_{f_if_j}$, or equivalently equality of local sections, not merely equality of values in residue fields. For example, take $A=k[\varepsilon]/(\varepsilon^2)$, $f_1=f_2=1$, and local elements $0$ and $\varepsilon$. They have the same values at every point, but no $a\in A$ equals both. The proof below establishes the localization-ring version, which is the form used to construct the structure sheaf.

## Hints

> [!hint]- Hint 1
> The covering condition $\bigcup_{i=1}^r U_i = \operatorname{spec}(A)$ means that the open sets $D(f_i)$ cover $\operatorname{spec}(A)$.
> 
> What does this imply about the ideal $(f_1, \ldots, f_r) \subseteq A$? (Recall Exercise 15).
> Write $1$ as a linear combination of the elements $f_i$: there exist $g_1, \ldots, g_r \in A$ such that $\sum_{i=1}^r g_i f_i = 1$.

> [!hint]- Hint 2
> What does the agreement condition on the overlap $U_i \cap U_j = \operatorname{spec}(A_{f_i f_j})$ mean in terms of the localized ring $A_{f_i f_j}$?
> 
> By definition of localization, $a_i/f_i = a_j/f_j$ in $A_{f_i f_j}$ means that $(f_i f_j)^N (a_i f_j - a_j f_i) = 0$ in $A$ for some $N$.
> Since there are only finitely many pairs $(i, j)$, choose a uniform $N$.
> Adjust the representations of the fractions (e.g. replacing $f_i$ by $f_i^{N+1}$ and $a_i$ by $a_i f_i^N$) so that $a_i f_j = a_j f_i$ holds directly in $A$.
> 
> Then consider the candidate $a = \sum_{j=1}^r g_j a_j$.

## Solution

> [!success]- Solution
> Let $A$ be a commutative ring with $1$, and let $X = \operatorname{spec}(A)$.
> For each $i \in \{1, \ldots, r\}$, let $f_i \in A$ and let $U_i = D(f_i) \cong \operatorname{spec}(A_{f_i})$.
> We are given:
> 1. **Covering condition:** $\bigcup_{i=1}^r U_i = X$.
> 2. **Local sections:** For each $i$, an element $s_i = a_i / f_i \in A_{f_i}$ (or more generally $s_i = a_i / f_i^{m_i}$; we first prove the statement in Lang's printed form $a_i/f_i$, and note that the general form reduces to this by taking powers of $f_i$).
> 3. **Overlap agreement:** On $U_i \cap U_j = D(f_i f_j) \cong \operatorname{spec}(A_{f_i f_j})$, the canonical images of $s_i$ and $s_j$ in the localized ring $A_{f_i f_j}$ are equal:
>    $$
>    \frac{a_i}{f_i} = \frac{a_j}{f_j} \quad \text{in } A_{f_i f_j} \quad \text{for all } i, j \in \{1, \ldots, r\}.
>    $$
> 
> We must prove that there exists a **unique** element $a \in A$ such that for all $i \in \{1, \ldots, r\}$, the image of $a$ in $A_{f_i}$ is $a_i / f_i$:
> $$
> \frac{a}{1} = \frac{a_i}{f_i} \quad \text{in } A_{f_i}.
> $$
> 
> ### Step 1: Partition of Unity from the Covering Condition
> 
> By Exercise 15, the open sets $D(f_1), \ldots, D(f_r)$ cover $X = \operatorname{spec}(A)$ if and only if
> $$
> \bigcap_{i=1}^r V(f_i) = V(f_1, \ldots, f_r) = \varnothing.
> $$
> This holds if and only if the ideal generated by $f_1, \ldots, f_r$ is the unit ideal:
> $$
> (f_1, f_2, \ldots, f_r) = A = (1).
> $$
> Therefore, there exist elements $g_1, \ldots, g_r \in A$ such that
> $$
> \sum_{k=1}^r g_k f_k = 1.
> $$
> 
> ### Step 2: Clearing Annihilators on Overlaps
> 
> By the definition of the localized ring $A_{f_i f_j}$, equality $a_i / f_i = a_j / f_j$ in $A_{f_i f_j}$ means that there exists an integer $N_{i, j} \ge 0$ such that
> $$
> (f_i f_j)^{N_{i, j}} (a_i f_j - a_j f_i) = 0 \quad \text{in } A.
> $$
> Since there are only finitely many pairs $(i, j) \in \{1, \ldots, r\}^2$, we can choose a single common integer
> $$
> N = \max_{1 \le i, j \le r} N_{i, j}.
> $$
> Then for all $i, j \in \{1, \ldots, r\}$,
> $$
> (f_i f_j)^N (a_i f_j - a_j f_i) = 0 \quad \text{in } A.
> $$
> 
> We can rewrite this identity as:
> $$
> (a_i f_i^N) f_j^{N+1} = (a_j f_j^N) f_i^{N+1}.
> $$
> Notice that in the localized ring $A_{f_i}$, we have
> $$
> \frac{a_i}{f_i} = \frac{a_i f_i^N}{f_i^{N+1}}.
> $$
> Setting
> $$
> f_i' = f_i^{N+1}, \qquad a_i' = a_i f_i^N,
> $$
> we have:
> 1. $a_i' / f_i' = a_i / f_i$ in $A_{f_i}$.
> 2. $D(f_i') = D(f_i) = U_i$, so the open sets $D(f_i')$ still cover $X$.
> 3. The ideals satisfy $(f_1', \ldots, f_r') = (f_1^{N+1}, \ldots, f_r^{N+1}) = A$ (since if an ideal contains $f_i$ it contains its power, and $\sqrt{(f_1', \ldots, f_r')} = \sqrt{(f_1, \ldots, f_r)} = A$).
>    Hence there exist elements $h_1, \ldots, h_r \in A$ such that
>    $$
>    \sum_{k=1}^r h_k f_k' = 1.
>    $$
> 4. Crucially, in the ring $A$ itself, we have exact equality:
>    $$
>    a_i' f_j' = a_j' f_i' \quad \text{for all } i, j \in \{1, \ldots, r\}.
>    $$
> 
> ### Step 3: Existence of the Glued Element $a$
> 
> Define the candidate element $a \in A$ by:
> $$
> a = \sum_{j=1}^r h_j a_j' \in A.
> $$
> 
> We check that $a$ restricts to $a_i' / f_i'$ in $A_{f_i'}$ for each $i \in \{1, \ldots, r\}$.
> Multiply $a$ by $f_i'$ in $A$:
> $$
> \begin{aligned}
> f_i' a &= f_i' \left( \sum_{j=1}^r h_j a_j' \right) \\
> &= \sum_{j=1}^r h_j (f_i' a_j').
> \end{aligned}
> $$
> Using the exact relation $f_i' a_j' = a_i' f_j'$ from Step 2:
> $$
> \begin{aligned}
> f_i' a &= \sum_{j=1}^r h_j (a_i' f_j') \\
> &= a_i' \left( \sum_{j=1}^r h_j f_j' \right).
> \end{aligned}
> $$
> Since $\sum_{j=1}^r h_j f_j' = 1$, we obtain:
> $$
> f_i' a = a_i' \cdot 1 = a_i' \quad \text{in } A.
> $$
> 
> In the localized ring $A_{f_i'} = A_{f_i}$, the element $f_i'$ is invertible.
> Dividing by $f_i'$ yields:
> $$
> \frac{a}{1} = \frac{a_i'}{f_i'} = \frac{a_i}{f_i} \quad \text{in } A_{f_i}.
> $$
> This holds for all $i \in \{1, \ldots, r\}$, proving the **existence** of $a$.
> 
> ### Step 4: Uniqueness of $a$
> 
> Suppose $a, b \in A$ both satisfy
> $$
> \frac{a}{1} = \frac{a_i}{f_i} = \frac{b}{1} \quad \text{in } A_{f_i} \quad \text{for all } i \in \{1, \ldots, r\}.
> $$
> Let $c = a - b \in A$.
> Then for each $i$, the image of $c$ in $A_{f_i}$ is $0$:
> $$
> \frac{c}{1} = 0 \quad \text{in } A_{f_i}.
> $$
> By definition of localization, this means that for each $i$, there exists an integer $M_i \ge 1$ such that
> $$
> f_i^{M_i} c = 0 \quad \text{in } A.
> $$
> Let $M = \max_{1 \le i \le r} M_i$. Then
> $$
> f_i^M c = 0 \quad \text{for all } i = 1, \ldots, r.
> $$
> 
> Since $(f_1, \ldots, f_r) = A$, the ideal generated by the powers $(f_1^M, \ldots, f_r^M)$ also equals $A$:
> $$
> \sqrt{(f_1^M, \ldots, f_r^M)} = \sqrt{(f_1, \ldots, f_r)} = A \implies (f_1^M, \ldots, f_r^M) = A.
> $$
> Thus there exist elements $p_1, \ldots, p_r \in A$ such that
> $$
> \sum_{i=1}^r p_i f_i^M = 1.
> $$
> Multiplying this identity by $c$:
> $$
> c = c \cdot 1 = c \left( \sum_{i=1}^r p_i f_i^M \right) = \sum_{i=1}^r p_i (f_i^M c).
> $$
> Since $f_i^M c = 0$ for each $i$, every term in the sum vanishes:
> $$
> c = \sum_{i=1}^r p_i \cdot 0 = 0.
> $$
> Therefore, $a - b = 0 \implies a = b$.
> 
> This establishes the **uniqueness** of $a$.

## Notes

- **Independent derivation:** The derivation carries out the standard sheaf axiom proof for affine schemes: first clearing annihilators across all overlap pairs $(i, j)$ by taking a uniform high power $N$, forming the global section via the partition of unity, and proving uniqueness via the annihilator of $a - b$.
- **Structure sheaf of affine schemes:** This exercise is the core algebraic lemma in proving that the assignment $D(f) \mapsto A_f$ defines a sheaf of rings $\mathcal{O}_X$ on the basis of principal open sets of $X = \operatorname{Spec}(A)$, and hence extends uniquely to the structure sheaf of the affine scheme $\operatorname{Spec}(A)$.
