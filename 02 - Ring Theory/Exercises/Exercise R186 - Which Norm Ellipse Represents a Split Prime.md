# Exercise R186: Which Norm Ellipse Represents a Split Prime

## Problem Statement

> [!question] Miscellaneous Problem M.2
> Let $R=\mathbb Z[\delta]$, where $\delta=\sqrt{-5}$, and let $p$ be a prime integer.
>
> **(a)** Prove that if $p$ splits in $R$, say $(p)=\overline P P$, then exactly one of the ellipses $x^2+5y^2=p$ or $x^2+5y^2=2p$ contains an integer point.
>
> **(b)** Find a property that determines which ellipse has an integer point.

## Hints

> [!hint]- Hint 1
> The class group of $\mathbb Z[\sqrt{-5}]$ has order two, generated by the ramified ideal $Q=(2,1+\delta)$.

> [!hint]- Hint 2
> Reduce the two norm equations modulo $4$, $5$, and $8$.

## Solution

> [!success]- Solution
> Let $Q=(2,1+\delta)$. The class group is $\{1,[Q]\}\cong C_2$.
>
> **(a)** If $P$ is principal, say $P=(x+y\delta)$, then
>
> $$
> p=N(P)=x^2+5y^2.
> $$
>
> If $P$ is nonprincipal, then $[P]=[Q]$, so $QP$ is principal. A generator $x+y\delta$ of $QP$ has norm
>
> $$
> N(QP)=2p=x^2+5y^2.
> $$
>
> These cases are mutually exclusive. A point on the first ellipse makes a prime ideal above $p$ principal; a point on the second makes its product with $Q$ principal and hence gives it the nontrivial class. Conjugate prime ideals have inverse classes, which are equal here because the class group has order two. Thus exactly one ellipse contains an integer point.
>
> **(b)** A split prime is distinct from $2$ and $5$. If
>
> $$
> p=x^2+5y^2,
> $$
>
> then $x$ and $y$ have opposite parity, so $p\equiv1\pmod4$; also $p\equiv x^2\pmod5$. Hence
>
> $$
> p\equiv1\text{ or }9\pmod{20}.
> $$
>
> If
>
> $$
> 2p=x^2+5y^2,
> $$
>
> then $x$ and $y$ are both odd. Modulo $8$ this gives $2p\equiv6$, so $p\equiv3\pmod4$; modulo $5$, $p$ is $2$ or $3$. Hence
>
> $$
> p\equiv3\text{ or }7\pmod{20}.
> $$
>
> Therefore, among split primes,
>
> $$
> \boxed{
> \begin{array}{c|c}
> p\pmod{20}&\text{ellipse containing an integer point}\\ \hline
> 1,9&x^2+5y^2=p\\
> 3,7&x^2+5y^2=2p.
> \end{array}}
> $$
