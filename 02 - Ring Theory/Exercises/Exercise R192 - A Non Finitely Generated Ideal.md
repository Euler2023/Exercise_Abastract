# Exercise R192: A Non-Finitely-Generated Ideal

## Problem Statement

> [!question] Exercise 6.2
> Find an example of a ring $R$ and an ideal $I$ of $R$ that is not finitely generated.

## Hints

> [!hint]- Hint 1
> Use a polynomial ring in infinitely many variables.

## Solution

> [!success]- Solution
> Let
>
> $$
> R=\mathbb C[x_1,x_2,x_3,\ldots]
> $$
>
> and take
>
> $$
> I=(x_1,x_2,x_3,\ldots).
> $$
>
> Suppose $I$ were generated by finitely many polynomials $g_1,\ldots,g_r$. Only finitely many variables occur in these polynomials, say $x_1,\ldots,x_N$. Evaluate those variables at zero while leaving $x_{N+1}$ unchanged. Every $g_j$ maps to zero, so every element of $(g_1,\ldots,g_r)$ maps to zero, whereas $x_{N+1}$ maps to itself. This contradicts $x_{N+1}\in I$. Hence $I$ is not finitely generated.
