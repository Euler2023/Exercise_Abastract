# Exercise R158: Lattice Bases and Conjugate Ideal Products

## Problem Statement

> [!question] Exercise 4.2
> Let $R$ be the ring $\mathbb Z[\delta]$, where $\delta=\sqrt{-5}$, and let $A$ denote the ideal generated by the elements
>
> **(a)** $3+5\delta$, $2+2\delta$;
>
> **(b)** $4+\delta$, $1+2\delta$.
>
> Decide whether or not the given generators form a lattice basis for $A$, and identify the ideal $\overline A A$.

## Hints

> [!hint]- Hint 1
> Put the coefficient vectors in the basis $(1,\delta)$. Their determinant is the index of their integer span; also test stability under multiplication by $\delta$.

> [!hint]- Hint 2
> In part (a), the generated ideal is $(2,1+\delta)$. In part (b), use the norms and mixed conjugate products to identify $\overline A A$.

## Solution

> [!success]- Solution
> **(a)** Let $x=3+5\delta$ and $y=2+2\delta$. Their coefficient determinant is
>
> $$
> \det\begin{pmatrix}3&2\\5&2\end{pmatrix}=-4.
> $$
>
> Their integer span is not stable under $\delta$: solving
>
> $$
> \delta x=-25+3\delta=mx+ny
> $$
>
> gives $m=14$ and $n=-67/2$, not integers. Hence the two displayed generators do not form a lattice basis of the ideal they generate.
>
> Every ideal generator has coefficients of the same parity, so $A\subseteq P=(2,1+\delta)$. Conversely, the additive lattice generated by $x,y,\delta x,\delta y$ has index equal to the greatest common divisor of its two-by-two minors:
>
> $$
> \gcd(4,134,56,56,24,20)=2.
> $$
>
> Since $P$ also has index $2$ in $R$, $A=P$. The ideal is fixed by conjugation because $1-\delta=2-(1+\delta)\in P$, and direct multiplication gives $P^2=(2)$. Therefore
>
> $$
> \boxed{\overline A A=(2)}.
> $$
>
> **(b)** Let $x=4+\delta$ and $y=1+2\delta$. Here
>
> $$
> \det\begin{pmatrix}4&1\\1&2\end{pmatrix}=7.
> $$
>
> Moreover,
>
> $$
> \delta x=-2x+3y,
> \qquad
> \delta y=-3x+2y.
> $$
>
> Thus their integer span is stable under $\delta$ and is already the ideal $A$; the two elements do form a lattice basis.
>
> The conjugate products are
>
> $$
> \overline x x=21,
> \qquad
> \overline y y=21,
> \qquad
> \overline x y=14+7\delta,
> \qquad
> \overline y x=14-7\delta.
> $$
>
> They all lie in $(7)$, while their ordinary-integer combinations have greatest common divisor
>
> $$
> \gcd(21,21,28)=7.
> $$
>
> Hence the conjugate product is
>
> $$
> \boxed{\overline A A=(7)}.
> $$
