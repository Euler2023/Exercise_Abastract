# Exercise R277: Principal Open Affines as a Topological Basis for Spec

## Problem Statement

> [!question]
> Let $f$ be an element of $A$. Let $S$ be the multiplicative subset $\{1, f, f^2, f^3, \ldots\}$ consisting of the powers of $f$. We denote by $A_f$ the ring $S^{-1} A$ as in Chapter II, §3. From the natural homomorphism $A \to A_f$ one gets the corresponding map $\operatorname{spec}(A_f) \to \operatorname{spec}(A)$.
> 
> **(a)** Show that $\operatorname{spec}(A_f)$ maps on the open set of points in $\operatorname{spec}(A)$ which are not zeros of $f$.
> 
> **(b)** Given a point $\mathfrak{p} \in \operatorname{spec}(A)$, and an open set $U$ containing $\mathfrak{p}$, show that there exists $f$ such that $\mathfrak{p} \in \operatorname{spec}(A_f) \subset U$.

## Hints

> [!hint]- Hint 1
> For part (a), review the correspondence of prime ideals under localization from Chapter II, §3:
> For any ring homomorphism $\phi: A \to B$, the induced map $\phi^*: \operatorname{spec}(B) \to \operatorname{spec}(A)$ sends $\mathfrak{q} \mapsto \phi^{-1}(\mathfrak{q})$.
> When $B = S^{-1} A$, which prime ideals of $A$ are preimages of prime ideals of $S^{-1} A$?
> What does it mean for a prime ideal $\mathfrak{p}$ to be a zero of $f$?

> [!hint]- Hint 2
> For part (b), an open set $U \subseteq \operatorname{spec}(A)$ is the complement of some closed set $V(\mathfrak{a})$.
> If $\mathfrak{p} \in U$, can $\mathfrak{a}$ be contained in $\mathfrak{p}$?
> Choose an element $f \in \mathfrak{a} \setminus \mathfrak{p}$. What is the relationship between $\operatorname{spec}(A_f) = D(f)$ and $U$?

## Solution

> [!success]- Solution
> Let $A$ be a commutative ring with $1$, and let $f \in A$.
> Let $S = \{f^n : n \ge 0\}$ be the multiplicative subset generated by $f$.
> The localized ring is $A_f = S^{-1} A$.
> The natural ring homomorphism is $\phi: A \to A_f$, given by $\phi(a) = a/1$.
> It induces the canonical map on spectra:
> $$
> \phi^*: \operatorname{spec}(A_f) \longrightarrow \operatorname{spec}(A), \qquad \mathfrak{q} \longmapsto \phi^{-1}(\mathfrak{q}).
> $$
> 
> Recall that a point $\mathfrak{p} \in \operatorname{spec}(A)$ is said to be a **zero of $f$** if $f \in \mathfrak{p}$ (i.e. $\mathfrak{p} \in V(f)$).
> Thus the points in $\operatorname{spec}(A)$ which are not zeros of $f$ form the **principal open set** (or distinguished open set):
> $$
> D(f) = \operatorname{spec}(A) \setminus V(f) = \{\mathfrak{p} \in \operatorname{spec}(A) : f \notin \mathfrak{p}\}.
> $$
> 
> ### Part (a): Image of $\operatorname{spec}(A_f)$ in $\operatorname{spec}(A)$
> 
> By the fundamental theorem on the prime spectrum of localized rings (Chapter II, §3, Theorem 3.1):
> For any multiplicative subset $S \subset A$, the map
> $$
> \phi^*: \operatorname{spec}(S^{-1} A) \longrightarrow \{\mathfrak{p} \in \operatorname{spec}(A) : \mathfrak{p} \cap S = \varnothing\}
> $$
> is a bijection (and in fact a homeomorphism onto its image).
> 
> Here $S = \{1, f, f^2, f^3, \ldots\}$.
> A prime ideal $\mathfrak{p} \in \operatorname{spec}(A)$ satisfies $\mathfrak{p} \cap S = \varnothing$ if and only if no power $f^n$ lies in $\mathfrak{p}$.
> Since $\mathfrak{p}$ is a prime ideal, by induction on $n$,
> $$
> f^n \in \mathfrak{p} \iff f \in \mathfrak{p}.
> $$
> Therefore,
> $$
> \mathfrak{p} \cap S = \varnothing \iff f \notin \mathfrak{p}.
> $$
> 
> 1. **Image is contained in $D(f)$:**
>    If $\mathfrak{q} \in \operatorname{spec}(A_f)$, let $\mathfrak{p} = \phi^{-1}(\mathfrak{q})$.
>    If $f \in \mathfrak{p}$, then $\phi(f) = f/1 \in \mathfrak{q}$.
>    However, $f/1$ is a unit in $A_f$ (its inverse is $1/f$).
>    Since a prime ideal cannot contain a unit, this is a contradiction.
>    Thus $f \notin \mathfrak{p}$, which means $\phi^*(\mathfrak{q}) \in D(f)$.
> 
> 2. **Surjectivity onto $D(f)$:**
>    Conversely, let $\mathfrak{p} \in D(f)$, so $f \notin \mathfrak{p}$.
>    Then $\mathfrak{p} \cap S = \varnothing$.
>    The ideal $\mathfrak{q} = S^{-1} \mathfrak{p} = \{a/f^n : a \in \mathfrak{p}, n \ge 0\}$ is a prime ideal of $A_f$, and its contraction is $\phi^{-1}(\mathfrak{q}) = \mathfrak{p}$.
>    Thus $\mathfrak{p} = \phi^*(\mathfrak{q}) \in \operatorname{Im}(\phi^*)$.
> 
> Therefore, the map $\phi^*$ maps $\operatorname{spec}(A_f)$ bijectively onto the open set of points in $\operatorname{spec}(A)$ which are not zeros of $f$, namely $D(f)$.
> Identifying $\operatorname{spec}(A_f)$ with its image, we write $\operatorname{spec}(A_f) = D(f)$.
> 
> ### Part (b): The Sets $\operatorname{spec}(A_f)$ Form a Topological Basis
> 
> Let $\mathfrak{p} \in \operatorname{spec}(A)$, and let $U \subseteq \operatorname{spec}(A)$ be an open set containing $\mathfrak{p}$.
> We must find an element $f \in A$ such that
> $$
> \mathfrak{p} \in \operatorname{spec}(A_f) \subseteq U.
> $$
> 
> 1. By definition of the Zariski topology, any open set $U$ is the complement of some closed set:
>    $$
>    U = \operatorname{spec}(A) \setminus V(\mathfrak{a})
>    $$
>    for some ideal $\mathfrak{a} \subseteq A$.
> 2. Since $\mathfrak{p} \in U$, we have $\mathfrak{p} \notin V(\mathfrak{a})$.
>    By definition of $V(\mathfrak{a})$, this means $\mathfrak{a}$ is not contained in $\mathfrak{p}$:
>    $$
>    \mathfrak{a} \not\subseteq \mathfrak{p}.
>    $$
> 3. Therefore, there exists at least one element $f \in \mathfrak{a}$ such that
>    $$
>    f \notin \mathfrak{p}.
>    $$
> 4. Since $f \notin \mathfrak{p}$, by part (a) we have $\mathfrak{p} \in D(f) = \operatorname{spec}(A_f)$.
> 5. Since $f \in \mathfrak{a}$, the principal ideal $(f)$ is contained in $\mathfrak{a}$:
>    $$
>    (f) \subseteq \mathfrak{a}.
>    $$
>    Taking zero loci reverses inclusions:
>    $$
>    V(\mathfrak{a}) \subseteq V(f).
>    $$
>    Taking complements in $\operatorname{spec}(A)$ preserves the reversed inclusion:
>    $$
>    \operatorname{spec}(A_f) = D(f) = \operatorname{spec}(A) \setminus V(f) \subseteq \operatorname{spec}(A) \setminus V(\mathfrak{a}) = U.
>    $$
> 
> Thus we have found $f \in A$ such that
> $$
> \mathfrak{p} \in \operatorname{spec}(A_f) \subseteq U.
> $$
> 
> This proves that the family of distinguished open sets $\{D(f) : f \in A\}$ forms a **basis for the Zariski topology** on $\operatorname{spec}(A)$.

## Notes

- **Independent derivation:** The solution provides the explicit localization correspondence and the ideal inclusion argument $\mathfrak{a} \not\subseteq \mathfrak{p} \implies \exists f \in \mathfrak{a} \setminus \mathfrak{p}$.
- **Standard open affines:** In scheme theory, the distinguished open sets $D(f) \cong \operatorname{Spec}(A_f)$ are called the standard (or principal) affine open subsets. They form the basic building blocks for defining sheaves and schemes.
- **Quasi-compactness:** Applying [[02 - Ring Theory/Exercises/Exercise R276 - Quasi-Compactness of the Zariski Topology|R276]] to $A_f$ shows that each $D(f)$ is quasi-compact. Thus an open subset of $\operatorname{Spec}(A)$ is quasi-compact exactly when it is a finite union of distinguished opens.
