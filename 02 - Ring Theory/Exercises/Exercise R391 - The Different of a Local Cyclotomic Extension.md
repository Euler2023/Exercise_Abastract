# Exercise R391: The Different of a Local Cyclotomic Extension

## Problem Statement

> [!question] Neukirch III.2.5
> If $\zeta$ is a primitive $p^n$-th root of unity, then
> $$
> \mathfrak D_{\mathbb Q_p(\zeta)|\mathbb Q_p}=\mathfrak P^{\,p^{n-1}(pn-n-1)}.
> $$

## Hints

> [!hint]- Hint 1
> Put $L=\mathbb Q_p(\zeta)$ and $e=p^{n-1}(p-1)$. The polynomial $\Phi_{p^n}(X+1)$ is Eisenstein, so $\zeta-1$ is a uniformizer and $\mathcal O_L=\mathbb Z_p[\zeta]$.

> [!hint]- Hint 2
> Differentiate $\Phi_{p^n}(X)=(X^{p^n}-1)/(X^{p^{n-1}}-1)$ at $\zeta$, then use the derivative formula for the different.

> [!hint]- Hint 3
> The denominator is $\zeta_p-1$, whose valuation in $L$ is $p^{n-1}$; the numerator $p^n$ has valuation $ne$.

## Solution

> [!success]- Solution
> **Proof scope:** Independent derivation
>
> Let $p$ be prime, $n\ge1$, $L=\mathbb Q_p(\zeta)$, and let $\mathfrak P$ be the maximal ideal of its ring of integers. Normalize the valuation by $v_L(\mathfrak P)=1$. Write
> $$
> f(X)=\Phi_{p^n}(X)=\frac{X^{p^n}-1}{X^{p^{n-1}}-1}=\sum_{j=0}^{p-1}X^{jp^{n-1}},\qquad e=\deg f=p^{n-1}(p-1).
> $$
> Modulo $p$, we have $f(X+1)\equiv X^e$, since $X^{p^n}-1=(X^{p^{n-1}}-1)^p$ in characteristic $p$. Also $f(1)=p\notin p^2\mathbb Z_p$. Thus $f(X+1)$ is Eisenstein, $[L:\mathbb Q_p]=e$, the extension is totally ramified, and $\pi=\zeta-1$ is a uniformizer.
>
> We also verify that the ring of integers is generated by one element. Every $x\in L$ has a unique expression $\sum_{i=0}^{e-1}a_i\pi^i$ with $a_i\in\mathbb Q_p$. The nonzero terms have valuations $e\,v_p(a_i)+i$ that are distinct modulo $e$, so the valuation of the sum is the smallest term valuation. If $x$ is integral, every term has nonnegative valuation; since $0\le i<e$, this implies $v_p(a_i)\ge0$. Hence $\mathcal O_L=\mathbb Z_p[\pi]=\mathbb Z_p[\zeta]$.
>
> Differentiate the identity $f(X)(X^{p^{n-1}}-1)=X^{p^n}-1$ and substitute $X=\zeta$ to obtain
> $$
> f'(\zeta)=\frac{p^n\zeta^{p^n-1}}{\zeta^{p^{n-1}}-1}=\frac{p^n\zeta^{-1}}{\zeta_p-1},\qquad \zeta_p=\zeta^{p^{n-1}}.
> $$
> The same Eisenstein argument shows that $\zeta_p-1$ is a uniformizer of $E=\mathbb Q_p(\zeta_p)$. The totally ramified tower has $[L:E]=p^{n-1}$, so
> $$
> v_L(p)=e,\qquad v_L(\zeta_p-1)=p^{n-1},\qquad v_L(\zeta)=0.
> $$
> The proved derivative formula for the different, III (2.4) [Jürgen Neukirch, Algebraic Number Theory, English ed., 1999, printed pp. 197–198, PDF pp. 216–217], gives
> $$
> \begin{aligned}
> v_L(\mathfrak D_{L|\mathbb Q_p})
> &=v_L(f'(\zeta))=ne-p^{n-1}\\
> &=p^{n-1}(np-n-1),
> \end{aligned}
> $$
> This is the required exponent.
>
> Boundary checks: if $n=1$, the exponent is $p-2=e-1$, as in the tame case. If $p=2,n=1$, then $L=\mathbb Q_2$ and the exponent is $0$. If $p=2,n=2$, the exponent is $2$, agreeing with the valuation of the derivative $2i$ for $\mathbb Q_2(i)$.

The exponent in the source image is $p^{n-1}(pn-n-1)$. The independent derivative and valuation calculation above also proves the required generation statement for the ring of integers.
- **Notation:** $\mathfrak P$ is the maximal ideal of $L$; this exponent must not be read as an exponent computed with the base-field normalization $v_p(p)=1$.
