# Exercise R458: An Ideal with Prescribed Generic and Special Fibres

## Problem Statement

> [!question] Original Exercise 8.5.1
> Given polynomials $\varphi_1,\ldots,\varphi_m,\phi,\psi_1,\ldots,\psi_l\in\mathbb Z_{(p)}[x_1,\ldots,x_n]$, find an ideal $I$ there such that its extension to $\mathbb Q[x_1,\ldots,x_n]$ is $\langle\varphi_1,\ldots,\varphi_m,p\phi-1\rangle$, but its reduction is $\langle\widetilde\psi_1,\ldots,\widetilde\psi_l\rangle$ in $\mathbb F_p[x_1,\ldots,x_n]$. (A hint for this exercise is at the end of the book.)

## Hints

> [!hint]- Hint 1
> Multiply generic generators by $p$ to make them disappear in the special fibre.

> [!hint]- Hint 2
> The polynomial $p\phi-1$ reduces to a unit, so it can transmit any chosen special generators.

## Solution

> [!success]- Solution
> **Proof provenance:** Independent derivation. Imported results are named at their point of use.
>
>
> Put $A=\mathbb Z_{(p)}[x_1,\ldots,x_n]$ and define
> $$
> I=\bigl(p\varphi_1,\ldots,p\varphi_m,\ p(p\phi-1),\
> (p\phi-1)\psi_1,\ldots,(p\phi-1)\psi_l\bigr).
> $$
> Over $\mathbb Q$, the scalar $p$ is invertible, so the first generators produce all $\varphi_i$ and the next produces $p\phi-1$. The remaining generators are multiples of that same polynomial. Therefore the extended ideal is exactly the requested generic ideal.
>
> Modulo $p$, the first $m+1$ generators become zero and the remaining ones become $-\widetilde\psi_j$, because $p\phi-1$ reduces to $-1$. Their ideal is the requested special ideal. Reduction of the whole generated ideal is generated by the reductions of its generators, so there are no additional conditions to check.
