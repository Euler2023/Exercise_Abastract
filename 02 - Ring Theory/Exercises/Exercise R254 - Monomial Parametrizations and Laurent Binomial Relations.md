# Exercise R254: Monomial Parametrizations and Laurent Binomial Relations

## Problem Statement

> [!question] Lang, Chapter V, Exercise 29
> Let $f(X,Y)$ be an irreducible polynomial in two variables over a field $k$. Let $t$ be transcendental over $k$, and assume that there exist integers $m,n\ne0$ and elements $a,b\in k$, $ab\ne0$, such that $f(at^n,bt^m)=0$. Show that after inverting possibly $X$ or $Y$, and up to a constant factor, $f$ is of type
> $$
> X^mY^n-c
> $$
> with some $c\in k$.

> [!warning] Source issue
> The printed exponents must be divided by $\gcd(|m|,|n|)$, and their signs must match the kernel vector $(m,-n)$. With $a=b=1$, $m=n=2$, the irreducible relation $X-Y$ vanishes at $(t^2,t^2)$ but cannot be a scalar multiple of the printed degree-four expression under variable inversion. The corrected statement uses the primitive Laurent relation below.

## Hints

> [!hint]- Hint 1
> Divide the exponents $m,n$ by their gcd.

> [!hint]- Hint 2
> Compute the kernel of the homomorphism of Laurent polynomial rings given by $X\mapsto at^n$, $Y\mapsto bt^m$.

## Solution

> [!success]- Solution and proof status
> Let $d=\gcd(|m|,|n|)$, $m_0=m/d$, and $n_0=n/d$. Rescale the variables by $U=X/a$, $V=Y/b$. The map
> $$
> k[U^{\pm1},V^{\pm1}]\longrightarrow k[t^{\pm1}],
> \qquad U\longmapsto t^n,\quad V\longmapsto t^m
> $$
> has exponent kernel generated by the primitive vector $(m_0,-n_0)$.
>
> Here is an explicit ring calculation. Choose integers $r,s$ with $n_0r+m_0s=1$, and put
> $$
> Z=U^{m_0}V^{-n_0},\qquad W=U^rV^s.
> $$
> The exponent matrix has determinant $m_0s+n_0r=1$, so $Z,W$ are Laurent coordinates:
> $$
> k[U^{\pm1},V^{\pm1}]=k[Z^{\pm1},W^{\pm1}].
> $$
> Their images are $1$ and $t^d$. Since $t^d$ is transcendental, the map on $k[W^{\pm1}]$ is injective, and the full kernel is $(Z-1)$.
>
> The original $f$ is neither associated to $X$ nor to $Y$, since these do not vanish at $(at^n,bt^m)$. Localizing the UFD $k[X,Y]$ at powers of $X,Y$ preserves its irreducibility and nonunit status. As its image lies in the principal prime ideal generated by $Z-1$, it is associated to that binomial in the Laurent ring. Restoring the scaling, the primitive relation is
> $$
> X^{m_0}Y^{-n_0}-a^{m_0}b^{-n_0}.
> $$
> More concretely, if $m_0,n_0>0$, its polynomial numerator is $X^{m_0}-a^{m_0}b^{-n_0}Y^{n_0}$; other sign patterns give the analogous two monomials after clearing negative powers. This numerator is not divisible by $X$ or $Y$ and is irreducible. It is associated to $f$ in $k[X,Y]$, so differs from it by a nonzero scalar. After choosing variable inversions, the relation has the asserted binomial shape with **primitive** exponents.

## Notes

- **Routing:** The proof computes a Laurent polynomial kernel by a unimodular change of exponent coordinates. It does not require a general theory of algebraic curves.
- **Unit convention:** Laurent associates may differ by a scalar times a monomial. Clearing negative powers to obtain a polynomial not divisible by either variable then leaves only a scalar ambiguity.
