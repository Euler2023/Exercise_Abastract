# Exercise R406: Failure of Galois Descent for Ideal Class Groups

## Problem Statement

> [!question] Neukirch VI.2.4
> The ideal class group, unlike the idèle class group, does not have Galois descent. More precisely, for a Galois extension $L|K$, the homomorphism $Cl_K\to Cl_L^{G(L|K)}$ is in general neither injective nor surjective.

## Hints

> [!hint]- Hint
> For failure of surjectivity start with $\mathbb Q$ and an imaginary quadratic field of class number two. For failure of injectivity use capitulation in a Hilbert class field.

> [!hint]- Stronger hint
> In Q(sqrt(-5)), the nonprincipal ideal p=(2,1+sqrt(-5)) has p^2=(2). In K(i), compare it with the principal ideal (1+i).

## Solution

> [!success]- Solution
> **Proof scope:** Independent derivation
>
> For failure of surjectivity, take $K=\mathbb Q$ and $L=\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-5})$. The Minkowski bound is $(2/\pi)\sqrt{20}<3$, so every ideal class has an integral representative of norm at most $2$. The unique prime ideal of norm $2$ is $\mathfrak p=(2,1+\sqrt{-5})$: the minimal polynomial modulo $2$ is $(X+1)^2$, and $\mathcal O_L=\mathbb Z[\sqrt{-5}]$. This ideal is nonprincipal, since a principal ideal of norm $2$ would give an integer solution of $x^2+5y^2=2$, which does not exist. Moreover $\mathfrak p^2=(2)$, so the class group is exactly $C_2$. Complex conjugation acts by inversion on the class group of a quadratic field: $\mathfrak a\overline{\mathfrak a}$ is principal, generated by a rational integer. Inversion on $C_2$ is the identity. Hence $\mathrm{Cl}_L^{G}=C_2$, while $\mathrm{Cl}_{\mathbb Q}=1$, and the original map is not surjective.
>
> Failure of injectivity also has a fully explicit example, without using the principal ideal theorem. Now take $K=\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-5})$ and $L=K(i)=\mathbb Q(i,\sqrt5)$. Since $i\notin K$, the extension $L/K$ is quadratic Galois. Retain the nonprincipal ideal $\mathfrak p$ above. Its extension satisfies
> $$
> (\mathfrak p\mathcal O_L)^2=(2)\mathcal O_L,
> \qquad ((1+i)\mathcal O_L)^2=(2i)\mathcal O_L=(2)\mathcal O_L.
> $$
> The group of nonzero fractional ideals of a Dedekind domain is free abelian on the prime ideals and is therefore torsion-free. Equality of the squares of two ideals forces equality of the ideals. Hence $\mathfrak p\mathcal O_L=(1+i)\mathcal O_L$: the nontrivial class $[\mathfrak p]\in\mathrm{Cl}_K$ becomes trivial in $L$. The original map is not injective. The two examples establish the two failures separately; no claim is made that one extension exhibits both.
