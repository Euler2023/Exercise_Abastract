# Hilbert Nullstellensatz

## Definition

Let $k$ be a field and $k^a$ its algebraic closure. For a subset of polynomials $S \subseteq k[X_1, \ldots, X_n]$, the **zero set defined over $k$** in $(k^a)^n$ is
$$
V(S) = \{x \in (k^a)^n : f(x) = 0 \text{ for all } f \in S\}.
$$
For a subset $W \subseteq (k^a)^n$, the **vanishing ideal** is
$$
I(W) = \{f \in k[X_1, \ldots, X_n] : f(x) = 0 \text{ for all } x \in W\}.
$$

> [!info] Weak Nullstellensatz
> Let $\mathfrak{a}$ be an ideal of $k[X_1, \ldots, X_n]$. The algebraic set $V(\mathfrak{a})$ is empty if and only if $\mathfrak{a} = k[X_1, \ldots, X_n] = (1)$.
> In particular, every maximal ideal of $k[X_1, \ldots, X_n]$ is of the form
> $$
> \mathfrak{m}_x = \{f \in k[X] : f(x) = 0\}
> $$
> for some $x \in (k^a)^n$. When $k = k^a$, every maximal ideal is generated by linear forms $(X_1 - c_1, \ldots, X_n - c_n)$ for $c \in k^n$.

> [!info] Strong Nullstellensatz
> For any ideal $\mathfrak{a} \subseteq k[X_1, \ldots, X_n]$, the vanishing ideal of the zero set is the radical of $\mathfrak{a}$:
> $$
> I(V(\mathfrak{a})) = \sqrt{\mathfrak{a}} = \{f \in k[X] : f^N \in \mathfrak{a} \text{ for some } N \ge 1\}.
> $$

> [!info] Homogeneous Nullstellensatz
> Let $n\ge1$, let $\mathfrak{a} \subseteq k[X_1, \ldots, X_n]$ be a **proper homogeneous ideal**, and let $\mathfrak{m} = (X_1, \ldots, X_n)$ be the irrelevant maximal ideal.
> 1. The zero set $V(\mathfrak{a}) \subseteq (k^a)^n$ consists solely of the origin $\{0\}$ if and only if $\mathfrak{m} \subseteq \sqrt{\mathfrak{a}}$, i.e., there exists $N \ge 1$ such that $\mathfrak{m}^N \subseteq \mathfrak{a}$.
> 2. The projective zero set $V_{\mathbb{P}}(\mathfrak{a}) \subseteq \mathbb{P}^{n-1}(k^a)$ is empty if and only if $\mathfrak{m} \subseteq \sqrt{\mathfrak{a}}$.
> 3. If $V_{\mathbb{P}}(\mathfrak{a})$ is non-empty, the homogeneous vanishing ideal of $V_{\mathbb{P}}(\mathfrak{a})$ is $\sqrt{\mathfrak{a}}$.

## Intuition and Proof Mechanics

The Nullstellensatz establishes a bijective, inclusion-reversing correspondence between radical ideals of $k[X]$ and zero sets in $(k^a)^n$ **defined over $k$**. If $k$ is not algebraically closed, these are not all algebraic sets over $k^a$.

The weak Nullstellensatz employs **Zariski's lemma**: if a field $K$ is finitely generated as an algebra over a subfield $k$, then $K$ is a finite algebraic extension of $k$.
The strong Nullstellensatz then follows from the weak version by **Rabinowitsch's trick**: introduce an auxiliary variable $Y$ and consider the ideal
$$
\mathfrak{b} = (\mathfrak{a}, 1 - Y f) \subseteq k[X_1, \ldots, X_n, Y].
$$
If $f\ne0$ vanishes at all zeros of $\mathfrak{a}$, then the polynomials in $\mathfrak{b}$ have no common zero in $(k^a)^{n+1}$, so $1 \in \mathfrak{b}$. Setting $Y=1/f$ in $k[X,1/f]$ and clearing denominators yields $f^N\in\mathfrak a$. The case $f=0$ is immediate.

The homogeneous statements are consequences of the affine theorem. A proper homogeneous ideal vanishes at the origin. If every $X_i$ lies in its radical, choose $n_i\ge1$ with $X_i^{n_i}\in\mathfrak a$; every monomial of degree $N=1+\sum_i(n_i-1)$ then belongs to $\mathfrak a$, proving $\mathfrak m^N\subseteq\mathfrak a$. For a nonempty projective zero set, its affine cone is $V(\mathfrak a)$, and a homogeneous polynomial vanishes on that cone exactly when it vanishes projectively. The strong theorem gives the claimed homogeneous radical.

## Key Properties

- **Radical correspondence:** The map $V \mapsto I(V)$ and $\mathfrak{a} \mapsto V(\mathfrak{a})$ gives an order-reversing bijection:
  $$
  \{\text{Zero sets in } (k^a)^n\text{ defined over }k\} \longleftrightarrow \{\text{Radical ideals in } k[X_1, \ldots, X_n]\}.
  $$
- **Prime ideals and irreducibility:** Prime ideals correspond to nonempty **$k$-irreducible** zero sets: those not expressible as a union of two proper zero sets defined over $k$. This need not imply geometric irreducibility over $k^a$.
- **Maximal ideals and points:** Maximal ideals correspond to Galois orbits of geometric points in $(k^a)^n$, and when $k = k^a$, to individual points in $k^n$.
- **Coordinate ring:** The coordinate ring $k[V]=k[X]/I(V)$ is reduced, and is an integral domain if and only if $V$ is nonempty and $k$-irreducible.

## Examples

- For $\mathfrak a=(X^2,Y^3)\subset k[X,Y]$, $V(\mathfrak a)=\{(0,0)\}$ and $\sqrt{\mathfrak a}=(X,Y)$. Every degree-four monomial is divisible by $X^2$ or $Y^3$, so $(X,Y)^4\subseteq\mathfrak a$.
- The properness hypothesis in the origin statement is necessary: $\mathfrak a=(1)$ contains every power of $\mathfrak m$, but $V(\mathfrak a)$ is empty.
- Over $k=\mathbf R$, $(X^2+1)$ is maximal and its zero set in $\mathbf C$ is $\{i,-i\}$. This set is $\mathbf R$-irreducible, whereas over $\mathbf C$ it is the union of two points. The maximal ideal corresponds to their conjugation orbit.
