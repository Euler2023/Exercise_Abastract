# Different and Discriminant Ideals

## Definition

> [!info] Trace dual and different
> Let $L/K$ be finite separable, $A$ a Dedekind domain with fraction field $K$, and $B$ its integral closure in $L$. The inverse different is
> $$
> \mathfrak D_{B/A}^{-1}=\{x\in L:\operatorname{Tr}_{L/K}(xB)\subset A\}.
> $$
> Its inverse fractional B-ideal is the **different**. The **discriminant ideal** is the A-ideal generated by all trace Gram determinants of integral $K$-bases.

## Intuition

The trace pairs field elements perfectly over $K$. The different measures how the integral lattice differs from its trace dual; the discriminant records the same failure on the base ring.

## Key Properties

- If $B=A[\alpha]$ and $f$ is the monic minimal polynomial, then $\mathfrak D=(f'(\alpha))$.
- Differents multiply in a tower: $\mathfrak D_{C/A}=\mathfrak D_{C/B}\mathfrak D_{B/A}C$.
- The discriminant ideal is the norm of the different. Different and discriminant therefore live in different rings and must not share an unspecified exponent convention.
- For number-field integer rings (and the finite henselian discrete valuation setting with separable residue extension), Kähler differentials are locally $B/\mathfrak D$; the exercise derives global cyclicity and a generator $dx$ without asserting the entire integer ring is monogenic.

## Examples

In $\mathbb Q_p(\zeta_{p^n})/\mathbb Q_p$, the different exponent in the upper field's normalized valuation is $p^{n-1}(pn-n-1)$. A global power basis may have a common index divisor: for $\alpha^3-\alpha^2-2\alpha-8=0$, the field discriminant is $-503$ but every primitive integer's element discriminant is divisible by $4\cdot503$.
