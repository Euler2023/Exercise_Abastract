# Integral Ring Extensions and Integrally Closed Domains

## Definition

Let $A$ and $B$ be commutative rings with $A\subseteq B$.

> [!info] Integral Element
> An element $\alpha\in B$ is **integral over $A$** if it satisfies the following three equivalent conditions:
>
> 1. **INT 1 (Polynomial root):** There exists a monic polynomial $f(X)\in A[X]$ of degree $n\ge1$ such that $f(\alpha)=0$, i.e.,
>    $$
>    \alpha^n + a_{n-1}\alpha^{n-1} + \cdots + a_1\alpha + a_0 = 0,\qquad a_i\in A.
>    $$
> 2. **INT 2 (Finitely generated module):** The subring $A[\alpha]$ is a finitely generated $A$-module.
> 3. **INT 3 (Faithful module):** There exists a faithful $A[\alpha]$-module $M$ that is finitely generated as an $A$-module.

> [!info] Integral Extension and Integral Closure
> - The ring $B$ is an **integral extension** of $A$ (or $B$ is **integral over $A$**) if every element of $B$ is integral over $A$.
> - The **integral closure** of $A$ in $B$ is the subset of all elements of $B$ that are integral over $A$. It is a subring of $B$ containing $A$.
> - If the integral closure of $A$ in $B$ is $A$ itself, $A$ is said to be **integrally closed in $B$**.
> - An integral domain $A$ is **integrally closed** (or **normal**) if $A$ is integrally closed in its fraction field $K=\operatorname{Frac}(A)$.

## Intuition

Integrality is the ring-theoretic generalization of algebraic field extensions. Requiring the leading coefficient of the annihilating polynomial to be $1$ prevents the introduction of denominators outside $A$, guaranteeing that elements generated by integral elements remain finite modules over the base ring.

## Key Properties

1. **Finite Generation Characterization:** A ring extension $B$ of $A$ is a finitely generated $A$-module if and only if $B=A[\alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_m]$ where each $\alpha_i$ is integral over $A$.
2. **Transitivity of Integrality:** If $A\subseteq B\subseteq C$ are commutative rings such that $B$ is integral over $A$ and $C$ is integral over $B$, then $C$ is integral over $A$.
3. **Integral Closure Is a Ring:** If $\alpha,\beta\in B$ are integral over $A$, then $\alpha+\beta$, $\alpha-\beta$, and $\alpha\beta$ are integral over $A$.
4. **UFDs Are Integrally Closed:** Every unique factorization domain is integrally closed in its fraction field.
5. **Polynomial Rings Preserve Normalcy:** If an integral domain $A$ is integrally closed, the polynomial ring $A[t]$ is also integrally closed in its fraction field $K(t)$.
6. **Valuation Ring Characterization:** Let $A$ be a ring contained in a field $L$. An element $x\in L$ is integral over $A$ if and only if $x$ belongs to every valuation ring $\mathfrak O$ of $L$ containing $A$.
7. **Cohen–Seidenberg Theorems:**
   - **Lying-Over:** If $B$ is integral over $A$ and $\mathfrak p\in\operatorname{Spec}(A)$, there exists $\mathfrak q\in\operatorname{Spec}(B)$ such that $\mathfrak q\cap A=\mathfrak p$.
   - **Going-Up:** If $\mathfrak p_1\subseteq\mathfrak p_2$ in $\operatorname{Spec}(A)$ and $\mathfrak q_1\in\operatorname{Spec}(B)$ with $\mathfrak q_1\cap A=\mathfrak p_1$, there exists $\mathfrak q_2\supseteq\mathfrak q_1$ in $\operatorname{Spec}(B)$ such that $\mathfrak q_2\cap A=\mathfrak p_2$.
   - **Incomparability:** If $\mathfrak q_1\subsetneq\mathfrak q_2$ are distinct prime ideals in $B$, then $\mathfrak q_1\cap A\ne\mathfrak q_2\cap A$.

## Examples

> [!example] Rings of Algebraic Integers
> Let $K$ be a number field (a finite extension of $\mathbb Q$). The ring of integers $\mathcal O_K$ is the integral closure of $\mathbb Z$ in $K$. By transitivity and trace duality, $\mathcal O_K$ is a free $\mathbb Z$-module of rank $[K:\mathbb Q]$ and a Dedekind domain.

> [!example] Symmetric Polynomials
> The multivariate polynomial ring $R=F[t_1,\ldots,t_n]$ is an integral extension of the elementary symmetric subring $S=F[s_1,\ldots,s_n]$, and $R$ is a free $S$-module of rank $n!$.

> [!example] Non-Normal Quadratic Order
> The order $\mathbb Z[\sqrt{5}]$ is not integrally closed in its quotient field $\mathbb Q(\sqrt{5})$: the golden ratio $\alpha=\frac{1+\sqrt{5}}{2}$ satisfies the monic equation $X^2-X-1=0$, so $\alpha$ is integral over $\mathbb Z$, yet $\alpha\notin\mathbb Z[\sqrt{5}]$. The integral closure is $\mathbb Z\left[\frac{1+\sqrt{5}}{2}\right]$.
