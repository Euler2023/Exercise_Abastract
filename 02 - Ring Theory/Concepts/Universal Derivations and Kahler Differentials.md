# Universal Derivations and Kahler Differentials

## Definition

For a homomorphism of commutative rings $R\to A$ and an $A$-module $M$, an **$R$-derivation** $D:A\to M$ is additive, vanishes on $R$, and satisfies $D(ab)=aD(b)+bD(a)$.

The **module of universal differentials** is an $A$-module $\Omega^1_{A/R}$ with an $R$-derivation $d:A\to\Omega^1_{A/R}$ such that composition with $d$ gives a natural bijection
$$
\operatorname{Hom}_A(\Omega^1_{A/R},M)\cong\operatorname{Der}_R(A,M)
$$
for every $A$-module $M$. It is generated by symbols $da$ subject to additivity, the product rule, and $dr=0$ for $r$ from $R$.

## Intuition

The symbol $da$ records every possible first-order variation of $a$ over the fixed base $R$. A particular derivation is obtained by applying a linear functional with values in the chosen target module. The base and the target module are part of the data.

This note concerns arbitrary commutative algebras and modules. [[03 - Field Theory/Concepts/Derivations and Differentials on Fields|Derivations and Differentials on Fields]] treats separability, transcendence bases, and dimension questions for fields.

## Key Properties

1. For $P=R[X_\alpha]$, the differentials $dX_\alpha$ freely generate $\Omega^1_{P/R}$, even with infinitely many variables; each polynomial uses finitely many.
2. For $B=A/I$ there is the right-exact **conormal sequence**
   $$
   I/I^2\longrightarrow B\otimes_A\Omega^1_{A/R}\longrightarrow\Omega^1_{B/R}\longrightarrow0,
   $$
   with $\bar i\mapsto1\otimes di$. The first map need not be injective.
3. Every algebra is a quotient of a polynomial algebra. The preceding two constructions prove existence without finiteness or smoothness assumptions.
4. For $R\to A\to B$, the transitivity sequence is
   $$
   B\otimes_A\Omega^1_{A/R}\longrightarrow\Omega^1_{B/R}\longrightarrow\Omega^1_{B/A}\longrightarrow0.
   $$
5. If $S_0\subseteq R$ and $S\subseteq A$ are multiplicatively closed subsets and $S_0$ maps into $S$, then
   $$
   \Omega^1_{S^{-1}A/S_0^{-1}R}\cong S^{-1}\Omega^1_{A/R},\qquad
   d(a/s)=s^{-1}da-as^{-2}ds.
   $$
6. An augmentation $\varepsilon:A\to R$ with kernel $I$ gives $\Omega^1_{A/R}/I\Omega^1_{A/R}\cong I/I^2$. This is the module for derivations whose target action factors through $\varepsilon$.

## Examples

For $A=R[t]/(f)$,
$$
\Omega^1_{A/R}\cong A\,dt/(f'(t)\,dt).
$$
This follows by differentiating the defining relation and its multiples. Thus for $A=k[t]/(t^p)$ in characteristic $p$, $\Omega^1_{A/k}=A\,dt$; the relation has zero derivative.

The square-zero algebra $B\oplus M$, with $(b,x)(c,y)=(bc,by+cx)$, interprets a derivation as the second component of an algebra map $A\to B\oplus M$.
