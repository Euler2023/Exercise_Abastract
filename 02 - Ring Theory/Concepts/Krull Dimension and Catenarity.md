# Krull Dimension and Catenarity

## Definition

Let $A$ be a commutative ring with identity.

> [!info] Height and Krull Dimension
> - A chain of prime ideals of **length** $m$ is a strictly increasing sequence:
>   $$
>   \mathfrak{p}_0 \subsetneq \mathfrak{p}_1 \subsetneq \cdots \subsetneq \mathfrak{p}_m.
>   $$
> - The **height** (or codimension) of a prime ideal $\mathfrak{p}$, denoted $\operatorname{ht}(\mathfrak{p})$, is the supremum of lengths of chains of prime ideals descending from $\mathfrak{p}$:
>   $$
>   \operatorname{ht}(\mathfrak{p}) = \sup \{m : \mathfrak{p}_0 \subsetneq \mathfrak{p}_1 \subsetneq \cdots \subsetneq \mathfrak{p}_m = \mathfrak{p}\}.
>   $$
> - The **coheight** (or dimension of the quotient) is $\dim(A/\mathfrak{p})$.
> - The **Krull dimension** of $A$, denoted $\dim(A)$, is the supremum of the heights of all its prime ideals:
>   $$
>   \dim(A) = \sup \{\operatorname{ht}(\mathfrak{p}) : \mathfrak{p} \in \operatorname{Spec}(A)\}.
>   $$

> [!info] Catenary Ring
> A commutative ring $A$ is **catenary** if for any pair of prime ideals $\mathfrak{q} \subseteq \mathfrak{p}$, all maximal saturated chains of prime ideals between $\mathfrak{q}$ and $\mathfrak{p}$:
> $$
> \mathfrak{q} = \mathfrak{p}_0 \subsetneq \mathfrak{p}_1 \subsetneq \cdots \subsetneq \mathfrak{p}_m = \mathfrak{p}
> $$
> have the same finite length, which is then equal to $\operatorname{ht}(\mathfrak{p}/\mathfrak{q})$.
> A Noetherian ring $A$ is **universally catenary** if every finitely generated $A$-algebra is catenary.

## Intuition

Height measures the number of strict prime-ideal steps below a given prime. Catenarity compares saturated chains with the **same two endpoints**. It does not say that different maximal ideals have the same height. To prove the chain-length conclusions in Lang IX.18–19, one must establish both catenarity and the height of every maximal ideal.

## External dimension-theory inputs

The following results are imported from the Stacks Project; their proofs are not reproduced in this note.

1. **Cohen–Macaulay rings are universally catenary.** A Noetherian Cohen–Macaulay ring is universally catenary. This applies to a field and to $\mathbf Z$: their local rings are respectively fields, or fields and discrete valuation rings. These are Cohen–Macaulay. [Stacks, Lemma 10.105.9, Tag 00NM](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/00NM).

2. **Dimension formula.** Let $B\subset C$ be domains, with $B$ Noetherian and universally catenary and $C$ finitely generated as a $B$-algebra. For $\mathfrak q\in\operatorname{Spec}C$ and $\mathfrak p=\mathfrak q\cap B$,
   $$
   \operatorname{ht}(\mathfrak q)
   =
   \operatorname{ht}(\mathfrak p)
   +\operatorname{tr.deg}_{\operatorname{Frac}B}\operatorname{Frac}C
   -\operatorname{tr.deg}_{\kappa(\mathfrak p)}\kappa(\mathfrak q).
   $$
   Here $\kappa(\mathfrak p)=\operatorname{Frac}(B/\mathfrak p)$ is the residue field at $\mathfrak p$, and similarly for $\mathfrak q$. Without universal catenarity, the cited theorem gives the corresponding upper bound. [Stacks, Lemma 10.113.1, Tag 02IJ](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/02IJ).

## Key Theorems and Properties

1. **Krull's height theorem (the principal ideal theorem when $r=1$):**
   In a Noetherian ring $A$, if a proper ideal $I$ is generated by $r$ elements, every minimal prime $\mathfrak p$ over $I$ has $\operatorname{ht}(\mathfrak p)\le r$. In particular, if $x$ is a nonunit and a non-zero-divisor, every minimal prime over $(x)$ has height $1$: the upper bound is the principal ideal theorem, and a non-zero-divisor belongs to no minimal prime of a Noetherian ring. This theorem is an external input, not proved here. [Stacks, Lemma 10.60.12(1), Tag 0BBZ](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/0BBZ).

2. **Dimension Formula for Affine Algebras over a Field:**
   Let $k$ be a field and let $A$ be an affine domain over $k$ (i.e. a finitely generated $k$-algebra that is an integral domain). Then:
   - $\dim(A) = \operatorname{tr.deg}_k(\operatorname{Frac}(A))$.
   - $A$ is catenary.
   - For every prime ideal $\mathfrak{p} \subset A$, the dimension formula holds:
     $$
     \operatorname{ht}(\mathfrak{p}) + \dim(A/\mathfrak{p}) = \dim(A).
     $$
   - Every maximal ideal $\mathfrak{m} \subset A$ has $\operatorname{ht}(\mathfrak{m}) = \dim(A)$.

3. **Dimension Formula for Affine Rings over $\mathbf{Z}$:**
   Let $A$ be a finitely generated domain over $\mathbf{Z}$ **of characteristic zero**, and let $r = \operatorname{tr.deg}_{\mathbf{Q}}(\operatorname{Frac}(A))$.
   - $\dim(A) = r + 1$.
   - The ring $\mathbf{Z}$ is universally catenary, so $A$ is catenary.
   - All maximal chains of prime ideals from $\{0\}$ to a maximal ideal have length $r + 1$.

For the field case, the dimension formula gives $\operatorname{ht}(\mathfrak p)=r-\operatorname{tr.deg}_k\kappa(\mathfrak p)$. Zariski's lemma makes the residue field at a maximal ideal finite over $k$, so every maximal ideal has height $r$; applying the same argument to $A/\mathfrak p$ proves the quotient formula. For the integer case, every maximal residue field is a finite field, and the dimension formula gives height $1+r$. The residue-field argument and the subsequent use of catenarity are detailed in [[02 - Ring Theory/Exercises/Exercise R279 - Dimension and Catenarity of Affine Domains over a Field|Lang IX.18]] and [[02 - Ring Theory/Exercises/Exercise R280 - Dimension and Catenarity of Affine Domains over the Integers|Lang IX.19]].

## Examples

> [!example] Polynomial Rings
> - For a field $k$, $\dim(k[X_1, \ldots, X_n]) = n$.
> - For the ring of integers $\mathbf{Z}$, $\dim(\mathbf{Z}) = 1$ (the only non-zero primes are $(p)$ of height 1).
> - For the polynomial ring over $\mathbf{Z}$, $\dim(\mathbf{Z}[X_1, \ldots, X_n]) = n + 1$. A maximal chain is
>   $$
>   \{0\} \subsetneq (X_1) \subsetneq (X_1, X_2) \subsetneq \cdots \subsetneq (X_1, \ldots, X_n) \subsetneq (p, X_1, \ldots, X_n).
>   $$
