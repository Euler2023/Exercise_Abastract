# Orders and Conductors

## Definitions

Let $L$ be a number field of degree $n$ and let $\mathcal O_L$ be its ring of algebraic integers.

> [!info] Order
> An **order** in $L$ is a subring $A\subseteq\mathcal O_L$ containing $1$ whose additive group is a free rank-$n$ integer lattice. Equivalently, $[\mathcal O_L:A]$ is finite. If $\theta$ is integral and $L=\mathbb Q(\theta)$, then $\mathbb Z[\theta]$ is an order with basis $1,\theta,\ldots,\theta^{n-1}$.

> [!info] Conductor
> The **conductor** of $A$ in $\mathcal O_L$ is
> $$
> \mathfrak f_A=\{c\in\mathcal O_L:c\mathcal O_L\subseteq A\}.
> $$
> It is the largest $\mathcal O_L$-ideal contained in $A$. An ideal $\mathfrak a$ is relatively prime to the conductor when $\mathfrak f_A+\mathfrak a=\mathcal O_L$.

> [!info] Monogeneity and a power integral basis
> The ring $\mathcal O_L$ is **monogenic** if $\mathcal O_L=\mathbb Z[\theta]$ for some integral $\theta$. Then the powers $1,\theta,\ldots,\theta^{n-1}$ form an integer basis. This is stronger than being a primitive element of the field, which requires only $L=\mathbb Q(\theta)$.

## Intuition

A rational power basis allows rational coefficients. An integral basis must express every algebraic integer using integer coefficients. An order can span the whole field while missing some integers. The conductor measures which multipliers send the entire integer ring back into that smaller order.

For Neukirch's more general notation, replace $\mathbb Z$ by a Dedekind domain $\mathfrak o$, $\mathbb Q$ by its fraction field $K$, and $\mathcal O_L$ by the integral closure $\mathcal O$ in a finite extension $L/K$. The formula $\{c\in\mathcal O:c\mathcal O\subseteq\mathfrak o[\theta]\}$ is still the conductor. The finite integer-lattice assertions above are stated specifically for number fields.

## Key Properties

1. **Largest contained ideal.** The conductor is closed under addition and multiplication by $\mathcal O_L$, and $c\in\mathfrak f_A$ implies $c=c\cdot1\in A$. If an $\mathcal O_L$-ideal $I$ lies in $A$, then $c\mathcal O_L\subseteq I\subseteq A$ for every $c\in I$, so $I\subseteq\mathfrak f_A$.
2. **Nonzero conductor.** If $m=[\mathcal O_L:A]$, multiplication by $m$ kills the finite additive quotient. Thus $m\mathcal O_L\subseteq A$, and then $m\mathcal O_L\subseteq\mathfrak f_A$. The conductor is the unit ideal exactly when $A=\mathcal O_L$.
3. **Discriminant and index.** For a number field, $\operatorname{Tr}_{L/\mathbb Q}(z)=\sum_\sigma\sigma(z)$, where the sum runs over its $n$ embeddings into $\mathbb C$. If $a_1,\ldots,a_n$ is a basis of $A$, define $\operatorname{disc}(A)=\det(\operatorname{Tr}_{L/\mathbb Q}(a_i a_j))$. Changing from an integer basis of $\mathcal O_L$ gives

$$
\operatorname{disc}(A)=[\mathcal O_L:A]^2\operatorname{disc}(\mathcal O_L).
$$

The coordinate matrix has determinant of absolute value equal to the index, and the trace matrix changes by multiplication on the left and right by that matrix and its transpose. In particular, a square-free order discriminant forces $A=\mathcal O_L$.

4. **A quotient obstruction.** If $A=\mathbb Z[\theta]$ and $\mathfrak f_A+\mathfrak a=\mathcal O_L$, reduction of $A$ surjects onto $\mathcal O_L/\mathfrak a$: choose $c\in\mathfrak f_A$ congruent to $1$ modulo $\mathfrak a$, and represent the residue of $z$ by $cz\in A$. For $\mathfrak a=p\mathcal O_L$, the quotient must therefore be generated by $\bar\theta$ as an $\mathbb F_p$-algebra. This is a necessary condition; no general converse is asserted here.

## Examples

> [!example] A proper quadratic order
> In $L=\mathbb Q(\sqrt5)$, write $\omega=(1+\sqrt5)/2$. Then $\mathcal O_L=\mathbb Z[\omega]$, while $A=\mathbb Z[\sqrt5]=\mathbb Z+2\mathbb Z\omega$ has index $2$. Its conductor is $2\mathcal O_L$. Indeed $2\mathcal O_L\subseteq A$; conversely, for $c=a+b\omega$, membership of $c$ and $c\omega$ in $A$, together with $\omega^2=\omega+1$, forces $b$ and $a+b$ even, hence both $a,b$ even. The field generator $\sqrt5$ does not give a power integral basis, although the different generator $\omega$ does.

> [!example] A number field with no power integral basis
> Let $\gamma^3-\gamma^2-2\gamma-8=0$. A complete calculation in [[02 - Ring Theory/Exercises/Exercise R329 - A Counterexample to Prescribed Coprime Conductors|Exercise R329]] gives
> $$
> \mathcal O_{\mathbb Q(\gamma)}
> =\mathbb Z\oplus\mathbb Z\gamma\oplus\mathbb Z\frac{\gamma^2-\gamma}{2},
> \qquad \mathcal O_{\mathbb Q(\gamma)}/2\mathcal O_{\mathbb Q(\gamma)}\simeq\mathbb F_2^3.
> $$
> Every element of the quotient is idempotent, so the algebra generated by any one element has dimension at most $2$. Therefore the quotient is not generated by one element. The full integer ring cannot be monogenic, and no primitive element's conductor can be relatively prime to $2\mathcal O_L$. This does not contradict the finite-separable primitive element theorem for the field.
