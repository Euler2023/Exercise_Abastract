# Exercise G246: The Commutator Subgroup Is Characteristic

## Problem Statement

> [!question] Exercise 10.7
> Prove that the commutator subgroup $[G,G]$ is characteristic and that $G/[G,G]$ is abelian.

> [!question] Lang Exercise 3
> A commutator is an element $aba^{-1}b^{-1}$. Let $G^c$ be the subgroup generated by all commutators. Show that $G^c$ is normal and that every homomorphism from $G$ to an abelian group factors through $G/G^c$.

## Hints

> [!hint]- Hint 1
> Automorphisms carry commutators to commutators.

## Solution

> [!success]- Solution
> For an automorphism $\alpha$, $\alpha([x,y])=[\alpha(x),\alpha(y)]$, so $\alpha([G,G])\subseteq[G,G]$; applying $\alpha^{-1}$ gives equality. Hence $[G,G]$ is characteristic, and therefore normal. In the quotient every commutator is trivial, so all cosets commute. Conversely any normal subgroup with abelian quotient contains every commutator, making $[G,G]$ the smallest such subgroup.
>
> If $f:G\to A$ and $A$ is abelian, then $f([x,y])=1$ for every commutator, so $[G,G]\subseteq\ker f$. The quotient universal property gives a unique homomorphism $\bar f:G/[G,G]\to A$ with $f=\bar f\circ\pi$. This is Lang's factorization assertion. $\square$
