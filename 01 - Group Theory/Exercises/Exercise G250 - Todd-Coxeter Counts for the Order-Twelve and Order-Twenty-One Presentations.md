# Exercise G250: Todd-Coxeter Counts for the Order-Twelve and Order-Twenty-One Presentations

## Problem Statement

> [!question] Exercise 11.2
> Use Todd–Coxeter to show that (7.8.2) defines a group of order $12$ and (7.7.8) one of order $21$.

## Hints

> [!hint]- Hint 1
> Enumerate cosets of the cyclic subgroup generated by the large-order generator.

## Solution

> [!success]- Solution
> For (7.8.2),
>
> $$
> G_{12}=\langle x,y:x^4=y^3=1, xy=y^2x\rangle.
> $$
>
> Enumeration over $\langle x\rangle$ closes with three cosets, so $|G_{12}|\le4\cdot3=12$. The matrices in Exercise 8.2 generate a quotient with $12$ elements, hence equality.
>
> For (7.7.8),
>
> $$
> G_{21}=\langle x,y:x^7=y^3=1, yx=x^2y\rangle.
> $$
>
> Enumeration over $\langle x\rangle$ again closes with three cosets, giving $|G_{21}|\le21$. The affine permutations $x:t\mapsto t+1$, $y:t\mapsto2t$ on $\mathbb F_7$ satisfy the relations and generate $21$ elements, so $|G_{21}|=21$. $\square$
