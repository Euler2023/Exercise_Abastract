# Exercise G268: Factorial Lattice Semigroups in Z Squared

## Problem Statement

> [!question] Exercise M.2
> Let $v_1,\ldots,v_n$ be elements of $\mathbb Z^2$, and let $S$ be the semigroup of all combinations
>
> $$
> a_1v_1+\cdots+a_nv_n
> $$
>
> with nonnegative integer coefficients $a_i$, the law of composition being addition. Determine which of these semigroups has unique factorization **(a)** when the coordinates of the vectors $v_i$ are nonnegative, and **(b)** in general.
>
> *Hint:* Begin by translating the terminology of §12.2.1 into additive notation.

## Hints

> [!hint]- Hint 1
> In additive notation, the identity is $0$, a unit is an element $u$ for which $-u\in S$, and associates differ by a unit. First pass to the reduced monoid $S/U$, where $U=S\cap(-S)$.

> [!hint]- Hint 2
> Let $H$ be the set of irreducible classes of $S/U$. It generates $S/U$. Factorization is unique exactly when the elements of $H$ are linearly independent in the group generated by $S/U$.

## Solution

> [!success]- Solution
> Write
>
> $$
> G=\langle S\rangle_{\mathbb Z}\subseteq\mathbb Z^2,
> \qquad
> U=S\cap(-S).
> $$
>
> Here $U$ is precisely the additive group of units of $S$: $u$ is a unit exactly when $u+(-u)=0$ with both terms in $S$. Two elements are associates exactly when their difference lies in $U$. Let
>
> $$
> \overline S=S/U\subseteq G/U
> $$
>
> and let $H$ be the set of nonzero irreducible elements of the reduced monoid $\overline S$.
>
> Because $S$ is finitely generated, so is $\overline S$. Its rational cone is pointed: any line in that cone lifts, after clearing rational denominators, to units and therefore disappears modulo $U$. Hence there is a linear functional strictly positive on every nonzero generator of $\overline S$. This functional bounds the length of every decomposition, so factoring terminates. Moreover, every element of $H$ is represented by one of the original generators; therefore $H$ is finite and generates $\overline S$.
>
> There is consequently a surjective homomorphism
>
> $$
> \Phi:\mathbb N^{H}\longrightarrow\overline S,
> \qquad
> (n_h)_{h\in H}\longmapsto\sum_{h\in H}n_hh.
> $$
>
> The semigroup $S$ has unique factorization if and only if $\Phi$ is injective. This is equivalent to the classes in $H$ being linearly independent over $\mathbb Z$ in $G/U$: an integral relation can be separated into its positive and negative parts to give two different factorizations, and two different factorizations give such a relation. Thus the complete criterion is
>
> $$
> \boxed{S\text{ is factorial}\iff H\text{ is }\mathbb Z\text{-linearly independent in }G/U.}
> $$
>
> **(a) Nonnegative coordinates.** If every $v_i$ has nonnegative coordinates, then $S\subseteq\mathbb N^2$ and $U=\{0\}$. Delete the zero vector, identify duplicates, and repeatedly delete every generator that is a nonnegative integer combination of the others. The remaining set is exactly the irreducible set $H$. Unique factorization holds precisely in the following cases:
>
> 1. $H=\varnothing$, so $S=\{0\}$;
> 2. $H=\{w\}$, so $S=\mathbb Nw$;
> 3. $H=\{w_1,w_2\}$ and $w_1,w_2$ are linearly independent, equivalently $\det(w_1,w_2)\neq0$.
>
> Any three lattice vectors are linearly dependent over $\mathbb Z$. Two distinct collinear irreducibles also satisfy a positive integer relation $rw_1=sw_2$. Either situation gives two different factorizations, so there are no other cases.
>
> **(b) General coordinates.** Compute $U=S\cap(-S)$, pass all $v_i$ to $G/U$, discard the zero classes and the decomposable classes, and call the remaining irreducible classes $H$. Then the boxed independence criterion is necessary and sufficient. Equivalently,
>
> $$
> S\cong U\oplus\mathbb N^r,
> \qquad
> r=|H|,
> \qquad
> \operatorname{rank}U+r\le2.
> $$
>
> Concretely, the possibilities are: a subgroup $U\le\mathbb Z^2$ (all elements are units); a free ray $\mathbb Nw$; a free two-generator cone $\mathbb Nw_1+\mathbb Nw_2$ with nonzero determinant; or a rank-one unit group together with one independent nonunit direction, $U\oplus\mathbb Nw$. The direct sums are understood inside the group $G$ generated by $S$, not necessarily as the standard coordinate axes of $\mathbb Z^2$.

## Notes

- **Routing rationale:** Although the generators lie in $\mathbb Z^2$, the decisive operations are units, associates, irreducibles, and the group completion of a commutative monoid; the note therefore follows the existing semigroup sequence in Group Theory.
- **Generator test:** The original list $v_1,\ldots,v_n$ need not itself be minimal. The criterion applies only after passing to associate classes and removing decomposable generators.
- **Vacuous case:** If $S=U$ is a group, it has no nonunit elements; the existence and uniqueness clauses are therefore vacuously true.
- **Source status:** The problem and hint are from [S1, Ch. 12, Misc. Problems, Ex. M.2, printed p. 381, PDF p. 393]. The quotient-by-units classification and proof are independent.
