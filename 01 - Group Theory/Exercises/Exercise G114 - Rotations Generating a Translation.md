# Exercise G114: Rotations Generating a Translation

## Problem Statement

> [!question] Exercise 5.10
> Let $f$ and $g$ be rotations of the plane about distinct points, with arbitrary nonzero angles of rotation $\theta$ and $\phi$. Prove that the group generated by $f$ and $g$ contains a translation.

## Hints

> [!hint]- Hint 1
> The orthogonal parts of plane rotations commute. Examine the orthogonal part of the commutator $fgf^{-1}g^{-1}$.

> [!hint]- Hint 2
> If the commutator were the identity, the two rotations would commute. Use uniqueness of the fixed point of a nontrivial rotation.

## Solution

> [!success]- Solution
> Write the orthogonal parts of $f$ and $g$ as $R_\theta$ and $R_\phi$. Since all planar rotations about the origin commute, the commutator
>
> $$
> c=fgf^{-1}g^{-1}
> $$
>
> has orthogonal part
>
> $$
> R_\theta R_\phi R_\theta^{-1}R_\phi^{-1}=I.
> $$
>
> Therefore $c$ is a translation.
>
> It remains to show that this translation is nontrivial. If $c=1$, then $fg=gf$. Let $p$ be the center of $f$. Commutativity gives
>
> $$
> f(g(p))=g(f(p))=g(p),
> $$
>
> so $g(p)$ is fixed by $f$. Because $f$ has nonzero angle, its only fixed point is $p$, and hence $g(p)=p$. A nontrivial rotation $g$ also has a unique fixed point, so $p$ would be the center of $g$, contrary to the assumption that the centers are distinct.
>
> Thus $c$ is a nonidentity translation contained in $\langle f,g\rangle$. $\square$
