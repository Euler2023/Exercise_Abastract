# Exercise G278: Finite-Order Elements of GL2(Z)

## Problem Statement

> [!question] Miscellaneous Exercise *M.5
> Prove that any element of $GL_2(\mathbb Z)$ of finite order has order $1$, $2$, $3$, $4$, or $6$
>
> **(a)** by using field theory.
>
> **(b)** by applying the Crystallographic Restriction.

## Hints

> [!hint]- Hint 1
> In (a), the eigenvalues are roots of unity whose minimal polynomials over $\mathbb Q$ have degree at most $2$.
>
> [!hint]- Hint 2
> In (b), average an inner product over the finite cyclic group generated by the matrix.

## Solution

> [!success]- Solution
> Let $A\in GL_2(\mathbb Z)$ have finite order.
>
> **(a) Field theory.** Since $A^m=I$ for some $m$, its minimal polynomial divides $x^m-1$, which has no repeated roots in characteristic zero. Thus $A$ is diagonalizable over $\mathbb C$, and its eigenvalues are roots of unity. Any nonrational eigenvalue $\lambda$ has degree at most $2$ over $\mathbb Q$, so if its order is $n$, then $\varphi(n)\le2$. Hence $n\in\{3,4,6\}$; the rational roots of unity give $n=1,2$. If both eigenvalues are rational, diagonalizability makes the order at most $2$; if they are nonreal, they are a conjugate pair of the same order. Therefore the order of $A$ belongs to
>
> $$
> \boxed{\{1,2,3,4,6\}}.
> $$
>
> **(b) Crystallographic restriction.** Average the usual inner product over the finite group $\langle A\rangle$. Relative to the resulting positive-definite inner product, $A$ is an isometry preserving the lattice $\mathbb Z^2$. If $\det A=-1$, a finite-order plane isometry fixing the origin is a reflection and has order $2$.
>
> If $\det A=1$, it is a rotation through an angle $\theta$, and
>
> $$
> 2\cos\theta=\operatorname{tr}A\in\mathbb Z.
> $$
>
> Since $|2\cos\theta|\le2$, the trace is $-2,-1,0,1$, or $2$. These values correspond to rotation orders $2,3,4,6$, and $1$, respectively. This is the two-dimensional crystallographic restriction.
