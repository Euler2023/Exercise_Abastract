# Exercise G249: Completing the Collapse in Example 7.11.8

## Problem Statement

> [!question] Exercise 11.1
> For $G=\langle x,y:x^3=y^3=yxyxy=1\rangle$, complete the proof that $G\cong C_3$.

## Hints

> [!hint]- Hint 1
> The coset table gives $G=\langle y\rangle$; still show that $y$ has order exactly $3$.

## Solution

> [!success]- Solution
> The Todd–Coxeter coincidences in Example 7.11.8 leave one coset of $H=\langle y\rangle$, so $G=H$ and $x\in H$. Directly, the relator implies both $y^2xy=x^{-1}$ and $yxy^2=x^{-1}$; hence $xy=yx$. Then $1=yxyxy=x^2y^3=x^2$, and with $x^3=1$ this gives $x=1$. Thus $G$ is generated by $y$ with $y^3=1$. The assignment $x\mapsto1$, $y\mapsto\bar1\in C_3$ respects all relators, so $y\ne1$ in the presented group. Therefore $G\cong C_3$. $\square$
