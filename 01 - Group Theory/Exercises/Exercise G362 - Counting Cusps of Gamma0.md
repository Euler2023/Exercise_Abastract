# Exercise G362: Counting Cusps of Gamma0

## Problem Statement

> [!question] Original Exercise 3.8.4
> Confirm the calculations that count the cusps of $\Gamma_0(N)$.
>
> The section obtains $\varepsilon_\infty(\Gamma_0(N))=\sum_{d\mid N}\varphi(\gcd(d,N/d))$ from the primitive-column equivalence
> $$
> (ya',c')\equiv(a+jc,yc)\pmod N\quad\text{for some }j\in\mathbb Z,\ y\in(\mathbb Z/N\mathbb Z)^\times.
> $$

## Hints

> [!hint]- Hint 1
> The unit orbit of a denominator is determined by $d=\gcd(c,N)$. Divide denominators by $d$ before inverting.

> [!hint]- Hint 2
> For a fixed denominator the remaining numerator modulus is $\gcd(d,N/d)$. Check that the required multiplier can still be a unit.

## Solution

> [!success]- Solution
> **Proof scope:** Independent derivation.
>
> First, $c'\equiv yc\pmod N$ with $y$ a unit implies $\gcd(c',N)=\gcd(c,N)$. Conversely let both gcds equal $d$ and put $M=N/d$, $c=dt$, $c'=dt'$. Then $t,t'$ are units modulo $M$, and the congruence is $y\equiv y_0=t't^{-1}\pmod M$. This unit class modulo $M$ lifts to a unit modulo $N$: at primes dividing $N$ but not $M$, choose a nonzero residue separately by the Chinese remainder theorem.
>
> Fix such a lower residue $c$ for each $d$. The top equation is $ya'\equiv a\pmod d$, because the subgroup of $\mathbb Z/N\mathbb Z$ generated by $c$ consists exactly of the multiples of $d$. Primitivity implies $a,a'$ are units modulo $d$. Hence the simultaneous requirements on $y$ are
> $$
> y\equiv y_0\pmod M,\qquad y\equiv a(a')^{-1}\pmod d.
> $$
> The generalized Chinese remainder theorem says these are solvable exactly when $a\equiv y_0a'\pmod h$, where $h=\gcd(d,M)$. Every resulting residue modulo $\operatorname{lcm}(d,M)$ is a unit: each prime dividing $N$ divides at least one of $d,M$, and each prescribed class is a unit on its modulus. It therefore lifts to a unit modulo $N$, proving sufficiency without discarding the unit requirement.
>
> For fixed $c$ itself, take $c'=c$ and $y_0=1$. The cusp classes are exactly numerator classes $a\pmod h$ relatively prime to $h$. Each is attainable by an $a$ relatively prime to $d$: prescribe nonzero residues at any primes of $d$ absent from $h$. The resulting column has a primitive lift, as in Lemma3.8.4. Thus there are $\varphi(h)$ classes for this $d$. Distinct gcds of denominators cannot mix, so summing gives
> $$
> \varepsilon_\infty(\Gamma_0(N))=\sum_{d\mid N}\varphi(\gcd(d,N/d)).
> $$
