# Exercise G187: Fixed-Point-Free Plane Crystallographic Groups

## Problem Statement

> [!question] Miscellaneous Exercise M.1
> Let $G$ be a two-dimensional crystallographic group such that no element $g\ne1$ fixes any point of the plane. Prove that $G$ is generated by two translations, or else by one translation and one glide.

## Hints

> [!hint]- Hint 1
> A nonidentity orientation-preserving plane isometry that is not a translation is a rotation and has a fixed point.

> [!hint]- Hint 2
> A fixed-point-free orientation-reversing plane isometry is a glide reflection. Its square is a translation along its axis.

## Solution

> [!success]- Solution
> Let $L$ be the rank-two translation lattice and $\overline G$ the point group. The hypothesis excludes every nonidentity rotation and every reflection, because these have fixed points.
>
> If $\overline G$ is trivial, then $G=L\cong\mathbb Z^2$, so a lattice basis gives two translation generators.
>
> Otherwise the point group contains an orientation-reversing element. Since the product of two distinct orientation-reversing point-group elements is a nontrivial rotation, the point group must be $D_1=\{1,\overline r\}$. A lift $g$ of $\overline r$ cannot be a reflection, so it is a glide. Choose its axis as the $x$-direction. Then $g^2=t_a$ is the primitive translation along that axis after replacing $g$ by a suitable lattice translate.
>
> The invariant lattice has a second generator $b$ transverse to the axis. Multiplying $b$ by a lattice-equivalent choice if necessary, every element of $G$ is
>
> $$
> t_b^m g^k,qquad m,k\in\mathbb Z.
> $$
>
> Thus $G$ is generated by the translation $t_b$ and the glide $g$. These are precisely the torsion-free wallpaper types $p1$ and $pg$. $\square$
