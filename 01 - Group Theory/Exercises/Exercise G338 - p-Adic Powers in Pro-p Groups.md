# Exercise G338: p-Adic Powers in Pro-p Groups

## Problem Statement

> [!question] Neukirch IV.2.3
> A pro-$p$-group is a profinite group $G$ whose quotients $G/N$, modulo all open normal subgroups $N$, are finite $p$-groups. Imitating exercise 1, make sense of the powers $\sigma^a$, for all $\sigma\in G$ and $a\in\mathbb Z_p$.

## Hints

> [!hint]- Hint 1
> The exponent of a finite $p$-group is a power of $p$, so only $a\bmod p^m$ is needed, rather than a full profinite integer.

> [!hint]- Hint 2
> After defining the coordinates in finite quotients, check compatibility with quotient maps and pass the integer exponent laws for a fixed element to the limit.

## Solution

> [!success]- Solution
> **Proof scope:** Independent derivation
>
> For each open normal subgroup $N$, write $\exp(G/N)=p^{m_N}$. Define
> $$
> (\sigma^a)_N=(\sigma N)^{a\bmod p^{m_N}}.
> $$
> Maps from finer finite quotients to coarser ones preserve integer powers. The coordinates are therefore compatible and specify a unique element $\sigma^a$ of $G$. Each coordinate depends on finite data, so $(\sigma,a)\mapsto\sigma^a$ is jointly continuous and extends integer powers. Density of $\mathbb Z$ in $\mathbb Z_p$ makes this extension unique.
>
> Checking in each finite quotient gives $(\sigma^a)^b=\sigma^{ab}$ and $\sigma^{a+b}=\sigma^a\sigma^b$. Thus $a\mapsto\sigma^a$ is a continuous homomorphism from $\mathbb Z_p$, with image the closed cyclic subgroup $\overline{\langle\sigma\rangle}$. Its kernel is a closed ideal $p^m\mathbb Z_p$ in the finite-order case, or zero in the infinite-order case. A pro-$p$ group generated by one element is therefore a finite cyclic $p$-group or $\mathbb Z_p$. If $G$ is abelian, then $(\sigma\tau)^a=\sigma^a\tau^a$, making $G$ a topological $\mathbb Z_p$-module.
