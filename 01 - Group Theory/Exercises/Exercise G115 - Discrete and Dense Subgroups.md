# Exercise G115: Discrete and Dense Subgroups

## Problem Statement

> [!question] Exercise 5.11
> If $S$ and $S'$ are subsets of $\mathbb R^n$ with $S\subseteq S'$, then $S$ is dense in $S'$ if for every element $s'$ of $S'$, there are elements of $S$ arbitrarily near to $s'$.
>
> **(a)** Prove that a subgroup $\Gamma$ of the additive group $\mathbb R^+$ is either dense in $\mathbb R$, or else discrete.
>
> **(b)** Prove that the subgroup of $\mathbb R^+$ generated by $1$ and $\sqrt2$ is dense in $\mathbb R^+$.
>
> **(c)** Let $H$ be a subgroup of the group $G$ of angles. Prove that $H$ is either a cyclic subgroup of $G$ or else it is dense in $G$.

## Hints

> [!hint]- Hint 1
> For part (a), take the infimum of the positive elements of $\Gamma$.

> [!hint]- Hint 2
> For part (b), use the pigeonhole principle on the fractional parts of $0,\sqrt2,\ldots,N\sqrt2$.

> [!hint]- Hint 3
> For part (c), take the inverse image of $H$ under $\mathbb R\to\mathbb R/(2\pi\mathbb Z)$.

## Solution

> [!success]- Solution
> ### (a) Subgroups of the additive real line
> Suppose $\Gamma\ne\{0\}$ and set
>
> $$
> \alpha=\inf\{\gamma>0:\gamma\in\Gamma\}.
> $$
>
> If $\alpha=0$, then every interval contains an element of $\Gamma$: choose $0<\gamma$ smaller than the interval length and take a suitable integral multiple of $\gamma$. Hence $\Gamma$ is dense.
>
> If $\alpha>0$, then $\alpha\in\Gamma$. Indeed, choose $\gamma\in\Gamma$ with $\alpha\le\gamma<2\alpha$. If $\gamma>\alpha$, choose $\delta\in\Gamma$ with $\alpha\le\delta<\gamma$; then $0<\gamma-\delta<\alpha$, a contradiction. Division by $\alpha$ now shows that every $x\in\Gamma$ is an integral multiple of $\alpha$. Thus $\Gamma=\alpha\mathbb Z$, which is discrete.
>
> ### (b) The subgroup generated by $1$ and $\sqrt2$
> Partition $[0,1)$ into $N$ equal intervals and consider the $N+1$ fractional parts of
>
> $$
> 0,\sqrt2,2\sqrt2,\ldots,N\sqrt2.
> $$
>
> Two lie in the same interval, so there are integers $m,k$, not both zero, with
>
> $$
> 0<|m\sqrt2-k|<\frac1N.
> $$
>
> The number is nonzero because $\sqrt2$ is irrational, and it belongs to $\mathbb Z+\mathbb Z\sqrt2$. Hence this subgroup has arbitrarily small positive elements and is dense by part (a).
>
> ### (c) Subgroups of the angle group
> Identify the angle group with $G=\mathbb R/(2\pi\mathbb Z)$ and let $\widetilde H$ be the inverse image of $H$ in $\mathbb R$. Then $2\pi\mathbb Z\subseteq\widetilde H$. By part (a), either $\widetilde H$ is dense, in which case $H$ is dense in $G$, or $\widetilde H=\alpha\mathbb Z$. In the latter case $2\pi=n\alpha$ for some positive integer $n$, and the image of $\alpha$ generates $H$. Thus $H$ is cyclic. $\square$
