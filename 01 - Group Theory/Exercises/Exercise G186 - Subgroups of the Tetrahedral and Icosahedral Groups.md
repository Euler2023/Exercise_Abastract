# Exercise G186: Subgroups of the Tetrahedral and Icosahedral Groups

## Problem Statement

> [!question] Exercise 12.6
> Determine all subgroups of (a) the tetrahedral group, (b) the icosahedral group.

## Hints

> [!hint]- Hint 1
> Use $T\cong A_4$ and $I\cong A_5$, then classify by element orders and Sylow subgroups.

> [!hint]- Hint 2
> For $A_5$, include normalizers of $C_3$ and $C_5$, point stabilizers $A_4$, and Klein four-groups.

## Solution

> [!success]- Solution
> **(a)** In $T\cong A_4$, the subgroup types are
>
> $$
> \{1\},quad C_2,quad C_3,quad V_4,quad A_4.
> $$
>
> There are three subgroups $C_2$, four subgroups $C_3$, and one $V_4$. Lagrange's theorem and the absence of an order-six subgroup make this complete.
>
> **(b)** In $I\cong A_5$, the subgroup types are
>
> $$
> \{1\},quad C_2,quad C_3,quad C_5,quad V_4,quad D_3,quad D_5,quad A_4,quad A_5.
> $$
>
> These all occur: cyclic groups are generated by rotations; $V_4$ consists of the three half-turns associated with an inscribed tetrahedron; $D_3$ and $D_5$ are normalizers of threefold and fivefold rotation axes; and $A_4$ stabilizes an inscribed tetrahedron (equivalently a point in the natural five-letter model of $A_5$).
>
> For completeness, let $H<A_5$. Its order divides $60$. Sylow counting inside $H$, together with the fact that $A_5$ has elements only of orders $1,2,3,5$, eliminates orders $15,20,30$ and the remaining unsupported divisors. If $|H|$ is divisible by two different odd primes, the Sylow counts force $H=A_5$; with one odd prime, its normalizer places $H$ in $D_3,D_5$, or $A_4$. The pure $2$-subgroups are $C_2$ or $V_4$. Thus the list is exhaustive. $\square$
