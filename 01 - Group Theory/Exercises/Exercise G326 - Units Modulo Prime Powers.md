# Exercise G326: Units Modulo Prime Powers

## Problem Statement

> [!question] Exercise 8
> Let $p$ be a prime number, and let
>
> $$
> A=\mathbb Z/p^r\mathbb Z,
> \qquad r\ge1.
> $$
>
> Let $G=A^\times$ be the group of units, that is, the group of integers prime to $p$ modulo $p^r$. Show that $G$ is cyclic except when $p=2$ and $r\ge3$, in which case it is of type $(2,2^{r-2})$.

## Hints

> [!hint]- Source hint for odd $p$
> Show that $G$ is the product of the cyclic group generated by $1+p$ and a cyclic group of order $p-1$.

> [!hint]- Source hint for $p=2$
> In the exceptional case, show that $G$ is the product of $\{\pm1\}$ with the cyclic group generated by the residue class of $5$ modulo $2^r$.

## Solution

> [!success]- Solution
> First suppose that $p$ is odd. Reduction modulo $p$ gives an exact sequence
>
> $$
> 1\longrightarrow U_1\longrightarrow G
> \longrightarrow(\mathbb Z/p\mathbb Z)^\times\longrightarrow1,
> $$
>
> where $U_1$ consists of the classes congruent to $1$ modulo $p$ and has order $p^{r-1}$.
>
> Write $v_p(n)$ for the exponent of $p$ in a nonzero integer $n$. A binomial induction gives
>
> $$
> v_p\left((1+p)^{p^k}-1\right)=k+1
> \qquad(k\ge0).
> $$
>
> Indeed, if $x=1+cp^{k+1}$ with $p\nmid c$, then the binomial expansion of $x^p$ has first nonconstant term $cp^{k+2}$ and every later term has higher $p$-adic valuation. It follows that $1+p$ has order $p^{r-1}$ modulo $p^r$, so
>
> $$
> U_1=\langle1+p\rangle.
> $$
>
> Choose an integer $g$ whose class modulo $p$ generates $(\mathbb Z/p\mathbb Z)^\times$, and put $h=g^{p^{r-1}}$ modulo $p^r$. Euler's theorem gives $h^{p-1}=1$, while the reduction of $h$ still generates $(\mathbb Z/p\mathbb Z)^\times$ because $p^{r-1}$ is coprime to $p-1$. Thus $h$ has order $p-1$. The subgroups $\langle1+p\rangle$ and $\langle h\rangle$ have coprime orders and trivial intersection, and their orders multiply to
>
> $$
> p^{r-1}(p-1)=|G|.
> $$
>
> Hence
>
> $$
> G\cong C_{p^{r-1}}\times C_{p-1}.
> $$
>
> Since the two orders are coprime, this product is cyclic.
>
> Now take $p=2$. For $r=1$, the unit group is trivial, and for $r=2$ it is $\{1,3\}\cong C_2$. Suppose $r\ge3$. We claim that the order of $5$ modulo $2^r$ is $2^{r-2}$. The identity
>
> $$
> 5^{2^{k+1}}-1=left(5^{2^k}-1\right)\left(5^{2^k}+1\right)
> $$
>
> and the congruence $5^{2^k}\equiv1\pmod4$ show inductively that
>
> $$
> v_2\left(5^{2^k}-1\right)=k+2.
> $$
>
> Therefore $\langle5\rangle$ has order $2^{r-2}$. Every power of $5$ is congruent to $1$ modulo $4$, and there are exactly $2^{r-2}$ unit classes with that congruence, so $\langle5\rangle$ is precisely that subgroup. Every odd residue is congruent to either $1$ or $-1$ modulo $4$, and hence belongs to exactly one of $\langle5\rangle$ and $-\langle5\rangle$. Since $-1\notin\langle5\rangle$,
>
> $$
> G=\{\pm1\}\times\langle5\rangle
> \cong C_2\times C_{2^{r-2}}.
> $$
>
> This is the exceptional type $(2,2^{r-2})$ stated in the exercise.

- **Routing rationale:** The main task is to classify the finite multiplicative group $A^\times$ as cyclic or as a direct product, so the primary computational toolkit is Group Theory even though the group comes from a ring.
- **Proof boundary:** The cyclicity of $(\mathbb Z/p\mathbb Z)^\times$ is used as a previously established finite-field result.
