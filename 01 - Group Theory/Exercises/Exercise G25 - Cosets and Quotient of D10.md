# Exercise G25: Cosets and Quotient of D10

## Problem Statement

> [!question] Exercise
> Let
> $$D_{10}=\langle x,y\mid x^{10}=1,\ y^2=1,\ yxy=x^{-1}\rangle$$
> and $H=\{1,x^5\}$.
>
> **(a)** Compute the left cosets of $H$.
>
> **(b)** Prove that $H$ is normal and that $D_{10}/H\cong D_5$.
>
> **(c)** Determine whether $D_{10}\cong D_5\times H$.

## Hints

> [!hint]- Hint 1
> Pair each exponent $k$ with $k+5$.

> [!hint]- Hint 2
> The element $x^5$ is central because $yx^5y^{-1}=x^{-5}=x^5$.

> [!hint]- Hint 3
> For part (c), consider the subgroup $K=\langle x^2,y\rangle$ and show that $D_{10}=KH$ with $K\cap H=\{1\}$.

## Solution

> [!success]- Solution
> ### (a) Left cosets
> The five cosets represented by rotations are
> $$x^kH=\{x^k,x^{k+5}\},\qquad 0\le k\le4.$$
> Explicitly,
> $$
> \begin{aligned}
> H&=\{1,x^5\},\\
> xH&=\{x,x^6\},\\
> x^2H&=\{x^2,x^7\},\\
> x^3H&=\{x^3,x^8\},\\
> x^4H&=\{x^4,x^9\}.
> \end{aligned}
> $$
> The five cosets represented by reflections are
> $$x^kyH=\{x^ky,x^{k+5}y\},\qquad0\le k\le4.$$
> These ten two-element cosets account for all $20$ elements of $D_{10}$.
>
> ### (b) Normality and the quotient
> The element $x^5$ commutes with $x$, and
> $$yx^5y^{-1}=x^{-5}=x^5$$
> because $x^{10}=1$. Thus $x^5$ is central, so $H\triangleleft D_{10}$.
>
> In the quotient, $xH$ has order $5$, $yH$ has order $2$, and
> $$(yH)(xH)(yH)=(xH)^{-1}.$$
> These two cosets generate the quotient, which has order
> $$|D_{10}/H|=20/2=10.$$
> Therefore the quotient has the standard presentation of the dihedral group of order $10$:
> $$D_{10}/H\cong D_5.$$
>
> ### (c) Direct-product decomposition
> Yes. Let
> $$K=\langle x^2,y\rangle.$$
> Since $x^2$ has order $5$ and $y$ inverts it, $K\cong D_5$ and $|K|=10$. Also,
> $$K\cap H=\{1\},$$
> because the only rotations in $K$ are even powers of $x$, while $x^5$ is an odd power.
>
> The subgroup $H$ is central, so every element of $K$ commutes with every element of $H$. Moreover,
> $$x=(x^2)^3x^5\in KH,$$
> and $y\in K$, so $KH=D_{10}$. Thus $D_{10}$ is the internal direct product of $K$ and $H$:
> $$D_{10}\cong K\times H\cong D_5\times H.$$
>
> Equivalently, if $D_5=\langle a,b\rangle$ and $H=\langle h\rangle$, an explicit isomorphism sends
> $$x\longmapsto(a,h),\qquad y\longmapsto(b,1).$$
> $\square$

## Notes

The parity condition matters: more generally, $D_{2m}\cong D_m\times C_2$ when $m$ is odd. The central factor is generated by the half-turn $x^m$.
