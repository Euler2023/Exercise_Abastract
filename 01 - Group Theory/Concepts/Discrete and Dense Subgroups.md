# Discrete and Dense Subgroups

## Definition

> [!info] Discrete subgroup
> A subgroup $\Gamma\le\mathbb R^n$ is **discrete** if some $\varepsilon>0$ satisfies $\lVert\gamma\rVert\ge\varepsilon$ for every nonzero $\gamma\in\Gamma$.

> [!info] Dense subgroup
> A subgroup $\Gamma\le X$ of a metric topological group is **dense** if every point of $X$ is arbitrarily close to elements of $\Gamma$, equivalently if $\overline\Gamma=X$.

## Intuition

Subgroup structure makes “intermediate spacing” unstable. On the real line, if there is a smallest positive step, the subgroup is a regular arithmetic progression. If there are arbitrarily small positive steps, integral multiples enter every interval, so the subgroup is dense.

## Key Properties

1. Every additive subgroup of $\mathbb R$ is either dense or of the form $a\mathbb Z$ for some $a\ge0$.
2. The subgroup $\mathbb Z+\alpha\mathbb Z$ is dense when $\alpha$ is irrational.
3. A subgroup of the circle group $\mathbb R/(2\pi\mathbb Z)$ is either finite cyclic or dense.
4. A discrete subgroup of $\mathbb R^n$ is free abelian of rank at most $n$.
5. Discreteness is equivalent to uniform separation of distinct subgroup elements because their differences remain in the subgroup.

## Examples

> [!example] Discrete
> $\sqrt2\mathbb Z$ is discrete in $\mathbb R$; consecutive elements are separated by $\sqrt2$.

> [!example] Dense
> $\mathbb Z+\sqrt2\mathbb Z$ has nonzero elements arbitrarily close to $0$, hence is dense in $\mathbb R$.

> [!example] Circle subgroup
> The angle generated by $2\pi/n$ gives a cyclic subgroup of order $n$. An irrational multiple of $2\pi$ generates a dense cyclic subgroup of the circle.
