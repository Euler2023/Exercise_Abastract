# Dihedral Groups

## Definition

> [!info] Definition (Artin's Convention)
> The **dihedral group** $D_n$ is the symmetry group of a regular $n$-gon. It has order $2n$ and presentation
> $$D_n=\langle x,y\mid x^n=1,\ y^2=1,\ yx=x^{-1}y\rangle,$$
> where $x$ is rotation through $2\pi/n$ and $y$ is a reflection.

Some authors write $D_{2n}$ for this group. This vault follows Artin's convention: $|D_n|=2n$.

## Normal Forms

The relation
$$yx^k=x^{-k}y$$
allows every word to be rewritten with at most one $y$, placed on the right. Thus every element has one of the forms
$$x^i\qquad\text{or}\qquad x^iy,
\qquad 0\le i<n.$$

These $2n$ elements are geometrically distinct: the first $n$ are rotations and the second $n$ are reflections. Consequently $|D_n|=2n$.

> [!abstract] Proposition (Artin)
> The relations
> $$x^n=1,\qquad y^2=1,
> \qquad yx=x^{-1}y$$
> describe the complete multiplication in $D_n$.

This result is **proved in the source**. [S1, Ch. 6, Prop. 6.4.3, printed p. 164, PDF p. 176]

## Multiplication

For exponents read modulo $n$,
$$x^ix^j=x^{i+j},$$
$$x^i(x^jy)=x^{i+j}y,$$
$$(x^iy)x^j=x^{i-j}y,$$
$$(x^iy)(x^jy)=x^{i-j}.$$

In particular, every reflection has order $2$:
$$(x^iy)^2=1.$$

## Subgroup Template

Every subgroup $H\le D_n$ is of one of the following forms:

1. a rotation subgroup $\langle x^d\rangle$ for some divisor $d\mid n$;
2. a subgroup
   $$\langle x^d,x^ky\rangle,$$
   where $d\mid n$ and $0\le k<d$.

> [!abstract] Proof
> The rotational part $H\cap\langle x\rangle$ is a subgroup of the cyclic group $\langle x\rangle$, so it equals $\langle x^d\rangle$ for a divisor $d$ of $n$. If $H$ contains no reflection, this is all of $H$. Otherwise choose $x^ky\in H$. Every other reflection $x^jy\in H$ satisfies
> $$(x^jy)(x^ky)=x^{j-k}\in H\cap\langle x\rangle,$$
> so $j\equiv k\pmod d$. Hence every element of $H$ lies in
> $$\langle x^d\rangle\cup\langle x^d\rangle x^ky,$$
> which is exactly $\langle x^d,x^ky\rangle$. $\square$

The second subgroup has order $2n/d$ and is itself dihedral.

## Normal Subgroups

Every rotation subgroup $\langle x^d\rangle$ is normal because
$$y x^d y^{-1}=x^{-d}\in\langle x^d\rangle.$$

For a subgroup containing a reflection, normality imposes additional congruence conditions. When $n$ is odd, all reflections are conjugate, so no proper normal subgroup can contain a reflection. When $n$ is even, the reflections split into two conjugacy classes, represented by $y$ and $xy$.

The center is
$$
Z(D_n)=
\begin{cases}
\{1\},&n\text{ odd},\\
\{1,x^{n/2}\},&n\text{ even}.
\end{cases}
$$

## Quotients by Rotation Subgroups

If $N=\langle x^m\rangle$ is normal, the quotient is generated by $xN$ and $yN$. Its exact dihedral index is determined by the order of $xN$. For example,
$$D_{15}/\langle x^5\rangle\cong D_5,
\qquad
D_{15}/\langle x^3\rangle\cong D_3.$$

## Key Properties

1. $D_n$ is nonabelian for $n\ge3$.
2. The rotation subgroup $\langle x\rangle$ is cyclic, normal, and has index $2$.
3. $D_3\cong S_3$.
4. $D_2\cong C_2\times C_2$ under Artin's convention.
5. If $m$ is odd, then
   $$D_{2m}\cong D_m\times C_2.$$
6. The infinite dihedral group has presentation
   $$D_\infty=\langle t,s\mid s^2=1,\ sts=t^{-1}\rangle.$$

## Examples

> [!example] The Square
> $D_4$ has eight elements: four rotations and four reflections. Its half-turn $x^2$ is central.

> [!example] A Word Calculation
> Using $yx^{-1}=xy$ and $y^2=1$,
> $$x^2yx^{-1}y^{-1}x^3y^3=x^6y.$$
