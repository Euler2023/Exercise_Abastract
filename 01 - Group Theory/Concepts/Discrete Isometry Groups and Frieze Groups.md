# Discrete Isometry Groups and Frieze Groups

## Definition

> [!info] Discrete group of plane isometries
> A subgroup $G$ of the plane-isometry group $M$ is **discrete** if there is an $\varepsilon>0$ such that every nonzero translation vector in $G$ has length at least $\varepsilon$ and every nonidentity rotation in $G$ has angle bounded away from $0$ modulo $2\pi$.

For such a group, two pieces of data organize the classification:

- the **translation group** $L=\{v\in\mathbb R^2:t_v\in G\}$;
- the **point group** $\overline G$, the image of $G$ in $O_2$ under the orthogonal-part homomorphism.

They fit into the exact sequence

$$
1\longrightarrow L\longrightarrow G\longrightarrow\overline G\longrightarrow1.
$$

Here $L$ is written additively and identified with the corresponding translations.

> [!info] Frieze group
> A **frieze group** is a discrete plane-isometry group whose translation group is infinite cyclic. After choosing a horizontal coordinate and unit length, its translation subgroup is $\langle t_1\rangle$.

## Intuition

A frieze repeats in one direction. The infinite part of its symmetry group comes from the least translation. All additional symmetries are finite choices of orthogonal behavior: vertical or horizontal reflection, half-turn, or glide reflection. The point group forgets where axes and centers lie and remembers only these orthogonal parts.

## Key Properties

1. The translation group $L$ is normal in $G$.
2. Every $q\in\overline G$ preserves $L$ because $qt_vq^{-1}=t_{q(v)}$.
3. If $L=\mathbb Za$, then $q(a)=\pm a$ and $\overline G\le D_2$.
4. A frieze group has one of exactly seven types up to horizontal rescaling and a change of origin.
5. The seven types are distinguished by the presence of translations, vertical mirrors, a horizontal mirror, half-turns, and a half-step glide.

## The Seven Frieze Types

Let $t=t_1$, let $s(x,y)=(-x,y)$, let $r(x,y)=(x,-y)$, and let $\rho=sr$. Representatives are

$$
\begin{array}{c|l}
\text{type}&\text{representative group}\\ \hline
1&\langle t\rangle\\
2&\langle t_{1/2}r\rangle\\
3&\langle t,s\rangle\\
4&\langle t,r\rangle\\
5&\langle t,\rho\rangle\\
6&\langle t,s,r\rangle\\
7&\langle t,s,t_{1/2}r\rangle.
\end{array}
$$

The second group is generated by a glide whose square is $t$. The last two have the full four-element point group; they differ in whether the lift of horizontal reflection is an actual reflection or a glide.

## Examples

> [!example] Translation and horizontal reflection
> A row of identical motifs individually symmetric across the horizontal axis has group $\langle t,r\rangle\cong C_\infty\times C_2$.

> [!example] Half-turn frieze
> If alternating corner motifs are exchanged by half-turns but by no reflections, then $\overline G=\{I,-I\}$ and the translation subgroup has index $2$.
