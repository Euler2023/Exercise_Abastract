# Lattices in Euclidean Space

## Definition

> [!info] Lattice
> Let $V$ be a finite-dimensional real inner-product space. A **lattice of rank $r$** is a subgroup
>
> $$
> L=\mathbb Zv_1\oplus\cdots\oplus\mathbb Zv_r
> $$
>
> generated by $r$ vectors that are linearly independent over $\mathbb R$. The ordered family $(v_1,\ldots,v_r)$ is a **lattice basis**.

If $r=\dim V$, the lattice is full. A rank-one lattice lies on a line, while a rank-two lattice in $\mathbb R^2$ is the vertex set of a periodic parallelogram grid.

## Intuition

A vector-space basis allows arbitrary real coefficients; a lattice basis allows only integer coefficients. Thus a lattice is simultaneously a discrete geometric set and a free $\mathbb Z$-module embedded in a real vector space.

## Key Properties

1. A lattice is discrete: every bounded region contains only finitely many lattice points.
2. Two bases of the same rank-$r$ lattice differ by a matrix in $GL_r(\mathbb Z)$.
3. A sublattice $L'\subseteq L$ of full rank has finite index, and the index equals the absolute determinant of a change-of-generators matrix.
4. Reduction modulo $pL$ identifies index-$p$ sublattices with hyperplanes in the vector space $L/pL$.
5. Every discrete additive subgroup of $\mathbb R^n$ is a lattice in its real span.
6. If an orthogonal group preserves $L$, it acts through integral matrices relative to a lattice basis.

## Fundamental Parallelepiped

For a lattice basis $B=(v_1,\ldots,v_r)$, a half-open fundamental parallelepiped is

$$
\Pi(B)=\left\{\sum_{i=1}^r t_iv_i:0\le t_i<1\right\}.
$$

Every vector in $\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb R}(L)$ has a unique expression $\ell+x$ with $\ell\in L$ and $x\in\Pi(B)$. This is the geometric analogue of division with remainder.

## Examples

> [!example] Square lattice
> $\mathbb Z^2=\mathbb Ze_1\oplus\mathbb Ze_2$ is a full lattice in $\mathbb R^2$.

> [!example] A sublattice
> $3\mathbb Ze_1\oplus\mathbb Ze_2$ has index $3$ in $\mathbb Z^2$.

> [!example] Irrational generators
> The subgroup $\mathbb Z+\sqrt2\mathbb Z$ of $\mathbb R$ is not a lattice: its two proposed generators are linearly dependent over $\mathbb R$, and the subgroup is dense rather than discrete.
