# Exercise LA316: The Ideal (x,y) Is Not a Free Module

## Problem Statement

> [!question] Exercise 2.1
> Let $R=\mathbb C[x,y]$, and let $M$ be the ideal of $R$ generated by the two elements $x$ and $y$. Is $M$ a free $R$-module?

## Hints

> [!hint]- Hint 1
> After tensoring with the fraction field, $M$ has rank one. If it were free, it would therefore be principal.

## Solution

> [!success]- Solution
> Let $K=\mathbb C(x,y)$ be the fraction field of $R$. Since $M$ is a nonzero ideal,
>
> $$
> M\otimes_RK\cong K,
> $$
>
> so a free $M$ would have rank one. It would then be generated by one polynomial $f$, giving $(x,y)=(f)$. The polynomial $f$ would divide both $x$ and $y$. Since $x$ and $y$ are relatively prime in the UFD $R$, $f$ would be a unit. That would imply $(x,y)=R$, contrary to $1\notin(x,y)$. Therefore
>
> $$
> \boxed{(x,y)\text{ is not a free }\mathbb C[x,y]\text{-module}.}
> $$
>
> The visible relation $y\cdot x-x\cdot y=0$ also explains why the displayed generators are not a basis, though by itself it does not exclude some other basis.
