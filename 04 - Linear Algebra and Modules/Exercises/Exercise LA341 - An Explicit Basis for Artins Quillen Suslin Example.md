# Exercise LA341: An Explicit Basis for Artin's Quillen-Suslin Example

## Problem Statement

> [!question] Exercise 9.2
> Prove that the module presented by (14.9.2) is free by exhibiting a basis.

## Hints

> [!hint]- Hint 1
> Adjoin two carefully chosen columns to the two relation columns and check that the resulting $4\times4$ determinant is a nonzero constant.

## Solution

> [!success]- Solution
> Let $R=\mathbb C[x,y]$. The two relation columns in (14.9.2) are
>
> $$
> r_1=\begin{pmatrix}1\\y\\x\\x^2\end{pmatrix},
> \qquad
> r_2=\begin{pmatrix}x\\x+3\\y\\y^2\end{pmatrix}.
> $$
>
> Define
>
> $$
> w_1=\begin{pmatrix}0\\-x\\1\\x^2+y\end{pmatrix}
> $$
>
> and
>
> $$
> w_2=\begin{pmatrix}
> 0\\
> -(x+2)(2x-1)\\
> 0\\
> 2x^3+x^2-3x+9
> \end{pmatrix}.
> $$
>
> Exact expansion gives
>
> $$
> \det(r_1,r_2,w_1,w_2)=27.
> $$
>
> Since $27$ is a unit in $R$, these four columns form an $R$-basis of $R^4$. Quotienting by the submodule generated by the first two columns leaves the residue classes of the last two as a basis. Therefore the presented module is free of rank two, with explicit basis
>
> $$
> \boxed{\overline w_1,\overline w_2.}
> $$
