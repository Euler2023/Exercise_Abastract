# Exercise LA386: Support Containment and Annihilation by an Ideal Power

## Problem Statement

> [!question] Lang, Chapter X, Exercise 12
> Let $A$ be a Noetherian ring and $M$ a finite $A$-module. Let $I$ be an ideal of $A$ such that $\operatorname{supp}(M)\subset\mathcal Z(I)$. Then $I^nM=0$ for some $n>0$.

The chapter uses commutative rings, and $\subset$ allows equality.

## Hints

> [!hint]- Hint 1
> By Exercise 11, every prime containing $\operatorname{ann}(M)$ contains $I$. Interpret this as a radical containment.

> [!hint]- Hint 2
> Write $I=(a_1,\ldots,a_t)$ with $a_j^{e_j}M=0$. Every monomial of degree $1+\sum_j(e_j-1)$ contains one of these powers.

## Solution

> [!success]- Independent derivation
> Put $J=\operatorname{ann}_A(M)$. By [[04 - Linear Algebra and Modules/Exercises/Exercise LA385 - Support of a Finite Module and Its Annihilator|Exercise LA385 (Lang X.11)]],
> $$
> \mathcal Z(J)=\operatorname{supp}(M)\subseteq\mathcal Z(I).
> $$
> Hence every prime containing $J$ contains $I$, so $I\subseteq\sqrt J$.
>
> **Radical criterion.** If $x\notin\sqrt J$, the multiplicative set $\{1,x,x^2,\ldots\}$ misses $J$. The corresponding localization of $A/J$ is a nonzero ring, which has a maximal ideal by Zorn's lemma. Its inverse image in $A$ is prime, contains $J$, and excludes $x$. Conversely any prime containing $J$ contains every element whose power lies in $J$. This proves $\sqrt J=\bigcap_{\mathfrak p\supseteq J}\mathfrak p$ and justifies the preceding step.
>
> Since $A$ is Noetherian, write $I=(a_1,\ldots,a_t)$. If $I=0$, take $n=1$. Otherwise choose $e_j\ge1$ with $a_j^{e_j}\in J$, and let
> $$
> n=1+\sum_{j=1}^t(e_j-1).
> $$
> The ideal $I^n$ is generated by monomials $\prod_j a_j^{u_j}$ with $\sum_j u_j=n$. At least one exponent satisfies $u_j\ge e_j$, for otherwise their sum is at most $n-1$. Every generating monomial is therefore in $J$. Thus $I^n\subseteq J$ and $I^nM=0$.
>
> If $M=0$, already $n=1$ works.

## Notes

- Statement checked on the original page [S2, Ch. X, Exercise 12, printed p. 444, PDF p. 459]. The proof, including the radical criterion, is independently supplied; the maximal-ideal existence step uses Zorn's lemma.
- It suffices that $M$ and $I$ be finitely generated; this is how Noetherianity is used.
- Support containment gives a power of $I$, not necessarily $IM=0$: for $A=k[x]$, $M=A/(x^2)$ and $I=(x)$, the square works but the first power does not.
