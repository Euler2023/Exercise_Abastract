# Exercise LA325: When Three Complex Generators Form a Lattice

## Problem Statement

> [!question] Exercise 4.5
> Let $\alpha,\beta,\gamma$ be complex numbers. Under what conditions is the set
>
> $$
> \{\ell\alpha+m\beta+n\gamma\mid \ell,m,n\in\mathbb Z\}
> $$
>
> a lattice in the complex plane?

## Hints

> [!hint]- Hint 1
> Choose two of the numbers that are linearly independent over $\mathbb R$ and express the third in their real basis.

## Solution

> [!success]- Solution
> The generated subgroup is a full lattice in $\mathbb C\cong\mathbb R^2$ exactly when:
>
> 1. $\alpha,\beta,\gamma$ span $\mathbb C$ over $\mathbb R$; and
> 2. their $\mathbb Z$-rank is two, equivalently they satisfy a nonzero integer relation.
>
> To see the equivalent coordinate form, suppose after relabeling that $\alpha,eta$ are real-linearly independent and write
>
> $$
> \gamma=r\alpha+s\beta.
> $$
>
> The subgroup is discrete exactly when $r,s\in\mathbb Q$. If $r=a/c$ and $s=b/c$, it is contained in the lattice generated by $\alpha/c$ and $\beta/c$, hence is discrete and full. Conversely, if the subgroup is a lattice, its three generators lie in a free abelian group of rank two and are integer-dependent; independence of $\alpha,eta$ then forces $r,s$ to be rational.
>
> Thus one compact criterion is
>
> $$
> \boxed{\dim_{\mathbb R}\operatorname{span}\{\alpha,\beta,\gamma\}=2
> \quad\text{and}\quad
> \ell\alpha+m\beta+n\gamma=0
> \text{ for some }(\ell,m,n)\ne0.}
> $$

## Notes

- **Routing:** Linear Algebra is primary because real span and rational coordinate dependence characterize discreteness.
- **Definition boundary:** “Lattice in the complex plane” is taken to mean a full rank-two lattice. If lower-rank discrete subgroups are allowed, rank zero and one cases must be added.
