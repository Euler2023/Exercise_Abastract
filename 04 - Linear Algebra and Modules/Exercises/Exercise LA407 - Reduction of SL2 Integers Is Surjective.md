# Exercise LA407: Reduction of SL2 Integers Is Surjective

## Problem Statement

> [!question] Lang, Chapter XIII, Exercise 18
> The reduction map $\mathbb Z\to\mathbb Z/N\mathbb Z$, where $N$ is a positive integer, defines a homomorphism
>
> $$
> SL_2(\mathbb Z)\longrightarrow SL_2(\mathbb Z/N\mathbb Z).
> $$
>
> Show that this homomorphism is surjective. **Printed hint.** Use elementary divisors, i.e. the structure of submodules of rank $2$ over the principal ring $\mathbb Z$.

## Hints

> [!hint]- Hint 1
> It is enough to express each determinant-one matrix over $R=\mathbb Z/N\mathbb Z$ as a product of elementary matrices $E_{12}(t)$ and $E_{21}(t)$.

> [!hint]- Hint 2
> For the first column $(a,c)^T$ of such a matrix, choose $t\in R$ so that $a+tc$ is a unit. Choose $t$ separately modulo every prime divisor of $N$ and combine the choices by the Chinese remainder theorem.

> [!hint]- Hint 3
> A diagonal matrix $\operatorname{diag}(u,u^{-1})$ with $u\in R^\times$ is itself a product of elementary matrices.

## Solution

> [!success]- Independently derived solution
> Put $R=\mathbb Z/N\mathbb Z$. The case $N=1$ has a trivial target, so suppose $N>1$. Let $A=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\c&d\end{pmatrix}\in SL_2(R)$. Because $ad-bc=1$, the pair $(a,c)$ generates the unit ideal of $R$. For every prime $p\mid N$, at least one of $a,c$ is nonzero modulo $p$. If $c\equiv0\pmod p$, then $a+tc\ne0\pmod p$ for every $t$; if $c\not\equiv0\pmod p$, choose $t$ to avoid the single residue $-a/c$. The Chinese remainder theorem supplies one $t\in R$ meeting these conditions for all $p\mid N$. Therefore $u=a+tc$ is a unit of $R$.
>
> Left multiplication by $E_{12}(t)=I+tE_{12}$ changes the first column to $(u,c)^T$. Left multiplication by $E_{21}(-cu^{-1})$ then changes it to $(u,0)^T$. The resulting determinant-one matrix has the shape $\begin{pmatrix}u&v\\0&u^{-1}\end{pmatrix}$, which is a product of $\operatorname{diag}(u,u^{-1})$ and an upper elementary matrix. The diagonal factor is elementary too: with
>
> $$
> w(u)=E_{12}(u)E_{21}(-u^{-1})E_{12}(u)
> =\begin{pmatrix}0&u\\-u^{-1}&0\end{pmatrix},
> \qquad
> \operatorname{diag}(u,u^{-1})=w(u)w(-1).
> $$
>
> Hence $SL_2(R)$ is generated by the elementary matrices. Each $E_{ij}(t)$ is the reduction of $E_{ij}(m)\in SL_2(\mathbb Z)$ for any integer $m$ representing $t$. Lifting every factor of an elementary factorization proves the asserted surjectivity. This gives an elementary-matrix proof independent of the printed elementary-divisor hint.
