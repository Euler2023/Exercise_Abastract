# Exercise LA524: Ext of Cyclic Modules over a Principal Ideal Domain

## Problem Statement

> [!question] Lang XX.28
> Let $R$ be a principal entire ring and $a\in R$. For every $R$-module $N$, prove:
>
> **(a)** $\operatorname{Ext}^1(R/aR,N)=N/aN$.
>
> **(b)** For $b\in R$, $\operatorname{Ext}^1(R/aR,R/bR)=R/(a,b)$, where $(a,b)$ is the gcd of $a,b$, assuming $ab\ne0$.

> [!warning] Source issue
> Part (a) omits the essential hypothesis $a\ne0$. The printed statement is retained, followed by the corrected proof and a counterexample at $a=0$.

## Hints

> [!hint]- Hint 1
> If $a\ne0$, use the resolution $0\to R\xrightarrow{a}R\to R/aR\to0$.

> [!hint]- Hint 2
> Reduce $(R/bR)/a(R/bR)$ to a quotient of $R$.

## Solution

> [!success]- Independent derivation
> **(a), corrected range.** Since $R$ is a domain, multiplication by $a\ne0$ is injective. Thus
> $$
> 0\longrightarrow R\xrightarrow{\cdot a}R\longrightarrow R/aR\longrightarrow0
> $$
> is a free resolution. Applying $\operatorname{Hom}_R(-,N)$ identifies its differential with multiplication by $a$ on $N$, so its first cohomology is $N/aN$. This proves the natural isomorphism for $a\ne0$.
>
> For $a=0$ the source formula is false in general: $R/aR=R$ is free, so $\operatorname{Ext}^1_R(R,N)=0$, whereas $N/aN=N$. Taking $N=R\ne0$ is a counterexample.
>
> **(b)** The stated $ab\ne0$ ensures $a\ne0$. Part (a) gives
> $$
> \operatorname{Ext}^1_R(R/aR,R/bR)
> \cong(R/bR)/a(R/bR)
> \cong R/(aR+bR).
> $$
> In a PID the ideal $aR+bR$ is generated by a gcd of $a,b$. This proves the formula, independently of the choice of an associate for the gcd.
