# Exercise LA522: Character Duality Flatness and Injectivity

## Problem Statement

> [!question] Lang XX.26
> Let $A$ be a commutative ring and $E$ an $A$-module. Put $E^\wedge=\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb Z}(E,\mathbb Q/\mathbb Z)$. Prove:
>
> **(a)** A sequence $0\to N\to M\to E\to0$ is exact if and only if its dual sequence $0\to E^\wedge\to M^\wedge\to N^\wedge\to0$ is exact.
>
> **(b)** If $F$ is flat and $I$ injective as $A$-modules, then $\operatorname{Hom}_A(F,I)$ is injective.
>
> **(c)** $E$ is flat if and only if $E^\wedge$ is injective.

## Hints

> [!hint]- Hint 1
> $\mathbb Q/\mathbb Z$ is both injective as an abelian group and able to detect nonzero elements.

> [!hint]- Hint 2
> Use the tensor–Hom adjunction on a short exact sequence.

## Solution

> [!success]- Independent derivation
> Give the character dual its $A$-action by $(a\lambda)(x)=\lambda(ax)$.
>
> First, $\mathbb Q/\mathbb Z$ is divisible, hence injective by XX.19. It also detects every nonzero element $x$ of an abelian group $X$: on the cyclic subgroup generated by $x$, send $x$ to $1/n+\mathbb Z$ if its order is $n>1$, and to $1/2+\mathbb Z$ if its order is infinite. Extend this nonzero character to $X$ by injectivity.
>
> **(a)** Exactness implies dual exactness by injectivity of $\mathbb Q/\mathbb Z$. Conversely write the proposed maps $u:N\to M$, $v:M\to E$. Dual exactness and detection imply $vu=0$. Surjectivity of $u^\wedge$ implies injectivity of $u$: any character nonzero on a nonzero element of $\ker u$ would not extend along $u$. Injectivity of $v^\wedge$ implies surjectivity of $v$, since a nonzero quotient $E/\operatorname{im}v$ would have a nonzero character.
>
> If $\ker v/\operatorname{im}u\ne0$, choose a nonzero character on it and extend it to $M/\operatorname{im}u$. Its pullback $\lambda\in M^\wedge$ vanishes on $\operatorname{im}u$, but not on $\ker v$. Dual exactness says $\lambda=\mu v$ for some $\mu$, which vanishes on $\ker v$, a contradiction. Thus the original sequence is exact.
>
> **(b)** For any module $X$ there is the natural adjunction
> $$
> \operatorname{Hom}_A(X,\operatorname{Hom}_A(F,I))
> \cong\operatorname{Hom}_A(X\otimes_AF,I).
> $$
> Flatness makes $-\otimes_AF$ exact, and injectivity makes $\operatorname{Hom}_A(-,I)$ contravariantly exact. The composite is therefore exact, which is the injectivity criterion for $\operatorname{Hom}_A(F,I)$.
>
> **(c)** There is a natural adjunction
> $$
> \operatorname{Hom}_A(X,E^\wedge)
> \cong\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb Z}(X\otimes_AE,\mathbb Q/\mathbb Z).
> $$
> If $E$ is flat, the right side is an exact contravariant functor by (a), so $E^\wedge$ is injective. Conversely, if $E^\wedge$ is injective, dualizing the tensor of any short exact sequence gives an exact sequence by the adjunction. Part (a) reflects exactness, so tensoring with $E$ is exact. This is flatness.
