# Exercise LA69: Path Components of Real General Linear Groups

## Problem Statement

> [!question] Exercise M.8
> **(a)** The group $SL_n(\mathbb R)$ is generated by elementary matrices of the first type. Use this fact to prove that $SL_n(\mathbb R)$ is path-connected.
> 
> **(b)** Show that $GL_n(\mathbb R)$ is a union of two path-connected subsets, and describe them.

## Hints

> [!hint]- Hint 1
> Join $I$ to $I+ce_{ij}$ by $t\mapsto I+tce_{ij}$.

> [!hint]- Hint 2
> The sign of the determinant is constant along a path in $GL_n(\mathbb R)$.

## Solution

> [!success]- Solution
> Each elementary shear $E=I+ce_{ij}$ is joined to $I$ by
> 
> $$
> E(t)=I+tce_{ij}\qquad(0\le t\le1),
> $$
> 
> whose determinant is $1$. A finite product of such paths, using pointwise multiplication, joins $I$ to any product of elementary shears. By the stated generation result, $SL_n(\mathbb R)$ is path-connected.
> 
> Now set
> 
> $$
> GL_n^+=\{A:\det A>0\},\qquad GL_n^-=\{A:\det A<0\}.
> $$
> 
> For $A\in GL_n^+$, continuously scale one column by a positive factor from $1$ to $(\det A)^{-1}$; this joins $A$ to a matrix of determinant $1$, so $GL_n^+$ is path-connected. If $J=\operatorname{diag}(-1,1,\ldots,1)$, multiplication by $J$ is a homeomorphism from $GL_n^+$ onto $GL_n^-$. Thus $GL_n^-$ is also path-connected. Since determinant cannot change sign without vanishing, these are exactly the two path components.
