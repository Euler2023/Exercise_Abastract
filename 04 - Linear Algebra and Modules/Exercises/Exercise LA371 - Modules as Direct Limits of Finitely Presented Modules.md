# Exercise LA371: Modules as Direct Limits of Finitely Presented Modules

## Problem Statement

> [!question] Exercise 25
> Show that any module is a direct limit of finitely presented modules, not necessarily submodules. In other words, given $M$, there exists a directed system $(M_i,f_j^i)$ with $M_i$ finitely presented for all $i$ such that
>
> $$
> M\cong\varinjlim_iM_i.
> $$
>
> **Printed hint.** Any finitely generated submodule is such a direct limit, since an infinitely generated module of relations can be viewed as a limit of finitely generated modules of relations. Make this precise to get a proof.

## Hints

> [!hint]- Hint 1
> Take a free module $F$ on symbols $e_m$ indexed by the elements $m\in M$, and let $F\twoheadrightarrow M$ send $e_m$ to $m$.

> [!hint]- Hint 2
> Keep only finitely many basis symbols and finitely many relations at a time. Order these finite pieces by enlarging both the generator set and the relation submodule.

> [!hint]- Hint 3
> If an element of one finite presentation maps to zero in $M$, add that one element to the finite relation module at a later stage and use the finite-stage vanishing criterion.

## Solution

> [!success]- Independently derived solution
> Let $R$ be the base ring and form the free module
>
> $$
> F=\bigoplus_{m\in M}Re_m.
> $$
>
> Define a surjection $\pi:F\to M$ by $\pi(e_m)=m$, and put $K=\ker\pi$.
>
> For a finite subset $S\subseteq M$, let
>
> $$
> F_S=\bigoplus_{m\in S}Re_m.
> $$
>
> Consider pairs $(S,L)$ in which $S$ is finite and $L$ is a finitely generated submodule of $K\cap F_S$. Order them by
>
> $$
> (S,L)\le(T,L')
> \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
> S\subseteq T\text{ and }L\subseteq L'.
> $$
>
> This index set is directed. Indeed, $(S\cup T,L+L')$ is an upper bound of $(S,L)$ and $(T,L')$, and $L+L'$ remains finitely generated and lies in $K\cap F_{S\cup T}$.
>
> Define
>
> $$
> M_{S,L}=F_S/L.
> $$
>
> If $L$ is generated by $\ell_1,\ldots,\ell_r$, the map from the finite free module $R^r$ to $F_S$ sending its standard basis to those generators yields an exact sequence
>
> $$
> R^r\longrightarrow F_S\longrightarrow M_{S,L}
> \longrightarrow0.
> $$
>
> Thus every $M_{S,L}$ is finitely presented. Whenever $(S,L)\le(T,L')$, inclusion $F_S\hookrightarrow F_T$ induces a transition map $M_{S,L}\to M_{T,L'}$.
>
> Because $L\subseteq K$, the restriction of $\pi$ to $F_S$ descends to a compatible map $M_{S,L}\to M$. Hence there is a canonical homomorphism
>
> $$
> \Phi:\varinjlim_{(S,L)}M_{S,L}\longrightarrow M.
> $$
>
> It is surjective: for $m\in M$, the class of $e_m$ in $M_{\{m\},0}$ maps to $m$.
>
> For injectivity, suppose a class represented by $x+L\in F_S/L$ maps to zero in $M$. Then $\pi(x)=0$, so $x\in K\cap F_S$. Enlarge the relation module to
>
> $$
> L'=L+Rx.
> $$
>
> The pair $(S,L')$ is a later index, and the image of $x+L$ in $F_S/L'$ is zero. By the finite-stage vanishing criterion, the original direct-limit class is zero. Thus $\Phi$ is injective and
>
> $$
> M\cong\varinjlim_{(S,L)}M_{S,L}.
> $$

## Notes

- **Why the stages need not be submodules:** A finite relation fragment $L$ can be smaller than the full kernel $K\cap F_S$, so $F_S/L$ need not inject into $M$.
- **Proof completeness:** The construction explicitly supplies the directed index set, transition maps, finite presentations, and the bijection with $M$; no Noetherian hypothesis is used.
- **Source status:** The statement, parenthetical qualification, formula, and printed hint were visually checked at [S2, Ch. III, Ex. 25, printed p. 171, PDF p. 186]. The proof is independent.
