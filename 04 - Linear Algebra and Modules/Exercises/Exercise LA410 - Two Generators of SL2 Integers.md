# Exercise LA410: Two Generators of SL2 Integers

## Problem Statement

> [!question] Lang, Chapter XIII, Exercise 21
> Show that $SL_2(\mathbb Z)$ is generated by the matrices
>
> $$
> U=\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\0&1\end{pmatrix}
> \quad\text{and}\quad
> S=\begin{pmatrix}0&-1\\1&0\end{pmatrix}.
> $$

## Hints

> [!hint]- Hint 1
> Powers of $U$ perform integer additions of the second row to the first.

> [!hint]- Hint 2
> Conjugating $U$ by $S$ gives lower elementary matrices. Apply the Euclidean algorithm to the first column of an arbitrary determinant-one matrix.

## Solution

> [!success]- Independently derived solution
> Let $A=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\c&d\end{pmatrix}\in SL_2(\mathbb Z)$. Since $ad-bc=1$, the integers $a,c$ are coprime. We have
>
> $$
> U^m=\begin{pmatrix}1&m\\0&1\end{pmatrix},
> \qquad
> S U^m S^{-1}=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\-m&1\end{pmatrix}.
> $$
>
> Thus left multiplication by words in $S,U$ permits adding an arbitrary integer multiple of one row to the other. Applying the Euclidean algorithm to the first-column pair $(a,c)^T$ reduces it to $(\pm1,0)^T$. Since $S^2=-I_2$, we can change the minus sign if necessary. We therefore obtain a word $W$ in $S,U$ for which $WA$ has first column $(1,0)^T$.
>
> Its determinant is $1$, so $WA=\begin{pmatrix}1&r\\0&1\end{pmatrix}=U^r$ for some integer $r$. Hence $A=W^{-1}U^r$ lies in the subgroup generated by $S$ and $U$. The reverse inclusion is immediate because both generators have determinant $1$.
