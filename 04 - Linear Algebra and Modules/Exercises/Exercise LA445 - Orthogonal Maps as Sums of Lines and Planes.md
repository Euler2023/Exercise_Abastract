# Exercise LA445: Orthogonal Maps as Sums of Lines and Planes

## Problem Statement

> [!question] Lang, Chapter XV, Exercise 3
> Let $E$ be a finite-dimensional, non-zero vector space over the reals, with a positive definite scalar product. Let $T:E\to E$ be a unitary automorphism of $E$. Show that $E$ is an orthogonal sum of subspaces
>
> $$
> E=E_1\perp\cdots\perp E_m
> $$
>
> such that each $E_i$ is $T$-invariant, and has dimension $1$ or $2$. If $E$ has dimension $2$, show that one can find a basis such that the matrix associated with $T$ with respect to this basis is
>
> $$
> \begin{pmatrix}\cos\theta&-\sin\theta\\\sin\theta&\cos\theta\end{pmatrix}
> \quad\text{or}\quad
> \begin{pmatrix}-\cos\theta&\sin\theta\\\sin\theta&\cos\theta\end{pmatrix},
> $$
>
> according as $\det T=1$ or $\det T=-1$.

## Hints

> [!hint]- Hint 1: Find a small real invariant subspace
> Factor an annihilating polynomial of $T$ over $\mathbb R$. Some irreducible factor $p$ has a nonzero vector in $\ker p(T)$, and $\deg p$ is one or two.

> [!hint]- Hint 2: Use a cyclic span
> For such a vector $v$, the space generated by $v,Tv,\ldots,T^{\deg p-1}v$ is invariant. Split off its orthogonal complement and proceed by induction.

## Solution

> [!success]- Independent solution
> Finite dimensionality makes $I,T,T^2,\ldots$ linearly dependent, so a nonzero real polynomial $q$ satisfies $q(T)=0$. Factor $q$ over $\mathbb R$ into irreducible factors, all of degree one or two by the fundamental theorem of algebra. At least one factor $p$ has $p(T)$ noninvertible, since otherwise the product $q(T)$ would be invertible. Choose $v\ne0$ with $p(T)v=0$.
>
> Let $d=\deg p\in\{1,2\}$, take $p$ monic, and put $W=\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb R}\{v,Tv,\ldots,T^{d-1}v\}$. The equation $p(T)v=0$ expresses $T^dv$ in this span, so $T(W)\subseteq W$ and $1\leq\dim W\leq2$. Since $T$ is invertible, its restriction to $W$ is injective and hence onto. For $x\in W^\perp$ and $w\in W$,
>
> $$
> \langle Tx,w\rangle=\langle x,T^{-1}w\rangle=0.
> $$
>
> Thus $W^\perp$ is invariant, and $E=W\perp W^\perp$. The restriction to $W^\perp$ remains unitary. Induction gives the required orthogonal sum of invariant lines and planes.
>
> For the two-dimensional assertion, choose an orthonormal basis. If the matrix has determinant $1$, its first column is $(\cos\theta,\sin\theta)^{\mathsf T}$ for some real $\theta$, and its unit second column must be $(-\sin\theta,\cos\theta)^{\mathsf T}$ to have the required determinant. If the determinant is $-1$, multiply the matrix on the left by $D=\operatorname{diag}(-1,1)$; the result is an orthogonal matrix of determinant $1$, hence a rotation $R_\theta$ by the preceding computation. The original matrix is
>
> $$
> DR_\theta=
> \begin{pmatrix}-\cos\theta&\sin\theta\\\sin\theta&\cos\theta\end{pmatrix}.
> $$
>
> This proves the stated forms, with an orthonormal basis in particular.
