# Exercise LA356: Height and Saturation in the Rational Multiplicative Group

## Problem Statement

> [!question] Lang, Chapter III, Exercise 8
> Consider the multiplicative group $\mathbb Q^*$ of nonzero rational numbers. If $x=a/b$ with $a,b\in\mathbb Z$ and $(a,b)=1$, define
>
> $$
> h(x)=\log\max(|a|,|b|).
> $$
>
> **(a)** Show that $h$ is a seminorm on $\mathbb Q^*$ whose kernel is $\{\pm1\}$, the torsion subgroup.
>
> **(b)** Let $M_1=\langle x_1,\ldots,x_m\rangle$ be a finitely generated subgroup of $\mathbb Q^*$, and let
>
> $$
> M=\{x\in\mathbb Q^*:x^s\in M_1\text{ for some positive integer }s\}.
> $$
>
> Show that $M$ is finitely generated and, using Exercise 7, bound the seminorms of a set of generators of $M$ in terms of $h(x_1),\ldots,h(x_m)$.

## Hints

> [!hint]- Hint 1
> Modulo $\{\pm1\}$, send a rational number to its vector of prime valuations:
>
> $$
> \mathbb Q^*/\{\pm1\}\cong\bigoplus_p\mathbb Z.
> $$

> [!hint]- Hint 2
> The image of $M$ is the saturation of the lattice generated by the valuation vectors of the $x_i$. It is contained in the finite coordinate lattice supported on the primes occurring in the $x_i$.

## Solution

> [!success]- Independently derived solution
> **(a)** Clearly $h(x)\ge0$. If $n>0$ and $x=a/b$ is reduced, then $a^n/b^n$ is reduced, so
>
> $$
> h(x^n)=\log\max(|a|^n,|b|^n)=n h(x).
> $$
>
> Negative $n$ interchange numerator and denominator, and $n=0$ gives $h(1)=0$; hence $h(x^n)=|n|h(x)$ for every $n\in\mathbb Z$.
>
> Write $x=a/b$ and $y=c/d$ in lowest terms. Reducing the fraction $ac/bd$ can only decrease the maximum of the absolute numerator and denominator, and
>
> $$
> \max(|ac|,|bd|)
> \le\max(|a|,|b|)\max(|c|,|d|).
> $$
>
> Taking logarithms yields $h(xy)\le h(x)+h(y)$. Finally, $h(a/b)=0$ precisely when $|a|=|b|=1$, so the kernel is $\{\pm1\}$, which is exactly the torsion subgroup of $\mathbb Q^*$.
>
> **(b)** For every prime $p$, let $v_p$ be the usual exponent valuation. The map
>
> $$
> \nu:\mathbb Q^*\longrightarrow\bigoplus_p\mathbb Z,
> \qquad
> x\longmapsto(v_p(x))_p
> $$
>
> is surjective and has kernel $\{\pm1\}$. Put $u_i=\nu(x_i)$ and $L_1=\sum_i\mathbb Zu_i$. Passing to the quotient by $\{\pm1\}$, the definition of $M$ says exactly that
>
> $$
> \nu(M)=L_1^{\mathrm{sat}}
> =\bigl(\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb Q}L_1\bigr)
> \cap\bigoplus_p\mathbb Z.
> $$
>
> Indeed, $x^s\in M_1$ implies $s\nu(x)\in L_1$. Conversely, if $s\nu(x)\in L_1$, then $x^s$ differs from an element of $M_1$ by at most a sign; after squaring, $x^{2s}\in M_1$.
>
> Let $P$ be the finite set of primes that occur in the numerators or denominators of the $x_i$. Then $L_1^{\mathrm{sat}}\subseteq\mathbb Z^P$. Every subgroup of $\mathbb Z^P$ is finitely generated: induct on $|P|$, project to the last coordinate, use that the image is cyclic, and combine a lift of its generator with finitely many generators of the kernel. Hence $L_1^{\mathrm{sat}}$ is finitely generated. Adding the kernel generator $-1$ proves that $M$ is finitely generated.
>
> Let $r=\operatorname{rank}L_1$ and choose a maximal independent subsequence
>
> $$
> x_{i_1},\ldots,x_{i_r}
> $$
>
> modulo $\{\pm1\}$. The saturation has the same rank and rational span as $L_1$. Applying Exercise 7 to $M$ with kernel $\{\pm1\}$ gives a basis $y_1,\ldots,y_r$ of $M/\{\pm1\}$ satisfying
>
> $$
> h(y_j)
> \le\sum_{k=1}^j h(x_{i_k})
> \le\sum_{i=1}^m h(x_i).
> $$
>
> Therefore
>
> $$
> \{-1,y_1,\ldots,y_r\}
> $$
>
> generates $M$, and every generator in this set has height at most $\sum_{i=1}^m h(x_i)$ (the torsion generator has height $0$).
