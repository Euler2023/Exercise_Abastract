# Exercise LA375: A Free Basis over the Symmetric Polynomial Ring

## Problem Statement

> [!question] Lang, Chapter IV, Exercise 19
>
> (a) Let $X_1,\ldots,X_n$ be variables. Show that every homogeneous polynomial in $\mathbb Z[X_1,\ldots,X_n]$ of degree $>n(n-1)$ lies in the ideal generated by the elementary symmetric functions $s_1,\ldots,s_n$.
>
> (b) Show that $\mathbb Z[X_1,\ldots,X_n]$ is a free $\mathbb Z[s_1,\ldots,s_n]$-module with basis
> $$
> X^{(r)}=X_1^{r_1}\cdots X_n^{r_n},\qquad0\le r_i\le n-i.
> $$
>
> (c) For two independent sets $X_1,\ldots,X_n$ and $Y_1,\ldots,Y_m$, with elementary symmetric functions $s$ and $s'$, show that $\mathbb Z[X,Y]$ is free over $\mathbb Z[s,s']$ with basis $X^{(r)}Y^{(q)}$ subject to the corresponding inequalities.
>
> (d) If $I$ is an ideal in $\mathbb Z[s,s']$ and $J$ its extension to $\mathbb Z[X,Y]$, show that $J\cap\mathbb Z[s,s']=I$.

## Hints

> [!hint]- Hint 1
> For (b), successively adjoin one root of a universal monic polynomial and divide out its linear factor. Each monic quotient gives a free module.

> [!hint]- Hint 2
> For (a), set every $s_i$ to zero in the homogeneous free-basis expansion. For (d), project onto the coefficient of the basis element $1$.

## Solution

> [!success]- Solution
> ### Approach
> For (b), successively adjoin one root of a universal monic polynomial and divide out its linear factor. Each monic quotient gives a free module.
>
> ### Proof
> **(b): universal root construction.** Start with $B_0=\mathbb Z[t_1,\ldots,t_n]$ and the monic polynomial
> $$
> P_0(T)=T^n-t_1T^{n-1}+\cdots+(-1)^nt_n.
> $$
> Define $B_1=B_0[x_1]/(P_0(x_1))$. Division by a monic polynomial over any commutative ring gives unique remainders, so $B_1$ is free over $B_0$ with basis $1,x_1,\ldots,x_1^{n-1}$; in particular the map $B_0\to B_1$ is injective. Since $P_0(x_1)=0$, monic division gives $P_0(T)=(T-x_1)P_1(T)$ in $B_1[T]$, where $P_1$ is monic of degree $n-1$.
>
> Repeat: $B_i=B_{i-1}[x_i]/(P_{i-1}(x_i))$, and divide out $T-x_i$. At stage $i$ the free basis has exponents $0\le r_i<n-i+1$. Composing these free expansions makes $B_n$ free over $B_0$ with basis $\prod_i x_i^{r_i}$, $0\le r_i\le n-i$. In $B_n[T]$,
> $$
> P_0(T)=\prod_{i=1}^n(T-x_i),
> $$
> so coefficient comparison gives $t_j=s_j(x_1,\ldots,x_n)$.
>
> Now define $\mathbb Z[X_1,\ldots,X_n]\to B_n$ by $X_i\mapsto x_i$. It is surjective because the $t_j$ are polynomials in the $x_i$. In the other direction, send $t_j$ to $s_j(X)$ and each $x_i$ to $X_i$. All the successive root relations hold, since the successive remaining polynomials specialize to $\prod_{k=i}^n(T-X_k)$. These maps are inverse on all generators. Thus $B_n$ is the desired polynomial ring, and its free $B_0$ basis proves (b), including algebraic independence of the $s_j$ and the rank $n!$.
>
> **(a)** Give $t_j$ degree $j$, and each $X_i$ degree $1$. All the root-construction relations are homogeneous, so the free expansion in (b) respects degrees. Modulo $(s_1,\ldots,s_n)$, only integer coefficients of the basis monomials remain. Their degrees are at most
> $$
> \sum_{i=1}^n(n-i)=n(n-1)/2.
> $$
> Hence every homogeneous polynomial of degree greater than this number maps to zero in the quotient and belongs to the ideal. This proves the source's weaker threshold $>n(n-1)$ as well.
>
> **(c)** Apply (b) first over the coefficient ring $\mathbb Z[Y]$: its monic-division proof works over any commutative coefficient ring. Then expand every coefficient in $\mathbb Z[Y]$ using the corresponding $Y$ basis over $\mathbb Z[s']$. Existence and uniqueness of both expansions give the product basis over $\mathbb Z[s,s']$.
>
> **(d)** Let $C=\mathbb Z[s,s']$ and $D=\mathbb Z[X,Y]$. The product basis includes $1$. Every element of $ID$ has all its basis coefficients in $I$, because it is a finite sum of $i\,d$ with $i\in I$. Conversely an expansion with all coefficients in $I$ belongs to $ID$. An element $c\in C$ has only its coefficient on the basis element $1$ nonzero, namely $c$. Thus $c\in ID$ iff $c\in I$, proving the contraction identity.
