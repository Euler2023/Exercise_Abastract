# Exercise LA585: Matrix Descent for Gamma Zero Four

## Problem Statement

> [!question] Original Exercise 1.2.4
> Let $\Gamma_\theta$ be the group generated by the matrices $\pm\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\0&1\end{pmatrix}$ and $\pm\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\4&1\end{pmatrix}$. This exercise shows that $\Gamma_\theta=\Gamma_0(4)$. The containment "$\subset$" is clear. For the other containment, let $\alpha=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\c&d\end{pmatrix}$ be a matrix in $\Gamma_0(4)$. Similarly to Exercise 1.1.1, the identity
> $$
> \begin{pmatrix}a&b\\c&d\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}1&n\\0&1\end{pmatrix}
> =\begin{pmatrix}a&b'\\c&nc+d\end{pmatrix}
> $$
> shows that unless $c=0$, some matrix $\alpha\gamma$ with $\gamma\in\Gamma_\theta$ has bottom row $(c',d')$ with $|d'|<|c'|/2$. (The inequality is strict because $c'\equiv0\pmod4$ and $d'$ is odd.) Use the identity
> $$
> \begin{pmatrix}a&b\\c&d\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\4n&1\end{pmatrix}
> =\begin{pmatrix}a'&b\\c+4nd&d\end{pmatrix}
> $$
> to show that because $d\ne0$ (no $\Gamma_0(4)$ element can have $d=0$), some matrix $\alpha\gamma$ with $\gamma\in\Gamma_\theta$ has bottom row $(c',d')$ with $|c'|<2|d'|$. (The inequality is strict because of properties of $c'$ and $d'$ modulo $4$.) Each multiplication reduces the positive integer quantity $\min\{|c|,2|d|\}$, so the process must stop with $c=0$. Show that in fact this means $\alpha\gamma\in\Gamma_\theta$ for some $\gamma\in\Gamma_\theta$, and so $\alpha\in\Gamma_\theta$.

## Hints

> [!hint]- Hint 1
> The determinant condition implies $d$ is odd and the lower-left entry stays divisible by $4$.

> [!hint]- Hint 2
> Apply the upper triangular reduction only when $|c|<2|d|$, and the lower triangular reduction otherwise. Track $\min(|c|,2|d|)$.

## Solution

> [!success]- Solution
> **Proof scope:** Independent solution
>
> Write $T=\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\0&1\end{pmatrix}$ and $V=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\4&1\end{pmatrix}$. Both and their negatives lie in $\Gamma_0(4)$, proving $\Gamma_\theta\subseteq\Gamma_0(4)$. For every matrix in $\Gamma_0(4)$, the relation $ad-bc=1$ shows $ad\equiv1\pmod4$, so $a,d$ are odd and in particular $d\ne0$.
>
> Suppose $c\ne0$. If $|c|<2|d|$, choose an integer $n$ nearest to $-d/c$. Right multiplication by $T^n$ replaces $d$ by $d'=d+nc$ and gives $|d'|\le|c|/2$. Equality would make the odd integer $d'$ equal, up to sign, to the even integer $c/2$; hence $2|d'|<|c|$. The positive measure $\min(|c|,2|d|)$ has strictly decreased.
>
> If $|c|\ge2|d|$, choose an integer $n$ nearest to $-c/(4d)$. Right multiplication by $V^n$ gives $c'=c+4nd$ with $|c'|\le2|d|$. Equality is impossible since $c'$ is divisible by $4$, whereas $2d$ is $2$ modulo $4$. Consequently $|c'|<2|d|$, again strictly decreasing the measure, unless $c'=0$ and the descent has already finished.
>
> The measure cannot decrease indefinitely through positive integers, so finitely many such multiplications give $\beta=\alpha\gamma$ with $\gamma\in\Gamma_\theta$ and lower-left entry $0$. Since $\det\beta=1$, its diagonal entries are both $1$ or both $-1$. Thus $\beta=T^b$ or $\beta=-T^{-b}$, respectively, for its top-right entry $b$. In either case $\beta\in\Gamma_\theta$, and $\alpha=\beta\gamma^{-1}\in\Gamma_\theta$.
>
> For instance, starting with bottom row $(4,3)$, multiplying by $T^{-1}$ gives $(4,-1)$ and then by $V$ gives $(0,-1)$; the measure changes $4\to2\to0$.

## Notes

The full source argument was checked on both pages. The solution makes the descent schedule precise: use the reduction appropriate to the current inequality. A redundant multiplication with exponent zero need not decrease the measure; the selected nontrivial reductions above do. No group presentation or later modular-curve theorem is imported. The proof is independent and follows the matrix-calculation routing.
