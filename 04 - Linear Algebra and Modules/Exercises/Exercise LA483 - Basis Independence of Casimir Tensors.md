# Exercise LA483: Basis Independence of Casimir Tensors

## Problem Statement

> [!question] Lang XVI.14 — The Casimir element
> Let $k$ be a commutative field and let $E$ be a vector space over $k$, of finite dimension $n$. Let $B$ be a nondegenerate symmetric bilinear form on $E$, inducing an isomorphism $E\to E^\vee$ of $E$ with its dual space. Let $\{v_1,\ldots,v_n\}$ be a basis of $E$. The $B$-dual basis $\{v_1',\ldots,v_n'\}$ consists of the elements of $E$ such that $B(v_i,v_j')=\delta_{ij}$.
>
> (a) Show that the element $\sum v_i\otimes v_i'$ in $E\otimes E$ is independent of the choice of basis. We call this element the **Casimir element** (see below).
>
> (b) In the symmetric algebra $S(E)$, let $Q_B=\sum v_iv_i'$. Show that $Q_B$ is independent of the choice of basis. We call $Q_B$ the **Casimir polynomial**. It depends on $B$, of course.
>
> (c) More generally, let $D$ be an (associative) algebra over $k$, let $\mathcal D:E\to D$ be an injective linear map of $E$ into $D$. Show that the element
> $$
> \sum_i\mathcal D(v_i)\mathcal D(v_i')=\omega_{B,\mathcal D}
> $$
> is independent of the choice of basis. We call it the **Casimir element in $D$**, determined by $\mathcal D$ and $B$.
>
> **Printed remark.** The terminology of the Casimir element is determined by the classical case, when $G$ is a Lie group, $E=\mathfrak g=\operatorname{Lie}(G)$ is the Lie algebra of $G$ (tangent space at the origin with the Lie algebra product determined by the Lie derivative), and $\mathcal D(v)$ is the differential operator associated with $v$ (Lie derivative in the direction of $v$). The Casimir element is then a partial differential operator in the algebra of all differential operators on $G$. Cf. basic books on manifolds and Lie theory, for instance [JoL 01], Chapter II, §1 and Chapter VII, §2.

## Hints

> [!hint]- Hint 1
> Use $B$ to turn the second tensor factor into a linear functional. Under the resulting map $E\otimes_k E\to\operatorname{End}_k(E)$, identify the image of the proposed Casimir element.

> [!hint]- Hint 2
> The map $x\otimes y\mapsto(z\mapsto xB(y,z))$ is an isomorphism and sends $\sum_i v_i\otimes v_i'$ to $\operatorname{id}_E$. Obtain (b) and (c) by applying fixed linear maps to this tensor.

## Solution

> [!success]- Independent derivation
> All tensor products are over $k$. Nondegeneracy says that
> $$
> b:E\longrightarrow E^\vee,\qquad b(y)(z)=B(y,z)
> $$
> is injective; equal finite dimensions make it an isomorphism. Symmetry gives $b(v_i')(v_j)=B(v_i',v_j)=\delta_{ij}$, so the $b(v_i')$ are the ordinary dual basis of the $v_i$.
>
> **(a) The tensor representing the identity.** Consider
> $$
> \Phi_B:E\otimes E\longrightarrow\operatorname{End}_k(E),
> \qquad\Phi_B(x\otimes y)(z)=xB(y,z).
> $$
> It is well-defined because the displayed expression is bilinear in $x,y$. For any basis $e_1,\ldots,e_n$ of $E$ and its ordinary dual basis $\varepsilon_1,\ldots,\varepsilon_n$, the maps $z\mapsto e_i\varepsilon_j(z)$ form a basis of $\operatorname{End}_k(E)$: these are exactly the matrix units in that basis. Therefore the canonical map $E\otimes E^\vee\to\operatorname{End}_k(E)$ is an isomorphism. Composing it with $\operatorname{id}_E\otimes b$ proves that $\Phi_B$ is an isomorphism.
>
> If $z=\sum_i a_i v_i$, then $B(v_i',z)=a_i$. Consequently
> $$
> \Phi_B\left(\sum_i v_i\otimes v_i'\right)(z)
> =\sum_i v_iB(v_i',z)=z.
> $$
> Thus
> $$
> C_B:=\sum_i v_i\otimes v_i'=\Phi_B^{-1}(\operatorname{id}_E).
> $$
> Both $\Phi_B$ and $\operatorname{id}_E$ are specified without a basis, proving the assertion.
>
> **(b) The image in the symmetric algebra.** The symmetric algebra is the quotient of the tensor algebra $T(E)=\bigoplus_{r\ge0}E^{\otimes r}$ by the ideal generated by $x\otimes y-y\otimes x$. Its degree-two quotient map
> $$
> q:E\otimes E\longrightarrow S^2(E)\subset S(E),
> \qquad q(x\otimes y)=xy
> $$
> is fixed independently of a basis. Hence $Q_B=q(C_B)$ is basis independent.
>
> **(c) The image in an associative algebra.** Multiplication in $D$ is $k$-bilinear, so it induces a linear map $\mu_D:D\otimes D\to D$. Define
> $$
> \Psi_{\mathcal D}=\mu_D\circ(\mathcal D\otimes\mathcal D):E\otimes E\longrightarrow D.
> $$
> It depends only on $D$ and $\mathcal D$. Therefore
> $$
> \omega_{B,\mathcal D}=\Psi_{\mathcal D}(C_B)
> =\sum_i\mathcal D(v_i)\mathcal D(v_i')
> $$
> is basis independent. This argument preserves the order of the two factors and does not require $D$ to be commutative. It proves the result even without injectivity of $\mathcal D$, although injectivity is part of the printed question.

## Notes

- Source: [S2, Ch. XVI, Exercise 14, printed pp. 639-640, PDF pp. 654-655], visually verified. All three parts and the printed Lie-theoretic remark are retained. The solution is an independent finite-dimensional tensor argument; it does not use the book referenced as [JoL 01] in the remark.
- No restriction on the characteristic is needed. If $E=0$, the empty sum gives $C_B=Q_B=\omega_{B,\mathcal D}=0$, consistently with the same proof.
- Basis independence is not centrality in $D$. Central Casimir elements in universal enveloping algebras require additional Lie-algebra hypotheses and a separate argument.
- The term “Casimir polynomial” here means an element of $S(E)$. It is distinct in type from the original bilinear form on $E$; no identification with a quadratic function on $E$ is being assumed.
