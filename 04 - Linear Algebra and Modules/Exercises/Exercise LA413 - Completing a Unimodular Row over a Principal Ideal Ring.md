# Exercise LA413: Completing a Unimodular Row over a Principal Ideal Ring

## Problem Statement

> [!question] Lang, Chapter XIII, Exercise 25
> Let $a_{11},\ldots,a_{1n}$ be elements from a principal ideal ring, and assume that they generate the unit ideal. Suppose $n>1$. Show that there exists a matrix $(a_{ij})$ with this given first row, and whose determinant is equal to $1$.

## Hints

> [!hint]- Hint 1: Use the principal-ring elementary-divisor theorem
> Smith reduction of a $1\times n$ matrix over a principal ideal ring sends the row to $(g,0,\ldots,0)$, where $(g)$ is the ideal generated by its entries.

> [!hint]- Hint 2: Correct the determinant last
> Once the row is $(1,0,\ldots,0)$, invert the column operation. The second row can be scaled by a unit to make the determinant $1$ without changing the first row.

## Solution

> [!success]- Solution
> Put $a=(a_{11},\ldots,a_{1n})$. By the elementary-divisor (Smith reduction) theorem for a commutative principal ideal ring, there are a unit $u\in R^\times$ and a matrix $Q\in GL_n(R)$ such that
>
> $$
> uaQ=(g,0,\ldots,0),
> $$
>
> where $(g)$ is the ideal generated by all entries of $a$. The row is unimodular, so $(g)=R$ and $g$ is a unit. Multiplying the first column of $Q$ by $ug^{-1}$ gives $Q'\in GL_n(R)$ with $aQ'=(1,0,\ldots,0)$. Set $M_0=(Q')^{-1}$. Its first row is $a$, and its determinant $\delta$ is a unit. Since $n>1$, multiplying the second row of $M_0$ by $\delta^{-1}$ leaves the first row unchanged and produces a matrix $M$ with determinant $1$. Thus
>
> $$
> a=(1,0,\ldots,0)M,
> $$
>
> so $a$ is exactly the first row of $M$.

## Notes

- **Routing:** The construction uses determinant-one column operations on a row, so its principal tool is matrix reduction.
- **Source and proof status:** [S2, Ch. XIII, Ex. 25, printed p. 547, PDF p. 562]. The source statement was checked against the original image. The proof applies the named elementary-divisor theorem for commutative principal ideal rings, then performs the determinant correction explicitly.
- **Scope:** Lang says “principal ideal ring,” which can have zero divisors. A naive two-entry Bézout matrix may fail to have determinant $1$ if one cancels a nonunit gcd; Smith reduction avoids that cancellation.
