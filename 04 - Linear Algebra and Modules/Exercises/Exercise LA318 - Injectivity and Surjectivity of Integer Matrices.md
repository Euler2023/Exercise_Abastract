# Exercise LA318: Injectivity and Surjectivity of Integer Matrices

## Problem Statement

> [!question] Exercise 2.3
> Let $A$ be the matrix of a homomorphism $\varphi:\mathbb Z^n\to\mathbb Z^m$ of free $\mathbb Z$-modules.
>
> **(a)** Prove that $\varphi$ is injective if and only if the rank of $A$, as a real matrix, is $n$.
>
> **(b)** Prove that $\varphi$ is surjective if and only if the greatest common divisor of the determinants of the $m\times m$ minors of $A$ is $1$.

## Hints

> [!hint]- Hint 1
> A nonzero vector in the rational kernel can be cleared of denominators.

> [!hint]- Hint 2
> Use Smith normal form and the determinantal divisor $d_1\cdots d_m$.

## Solution

> [!success]- Solution
> **(a)** If the real rank is $n$, the real kernel is zero, so the integer kernel is zero. Conversely, if the real rank is smaller than $n$, row reduction over $\mathbb Q$ gives a nonzero vector in $\ker A\cap\mathbb Q^n$. Clearing denominators produces a nonzero integer vector in the kernel. Thus injectivity is equivalent to real column rank $n$.
>
> **(b)** Choose unimodular matrices putting $A$ into Smith normal form
>
> $$
> UAV=\operatorname{diag}(d_1,\ldots,d_r,0,\ldots),
> \qquad d_1\mid\cdots\mid d_r.
> $$
>
> The map is surjective exactly when $r=m$ and every $d_i=1$. Unimodular row and column operations preserve the ideal generated by all $m\times m$ minors. For the Smith form, that ideal is generated by
>
> $$
> d_1d_2\cdots d_m
> $$
>
> when $r=m$, and is zero when $r<m$. Hence the gcd of the maximal minors is $1$ exactly when $r=m$ and all invariant factors are $1$, which is exactly surjectivity.

## Notes

- **Routing:** Module Theory is primary because the matrix represents a homomorphism of free abelian groups and Smith invariants control its cokernel.
- **Empty-minor convention:** If $n<m$, there are no nonzero $m\times m$ minors and the criterion correctly fails.
