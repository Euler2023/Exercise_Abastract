# Exercise LA370: Modules as Direct Limits of Finitely Generated Submodules

## Problem Statement

> [!question] Exercise 24
> Show that any module is a direct limit of finitely generated submodules.

## Hints

> [!hint]- Hint 1
> Order the finitely generated submodules of $M$ by inclusion.

> [!hint]- Hint 2
> The sum of two finitely generated submodules is finitely generated, and every element of $M$ lies in a cyclic submodule.

## Solution

> [!success]- Independently derived solution
> Let $\mathcal I$ be the set of finitely generated submodules of $M$, ordered by inclusion. This is directed: if $P,Q\in\mathcal I$, then
>
> $$
> P,Q\subseteq P+Q,
> $$
>
> and $P+Q$ is finitely generated by the union of finite generating sets for $P$ and $Q$.
>
> For $P\subseteq Q$, use the inclusion $P\hookrightarrow Q$ as the transition map. The inclusions $P\hookrightarrow M$ form a compatible family, hence induce a homomorphism
>
> $$
> \Phi:\varinjlim_{P\in\mathcal I}P\longrightarrow M.
> $$
>
> The map is surjective because every $m\in M$ belongs to the cyclic submodule $Rm$, which is an object of $\mathcal I$.
>
> To prove injectivity, let a class represented by $x\in P$ map to zero in $M$. The transition map $P\hookrightarrow M$ is literally inclusion, so $x=0$ already in $P$. Hence its direct-limit class is zero. Therefore $\Phi$ is injective.
>
> We have obtained the canonical isomorphism
>
> $$
> M\cong\varinjlim_{P\subseteq M\,\text{finitely generated}}P.
> $$
