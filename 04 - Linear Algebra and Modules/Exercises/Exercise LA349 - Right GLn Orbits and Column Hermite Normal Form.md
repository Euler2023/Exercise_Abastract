# Exercise LA349: Right GLn Orbits and Column Hermite Normal Form

## Problem Statement

> [!question] Miscellaneous Problem M.6
> **(a)** Describe the orbits of right multiplication by $G=\operatorname{GL}_2(\mathbb Z)$ on the space of $2\times2$ integer matrices.
>
> **(b)** Show that for any integer matrix $A$, there is an invertible integer matrix $P$ such that $AP$ has the following “Hermitian normal form”:
>
> $$
> \begin{pmatrix}
> d_1&0&0&0&\cdots\\
> a_2&d_2&0&0&\cdots\\
> a_3&b_3&d_3&0&\cdots\\
> \vdots&&&\ddots
> \end{pmatrix},
> $$
>
> where the entries are nonnegative, $a_2<d_2$, $a_3,b_3<d_3$, and so on.

> [!warning] Terminology issue
> The printed phrase is “Hermitian normal form.” The standard name for this integer-matrix form is **Hermite normal form**.

## Hints

> [!hint]- Hint 1
> Right multiplication performs invertible integer column operations and preserves the subgroup generated by the columns.

## Solution

> [!success]- Solution
> **(a)** The orbit of a matrix $A$ is determined by its column lattice
>
> $$
> \Lambda_A=A\mathbb Z^2\subseteq\mathbb Z^2.
> $$
>
> Right multiplication changes the ordered generating pair but not this subgroup. Conversely, two full-rank matrices with the same column lattice are two bases of that lattice and differ on the right by an element of $\operatorname{GL}_2(\mathbb Z)$. In rank one, each matrix is a primitive generating row of coefficients times the generator of its column subgroup, and $\operatorname{GL}_2(\mathbb Z)$ acts transitively on primitive rows. Rank zero is immediate. Thus
>
> $$
> \boxed{A\text{ and }B\text{ are in the same right orbit iff }A\mathbb Z^2=B\mathbb Z^2.}
> $$
>
> Equivalently, the unique column Hermite normal form is a canonical representative of each orbit.
>
> **(b)** Apply the extended Euclidean algorithm through column operations to the first row, replacing it by $(d_1,0,\ldots)$, where $d_1$ is its nonnegative entry gcd. Recurse on the submatrix formed by the remaining rows and columns. This produces a lower-triangular echelon matrix with nonnegative pivots.
>
> Finally, working from the last pivot upward, add multiples of pivot column $i$ to each earlier column. Because pivot column $i$ is zero above row $i$, this does not disturb earlier rows, and Euclidean division makes every entry to the left of $d_i$ lie in the unique range $0\le a<d_i$. All operations are right multiplication by elementary unimodular matrices; their product is $P\in\operatorname{GL}_n(\mathbb Z)$. The remainder conditions also prove uniqueness by induction.
