# Exercise LA486: Semisimple Operators and Squarefree Minimal Polynomials

## Problem Statement

> [!question] Lang, Chapter XVII, Exercise 10
> Let $E$ be a finite-dimensional vector space over a field $k$. Let $A\in\operatorname{End}_k(E)$. We say that $A$ is **semisimple** if $E$ is a semisimple $A$-space, or equivalently, let $R$ be the $k$-algebra generated by $A$, then $E$ is semisimple over $R$. Show that $A$ is semisimple if and only if its minimal polynomial has no factors of multiplicity $>1$ over $k$.

Here “no factors of multiplicity $>1$ over $k$” means that each monic irreducible factor in $k[X]$ has exponent one. Over an imperfect field this does not assert that the minimal polynomial has distinct roots in an algebraic closure.

## Hints

> [!hint]- Hint 1: Regard $E$ as a polynomial module
> Give $E$ a $k[X]$-module structure by $Xv=Av$. If its minimal polynomial is $m_A$, then $k[A]\cong k[X]/(m_A)$.

> [!hint]- Hint 2: Use simple polynomial modules in one direction and CRT in the other
> A simple $k[X]$-module occurring in $E$ is cyclic and isomorphic to $k[X]/(p)$ for an irreducible polynomial $p$. Conversely, if $m_A$ is a product of distinct irreducibles, the Chinese remainder theorem expresses $k[A]$ as a product of fields.

## Solution

> [!success]- Independent proof over an arbitrary field
> Assume first that $E\ne0$. Make $E$ a $k[X]$-module by $f(X)v=f(A)v$. Its submodules are exactly its $k[A]$-submodules, because invariance under $A$ implies invariance under every polynomial in $A$. The kernel of the algebra map $k[X]\to\operatorname{End}_k(E)$ is generated by the monic minimal polynomial $m_A$, and its image is $R=k[A]$. Thus $R\cong k[X]/(m_A)$.
>
> **Semisimplicity implies distinct irreducible factors.** If $E$ is semisimple, write $E=V_1\oplus\cdots\oplus V_t$ with each $V_i$ simple. There are finitely many summands because they are nonzero $k$-subspaces and $E$ is finite-dimensional. Choose $0\ne v_i\in V_i$. Simplicity gives $V_i=k[X]v_i$. The surjection $k[X]\to V_i$, $f\mapsto f(A)v_i$, has a maximal ideal as kernel. Since $V_i$ is finite-dimensional, this kernel is nonzero, and it has the form $(p_i)$ with $p_i$ monic irreducible. Therefore
>
> $$
> V_i\cong k[X]/(p_i).
> $$
>
> A polynomial annihilates $E$ exactly when it annihilates every $V_i$. Hence
>
> $$
> (m_A)=\bigcap_{i=1}^t(p_i)
> =\left(\prod_{p\in\{p_1,\ldots,p_t\}}p\right),
> $$
>
> where the product includes each distinct polynomial once. The last equality follows from coprimality of distinct irreducible polynomials and the Chinese remainder theorem. Monicity now gives the required factorization of $m_A$.
>
> **Distinct irreducible factors imply semisimplicity.** Suppose $m_A=p_1\cdots p_s$ with the $p_i$ distinct monic irreducibles. The Chinese remainder theorem gives
>
> $$
> R\cong\prod_{i=1}^s K_i,\qquad K_i=k[X]/(p_i).
> $$
>
> Let $e_i\in R$ be the central idempotent corresponding to $1$ in the $i$th field and $0$ elsewhere. Since $1=\sum_i e_i$ and $e_ie_j=0$ for $i\ne j$,
>
> $$
> E=\bigoplus_{i=1}^s e_iE.
> $$
>
> Each $e_iE$ is a vector space over $K_i$. Choose a $K_i$-basis. Each one-dimensional $K_i$-span of a basis vector is a simple $R$-module: its nonzero $R$-submodules are nonzero $K_i$-subspaces of a one-dimensional space. These decompositions express $E$ as a direct sum of simple modules. Thus $A$ is semisimple.
>
> If $E=0$, it is the empty direct sum of simple modules. With the usual convention $m_A=1$ for the zero space, the polynomial condition also holds. This case makes no assertion that its zero image algebra is a nonzero semisimple ring in Lang's ring convention.

## Notes

- **Source and proof status:** [S2, Ch. XVII, Ex. 10, printed p. 662, PDF p. 677] was checked on the original page image. The cyclic-simple-module and Chinese-remainder arguments are independent derivations. The named algebraic inputs are the ideal classification in $k[X]$ and the Chinese remainder theorem.
- **Inseparable example:** Let $k=\mathbb F_p(t)$ and $L=k(u)$ with $u^p=t$. The polynomial $X^p-t$ is irreducible over $k$ and has derivative zero. For example, taking $t=u^p$ with $u$ transcendental, independence of the exponent classes modulo $p$ proves that $1,u,\ldots,u^{p-1}$ are linearly independent over $\mathbb F_p(u^p)$, while the displayed relation shows that they span $L$. Multiplication by $u$ on the $k$-space $L$ has minimal polynomial $X^p-t$ and is semisimple because $k[A]=L$ acts on its one-dimensional regular module. Nevertheless, over an algebraic closure the polynomial becomes $(X-u)^p$, and the extended operator is not diagonalizable.
- **Terminology:** The title uses “squarefree” in the factorization sense inside $k[X]$. Separability, or becoming diagonalizable over an algebraic closure, is a stronger condition over imperfect fields.
