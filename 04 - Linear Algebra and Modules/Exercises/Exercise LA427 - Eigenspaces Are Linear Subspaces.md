# Exercise LA427: Eigenspaces Are Linear Subspaces

## Problem Statement

> [!question] Lang, Chapter XIV, Exercise 11
> Let $E$ be a finite-dimensional space over the field $k$. Let $\alpha\in k$. Let $E_\alpha$ be the subspace of $E$ generated by all eigenvectors of a given endomorphism $A$ of $E$, having $\alpha$ as an eigenvalue. Show that every non-zero element of $E_\alpha$ is an eigenvector of $A$ having $\alpha$ as an eigenvalue.

## Hints

> [!hint]- Hint 1
> Write an element of $E_\alpha$ as a finite linear combination of eigenvectors belonging to $\alpha$.

> [!hint]- Hint 2
> Apply $A$ to this linear combination and use linearity. Equivalently, identify $E_\alpha$ with the kernel of $A-\alpha I$.

## Solution

> [!success]- Independent derivation
> Let $x\in E_\alpha$. By the definition of a span, there are eigenvectors $v_1,\ldots,v_r$ with $Av_i=\alpha v_i$ and scalars $c_1,\ldots,c_r\in k$ such that $x=\sum_i c_iv_i$. Linearity gives
>
> $$
> Ax=\sum_{i=1}^r c_iAv_i
> =\sum_{i=1}^r c_i\alpha v_i
> =\alpha x.
> $$
>
> If $x\ne0$, this is exactly the assertion that $x$ is an eigenvector with eigenvalue $\alpha$.
>
> More precisely, every generating eigenvector belongs to $\ker(A-\alpha I)$, and this kernel is a subspace, so $E_\alpha\subseteq\ker(A-\alpha I)$. Conversely, each nonzero vector of this kernel is one of the eigenvectors used to generate $E_\alpha$, while its zero vector belongs to every subspace. Therefore
>
> $$
> E_\alpha=\ker(A-\alpha I).
> $$

## Notes

- **Source and proof status:** The statement was checked against the original page image at [S2, Ch. XIV, Ex. 11, printed p. 568, PDF p. 583]. The solution is an independent derivation from linearity and the definition of an eigenvector.
- **Boundary:** If $\alpha$ is not an eigenvalue, the generating set is empty and $E_\alpha=0$, so the requested assertion is vacuous. Zero is included in the eigenspace but is never an eigenvector. The argument also works without the finite-dimensional hypothesis.
