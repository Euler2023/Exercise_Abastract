# Exercise LA359: Torsion-Free Modules over Dedekind Rings Are Projective

## Problem Statement

> [!question] Exercise 11
> Let $\mathfrak o$ be a Dedekind ring with quotient field $K$. Let $M$ be a finitely generated torsion-free module over $\mathfrak o$. Prove that $M$ is projective.

## Hints

> [!hint]- Hint 1
> For every prime ideal $\mathfrak p$, the localized ring $\mathfrak o_{\mathfrak p}$ is principal, and $M_{\mathfrak p}$ is finitely generated and torsion-free over it.

> [!hint]- Source hint, localized splittings
> Choose a finite free module $F$ and a surjection $f:F\to M$. Split each localized map $f_{\mathfrak p}:F_{\mathfrak p}\to M_{\mathfrak p}$, then clear denominators to obtain $c_{\mathfrak p}\notin\mathfrak p$ and an $\mathfrak o$-linear map $h_{\mathfrak p}:M\to F$ satisfying
>
> $$
> f\circ h_{\mathfrak p}=c_{\mathfrak p}\operatorname{id}_M.
> $$

> [!hint]- Hint 3
> The elements $c_{\mathfrak p}$ generate the unit ideal. Take a finite linear combination equal to $1$ and combine the corresponding local splittings.

## Solution

> [!success]- Solution
> Choose generators of $M$. They give a surjection
>
> $$
> f:F\longrightarrow M
> $$
>
> from a finite free $\mathfrak o$-module $F$.
>
> Fix a prime ideal $\mathfrak p$. The localization $\mathfrak o_{\mathfrak p}$ is a principal ring, and $M_{\mathfrak p}$ remains finitely generated and torsion-free. The structure theorem over a principal ring therefore makes $M_{\mathfrak p}$ free, hence projective. Consequently the localized surjection has an $\mathfrak o_{\mathfrak p}$-linear splitting
>
> $$
> g_{\mathfrak p}:M_{\mathfrak p}\longrightarrow F_{\mathfrak p},
> \qquad
> f_{\mathfrak p}\circ g_{\mathfrak p}
> =\operatorname{id}_{M_{\mathfrak p}}.
> $$
>
> Restrict $g_{\mathfrak p}$ along $M\to M_{\mathfrak p}$. Because $M$ is finitely generated, one can clear the finitely many denominators occurring in the images of a generating set. Thus there is
>
> $$
> c_{\mathfrak p}\in\mathfrak o\setminus\mathfrak p
> $$
>
> such that
>
> $$
> c_{\mathfrak p}g_{\mathfrak p}(M)\subseteq F.
> $$
>
> Define
>
> $$
> h_{\mathfrak p}
> =c_{\mathfrak p}g_{\mathfrak p}|_M:M\longrightarrow F.
> $$
>
> Inside $M_{\mathfrak p}$ one has
>
> $$
> f\circ h_{\mathfrak p}
> =c_{\mathfrak p}\operatorname{id}_M.
> $$
>
> Both sides actually take values in $M$, and $M\to M_{\mathfrak p}$ is injective because $M$ is torsion-free, so this equality holds in $M$.
>
> Let $J$ be the ideal generated by all $c_{\mathfrak p}$. If $J$ were proper, it would be contained in a maximal ideal $\mathfrak m$. But $c_{\mathfrak m}\notin\mathfrak m$, a contradiction. Hence $J=\mathfrak o$. Therefore finitely many primes $\mathfrak p_1,\ldots,\mathfrak p_r$ and elements $x_1,\ldots,x_r\in\mathfrak o$ satisfy
>
> $$
> \sum_{i=1}^r x_i c_{\mathfrak p_i}=1.
> $$
>
> Set
>
> $$
> g=\sum_{i=1}^r x_i h_{\mathfrak p_i}:M\longrightarrow F.
> $$
>
> Then
>
> $$
> f\circ g
> =\sum_{i=1}^r x_i(f\circ h_{\mathfrak p_i})
> =\left(\sum_{i=1}^r x_i c_{\mathfrak p_i}\right)
> \operatorname{id}_M
> =\operatorname{id}_M.
> $$
>
> Thus $f$ splits, so $M$ is a direct summand of the finite free module $F$. Hence $M$ is projective.
