# Complex Tori and Isogenies

## Definition

> [!info] Complex torus and isogeny
> A lattice is $\Lambda=\omega_1\mathbb Z\oplus\omega_2\mathbb Z\subset\mathbb C$ with $\omega_1,\omega_2$ linearly independent over $\mathbb R$. Use the orientation convention $\omega_1/\omega_2\in\mathcal H$. The one-dimensional complex torus $E=\mathbb C/\Lambda$ has addition $(z+\Lambda)+(w+\Lambda)=z+w+\Lambda$.
>
> An isogeny $E\to E'=\mathbb C/\Lambda'$ is a nonzero holomorphic group homomorphism. It has the form $z+\Lambda\mapsto mz+\Lambda'$ with $m\ne0$ and $m\Lambda\subseteq\Lambda'$. An isomorphism has $m\Lambda=\Lambda'$.

## Intuition

A fundamental parallelogram has opposite sides identified. Enlarging its lattice identifies more points and produces a finite quotient of the torus. A scalar homothety changes the lattice presentation while giving an isomorphic torus. A general holomorphic map may also translate the origin; group homomorphisms must preserve it.

## Key Properties

1. **Compact complex curve.** The image of a closed fundamental parallelogram covers the quotient, so the torus is compact and connected. Lattice discreteness gives local complex coordinates by projection of sufficiently small discs.
2. **Affine form of maps.** Proposition 1.3.2 gives every holomorphic map as $z\mapsto mz+b$ with $m\Lambda\subseteq\Lambda'$. Its proof uses the covering-space lifting theorem: a lift to $\mathbb C$ has lattice-periodic derivative, hence bounded derivative and therefore constant derivative by Liouville's theorem. It is a group map exactly when $b\in\Lambda'$, equivalently its value at zero is zero. The proof of that equivalence is Exercise 1.3.2, independently established in this vault.
3. **Kernel and degree.** For $m\ne0$, every point of $E'$ has preimage obtained by dividing a representative by $m$, and
   $$
   \ker\varphi=m^{-1}\Lambda'/\Lambda,\qquad
   \deg\varphi=[\Lambda':m\Lambda].
   $$
   The index is finite because both lattices have real rank two. Fibres are translates of the kernel, so this index is the common number of points in a fibre. If $(m\omega_1,m\omega_2)^t=A(\omega'_1,\omega'_2)^t$ for oriented bases, then $A\in M_2(\mathbb Z)$ and the index is $\det A>0$ (the lattice index/determinant theorem).
4. **Torsion.** For $N\ge1$,
   $$
   E[N]=(N^{-1}\Lambda)/\Lambda\cong(\mathbb Z/N\mathbb Z)^2.
   $$
   The coefficients of $\omega_1/N,\omega_2/N$ give the isomorphism; hence multiplication by $N$ has degree $N^2$.
5. **Weil pairing convention.** Write $P=(a\omega_1+b\omega_2)/N$, $Q=(c\omega_1+d\omega_2)/N$ modulo $\Lambda$. Then
   $$
   e_N(P,Q)=\exp\left(\frac{2\pi i(ad-bc)}N\right).
   $$
   An oriented basis change has determinant $1$, preserving this expression. Bilinearity and alternation follow from the determinant, and nondegeneracy follows by pairing a nonzero vector with the two coordinate vectors. On the same torus, $e_N(dP,dQ)=e_{dN}(P,Q)^d$ for $P,Q\in E[dN]$: after multiplication by $d$ the denominator changes from $dN$ to $N$ while the coefficient rows stay the same. Scalar isomorphisms preserve the pairing.

## Examples

- $E_\tau=\mathbb C/(\tau\mathbb Z+\mathbb Z)$ for $\tau\in\mathcal H$. Scaling by $1/\omega_2$ puts every torus in this form.
- Multiplication by $N$ is an isogeny of $E_\tau$ with kernel generated by $\tau/N$ and $1/N$.
- Quotienting by $\langle1/N\rangle$ replaces $\Lambda_\tau$ by $\tau\mathbb Z+(1/N)\mathbb Z$. Scaling the latter lattice by $N$ identifies the quotient with $E_{N\tau}$.
- Multiplication by $i$ preserves $i\mathbb Z+\mathbb Z$, giving an endomorphism not arising from an integer scalar.
