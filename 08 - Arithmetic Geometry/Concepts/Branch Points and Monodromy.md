# Branch Points and Monodromy

## Definition

> [!info] Branched covering and branch point
> For a polynomial $f(t,x)\in\mathbb C[t,x]$ of degree $n$ in $x$, the projection
> $$
> X=\{(t,x):f(t,x)=0\}\longrightarrow\mathbb C_t,
> \qquad(t,x)\longmapsto t,
> $$
> is generically an $n$-sheeted covering. A value $p$ is a **branch candidate** when the fibre has fewer than $n$ distinct roots, usually detected by
> $$
> \operatorname{Res}_x(f,f_x)(p)=0.
> $$
> It is a **true branch point** when analytic continuation around $p$ permutes the sheets nontrivially.

> [!info] Monodromy or gluing permutation
> Choose a base point away from the branch values and number the $n$ roots there. Continuing the roots around a counterclockwise loop about $p_\nu$ produces a permutation
> $$
> \sigma_\nu\in S_n.
> $$
> These permutations are the gluing data for the cut-and-paste model of the covering.

## Intuition

Cut the $t$-plane from each branch point to infinity. Above the cut plane, the multivalued roots separate into $n$ single-valued sheets. If $\sigma_\nu(i)=j$, side A of the cut on sheet $i$ is glued to side B on sheet $j$.

## Key Properties

1. Renumbering the sheets by $\rho\in S_n$ replaces every $\sigma_\nu$ by $\rho^{-1}\sigma_\nu\rho$. Only the simultaneous-conjugacy class is intrinsic.
2. The covering is connected exactly when the subgroup generated by the $\sigma_\nu$ acts transitively on $\{1,\ldots,n\}$.
3. If $x_0$ is a $k$-fold root of $f(p,x)$ and $f_t(p,x_0)\ne0$, the local monodromy contains a $k$-cycle.
4. A repeated fibre root need not be a true branch point when the affine curve is singular. The local normalization may have separate analytic branches and trivial monodromy.
5. The monodromy around a large circle records ramification at infinity and constrains the product of the finite permutations.

## Examples

> [!example] Square root
> For $x^2-t=0$, the only finite branch point is $t=0$, and the monodromy is $(12)$.

> [!example] Cubic root
> For $x^3-t=0$, a positive loop around $0$ multiplies a chosen root by $e^{2\pi i/3}$, giving the $3$-cycle $(123)$.

> [!example] False branch candidate
> In Exercise AG11(h), $t=1$ gives a repeated fibre root but a nodal local equation. The two normalized branches return to themselves, so the permutation is the identity.

## Theorems and Proof Status

> [!abstract] Riemann Existence Theorem
> Isomorphism classes of degree-$n$ extensions of $\mathbb C(t)$ correspond to connected $n$-sheeted branched coverings of the complex $t$-plane.
>
> **Source status:** S1 states this theorem at printed p. 465, PDF p. 477, and explicitly says that its general proof is too long to include. In this vault it is therefore an external standard input except for the independently proved quadratic case in Exercise AG13.
