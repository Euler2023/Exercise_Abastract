# Local Langlands Correspondence

## Introduction

The **local Langlands correspondence** (LLC) relates irreducible admissible representations of a reductive group over a local field to $L$-parameters. It is a theorem for $\mathrm{GL}_n$ over every local field, while for an arbitrary reductive group its precise form and proof status depend on the group, the field, and the normalization.

> [!note] Scope used in S11
> Getz–Hahn quote a complete theorem for $\mathrm{GL}_n$, then formulate the general correspondence only for **tempered representations of quasi-split reductive groups**. The broader non-tempered and non-quasi-split theories require additional refinements. [S11, §§12.4–12.5, printed pp. 327–333, PDF pp. 338–344]

## Setup

### Local Fields

Let $F$ be a **local field**:
- **Non-archimedean**: $\mathbb{Q}_p$, finite extensions, $\mathbb{F}_q((t))$
- **Archimedean**: $\mathbb{R}$, $\mathbb{C}$

### The Weil Group

> [!info] Definition (Weil Group)
> For a **non-archimedean** local field $F$ with residue field $\mathbb F_q$, the Weil group $W_F$ fits in:
> $$
> 1 \to I_F \to W_F \to \mathbb{Z} \to 0
> $$
>
> where $I_F$ is the inertia group and the quotient is generated by a choice of arithmetic or geometric Frobenius. The archimedean Weil groups are defined separately.
>
> For $p$-adic $F$: $W_F \subset \text{Gal}(\overline{F}/F)$ is a dense subgroup.

### Weil-Deligne Representations

> [!info] Definition
> A **Weil-Deligne representation** of $W_F$ is a pair $(\rho, N)$ where:
> - $\rho: W_F \to \text{GL}(V)$ is continuous and has open kernel on inertia (with the usual Frobenius-semisimplicity imposed when classifying parameters)
> - $N: V \to V$ is nilpotent (the **monodromy operator**)
> - $\rho(w)N\rho(w)^{-1}=|w|N$ for all $w\in W_F$, in one standard norm/Frobenius convention; reversing Frobenius reverses the norm factor

These capture the "motivic" Galois representations at a prime.

## The Correspondence for GLₙ

### Statement

> [!abstract] Theorem 12.4.1 (Local Langlands for $\mathrm{GL}_n$)
>
> For every local field $F$, there is a unique local Langlands correspondence
> $$
> \text{rec}_F: \mathcal{A}_n(F) \xrightarrow{\sim} \mathcal{G}_n(F)
> $$
>
> where $\mathcal A_n(F)$ is the set of isomorphism classes of irreducible admissible representations of $\mathrm{GL}_n(F)$ and $\mathcal G_n(F)$ is the set of $\mathrm{GL}_n(\mathbb C)$-conjugacy classes of Frobenius-semisimple $L$-parameters $W_F'\to{}^L\mathrm{GL}_n$.
>
> The archimedean case goes back to Langlands; the positive-characteristic case is due to Laumon–Rapoport–Stuhler; the mixed-characteristic case is due to Harris–Taylor and Henniart. [S11, Theorem 12.4.1, printed p. 327, PDF p. 338]

### Characterization

The correspondence is uniquely characterized by:

1. **L-function matching**:
$$
L(s, \pi \times \pi') = L(s, \text{rec}(\pi) \otimes \text{rec}(\pi'))
$$

2. **ε-factor matching**:
$$
\varepsilon(s, \pi \times \pi', \psi) = \varepsilon(s, \text{rec}(\pi) \otimes \text{rec}(\pi'), \psi)
$$

3. **Compatibility with class field theory** for $n = 1$

4. **Central character**: $\omega_\pi = \det \circ \text{rec}(\pi)$ via class field theory

## Examples

### n = 1: Local Class Field Theory

> [!example] GL₁ case
> The correspondence is the **local Artin map**:
> $$
> F^\times \xrightarrow{\sim} W_F^{\text{ab}}
> $$
>
> Characters of $F^\times$ ↔ 1-dimensional Weil representations

### n = 2: Examples

> [!example] Unramified representations
> For $\pi$ unramified (spherical), $\text{rec}(\pi)$ is unramified:
> $$
> \text{rec}(\pi)(\text{Frob}) = \begin{pmatrix} \alpha & 0 \\ 0 & \beta \end{pmatrix}
> $$
>
> where $\alpha, \beta$ are the Satake parameters of $\pi$.

> [!example] Steinberg representation
> The Steinberg representation corresponds to a two-dimensional special Weil–Deligne representation with nonzero monodromy $N$. The precise powers of $|\cdot|$ depend on whether LLC is normalized arithmetically or unitary-normalized.

> [!example] Supercuspidal representations
> Supercuspidal $\pi$ ↔ irreducible Weil representations (no monodromy)

### Classification Table

| $\pi$ on $\text{GL}_2(F)$ | Weil-Deligne rep | Type |
|---------------------------|------------------|------|
| Unramified principal series | Unramified, $N=0$ | Split |
| Ramified principal series | Ramified, $N=0$ | Characters |
| Steinberg (twisted) | $N \neq 0$ | Non-semisimple |
| Supercuspidal | Irreducible, $N=0$ | Not necessarily induced from a quadratic extension |

## General Reductive Groups

### Langlands Parameters

> [!info] Definition (L-parameter)
> A **Langlands parameter** (or L-parameter) for a reductive group $G$ over $F$ is a homomorphism:
> $$
> \phi: W_F \times \text{SL}_2(\mathbb{C}) \to {}^L G
> $$
>
> satisfying certain conditions (admissibility).

The $\text{SL}_2(\mathbb{C})$ factor encodes the monodromy (Jacobson-Morozov).

### L-Packets

> [!info] Definition (L-packet)
> An **L-packet** $\Pi_\phi$ is the (finite) set of irreducible representations of $G(F)$ conjecturally associated to an L-parameter $\phi$.

For $\text{GL}_n$: L-packets are singletons (multiplicity one).
For other groups: L-packets can have multiple elements.

### The Conjecture

> [!abstract] Conjecture 12.5.1 (Tempered quasi-split form)
> If $G$ is quasi-split, there should be a map
> $$
> \operatorname{LL}:\Pi_t(G)\longrightarrow\Phi_t(G)
> $$
>
> with finite nonempty fibers $\Pi(\phi)$. Essential square-integrability and normalized parabolic induction should be compatible with the parameter in the precise manner stated in S11. [S11, Conjecture 12.5.1, printed p. 329, PDF p. 340]

### Status

The correspondence is a theorem for $\mathrm{GL}_n$. S11 records conditional theorems for several classical groups. In particular, for a quasi-split unitary group attached to a quadratic extension $E/F$, Theorem 12.5.7 assumes: (i) $\operatorname{char}F=0$; (ii) Arthur's stated local claims; and (iii) stabilization of the twisted trace formula. Under those hypotheses, parts (a)–(b) of Conjecture 12.5.1 and the component-group parametrization of Conjecture 12.5.4 hold. This is not an unconditional theorem for all reductive groups. [S11, printed pp. 331–332, PDF pp. 342–343]

## Supercuspidal Representations

### Characterization

> [!info] Definition
> $\pi$ is **supercuspidal** if it doesn't appear as a subquotient of any parabolically induced representation.

For $\mathrm{GL}_n$, supercuspidal representations correspond to irreducible Weil representations with $N=0$. For general reductive groups, discreteness of a parameter and supercuspidality of every member of its packet are subtler and are not equivalent without additional hypotheses.

### Construction

Supercuspidals are constructed via:
1. **Compact induction** from open compact subgroups
2. **Type theory** (Bushnell-Kutzko)
3. **Yu's construction** (general reductive groups)

## Depth and Conductors

### Depth

> [!info] Definition
> The **depth** of a representation $\pi$ measures its ramification:
> $$
> \text{depth}(\pi) = \min\{r : \pi^{G_r} \neq 0\}
> $$
>
> where $G_r$ is the Moy-Prasad filtration.

### Conductor

Depth preservation and matching of Artin/conductor exponents are distinct compatibility questions. They are related to ramification but are not the same numerical invariant, so no direct equality is asserted here.

## Archimedean Case

### Real Case

For $F = \mathbb{R}$:
- $W_\mathbb{R} = \mathbb{C}^\times \cup j\mathbb{C}^\times$ where $j^2 = -1$
- Irreducible reps of $\text{GL}_n(\mathbb{R})$ ↔ $n$-dimensional representations of $W_\mathbb{R}$

### Complex Case

For $F = \mathbb{C}$:
- $W_\mathbb{C} = \mathbb{C}^\times$
- The correspondence is simpler (Langlands classification)

## Applications

### To Global Langlands

Local LLC is a key ingredient in:
- Proving global cases (modularity theorems)
- Understanding local-global compatibility
- Computing local factors of L-functions

### To Representation Theory

- Classification of representations
- Character formulas
- Branching rules
