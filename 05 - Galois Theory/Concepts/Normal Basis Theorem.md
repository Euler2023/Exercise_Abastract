# Normal Basis Theorem

## Definition

Let $K/k$ be a finite Galois extension with Galois group $G = \operatorname{Gal}(K/k)$ of order $n = [K:k]$.

> [!info] Normal Basis
> An element $\alpha \in K$ generates a **normal basis** of $K$ over $k$ if the set of its Galois conjugates
>
> $$
> \{\sigma(\alpha) : \sigma \in G\}
> $$
>
> forms a basis of $K$ as a vector space over $k$.

In terms of representation theory, this means that the Galois group $G$ acting on $K$ makes $K$ isomorphic to the regular representation $k[G]$ as a $k[G]$-module.

## The Normal Basis Theorem

> [!abstract] Theorem (Normal Basis Theorem)
> Let $K/k$ be a finite Galois extension with Galois group $G$. Then there exists an element $\alpha \in K$ such that $\{\sigma(\alpha) : \sigma \in G\}$ is a basis of $K$ over $k$.

### Case 1: Finite Fields

When $k = \mathbb F_q$ is a finite field and $K = \mathbb F_{q^n}$, the Galois group $G$ is cyclic generated by the Frobenius automorphism $\sigma: x \mapsto x^q$. The field $K$ is a $k[T]$-module where $T$ acts as $\sigma$. Since $\sigma^n = \operatorname{id}$, the minimal polynomial of $\sigma$ on $K$ divides $T^n - 1$. By the linear independence of characters (Dedekind's lemma), the distinct automorphisms $\operatorname{id}, \sigma, \dots, \sigma^{n-1}$ are linearly independent over $K$, so the minimal polynomial of $\sigma$ is precisely $T^n - 1$, which has degree $n = \dim_k K$.

By the structure theorem for finitely generated modules over the principal ideal domain $k[T]$, $K \cong k[T]/(T^n - 1)$ as a $k[T]$-module. Any generator of this cyclic module corresponds to an element $\alpha \in K$ whose conjugates under $\langle\sigma\rangle$ span $K$, forming a normal basis.

### Case 2: Infinite Fields

When $k$ is infinite, let $G = \{\sigma_1, \dots, \sigma_n\}$ with $\sigma_1 = \operatorname{id}$. Consider the determinant polynomial
$$
\Delta(X_{\sigma})_{\sigma \in G} = \det\left( \sigma_i \sigma_j(X) \right)_{i,j=1}^n \in K[X_{\sigma}].
$$
Using Dedekind's lemma on linear independence of automorphisms and properties of group algebras, one shows that this polynomial is not identically zero. Because $k$ is infinite, a non-identically-zero polynomial over $K$ has non-vanishing evaluations in $k$. Specializing $X$ to an appropriate element $\alpha \in K$ yields a non-zero determinant $\det(\sigma_i \sigma_j(\alpha)) \neq 0$, which implies that the vectors $\{\sigma(\alpha) : \sigma \in G\}$ are linearly independent over $k$.

## Subfield Bases from a Normal Basis

Let $H \le G$ be a subgroup of $G$, and let $F = K^H$ be the fixed field of $H$. Let $\{g_1, \dots, g_m\}$ be a complete set of right coset representatives for $H$ in $G$, where $m = [G:H] = [F:k]$, so that $G = \bigcup_{i=1}^m H g_i$.

For any subset $S \subseteq G$, define the sum operator
$$
S(\alpha) = \sum_{\sigma \in S} \sigma(\alpha).
$$
If $\{\sigma(\alpha) : \sigma \in G\}$ is a normal basis of $K/k$, then for each right coset $H g_i$, the element
$$
(H g_i)(\alpha) = \sum_{\tau \in H} (\tau g_i)(\alpha)
$$
is invariant under every element of $H$ (since $\tau_0 H g_i = H g_i$), and therefore lies in $F$. Moreover, the set
$$
\{(H g_1)(\alpha), (H g_2)(\alpha), \dots, (H g_m)(\alpha)\}
$$
is a basis of $F$ as a vector space over $k$.

## Key Properties

1. **Regular Module:** A normal basis exists if and only if $K \cong k[G]$ as $k[G]$-modules.
2. **Computational Utility:** In cryptography and coding theory over finite fields $\mathbb F_{2^m}$, normal bases are especially valuable because the Frobenius map $\sigma(x) = x^2$ acts simply as a cyclic shift of coordinates.
3. **Trace Non-degeneracy:** If $\alpha$ generates a normal basis, then $\operatorname{Tr}_{K/k}(\alpha) \neq 0$, because otherwise the sum $\sum_{\sigma \in G} \sigma(\alpha) = 0$ would give a non-trivial linear relation over $k$.
