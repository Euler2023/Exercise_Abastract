# Decomposition and Inertia Groups

## Definition

Let $A$ be an integrally closed integral domain with quotient field $K$, and let $L$ be a Galois extension of $K$ with Galois group $G=\operatorname{Gal}(L/K)$. Let $B$ be the integral closure of $A$ in $L$.

Let $\mathfrak p$ be a prime ideal of $A$, and let $\mathfrak P$ be a prime ideal of $B$ lying above $\mathfrak p$ (i.e., $\mathfrak P\cap A=\mathfrak p$).

> [!info] Decomposition Group
> The **decomposition group** (or decomposition subgroup) of $\mathfrak P$ over $K$ is the stabilizer of $\mathfrak P$ in $G$:
> $$
> G_{\mathfrak P} = D(\mathfrak P|K) = \{\sigma\in G : \sigma(\mathfrak P) = \mathfrak P\}.
> $$
> The fixed subfield $L^{G_{\mathfrak P}}$ is called the **decomposition field** of $\mathfrak P$.

> [!info] Residue Class Field Homomorphism and Inertia Group
> Each $\sigma\in G_{\mathfrak P}$ induces a ring automorphism $\bar{\sigma}$ of the residue class field $k_{\mathfrak P}=B/\mathfrak P$ fixing $k_{\mathfrak p}=A/\mathfrak p$, defined by:
> $$
> \bar{\sigma}(x\bmod\mathfrak P) = \sigma(x)\bmod\mathfrak P.
> $$
> The **inertia group** $I_{\mathfrak P}$ of $\mathfrak P$ over $K$ is the kernel of the canonical reduction homomorphism $\mu: G_{\mathfrak P} \to \operatorname{Aut}(k_{\mathfrak P}/k_{\mathfrak p})$:
> $$
> I_{\mathfrak P} = \{\sigma\in G_{\mathfrak P} : \sigma(x)\equiv x\pmod{\mathfrak P}\text{ for all }x\in B\}.
> $$
> The fixed subfield $L^{I_{\mathfrak P}}$ is called the **inertia field** of $\mathfrak P$.

When the residue field extension $k_{\mathfrak P}/k_{\mathfrak p}$ is normal and separable, the homomorphism $\mu$ is surjective, yielding the fundamental exact sequence:
$$
1 \longrightarrow I_{\mathfrak P} \longrightarrow G_{\mathfrak P} \xrightarrow{\quad\mu\quad} \operatorname{Gal}(k_{\mathfrak P}/k_{\mathfrak p}) \longrightarrow 1.
$$

> [!info] Frobenius Automorphism
> If $I_{\mathfrak P}=\{1\}$ (i.e., $\mathfrak P$ is unramified over $\mathfrak p$) and $k_{\mathfrak p}$ is a finite field $\mathbb F_q$, the Galois group $\operatorname{Gal}(k_{\mathfrak P}/k_{\mathfrak p})$ is cyclic, generated by the canonical Frobenius automorphism $\operatorname{Frob}_q: \xi \mapsto \xi^q$.
> The unique element in $G_{\mathfrak P}$ mapped to $\operatorname{Frob}_q$ by $\mu$ is called the **Frobenius element** (or Frobenius automorphism) of $\mathfrak P$, denoted:
> $$
> \sigma_{\mathfrak P} = \left(\frac{L/K}{\mathfrak P}\right)\in G_{\mathfrak P}.
> $$

## Intuition

The decomposition and inertia groups connect the global Galois symmetries of field extensions with local arithmetic at prime ideals. The decomposition group captures symmetries that preserve the local prime; the inertia group measures local ramification (loss of separability or multiple roots upon reduction); and the quotient reflects the Galois group of the residue field.

## Key Properties

1. **Transitivity of Galois Action:** The Galois group $G=\operatorname{Gal}(L/K)$ acts transitively on the set of all prime ideals of $B$ lying above $\mathfrak p$. That is, if $\mathfrak P_1$ and $\mathfrak P_2$ both lie above $\mathfrak p$, there exists $\sigma\in G$ such that $\sigma(\mathfrak P_1)=\mathfrak P_2$.
2. **Conjugacy of Subgroups:** If $\mathfrak P'=\sigma(\mathfrak P)$ for $\sigma\in G$, then:
   $$
   G_{\mathfrak P'} = \sigma G_{\mathfrak P} \sigma^{-1},\qquad I_{\mathfrak P'} = \sigma I_{\mathfrak P} \sigma^{-1}.
   $$
3. **Fundamental Degree Identity:** When $[L:K]<\infty$, let $g$ be the number of prime ideals of $B$ lying above $\mathfrak p$, $e=|I_{\mathfrak P}|$ the ramification index, and $f=[k_{\mathfrak P}:k_{\mathfrak p}]$ the residue class degree. Then:
   $$
   [L:K] = e\cdot f\cdot g,\qquad |G_{\mathfrak P}| = e\cdot f.
   $$
4. **Classification of Ramification:**
   - $\mathfrak p$ is **unramified** if $e=1$, so $I_{\mathfrak P}=\{1\}$ and $G_{\mathfrak P}\cong\operatorname{Gal}(k_{\mathfrak P}/k_{\mathfrak p})$.
   - $\mathfrak p$ is **inert** if $g=1$ and $e=1$, so $f=[L:K]$ and $G_{\mathfrak P}=G$.
   - $\mathfrak p$ is **totally ramified** if $e=[L:K]$, so $f=1$ and $g=1$.
5. **Infinite Galois Generalization:** Transitivity of $G$ on prime ideals lying above $\mathfrak p$ continues to hold for infinite Galois extensions via Zorn's lemma or the compactness of the Krull topology.

## Examples

> [!example] Inert Prime in Quadratic Field
> Consider $K=\mathbb Q$ and $L=\mathbb Q(i)$, with $G=\{1, \tau\}$ where $\tau$ is complex conjugation. The prime $p=3$ remains prime in $\mathbb Z[i]$, so $g=1$, $e=1$, and $f=2$. Here $G_{(3)}=G$, $I_{(3)}=\{1\}$, and the Frobenius automorphism is complex conjugation $\tau$, which induces $a+bi \mapsto (a+bi)^3 = a-bi \pmod 3$.

> [!example] Modulo $p$ Irreducibility and Cyclic Groups
> If a monic irreducible polynomial $f(X)\in\mathbb Z[X]$ of degree $n$ remains irreducible modulo $p$, then the residue extension is $\mathbb F_{p^n}/\mathbb F_p$, so $f=n$. In the Galois closure, $e\cdot f\cdot g = [K:\mathbb Q] = n$, forcing $e=1$, $g=1$, and $G_{\mathfrak P}=G$. Hence $G\cong\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb F_{p^n}/\mathbb F_p)$ is cyclic of order $n$.
