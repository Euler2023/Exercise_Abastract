# Cyclic Extensions

## Definition

> [!info] Definition (Cyclic Extension)
> A field extension $K/k$ is called a **cyclic extension** if it is a finite [[05 - Galois Theory/Concepts/Galois Extensions|Galois extension]] whose [[05 - Galois Theory/Concepts/Galois Group|Galois group]] $\operatorname{Gal}(K/k)$ is a [[01 - Group Theory/Concepts/Cyclic Groups|cyclic group]]:
> $$
> \operatorname{Gal}(K/k) \cong \mathbf{Z}/n\mathbf{Z} = \langle \sigma \rangle,
> $$
> where $n = [K : k]$ is the degree of the extension and $\sigma$ is a generator of the Galois group.

More generally, an infinite algebraic Galois extension $K/k$ is called **pro-cyclic** if its Krull Galois group $\operatorname{Gal}(K/k)$ is topologically generated by a single automorphism $\sigma$, so that $\operatorname{Gal}(K/k) \cong \widehat{\mathbf{Z}}$ or $\mathbf{Z}_p$ for some prime $p$.

---

## Intuition and Structural Role

Cyclic extensions are the fundamental "atoms" of solvable Galois theory:

1. **Jordan–Hölder Building Blocks:** By the Galois correspondence, any finite solvable Galois extension decomposes into a tower of cyclic extensions of prime degree:
   $$
   k = K_0 \subset K_1 \subset K_2 \subset \dots \subset K_m = K,
   $$
   where each step $K_{i+1}/K_i$ is cyclic with $[K_{i+1} : K_i] = p_i$.
2. **Linear Action of the Generator:** Because $\operatorname{Gal}(K/k) = \langle \sigma \rangle$ is generated by a single element satisfying $\sigma^n = \operatorname{id}_K$, the Galois action can be analyzed via linear algebra on $K$ as a $k$-vector space, viewing $\sigma$ as an endomorphism whose minimal polynomial divides $X^n - 1$.

## Classification Theorems

The structure of cyclic extensions depends decisively on the characteristic of $k$ and the presence of roots of unity:

### 1. Kummer Theory ($\boldsymbol{\mu}_n \subset k$, $\operatorname{char}(k) \nmid n$)
> [!abstract] Theorem (Cyclic Extensions via Radicals)
> Let $k$ be a field containing a primitive $n$-th root of unity $\zeta_n$, with $\operatorname{char}(k) \nmid n$.
> 1. If $a \in k^\times$ and $\alpha$ is a root of $X^n - a$, then $k(\alpha)/k$ is a cyclic extension of degree dividing $n$. Its degree is exactly $n$ if and only if $a \notin k^{\times p}$ for all primes $p \mid n$.
> 2. Conversely, every cyclic extension $K/k$ of degree $n$ is of the form $K = k(\alpha)$ with $\alpha^n = a \in k^\times$.
> 3. Two elements $a, b \in k^\times$ generate the same extension $k(a^{1/n}) = k(b^{1/n})$ if and only if they generate the same cyclic subgroup in the quotient group $k^\times / k^{\times n}$.

### 2. Artin–Schreier Theory ($\operatorname{char}(k) = p$, degree $p$)
> [!abstract] Theorem (Cyclic Degree-$p$ Extensions in Characteristic $p$)
> Let $k$ be a field of characteristic $p > 0$.
> 1. For any $a \in k$, the polynomial $X^p - X - a$ either splits completely in $k$ (if $a \in \wp(k) = \{x^p - x \mid x \in k\}$) or is irreducible over $k$.
> 2. In the irreducible case, adjoining a root $\alpha$ yields a cyclic extension $k(\alpha)/k$ of degree $p$, with Galois generator $\sigma(\alpha) = \alpha + 1$.
> 3. Conversely, every cyclic extension of degree $p$ in characteristic $p$ is of this form.

### 3. Artin–Schreier–Witt Theory ($\operatorname{char}(k) = p$, degree $p^m$)
> [!abstract] Theorem (Cyclic Extensions of Degree $p^m$)
> Using the ring of truncated Witt vectors $W_m(k)$ and the Artin–Schreier–Witt operator $\wp(x) = F(x) - x$, every cyclic extension of degree $p^m$ over a field of characteristic $p$ is generated by the components of $\wp^{-1}(a)$ for a suitable Witt vector $a \in W_m(k)$ with $a_0 \notin \wp(k)$.

### 4. Finite Fields
> [!abstract] Theorem (Every Finite Extension of Finite Fields is Cyclic)
> Let $k = \mathbf{F}_q$ be a finite field of order $q = p^r$. For every integer $n \ge 1$:
> - There exists a unique extension of degree $n$, namely $\mathbf{F}_{q^n}$.
> - The extension $\mathbf{F}_{q^n}/\mathbf{F}_q$ is cyclic of degree $n$, canonically generated by the **Frobenius automorphism**:
>   $$
>   \sigma_q: x \mapsto x^q.
>   $$

### 5. Number Fields (Kronecker–Weber Theorem)
> [!abstract] Theorem (Kronecker–Weber)
> Every abelian (and in particular, every cyclic) extension of the rational field $\mathbf{Q}$ is contained in a cyclotomic field $\mathbf{Q}(\zeta_m)$ for some integer $m \ge 1$.

## Cohomological Perspective: Hilbert's Theorem 90

The cohomological signature of a cyclic extension $K/k$ with Galois group $G = \langle \sigma \rangle$ of order $n$ is captured by the Herbrand quotient and the vanishing of $H^1$:

1. **Multiplicative Form ($A = K^\times$):**
   $$
   H^1(G, K^\times) = 0 \iff \ker(N_{K/k}) = \{\beta / \sigma(\beta) \mid \beta \in K^\times\} = (1 - \sigma)K^\times.
   $$
2. **Additive Form ($A = K$):**
   $$
   H^1(G, K) = 0 \iff \ker(\operatorname{Tr}_{K/k}) = \{\beta - \sigma(\beta) \mid \beta \in K\} = (1 - \sigma)K.
   $$
3. **Periodicity:** For any $G$-module $A$, the cohomology of a finite cyclic group is periodic of period 2:
   $$
   H^r(G, A) \cong H^{r+2}(G, A) \quad \text{for all } r \ge 1.
   $$

---

## Key Properties

1. **Subextensions:**
   Every subgroup and every quotient group of a cyclic group is cyclic. Therefore:
   - If $K/k$ is cyclic and $F$ is an intermediate field ($k \subseteq F \subseteq K$), then both $F/k$ and $K/F$ are cyclic.
   - For each divisor $d \mid [K : k]$, there exists a unique intermediate field $F$ with $[F : k] = d$.
2. **Normal Basis:**
   By the [[05 - Galois Theory/Concepts/Normal Basis Theorem|Normal Basis Theorem]], every finite cyclic extension $K/k$ possesses a normal basis:
   $$
   \{\theta, \sigma(\theta), \sigma^2(\theta), \dots, \sigma^{n-1}(\theta)\}.
   $$
3. **Composita:**
   The compositum of two linearly disjoint cyclic extensions $K_1/k$ and $K_2/k$ has Galois group $\operatorname{Gal}(K_1 K_2 / k) \cong \operatorname{Gal}(K_1/k) \times \operatorname{Gal}(K_2/k)$. The compositum is cyclic if and only if $\gcd([K_1 : k], [K_2 : k]) = 1$.

## Examples and Non-Examples

> [!example] Quadratic Extensions
> Every extension of degree 2 in characteristic $\ne 2$ is cyclic:
> $$
> K = k(\sqrt{d}), \quad \operatorname{Gal}(K/k) \cong \mathbf{Z}/2\mathbf{Z} = \{\operatorname{id}, \sqrt{d} \mapsto -\sqrt{d}\}.
> $$

> [!example] Maximal Real Subfield of a Cyclotomic Field
> The subfield $\mathbf{Q}(\zeta_p)^+ = \mathbf{Q}(\zeta_p + \zeta_p^{-1})$ of the cyclotomic field $\mathbf{Q}(\zeta_p)$ is a cyclic extension of $\mathbf{Q}$ of degree $(p-1)/2$, fixed by complex conjugation.

> [!example] Cyclic Cubic over $\mathbf{Q}$
> The splitting field of $f(X) = X^3 - 3X - 1$ over $\mathbf{Q}$ has discriminant $\Delta = 81 = 9^2$. Since the discriminant is a rational square and $f$ is irreducible, $\operatorname{Gal}(f/\mathbf{Q}) \cong A_3 \cong \mathbf{Z}/3\mathbf{Z}$.

> [!warning] Non-Example: Biquadratic Extension
> The extension $\mathbf{Q}(\sqrt{2}, \sqrt{3})/\mathbf{Q}$ has Galois group:
> $$
> \operatorname{Gal}(\mathbf{Q}(\sqrt{2}, \sqrt{3})/\mathbf{Q}) \cong \mathbf{Z}/2\mathbf{Z} \times \mathbf{Z}/2\mathbf{Z}.
> $$
> This extension is abelian, but it is **not cyclic** because no single automorphism has order 4.

> [!warning] Non-Example: Non-Abelian Extension
> The splitting field of $X^3 - 2$ over $\mathbf{Q}$ is $\mathbf{Q}(\sqrt[3]{2}, \omega)$. Its Galois group is isomorphic to $S_3$ (order 6), which is non-abelian and therefore not cyclic.
