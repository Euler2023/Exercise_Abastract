# Exercise Gal113: Artin-Schreier Tower and Cyclic Extensions of Degree p to the m

## Problem Statement

> [!question] Exercise VI.29
> (a) Let $K$ be a cyclic extension of a field $F$, with Galois group $G$ generated by $\sigma$. Assume that the characteristic is $p$, and that $[K : F] = p^{m-1}$ for some integer $m \ge 2$. Let $\beta$ be an element of $K$ such that $\operatorname{Tr}_F^K(\beta) = 1$. Show that there exists an element $\alpha$ in $K$ such that
> $$
> \sigma\alpha - \alpha = \beta^p - \beta.
> $$
>
> (b) Prove that the polynomial $X^p - X - \alpha$ is irreducible in $K[X]$.
>
> (c) If $\theta$ is a root of this polynomial, prove that $F(\theta)$ is a Galois, cyclic extension of degree $p^m$ of $F$, and that its Galois group is generated by an extension $\sigma^*$ of $\sigma$ such that
> $$
> \sigma^*(\theta) = \theta + \beta.
> $$

## Hints

> [!hint]- Hint 1: Trace of the Artin-Schreier Operator and Hilbert 90
> Show that the Frobenius map commutes with all Galois automorphisms in characteristic $p$, so $\operatorname{Tr}_F^K(\beta^p) = (\operatorname{Tr}_F^K(\beta))^p = 1$. Deduce $\operatorname{Tr}_F^K(\beta^p - \beta) = 0$, and apply additive Hilbert's Theorem 90.

> [!hint]- Hint 2: Irreducibility via Trace
> If $\alpha = y^p - y$ for some $y \in K$, show that $\sigma y - y - \beta = c \in \mathbb F_p$. Take the trace $\operatorname{Tr}_F^K$ of both sides and use $p \mid [K:F] = p^{m-1}$ to derive a contradiction with $\operatorname{Tr}_F^K(\beta) = 1$.

> [!hint]- Hint 3: Order of the Extension Automorphism
> Define $\sigma^*(\theta) = \theta + \beta$ and check compatibility with the relation $\theta^p - \theta = \alpha$. Compute the powers $(\sigma^*)^k(\theta)$, and evaluate at $k = p^{m-1}$ to find $(\sigma^*)^{p^{m-1}}(\theta) = \theta + 1$.

## Solution

> [!success]- Solution
>
> Let $F$ be a field of characteristic $p > 0$, and let $K/F$ be a cyclic extension of degree $[K:F] = p^{m-1}$ ($m \ge 2$) with Galois group $G = \operatorname{Gal}(K/F) = \langle \sigma \rangle$.
>
> ### (a) Existence of $\alpha \in K$ via Additive Hilbert 90
>
> Let $\beta \in K$ satisfy $\operatorname{Tr}_F^K(\beta) = 1$.
> In characteristic $p$, the Frobenius map $x \mapsto x^p$ commutes with every field automorphism:
> $$
> \tau(x^p) = (\tau(x))^p \quad \text{for all } \tau \in G.
> $$
> Consequently, the field trace commutes with Frobenius:
> $$
> \operatorname{Tr}_F^K(\beta^p) = \sum_{\tau \in G} \tau(\beta^p) = \sum_{\tau \in G} (\tau\beta)^p = \left(\sum_{\tau \in G} \tau\beta\right)^p = (\operatorname{Tr}_F^K(\beta))^p.
> $$
> Since $\operatorname{Tr}_F^K(\beta) = 1 \in \mathbb F_p$, we have
> $$
> \operatorname{Tr}_F^K(\beta^p) = 1^p = 1.
> $$
> It follows that
> $$
> \operatorname{Tr}_F^K(\beta^p - \beta) = \operatorname{Tr}_F^K(\beta^p) - \operatorname{Tr}_F^K(\beta) = 1 - 1 = 0.
> $$
>
> By [[05 - Galois Theory/Concepts/Hilbert's Theorem 90|additive Hilbert's Theorem 90]] for the cyclic extension $K/F$, an element of $K$ has trace 0 if and only if it lies in the image of $\sigma - \operatorname{id}$.
> Therefore, there exists an element $\alpha \in K$ such that
> $$
> \sigma\alpha - \alpha = \beta^p - \beta.
> $$
>
> ---
>
> ### (b) Irreducibility of $X^p - X - \alpha$ over $K$
>
> By the [[05 - Galois Theory/Concepts/Artin-Schreier Extensions|Artin–Schreier theorem]], the polynomial $f(X) = X^p - X - \alpha$ is irreducible over $K$ if and only if $\alpha \notin \wp(K)$, where $\wp(x) = x^p - x$.
>
> Suppose, for contradiction, that $\alpha \in \wp(K)$, meaning there exists $y \in K$ such that
> $$
> \alpha = y^p - y.
> $$
> Applying $\sigma - \operatorname{id}$ to this equation:
> $$
> \sigma\alpha - \alpha = \sigma(y^p - y) - (y^p - y) = (\sigma y - y)^p - (\sigma y - y).
> $$
> Combining this with the equation from part (a):
> $$
> (\sigma y - y)^p - (\sigma y - y) = \beta^p - \beta.
> $$
> Rearranging gives:
> $$
> (\sigma y - y - \beta)^p - (\sigma y - y - \beta) = 0.
> $$
> This means that $w = \sigma y - y - \beta$ is a root of $Z^p - Z = 0$.
> In characteristic $p$, the roots of $Z^p - Z$ are precisely the elements of the prime field $\mathbb F_p \subset F$.
> Hence there exists a constant $c \in \mathbb F_p$ such that
> $$
> \sigma y - y - \beta = c \implies \beta = \sigma y - y - c.
> $$
>
> We now compute the trace $\operatorname{Tr}_F^K$ of both sides:
> $$
> \operatorname{Tr}_F^K(\beta) = \operatorname{Tr}_F^K(\sigma y - y) - \operatorname{Tr}_F^K(c).
> $$
> - The trace of any difference $\sigma y - y$ is 0.
> - Because $c \in \mathbb F_p \subseteq F$, we have:
>   $$
>   \operatorname{Tr}_F^K(c) = [K:F] \cdot c = p^{m-1} \cdot c.
>   $$
>   Since $m \ge 2$, the exponent $m - 1 \ge 1$, so $p^{m-1}$ is a multiple of $p$.
>   In characteristic $p$, $p = 0$, so $p^{m-1} \cdot c = 0$.
>
> Therefore:
> $$
> \operatorname{Tr}_F^K(\beta) = 0 - 0 = 0.
> $$
> But by hypothesis, $\operatorname{Tr}_F^K(\beta) = 1 \neq 0$.
> This contradiction proves that $\alpha \notin \wp(K)$.
> Consequently, $X^p - X - \alpha$ is irreducible in $K[X]$.
>
> ---
>
> ### (c) The Cyclic Extension $F(\theta)/F$ of Degree $p^m$
>
> Let $\theta$ be a root of $X^p - X - \alpha = 0$ in an algebraic closure.
> Since $X^p - X - \alpha$ is irreducible of degree $p$ over $K$, $[K(\theta) : K] = p$.
> The extension $K(\theta)/K$ is an Artin–Schreier extension, so its other roots are $\theta + j$ for $j \in \mathbb F_p$, all lying in $K(\theta)$.
> The tower degree is
> $$
> [K(\theta) : F] = [K(\theta) : K] \cdot [K : F] = p \cdot p^{m-1} = p^m.
> $$
>
> 1. **Extending $\sigma$ to an Automorphism $\sigma^*$ of $K(\theta)$:**
>    To extend the $F$-automorphism $\sigma$ of $K$ to $K(\theta)$, we must map $\theta$ to a root of $\sigma(X^p - X - \alpha) = X^p - X - \sigma\alpha$.
>    Evaluate $X^p - X - \sigma\alpha$ at $\theta + \beta$:
>    $$
>    (\theta + \beta)^p - (\theta + \beta) - \sigma\alpha = (\theta^p - \theta) + (\beta^p - \beta) - \sigma\alpha = \alpha + (\sigma\alpha - \alpha) - \sigma\alpha = 0.
>    $$
>    Thus $\theta + \beta$ is indeed a root of $X^p - X - \sigma\alpha$.
>    By the extension theorem for field isomorphisms, there exists a unique $F$-automorphism $\sigma^* \in \operatorname{Aut}_F(K(\theta))$ extending $\sigma$ on $K$ and satisfying:
>    $$
>    \sigma^*(\theta) = \theta + \beta.
>    $$
>
> 2. **Order of $\sigma^*$:**
>    We iterate $\sigma^*$ on $\theta$:
>    $$
>    \begin{aligned}
>    \sigma^*(\theta) &= \theta + \beta, \\
>    (\sigma^*)^2(\theta) &= \sigma^*(\theta + \beta) = (\theta + \beta) + \sigma(\beta) = \theta + \beta + \sigma\beta, \\
>    &\ \ \vdots \\
>    (\sigma^*)^k(\theta) &= \theta + \sum_{j=0}^{k-1} \sigma^j(\beta).
>    \end{aligned}
>    $$
>    Specializing to $k = p^{m-1} = [K:F]$:
>    $$
>    (\sigma^*)^{p^{m-1}}(\theta) = \theta + \sum_{j=0}^{p^{m-1}-1} \sigma^j(\beta) = \theta + \operatorname{Tr}_F^K(\beta) = \theta + 1.
>    $$
>    Because $\sigma^{p^{m-1}} = \operatorname{id}_K$, the automorphism $(\sigma^*)^{p^{m-1}}$ fixes $K$ pointwise and acts on $\theta$ as translation by $1$.
>    Iterating this translation $r$ times:
>    $$
>    (\sigma^*)^{r p^{m-1}}(\theta) = \theta + r \quad (r \in \mathbb Z).
>    $$
>    In characteristic $p$, $\theta + r = \theta$ if and only if $p \mid r$.
>    Therefore, the smallest positive multiple of $p^{m-1}$ fixing $\theta$ is $p \cdot p^{m-1} = p^m$:
>    $$
>    (\sigma^*)^{p^m}(\theta) = \theta + p = \theta.
>    $$
>    Since $\sigma^*$ also satisfies $(\sigma^*)^{p^m}|_K = (\sigma^{p^{m-1}})^p = \operatorname{id}_K$, $\sigma^*$ fixes both $K$ and $\theta$, hence
>    $$
>    (\sigma^*)^{p^m} = \operatorname{id}_{K(\theta)}.
>    $$
>    Because no smaller power fixes $\theta$, $\sigma^*$ has exact order $p^m$.
>
> 3. **Generation of $F(\theta)$:**
>    The cyclic group $\langle \sigma^* \rangle$ has order $p^m = [K(\theta) : F]$.
>    Therefore, $K(\theta)/F$ is a Galois extension with cyclic Galois group:
>    $$
>    \operatorname{Gal}(K(\theta)/F) = \langle \sigma^* \rangle \cong \mathbb Z/p^m\mathbb Z.
>    $$
>    Furthermore, the orbit of $\theta$ under $\langle \sigma^* \rangle$ has size $p^m$, so $[F(\theta) : F] = p^m$.
>    Because $F(\theta) \subseteq K(\theta)$ and both have the same finite degree $p^m$ over $F$, we have:
>    $$
>    F(\theta) = K(\theta).
>    $$
>    Thus $F(\theta)/F$ is Galois and cyclic of degree $p^m$, generated by $\sigma^*$.
