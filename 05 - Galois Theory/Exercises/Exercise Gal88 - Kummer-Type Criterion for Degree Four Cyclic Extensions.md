# Exercise Gal88: Kummer-Type Criterion for Degree Four Cyclic Extensions

## Problem Statement

> [!question] Exercise VI.4
> Let $k$ be a field of characteristic $\neq 2$. Let $c \in k, c \notin k^2$. Let $F = k(\sqrt{c})$. Let $\alpha = a + b\sqrt{c}$ with $a, b \in k$ and not both $a, b = 0$. Let $E = F(\sqrt{\alpha})$. Prove that the following conditions are equivalent:
>
> (1) $E$ is Galois over $k$.
>
> (2) $E = F(\sqrt{\alpha'})$, where $\alpha' = a - b\sqrt{c}$.
>
> (3) Either $\alpha\alpha' = a^2 - cb^2 \in k^2$ or $c\alpha\alpha' \in k^2$.
>
> Show that when these conditions are satisfied, then $E$ is cyclic over $k$ of degree 4 if and only if $c\alpha\alpha' \in k^2$ (assuming $[E:k] = 4$).

## Hints

> [!hint]- Hint 1: Action of the Base Automorphism
> The extension $F/k$ is quadratic, generated by the involution $\tau: \sqrt{c} \mapsto -\sqrt{c}$, which sends $\alpha = a + b\sqrt{c}$ to $\alpha' = a - b\sqrt{c}$. Any $k$-automorphism of $E$ restricting non-trivially to $F$ must send $\sqrt{\alpha}$ to a square root of $\alpha'$.

> [!hint]- Hint 2: Squares in a Quadratic Extension
> A base field element $d \in k^\times$ is a square in $F = k(\sqrt{c})$ if and only if $d \in (k^\times)^2$ or $d \in c(k^\times)^2$. Use this to characterize when $\alpha\alpha'$ is a square in $F$.

> [!hint]- Hint 3: Squaring the Generator
> For an extension $\sigma$ of $\tau$ to $E$, compute $\sigma^2(\sqrt{\alpha})$ using the relation $\sqrt{\alpha}\sqrt{\alpha'} = \sqrt{\alpha\alpha'}$, noting whether $\sqrt{\alpha\alpha'}$ is fixed or inverted by $\tau$.

## Solution

> [!success]- Solution
>
> ### Equivalence of Conditions (1), (2), and (3)
>
> **(1) $\implies$ (2):**
> Suppose $E/k$ is Galois. Let $\tau \in \operatorname{Gal}(F/k)$ be the non-trivial automorphism, sending $\sqrt{c} \mapsto -\sqrt{c}$ and therefore $\alpha = a + b\sqrt{c} \mapsto \alpha' = a - b\sqrt{c}$.
> Since $E/k$ is Galois, any $k$-embedding of $E$ into an algebraic closure $\overline{k}$ maps $E$ to $E$. Extending $\tau$ to an automorphism $\sigma \in \operatorname{Gal}(E/k)$, we have
> $$
> (\sigma(\sqrt{\alpha}))^2 = \sigma(\alpha) = \alpha'.
> $$
> Thus $\pm\sqrt{\alpha'} = \sigma(\sqrt{\alpha}) \in E$. Since $F \subset E$, this implies $F(\sqrt{\alpha'}) \subseteq E$.
> Moreover, $[F(\sqrt{\alpha'}):F] = [F(\sqrt{\alpha}):F]$. Thus $E = F(\sqrt{\alpha}) = F(\sqrt{\alpha'})$.
>
> **(2) $\implies$ (3):**
> By assumption, $E = F(\sqrt{\alpha}) = F(\sqrt{\alpha'})$.
> By Kummer theory of quadratic extensions, two elements generate the same quadratic extension of $F$ (or both are squares in $F$) if and only if their ratio belongs to $(F^\times)^2$.
> Thus $\alpha / \alpha' \in (F^\times)^2$, or equivalently,
> $$
> \alpha \alpha' = \alpha'^2 \left(\frac{\alpha}{\alpha'}\right) \in (F^\times)^2.
> $$
> Note that $\alpha\alpha' = (a + b\sqrt{c})(a - b\sqrt{c}) = a^2 - cb^2 \in k$.
> We now determine when an element $d \in k^\times$ is a square in $F = k(\sqrt{c})$.
> If $d = (u + v\sqrt{c})^2 = u^2 + cv^2 + 2uv\sqrt{c}$ with $u, v \in k$, then because $\operatorname{char}(k) \neq 2$ and $\sqrt{c} \notin k$, we must have $2uv = 0$, hence $u = 0$ or $v = 0$:
> - If $v = 0$, then $d = u^2 \in k^2$.
> - If $u = 0$, then $d = cv^2$, so $cd = c^2 v^2 \in k^2$.
>
> Applying this to $d = \alpha\alpha'$, we conclude that either $\alpha\alpha' \in k^2$ or $c\alpha\alpha' \in k^2$.
>
> **(3) $\implies$ (1):**
> If $\alpha\alpha' \in k^2$, let $\alpha\alpha' = u^2$ for some $u \in k^\times$. Then $\sqrt{\alpha'} = \pm u / \sqrt{\alpha} \in F(\sqrt{\alpha}) = E$.
> If $c\alpha\alpha' \in k^2$, let $c\alpha\alpha' = v^2$ for some $v \in k^\times$. Then $\alpha\alpha' = v^2/c = (v/\sqrt{c})^2$, so $\sqrt{\alpha'} = \pm \frac{v}{\sqrt{c}\sqrt{\alpha}} \in F(\sqrt{\alpha}) = E$.
> In either case, $\sqrt{\alpha'} \in E$. The minimal polynomial of $\sqrt{\alpha}$ over $k$ divides
> $$
> (X^2 - \alpha)(X^2 - \alpha') = X^4 - 2a X^2 + (a^2 - cb^2),
> $$
> whose four roots are $\pm\sqrt{\alpha}, \pm\sqrt{\alpha'}$. Since all four roots lie in $E$, $E$ is the splitting field over $k$ of a separable polynomial (as $\operatorname{char}(k) \neq 2$ and $b \neq 0$ or $\alpha \notin k^2$), hence $E/k$ is Galois.
>
> ---
>
> ### Cyclicity of Degree 4
>
> Assume $[E:k] = 4$, so $[E:F] = 2$ and $\operatorname{Gal}(E/k)$ is a group of order 4.
> Any automorphism $\sigma \in \operatorname{Gal}(E/k)$ that does not fix $F$ restricts to $\tau$ on $F$, meaning $\sigma(\sqrt{c}) = -\sqrt{c}$.
> As shown above, $\sigma(\sqrt{\alpha}) = \pm \sqrt{\alpha'}$.
>
> - **Case A: $\alpha\alpha' = u^2 \in k^2$.**
>   Then $\sqrt{\alpha\alpha'} = u \in k$.
>   We can choose the sign of the radical such that $\sqrt{\alpha}\sqrt{\alpha'} = u$.
>   Then $\sqrt{\alpha'} = u / \sqrt{\alpha}$.
>   Applying $\sigma$ to $\sqrt{\alpha}$, we have $\sigma(\sqrt{\alpha}) = \varepsilon u / \sqrt{\alpha}$ where $\varepsilon = \pm 1$.
>   Then:
>   $$
>   \sigma^2(\sqrt{\alpha}) = \sigma\left(\frac{\varepsilon u}{\sqrt{\alpha}}\right) = \frac{\varepsilon u}{\sigma(\sqrt{\alpha})} = \frac{\varepsilon u}{\varepsilon u / \sqrt{\alpha}} = \sqrt{\alpha}.
>   $$
>   Since $\sigma^2$ also fixes $F$ (as $\tau^2 = \operatorname{id}$), $\sigma^2$ fixes both $F$ and $\sqrt{\alpha}$, so $\sigma^2 = \operatorname{id}_E$.
>   Thus every element of $\operatorname{Gal}(E/k)$ has order dividing 2, which means
>   $$
>   \operatorname{Gal}(E/k) \cong C_2 \times C_2 \quad (\text{Klein four-group}).
>   $$
>
> - **Case B: $c\alpha\alpha' = v^2 \in k^2$.**
>   Then $\alpha\alpha' = v^2 / c$, so $\sqrt{\alpha\alpha'} = v / \sqrt{c} \in F \setminus k$.
>   Crucially, $\tau$ acts non-trivially on this square root:
>   $$
>   \sigma(\sqrt{\alpha\alpha'}) = \sigma\left(\frac{v}{\sqrt{c}}\right) = -\frac{v}{\sqrt{c}} = -\sqrt{\alpha\alpha'}.
>   $$
>   Using $\sqrt{\alpha'} = \frac{\sqrt{\alpha\alpha'}}{\sqrt{\alpha}}$, we compute $\sigma^2(\sqrt{\alpha})$:
>   $$
>   \sigma(\sqrt{\alpha}) = \varepsilon \frac{\sqrt{\alpha\alpha'}}{\sqrt{\alpha}} \quad (\varepsilon = \pm 1),
>   $$
>   $$
>   \sigma^2(\sqrt{\alpha}) = \sigma\left(\varepsilon \frac{\sqrt{\alpha\alpha'}}{\sqrt{\alpha}}\right) = \varepsilon \frac{\sigma(\sqrt{\alpha\alpha'})}{\sigma(\sqrt{\alpha})} = \varepsilon \frac{-\sqrt{\alpha\alpha'}}{\varepsilon \sqrt{\alpha\alpha'}/\sqrt{\alpha}} = -\sqrt{\alpha}.
>   $$
>   Since $-\sqrt{\alpha} \neq \sqrt{\alpha}$ (because $\operatorname{char}(k) \neq 2$ and $\sqrt{\alpha} \neq 0$), $\sigma^2 \neq \operatorname{id}$.
>   Therefore, $\sigma$ has order 4, which proves that
>   $$
>   \operatorname{Gal}(E/k) \cong C_4 \cong \mathbb Z/4\mathbb Z.
>   $$
>
> Hence, $E/k$ is cyclic of degree 4 if and only if $c\alpha\alpha' \in k^2$.
