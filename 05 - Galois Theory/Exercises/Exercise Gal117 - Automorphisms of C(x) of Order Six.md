# Exercise Gal117: Automorphisms of C(x) of Order Six

## Problem Statement

> [!question]
> Let $K = \mathbf{C}(x)$ where $x$ is transcendental over $\mathbf{C}$, and let $\zeta$ be a primitive cube root of unity in $\mathbf{C}$. Let $\sigma$ be the automorphism of $K$ over $\mathbf{C}$ such that $\sigma x = \zeta x$. Let $\tau$ be the automorphism of $K$ over $\mathbf{C}$ such that $\tau x = x^{-1}$. Show that
> $$
> \sigma^3 = 1 = \tau^2 \quad \text{and} \quad \tau \sigma = \sigma^{-1} \tau.
> $$
> Show that the group of automorphisms $G$ generated by $\sigma$ and $\tau$ has order 6 and the subfield $F$ of $K$ fixed by $G$ is the field $\mathbf{C}(y)$ where $y = x^3 + x^{-3}$.

## Hints

> [!hint]- Weak Hint
> Recall that a $\mathbf{C}$-automorphism of $\mathbf{C}(x)$ is completely determined by its action on the generator $x$. Compute the composite automorphisms by evaluating them on $x$.

> [!hint]- Moderate Hint
> - Verify the relations $\sigma^3(x) = x$, $\tau^2(x) = x$, and $(\tau \sigma)(x) = (\sigma^{-1}\tau)(x)$.
> - These relations define the dihedral group $D_3 \cong S_3$.
> - To find the fixed field, check that $y = x^3 + x^{-3}$ is fixed by both $\sigma$ and $\tau$, and then determine the degree $[\mathbf{C}(x) : \mathbf{C}(y)]$.

> [!hint]- Strong Hint
> Show that $x$ satisfies the polynomial $X^6 - y X^3 + 1 = 0$ over $\mathbf{C}(y)$, which has degree 6. By Artin's theorem on the fixed field of a finite group of automorphisms, $[K : K^G] = |G| = 6$. Combine these degree relations to conclude $K^G = \mathbf{C}(y)$.

## Solution

> [!success]- Complete Derivation
> Let $K = \mathbf{C}(x)$ where $x$ is transcendental over $\mathbf{C}$, and let $\zeta = e^{2\pi i/3} \in \mathbf{C}$.
>
> ### 1. Verification of the Group Relations
> An automorphism $\varphi \in \operatorname{Aut}_{\mathbf{C}}(K)$ fixing the base field $\mathbf{C}$ is uniquely determined by its value on the indeterminate $x$.
>
> 1. **Order of $\sigma$:**
>    $$
>    \sigma(x) = \zeta x \implies \sigma^2(x) = \sigma(\zeta x) = \zeta \sigma(x) = \zeta^2 x \implies \sigma^3(x) = \zeta^3 x = x.
>    $$
>    Since $\sigma^3(x) = x$ and $\sigma$ fixes $\mathbf{C}$, $\sigma^3 = \operatorname{id}_K$. Since $\zeta \ne 1$, $\sigma \ne \operatorname{id}_K$, so $\sigma$ has order 3.
>
> 2. **Order of $\tau$:**
>    $$
>    \tau(x) = x^{-1} \implies \tau^2(x) = \tau(x^{-1}) = (\tau(x))^{-1} = (x^{-1})^{-1} = x.
>    $$
>    Thus $\tau^2 = \operatorname{id}_K$. Since $x^{-1} \ne x$, $\tau$ has order 2.
>
> 3. **The Dihedral Relation $\tau \sigma = \sigma^{-1} \tau$:**
>    We evaluate both sides on $x$:
>    $$
>    (\tau \sigma)(x) = \tau(\sigma(x)) = \tau(\zeta x) = \zeta x^{-1},
>    $$
>    since $\tau$ fixes $\mathbf{C}$ pointwise.
>    On the other hand, since $\sigma^3 = \operatorname{id}$, we have $\sigma^{-1} = \sigma^2$, so $\sigma^{-1}(x) = \zeta^{-1} x = \zeta^2 x$. Thus:
>    $$
>    (\sigma^{-1} \tau)(x) = \sigma^{-1}(\tau(x)) = \sigma^{-1}(x^{-1}) = (\sigma^{-1}(x))^{-1} = (\zeta^2 x)^{-1} = \zeta^{-2} x^{-1} = \zeta x^{-1}.
>    $$
>    Since $(\tau \sigma)(x) = (\sigma^{-1} \tau)(x)$, we conclude that
>    $$
>    \tau \sigma = \sigma^{-1} \tau.
>    $$
>
> ### 2. Order of the Group $G$
> The group $G = \langle \sigma, \tau \rangle$ satisfies the relations $\sigma^3 = 1$, $\tau^2 = 1$, and $\tau \sigma = \sigma^2 \tau$.
> Every word in $\sigma$ and $\tau$ can be rewritten in the form $\sigma^i \tau^j$ with $0 \le i < 3$ and $0 \le j < 2$. Thus $G$ has at most 6 elements:
> $$
> G = \{1, \sigma, \sigma^2, \tau, \sigma \tau, \sigma^2 \tau\}.
> $$
> These 6 mappings evaluate on $x$ as:
> $$
> x, \quad \zeta x, \quad \zeta^2 x, \quad x^{-1}, \quad \zeta x^{-1}, \quad \zeta^2 x^{-1}.
> $$
> Since $x$ is transcendental over $\mathbf{C}$, these 6 rational functions are pairwise distinct. Therefore,
> $$
> |G| = 6,
> $$
> and $G \cong D_3 \cong S_3$ is the symmetric group on 3 letters.
>
> ### 3. Fixed Field of $G$
> Let $F = K^G$ be the subfield of $K$ fixed by $G$.
> By Artin's theorem on finite groups of field automorphisms, the extension $K/F$ is a Galois extension of degree
> $$
> [K : F] = |G| = 6.
> $$
> Now consider the element $y = x^3 + x^{-3} \in K$.
> We test the action of the generators $\sigma$ and $\tau$ on $y$:
> $$
> \sigma(y) = \sigma(x^3 + x^{-3}) = (\zeta x)^3 + (\zeta x)^{-3} = \zeta^3 x^3 + \zeta^{-3} x^{-3} = x^3 + x^{-3} = y,
> $$
> $$
> \tau(y) = \tau(x^3 + x^{-3}) = (x^{-1})^3 + (x^{-1})^{-3} = x^{-3} + x^3 = y.
> $$
> Since $\sigma(y) = y$ and $\tau(y) = y$, the element $y$ is invariant under the generators of $G$, and hence under all of $G$.
> Therefore:
> $$
> \mathbf{C}(y) \subseteq F = K^G.
> $$
> Next, we determine the degree $[K : \mathbf{C}(y)] = [\mathbf{C}(x) : \mathbf{C}(y)]$.
> Rewriting $y = x^3 + x^{-3}$ gives:
> $$
> y = \frac{x^6 + 1}{x^3} \implies x^6 - y x^3 + 1 = 0.
> $$
> Thus $x$ is a root of the polynomial
> $$
> P(T) = T^6 - y T^3 + 1 \in \mathbf{C}(y)[T].
> $$
> Since $\deg P(T) = 6$, we have
> $$
> [\mathbf{C}(x) : \mathbf{C}(y)] \le 6.
> $$
> On the other hand, by the tower law for the inclusions $\mathbf{C}(y) \subseteq F \subseteq K$:
> $$
> 6 \ge [K : \mathbf{C}(y)] = [K : F] [F : \mathbf{C}(y)] = 6 [F : \mathbf{C}(y)].
> $$
> Since $[F : \mathbf{C}(y)]$ is a positive integer, we must have
> $$
> [F : \mathbf{C}(y)] = 1 \implies F = \mathbf{C}(y).
> $$
> Thus the fixed field of $G$ is precisely $\mathbf{C}(y) = \mathbf{C}(x^3 + x^{-3})$.

## Notes

- The 6 conjugates of $x$ under $G$ are the roots of the polynomial $T^6 - y T^3 + 1 = 0$.
- This is a concrete realization of $S_3$ as the Galois group of a rational function field extension $\mathbf{C}(x)/\mathbf{C}(y)$ of degree 6.
