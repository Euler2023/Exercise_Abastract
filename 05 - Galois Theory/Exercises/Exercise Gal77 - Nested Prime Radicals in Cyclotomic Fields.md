# Exercise Gal77: Nested Prime Radicals in Cyclotomic Fields

## Problem Statement

> [!question] Problem 4
> Find all real numbers of the form
> $$
> \sqrt[p]{2021+\sqrt[q]{a}}
> $$
> that can be expressed as rational linear combinations of roots of unity, where
>
> - $p$ and $q$ are primes, possibly equal;
> - $a>1$ is an integer and is not the $q$-th power of another integer.

## Hints

> [!hint]- Hint 1: Translate the linear-combination condition into field theory
> Any finite collection of roots of unity lies in a common cyclotomic field. Conversely, every element of a cyclotomic field is a rational linear combination of powers of a root of unity. What Galois properties must the field generated by a real element of a cyclotomic field have?

> [!hint]- Hint 2: Determine the inner exponent first
> From
> $$
> \sqrt[q]{a}=\left(\sqrt[p]{2021+\sqrt[q]{a}}\right)^p-2021
> $$
> the inner radical also lies in a cyclotomic field. A field generated by an irrational pure radical of odd prime degree is real but not normal over $\mathbb Q$.

> [!hint]- Hint 3: For $p=2$, consider the conjugate radical
> Put
> $$
> \alpha=\sqrt{2021+\sqrt a},
> \qquad R=2021^2-a.
> $$
> Normality forces $\sqrt{2021-\sqrt a}$ to lie in $\mathbb Q(\alpha)$, so $\sqrt R$ lies there as well. Use square classes in a quadratic extension to prove
> $$
> R\in\mathbb Q^{\times2}
> \quad\text{or}\quad
> R/a\in\mathbb Q^{\times2}.
> $$

> [!hint]- Hint 4: For odd $p$, use trace and norm
> First prove that $\mathbb Q(\alpha)=\mathbb Q(\sqrt a)$. If $\gamma$ is the quadratic conjugate of $\alpha$, define
> $$
> s=\alpha+\gamma,
> \qquad t=\alpha\gamma,
> \qquad V_n=\alpha^n+\gamma^n.
> $$
> Then $s,t\in\mathbb Z$ and
> $$
> V_0=2,
> \quad V_1=s,
> \quad V_n=sV_{n-1}-tV_{n-2},
> \quad V_p=4042.
> $$

## Solution

> [!success]- Complete solution
> Put
> $$
> \theta=\sqrt[p]{2021+\sqrt[q]{a}}.
> $$
> All radicals below denote real roots, and an even root denotes the positive root. The complete answer consists of exactly the following two families:
>
> **Family 1.** We have $p=q=2$ and
> $$
> a=2021^2-r^2,
> \qquad 1\le r\le2020.
> $$
>
> **Family 2.** We have $(p,q)=(3,2)$ and
> $$
> a=674^2\cdot5389=2\,448\,093\,364.
> $$
>
> ### 1. The field-theoretic meaning of the hypothesis
>
> An algebraic number $x$ is a rational linear combination of finitely many roots of unity if and only if
> $$
> x\in\mathbb Q(\zeta_N)
> $$
> for some positive integer $N$. Indeed, finitely many roots of unity lie in a common cyclotomic field; conversely, the powers of $\zeta_N$ span $\mathbb Q(\zeta_N)$ over $\mathbb Q$.
>
> Suppose that $x$ is real and put $K=\mathbb Q(x)$. Since
> $$
> \operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb Q(\zeta_N)/\mathbb Q)
> $$
> is abelian, all of its subgroups are normal. Hence every intermediate field is an abelian Galois extension of $\mathbb Q$. Moreover, $K\subseteq\mathbb R$. Thus $K/\mathbb Q$ is a totally real abelian Galois extension.
>
> ### 2. The inner exponent must be $q=2$
>
> We have
> $$
> \sqrt[q]{a}=\theta^p-2021,
> $$
> so $\sqrt[q]{a}$ also belongs to a cyclotomic field.
>
> Suppose that $q$ is odd. Since $a$ is an integer that is not an integral $q$-th power, its $q$-th root cannot be rational: a rational root of a monic polynomial with integral coefficients is an integer. For prime $q$, the prime-degree binomial criterion therefore makes $X^q-a$ irreducible over $\mathbb Q$.
>
> The field $\mathbb Q(\sqrt[q]{a})$ is real, while $X^q-a$ has nonreal roots in addition to its unique real root. Consequently this field is not normal over $\mathbb Q$. That is impossible for an intermediate field of an abelian cyclotomic extension. Therefore
> $$
> q=2.
> $$
> From now on, write
> $$
> A=2021,
> \qquad E=\mathbb Q(\sqrt a),
> \qquad \alpha=(A+\sqrt a)^{1/p}.
> $$
>
> ### 3. The case $p=2$
>
> Assume that
> $$
> \alpha=\sqrt{A+\sqrt a}
> $$
> lies in a cyclotomic field, and put $K=\mathbb Q(\alpha)$. Then $E\subseteq K$, and $K/\mathbb Q$ is a totally real Galois extension. The nontrivial automorphism of $E/\mathbb Q$ extends to an automorphism of $K$, so the conjugate
> $$
> \alpha'=\sqrt{A-\sqrt a}
> $$
> also belongs to $K$. In particular, $A-\sqrt a>0$. Put
> $$
> R=A^2-a>0.
> $$
> Then
> $$
> \sqrt R=\alpha\alpha'\in K.
> $$
>
> Since $K=E(\alpha)$ and $\alpha^2\in E$, we have $[K:E]\le2$. If $K=E$, then
> $$
> R=N_{E/\mathbb Q}(\alpha)^2\in\mathbb Q^{\times2}.
> $$
>
> Now suppose that $[K:E]=2$. Then $K=E\oplus E\alpha$. If the square of $x+y\alpha$ lies in $E$, comparison of the coefficient of $\alpha$ gives $2xy=0$. Applying this observation to $\sqrt R\in K$ yields
> $$
> R\in E^{\times2}
> \quad\text{or}\quad
> \frac{R}{A+\sqrt a}=A-\sqrt a\in E^{\times2}.
> $$
> In the second case, taking the norm from $E$ to $\mathbb Q$ gives $R\in\mathbb Q^{\times2}$. In the first case, write
> $$
> R=(x+y\sqrt a)^2,
> \qquad x,y\in\mathbb Q.
> $$
> Comparison of the coefficient of $\sqrt a$ gives $xy=0$, and hence
> $$
> R\in\mathbb Q^{\times2}
> \quad\text{or}\quad
> R/a\in\mathbb Q^{\times2}.
> $$
> We have proved the necessary condition
> $$
> R\in\mathbb Q^{\times2}
> \quad\text{or}\quad
> R/a\in\mathbb Q^{\times2}. \tag{1}
> $$
>
> We now exploit the factorization
> $$
> A=2021=43\cdot47.
> $$
> Suppose that $R/a$ is a rational square. The positive integers $a$ and $R$ then have the same squarefree part, so
> $$
> a=dx^2,
> \qquad R=dy^2
> $$
> for a positive squarefree integer $d$ and positive integers $x,y$. Hence
> $$
> A^2=d(x^2+y^2).
> $$
> Thus $d\mid A^2$. Every prime divisor $\ell$ of $d$ must be $43$ or $47$, and it would satisfy
> $$
> v_\ell(x^2+y^2)=1.
> $$
> This is impossible. Indeed, if $\ell\equiv3\pmod4$ and $\ell\mid x^2+y^2$, then $-1$ is not a square modulo $\ell$, so $\ell\mid x$ and $\ell\mid y$; removing the resulting factor $\ell^2$ and repeating shows that the valuation must be even. Since $43,47\equiv3\pmod4$, no such $\ell$ can divide $d$. Therefore $d=1$, which would make $a=x^2$, again contrary to the hypothesis.
>
> Hence only the first alternative in (1) remains. Write $R=r^2$. Since $0<R<A^2$,
> $$
> 1\le r\le2020,
> \qquad a=A^2-r^2.
> $$
> None of these values of $a$ is a square. If $a=z^2$, then
> $$
> r^2+z^2=A^2.
> $$
> Each of the primes $43$ and $47$ divides the sum of the two squares and is congruent to $3$ modulo $4$, so each divides both $r$ and $z$. Thus $A=43\cdot47$ divides both $r$ and $z$, contradicting $0<r,z<A$.
>
> Conversely, for every $1\le r\le2020$, direct squaring gives
> $$
> \sqrt{A+\sqrt{A^2-r^2}}
> =
> \sqrt{\frac{A+r}{2}}+
> \sqrt{\frac{A-r}{2}}. \tag{2}
> $$
> Each term on the right belongs to an extension of $\mathbb Q$ of degree at most $2$. By the quadratic Kronecker-Weber theorem proved in [[05 - Galois Theory/Concepts/Cyclotomic Extensions#The Quadratic Case of Kronecker-Weber|Cyclotomic Extensions]], each such field is contained in a cyclotomic field, and the two resulting cyclotomic fields lie in a common larger cyclotomic field. Thus (2) is indeed a rational linear combination of roots of unity.
>
> The complete first family is therefore
> $$
> (p,q,a)=(2,2,2021^2-r^2),
> \qquad 1\le r\le2020.
> $$
>
> ### 4. The case of an odd prime $p$
>
> Again put $K=\mathbb Q(\alpha)$. The extension $K/\mathbb Q$ is totally real and abelian Galois, and $E\subseteq K$. Hence $K/E$ is Galois. If
> $$
> \sigma\in\operatorname{Gal}(K/E),
> $$
> then
> $$
> \sigma(\alpha)^p=\alpha^p.
> $$
> Both sides are real, and the odd-power map is injective on $\mathbb R$. Thus $\sigma(\alpha)=\alpha$. Since $K=\mathbb Q(\alpha)$, this forces $\sigma=1$. It follows that
> $$
> K=E.
> $$
>
> Let $\gamma$ be the image of $\alpha$ under the nontrivial automorphism of $E/\mathbb Q$. Then
> $$
> \alpha^p=A+\sqrt a,
> \qquad
> \gamma^p=A-\sqrt a,
> \qquad
> \alpha^p+\gamma^p=2A=4042. \tag{3}
> $$
> Moreover, $\alpha$ is a root of the monic integral polynomial
> $$
> X^{2p}-2AX^p+(A^2-a),
> $$
> so $\alpha$ is an algebraic integer. Its trace and norm
> $$
> s=\alpha+\gamma,
> \qquad t=\alpha\gamma
> $$
> are therefore integers.
>
> Define
> $$
> V_n=\alpha^n+\gamma^n.
> $$
> Since $\alpha$ and $\gamma$ are the roots of $X^2-sX+t$, the sequence satisfies
> $$
> V_0=2,
> \qquad V_1=s,
> \qquad V_n=sV_{n-1}-tV_{n-2}. \tag{4}
> $$
> Equation (3) says that $V_p=4042$.
>
> For odd $p$, the quotient
> $$
> \frac{X^p+Y^p}{X+Y}
> $$
> is positive for real $X,Y$ whenever $X+Y\ne0$. Applying this to $X=\alpha$ and $Y=\gamma$ in (3) gives $s>0$. Also, $V_p$, viewed as an integral polynomial in $s$ and $t$, is divisible by $s$ for odd $p$. Hence
> $$
> s\mid4042=2\cdot43\cdot47. \tag{5}
> $$
>
> Suppose that $t>0$. Then $\alpha$ and $\gamma$ have the same sign, and $s>0$ makes both positive. The discriminant
> $$
> s^2-4t=(\alpha-\gamma)^2
> $$
> is a positive nonsquare integer; otherwise $E=\mathbb Q$. Since $t\ge1$, we have $s\ge3$, and (5) then gives $s\ge43$. Convexity yields
> $$
> 4042=\alpha^p+\gamma^p
> \ge2\left(\frac{s}{2}\right)^p
> \ge2\left(\frac{43}{2}\right)^3
> >4042,
> $$
> a contradiction. If $t=0$, then one of $\alpha,\gamma$ is zero, which gives $a=A^2$, also impossible. Therefore
> $$
> t=-m,
> \qquad m\in\mathbb Z_{>0}.
> $$
> The recurrence becomes
> $$
> V_0=2,
> \qquad V_1=s,
> \qquad V_n=sV_{n-1}+mV_{n-2}. \tag{6}
> $$
> For fixed odd $n$, $V_n(s,m)$ is a polynomial with positive coefficients and is strictly increasing in the positive integers $s$ and $m$. If $p\ge19$, then
> $$
> V_p(s,m)\ge V_{19}(1,1)=9349>4042.
> $$
> It remains only to check
> $$
> p\in\{3,5,7,11,13,17\}.
> $$
>
> Use $s\mid4042$ and first discard every $s$ for which $V_p(s,1)>4042$. Strict monotonicity in $m$ then reduces each remaining case to the adjacent values displayed below.
>
> | $p$ | Remaining $s$ | Adjacent values of $m$ | Corresponding $V_p(s,m)$ |
> |---:|---:|:---:|:---:|
> | $3$ | $1$ | $1346,1347$ | $4039,4042$ |
> | $3$ | $2$ | $672,673$ | $4040,4046$ |
> | $5$ | $1$ | $27,28$ | $3781,4061$ |
> | $5$ | $2$ | $18,19$ | $3992,4402$ |
> | $7$ | $1$ | $7,8$ | $3137,4537$ |
> | $7$ | $2$ | $4,5$ | $3712,5798$ |
> | $11$ | $1$ | $2,3$ | $2047,9637$ |
> | $13$ | $1$ | $1,2$ | $521,8191$ |
> | $17$ | $1$ | $1,2$ | $3571,131071$ |
>
> The only possibility is
> $$
> p=3,
> \qquad s=1,
> \qquad m=1347,
> \qquad t=-1347.
> $$
> The discriminant of $X^2-sX+t$ is
> $$
> \Delta=s^2-4t=1+4\cdot1347=5389,
> $$
> so its positive root is
> $$
> \alpha=\frac{1+\sqrt{5389}}{2}.
> $$
> Direct expansion gives
> $$
> \alpha^3=2021+674\sqrt{5389}.
> $$
> Therefore
> $$
> \sqrt a=674\sqrt{5389},
> \qquad
> a=674^2\cdot5389=2\,448\,093\,364.
> $$
> Since $73^2<5389<74^2$, this $a$ is not a square. Conversely, $\alpha\in\mathbb Q(\sqrt{5389})$, and the quadratic Kronecker-Weber theorem proved in [[05 - Galois Theory/Concepts/Cyclotomic Extensions#The Quadratic Case of Kronecker-Weber|Cyclotomic Extensions]] places this quadratic field inside a cyclotomic field. Thus this candidate really satisfies the original condition.
>
> ### 5. Conclusion
>
> All solutions are
> $$
> (p,q,a)=(2,2,2021^2-r^2),
> \qquad 1\le r\le2020,
> $$
> and the single exceptional solution
> $$
> (p,q,a)=\left(3,2,674^2\cdot5389\right).
> $$
> The corresponding real numbers are respectively
> $$
> \sqrt{\frac{2021+r}{2}}+
> \sqrt{\frac{2021-r}{2}}
> $$
> and
> $$
> \frac{1+\sqrt{5389}}2.
> $$

## Notes

- **Problem source:** The statement is a faithful English translation of the user-provided Chinese screenshot. The attribution to Problem 4 of the 2021 Alibaba mathematics competition final was supplied by the user and was not independently checked online.
- **Vault dependencies:** The facts that cyclotomic intermediate fields are abelian Galois and that odd-prime irrational pure radicals cannot lie in a cyclotomic field are proved directly in Sections 1–2 above. The only other vault dependency is the self-contained proof of the quadratic Kronecker-Weber theorem in [[05 - Galois Theory/Concepts/Cyclotomic Extensions#The Quadratic Case of Kronecker-Weber|Cyclotomic Extensions]].
- **Textbook support:** The quadratic Kronecker-Weber theorem is proved in Serge Lang, *Algebra*, Revised Third Edition, Ch. VI, §3 [S2, printed pp. 281–282, PDF pp. 296–297]. Michael Artin computes the quadratic subfields of prime cyclotomic fields and states the general Kronecker–Weber theorem [S1, Ch. 16, §16.10, Thms. 16.10.12–16.10.13, printed pp. 499–500, PDF pp. 511–512].
- **Proof status:** Apart from the odd-prime binomial irreducibility criterion, the square-class argument for $p=2$, the exclusion using $2021=43\cdot47$, the quadratic-field reduction for odd $p$, the Lucas bounds, and the finite exhaustion are proved in this note. General Kronecker-Weber is not used.
- **Computation boundary:** The finite table was independently checked using exact integer recurrence arithmetic. The computation verifies the displayed values and the unique candidate; it does not replace the preceding proof that the search is finite.
