# Exercise Gal85: Galois Groups of Fourteen Polynomials

## Problem Statement

> [!question] Exercise VI.1
> What is the Galois group of the following polynomials?
>
> (a) $X^3 - X - 1$ over $\mathbb Q$.
>
> (b) $X^3 - 10$ over $\mathbb Q$.
>
> (c) $X^3 - 10$ over $\mathbb Q(\sqrt{2})$.
>
> (d) $X^3 - 10$ over $\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-3})$.
>
> (e) $X^3 - X - 1$ over $\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-23})$.
>
> (f) $X^4 - 5$ over $\mathbb Q$, $\mathbb Q(\sqrt{5})$, $\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-5})$, $\mathbb Q(i)$.
>
> (g) $X^4 - a$ where $a$ is any integer $\neq 0, \neq \pm 1$ and is square-free, over $\mathbb Q$.
>
> (h) $X^3 - a$ where $a$ is any square-free integer $\ge 2$, over $\mathbb Q$.
>
> (i) $X^4 + 2$ over $\mathbb Q$, $\mathbb Q(i)$.
>
> (j) $(X^2 - 2)(X^2 - 3)(X^2 - 5)(X^2 - 7)$ over $\mathbb Q$.
>
> (k) Let $p_1, \dots, p_n$ be distinct prime numbers. What is the Galois group of $(X^2 - p_1)\dots(X^2 - p_n)$ over $\mathbb Q$?
>
> (l) $(X^3 - 2)(X^3 - 3)(X^2 - 2)$ over $\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-3})$.
>
> (m) $X^n - t$, where $t$ is transcendental over the complex numbers $\mathbb C$ and $n$ is a positive integer, over $\mathbb C(t)$.
>
> (n) $X^4 - t$, where $t$ is as before, over $\mathbb R(t)$.

## Hints

> [!hint]- Hint 1: Cubics
> For an irreducible cubic $X^3 + pX + q$, the discriminant is $\Delta = -4p^3 - 27q^2$. The Galois group over a field $k$ of characteristic $\neq 2, 3$ is $A_3 \cong \mathbb Z/3\mathbb Z$ if $\Delta \in (k^\times)^2$, and $S_3$ otherwise.

> [!hint]- Hint 2: Binomials and Kummer Extensions
> For $X^n - a$, the splitting field is generated by an $n$-th root $\sqrt[n]{a}$ and a primitive $n$-th root of unity $\zeta_n$. When $\zeta_n$ belongs to the base field, the extension is cyclic.

> [!hint]- Hint 3: Multiquadratic Extensions
> Square-free integers (or distinct primes) generate an $\mathbb F_2$-vector space of square classes in $\mathbb Q^\times / (\mathbb Q^\times)^2$, so adjoining their square roots yields a multiquadratic extension with Galois group $(\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z)^m$.

## Solution

> [!success]- Solution
>
> ### (a) $X^3 - X - 1$ over $\mathbb Q$
> The polynomial $f(X) = X^3 - X - 1$ has no rational roots by the rational root theorem (possible roots are $\pm 1$, and $f(1) = -1 \neq 0$, $f(-1) = -1 \neq 0$). Thus $f$ is irreducible over $\mathbb Q$.
> Its discriminant is
> $$
> \Delta = -4(-1)^3 - 27(-1)^2 = 4 - 27 = -23.
> $$
> Since $-23$ is negative, it is not a square in $\mathbb Q$. Hence the Galois group is
> $$
> \operatorname{Gal}(f/\mathbb Q) \cong S_3.
> $$
>
> ### (b) $X^3 - 10$ over $\mathbb Q$
> The polynomial $X^3 - 10$ is irreducible by Eisenstein's criterion at $p=2$ (or $p=5$). The roots are $\sqrt[3]{10}, \zeta_3 \sqrt[3]{10}, \zeta_3^2 \sqrt[3]{10}$, where $\zeta_3 = e^{2\pi i / 3}$. The splitting field is $K = \mathbb Q(\sqrt[3]{10}, \zeta_3)$.
> We have $[\mathbb Q(\sqrt[3]{10}) : \mathbb Q] = 3$, and $\zeta_3 \notin \mathbb R$ implies $[K : \mathbb Q(\sqrt[3]{10})] = 2$. Thus $[K : \mathbb Q] = 6$, and
> $$
> \operatorname{Gal}(K/\mathbb Q) \cong S_3.
> $$
>
> ### (c) $X^3 - 10$ over $\mathbb Q(\sqrt{2})$
> The splitting field over $\mathbb Q$ is $K = \mathbb Q(\sqrt[3]{10}, \zeta_3)$. Its unique quadratic subfield is $\mathbb Q(\sqrt{\Delta}) = \mathbb Q(\zeta_3) = \mathbb Q(\sqrt{-3})$. Since $\mathbb Q(\sqrt{2}) \neq \mathbb Q(\sqrt{-3})$, the intersection $K \cap \mathbb Q(\sqrt{2}) = \mathbb Q$.
> Therefore, the Galois group over $\mathbb Q(\sqrt{2})$ remains isomorphic to the full Galois group:
> $$
> \operatorname{Gal}(K(\sqrt{2})/\mathbb Q(\sqrt{2})) \cong S_3.
> $$
>
> ### (d) $X^3 - 10$ over $\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-3})$
> Here the base field $k = \mathbb Q(\sqrt{-3}) = \mathbb Q(\zeta_3)$ contains the primitive cube roots of unity. The degree $[k : \mathbb Q] = 2$ is coprime to 3, so $X^3 - 10$ remains irreducible over $k$. Adjoining one root $\sqrt[3]{10}$ automatically adjoining all three roots because $\zeta_3 \in k$.
> Hence the splitting field is $k(\sqrt[3]{10})$, which has degree 3 over $k$, and
> $$
> \operatorname{Gal}(k(\sqrt[3]{10})/k) \cong C_3 \cong \mathbb Z/3\mathbb Z.
> $$
>
> ### (e) $X^3 - X - 1$ over $\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-23})$
> From part (a), the splitting field of $X^3 - X - 1$ over $\mathbb Q$ is $K$ with $[K : \mathbb Q] = 6$ and $\operatorname{Gal}(K/\mathbb Q) \cong S_3$. The unique quadratic intermediate field corresponding to the alternating subgroup $A_3 \le S_3$ is $\mathbb Q(\sqrt{\Delta}) = \mathbb Q(\sqrt{-23})$.
> Therefore, over $\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-23})$, the Galois group is
> $$
> A_3 \cong C_3 \cong \mathbb Z/3\mathbb Z.
> $$
>
> ### (f) $X^4 - 5$ over $\mathbb Q, \mathbb Q(\sqrt{5}), \mathbb Q(\sqrt{-5}), \mathbb Q(i)$
> The roots are $\pm \sqrt[4]{5}, \pm i \sqrt[4]{5}$. The splitting field over $\mathbb Q$ is $K = \mathbb Q(\sqrt[4]{5}, i)$.
> - Over $\mathbb Q$: $X^4 - 5$ is irreducible by Eisenstein at 5. Since $[\mathbb Q(\sqrt[4]{5}) : \mathbb Q] = 4$ and $i \notin \mathbb R$, $[K : \mathbb Q] = 8$. The Galois group is the dihedral group of order 8:
>   $$
>   \operatorname{Gal}(K/\mathbb Q) \cong D_8.
>   $$
> - Over $\mathbb Q(\sqrt{5})$: $X^4 - 5 = (X^2 - \sqrt{5})(X^2 + \sqrt{5})$. The roots generate extensions of degree 2: $\mathbb Q(\sqrt{5})(\sqrt[4]{5})$ and $\mathbb Q(\sqrt{5})(i)$. These are distinct quadratic extensions whose compositum is $K$. Hence
>   $$
>   \operatorname{Gal}(K/\mathbb Q(\sqrt{5})) \cong C_2 \times C_2 \cong \mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z \times \mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z.
>   $$
> - Over $\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-5}) = \mathbb Q(i\sqrt{5})$: In $D_8$, let $\sigma(\sqrt[4]{5}) = i\sqrt[4]{5}, \sigma(i) = i$ and $\tau(\sqrt[4]{5}) = \sqrt[4]{5}, \tau(i) = -i$. Then $\sigma^2(\sqrt{-5}) = \sigma^2(i\sqrt{5}) = i(-\sqrt{5}) = -\sqrt{-5}$, but $\sigma\tau(i\sqrt{5}) = (-i)(-\sqrt{5}) = i\sqrt{5} = \sqrt{-5}$. The subgroup fixing $\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-5})$ is $\{1, \sigma^2, \sigma\tau, \sigma^3\tau\}$, all of whose non-identity elements have order 2. Thus
>   $$
>   \operatorname{Gal}(K/\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-5})) \cong C_2 \times C_2 \cong \mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z \times \mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z.
>   $$
> - Over $\mathbb Q(i)$: The automorphism $\sigma$ fixes $i$ and sends $\sqrt[4]{5} \mapsto i\sqrt[4]{5}$, which has order 4. Thus the subgroup fixing $\mathbb Q(i)$ is $\langle \sigma \rangle$, so
>   $$
>   \operatorname{Gal}(K/\mathbb Q(i)) \cong C_4 \cong \mathbb Z/4\mathbb Z.
>   $$
>
> ### (g) $X^4 - a$ over $\mathbb Q$ ($a \in \mathbb Z \setminus \{0, \pm 1\}$ square-free)
> Since $a$ is square-free and $a \neq \pm 1$, there exists at least one prime $p \mid a$ with $p^2 \nmid a$. By Eisenstein's criterion at $p$, $X^4 - a$ is irreducible over $\mathbb Q$.
> The splitting field is $K = \mathbb Q(\sqrt[4]{a}, i)$.
> - If $a > 0$, $\sqrt[4]{a} \in \mathbb R$, while $i \notin \mathbb R$, so $[K : \mathbb Q] = 8$ and $\operatorname{Gal}(K/\mathbb Q) \cong D_8$.
> - If $a < 0$, write $a = -b$ with $b > 1$ square-free. Then the roots are $\zeta_8 \sqrt[4]{b}, \dots$ and the splitting field is $\mathbb Q(\sqrt[4]{b}, \zeta_8) = \mathbb Q(\sqrt[4]{b}, i, \sqrt{2})$. Since $b$ is square-free and not $1$, $[\mathbb Q(\sqrt[4]{b}) : \mathbb Q] = 4$, and $K$ has degree 8 over $\mathbb Q$, yielding
>   $$
>   \operatorname{Gal}(K/\mathbb Q) \cong D_8.
>   $$
>
> ### (h) $X^3 - a$ over $\mathbb Q$ ($a \ge 2$ square-free integer)
> By Eisenstein's criterion at any prime divisor of $a$, $X^3 - a$ is irreducible over $\mathbb Q$. The splitting field is $\mathbb Q(\sqrt[3]{a}, \zeta_3)$. Since $\sqrt[3]{a} \in \mathbb R$ has degree 3 and $\zeta_3 \notin \mathbb R$ has degree 2, the degree is $3 \times 2 = 6$, and
> $$
> \operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb Q(\sqrt[3]{a}, \zeta_3)/\mathbb Q) \cong S_3.
> $$
>
> ### (i) $X^4 + 2$ over $\mathbb Q$ and $\mathbb Q(i)$
> $X^4 + 2$ is irreducible by Eisenstein at $p=2$. Its roots are $\sqrt[4]{2} e^{(2k+1)\pi i / 4}$ for $k=0,1,2,3$. Since $e^{\pi i / 4} = (1+i)/\sqrt{2}$, the roots lie in $\mathbb Q(\sqrt[4]{2}, i)$ because $\sqrt{2} = (\sqrt[4]{2})^2$.
> Conversely, any root $\alpha$ satisfies $\alpha^2 = i\sqrt{2}$ (up to sign), so $\alpha^2 / \sqrt{2} = i$, which means the splitting field contains $\sqrt{2}$ and $i$, hence also $\sqrt[4]{2} = \alpha / e^{\pi i / 4}$. Thus the splitting field is $K = \mathbb Q(\sqrt[4]{2}, i)$.
> - Over $\mathbb Q$, $[K : \mathbb Q] = 8$ and $\operatorname{Gal}(K/\mathbb Q) \cong D_8$.
> - Over $\mathbb Q(i)$, $X^4 + 2 = (X^2 - i\sqrt{2})(X^2 + i\sqrt{2})$. Over $\mathbb Q(i)$, $[K : \mathbb Q(i)] = 4$. The map $\alpha \mapsto i\alpha$ generates an automorphism of order 4 fixing $i$. Hence
>   $$
>   \operatorname{Gal}(K/\mathbb Q(i)) \cong C_4 \cong \mathbb Z/4\mathbb Z.
>   $$
>
> ### (j) $(X^2 - 2)(X^2 - 3)(X^2 - 5)(X^2 - 7)$ over $\mathbb Q$
> The splitting field is $K = \mathbb Q(\sqrt{2}, \sqrt{3}, \sqrt{5}, \sqrt{7})$. The integers $2, 3, 5, 7$ are prime numbers, hence their classes in $\mathbb Q^\times / (\mathbb Q^\times)^2$ are $\mathbb F_2$-linearly independent. Thus $[K : \mathbb Q] = 2^4 = 16$, and each generator can be independently sign-flipped:
> $$
> \operatorname{Gal}(K/\mathbb Q) \cong (\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z)^4 \cong C_2 \times C_2 \times C_2 \times C_2.
> $$
>
> ### (k) $(X^2 - p_1)\dots(X^2 - p_n)$ over $\mathbb Q$
> For distinct primes $p_1, \dots, p_n$, the images of $p_1, \dots, p_n$ in the square-class group $\mathbb Q^\times / (\mathbb Q^\times)^2$ form an $\mathbb F_2$-linearly independent set of size $n$. By Kummer theory for exponent 2, the splitting field $K = \mathbb Q(\sqrt{p_1}, \dots, \sqrt{p_n})$ satisfies $[K : \mathbb Q] = 2^n$, and
> $$
> \operatorname{Gal}(K/\mathbb Q) \cong (\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z)^n \cong C_2^n.
> $$
>
> ### (l) $(X^3 - 2)(X^3 - 3)(X^2 - 2)$ over $\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-3})$
> Let $k = \mathbb Q(\sqrt{-3}) = \mathbb Q(\zeta_3)$.
> The splitting field of $X^2 - 2$ over $k$ is $k(\sqrt{2})$, which is quadratic since $\sqrt{2} \notin \mathbb Q(\sqrt{-3})$.
> Over $k(\sqrt{2})$, $X^3 - 2$ and $X^3 - 3$ are pure cubic binomials. Since $\mu_3 \subset k$, adjoining $\sqrt[3]{2}$ gives a cyclic degree 3 extension $k(\sqrt{2}, \sqrt[3]{2})$.
> The ratio $3/2$ or $3 \cdot 2^2 = 12$ is not a cube in $k(\sqrt{2})$, so the square classes $2, 3 \in k(\sqrt{2})^\times / (k(\sqrt{2})^\times)^3$ are $\mathbb F_3$-linearly independent.
> Therefore, $[k(\sqrt{2}, \sqrt[3]{2}, \sqrt[3]{3}) : k] = [k(\sqrt{2}):k] \cdot [k(\sqrt[3]{2}):k] \cdot [k(\sqrt[3]{3}):k] = 2 \times 3 \times 3 = 18$.
> The Galois group is the direct product:
> $$
> \operatorname{Gal}(K/k) \cong \mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z \times \mathbb Z/3\mathbb Z \times \mathbb Z/3\mathbb Z \cong C_2 \times C_3 \times C_3.
> $$
>
> ### (m) $X^n - t$ over $\mathbb C(t)$
> In the polynomial ring $\mathbb C[t]$, $t$ is an irreducible element (a prime). By Eisenstein's criterion at $(t)$, $X^n - t$ is irreducible over $\mathbb C(t)$.
> Since $\mathbb C$ contains all $n$-th roots of unity $\mu_n$, adjoining one root $t^{1/n}$ automatically adjoins all roots $\zeta_n^k t^{1/n}$ ($0 \le k < n$).
> Thus the splitting field is $\mathbb C(t^{1/n})$, with degree $[\mathbb C(t^{1/n}) : \mathbb C(t)] = n$, and
> $$
> \operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb C(t^{1/n})/\mathbb C(t)) \cong C_n \cong \mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z.
> $$
>
> ### (n) $X^4 - t$ over $\mathbb R(t)$
> $X^4 - t$ is irreducible over $\mathbb R(t)$ by Eisenstein at $t$. The roots are $\pm t^{1/4}, \pm i t^{1/4}$.
> The splitting field is $K = \mathbb R(t^{1/4}, i)$. We have $[\mathbb R(t^{1/4}) : \mathbb R(t)] = 4$. Since $\mathbb R(t^{1/4}) \subset \mathbb R(t^{1/4})$ is a subfield of the field of real rational functions $\mathbb R(u)$ (with $u = t^{1/4}$), $i \notin \mathbb R(t^{1/4})$.
> Thus $[K : \mathbb R(t)] = 8$. The automorphisms are $\sigma: t^{1/4} \mapsto i t^{1/4}, i \mapsto i$ (order 4) and $\tau: t^{1/4} \mapsto t^{1/4}, i \mapsto -i$ (order 2), satisfying $\tau \sigma \tau = \sigma^{-1}$.
> Hence the Galois group is the dihedral group of order 8:
> $$
> \operatorname{Gal}(K/\mathbb R(t)) \cong D_8.
> $$
