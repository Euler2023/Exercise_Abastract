# Exercise Gal118: Degree Two Extensions over Subfields Not Algebraic over Intersection

## Problem Statement

> [!question]
> Give an example of a field $K$ which is of degree 2 over two distinct subfields $E$ and $F$ respectively, but such that $K$ is not algebraic over $E \cap F$.

## Hints

> [!hint]- Weak Hint
> Look for rational function fields $K = k(t)$. Subfields of degree 2 can be realized as the fixed fields of involutive automorphisms of $K$.

> [!hint]- Moderate Hint
> Consider the field $K = \mathbf{Q}(t)$ and the two involutions $\sigma(t) = -t$ and $\tau(t) = 2 - t$. Their fixed fields $E$ and $F$ both have index 2 in $K$.

> [!hint]- Strong Hint
> What is the composite $\tau \circ \sigma$? It is the translation $t \mapsto t + 2$, which has infinite order. What rational functions in $\mathbf{Q}(t)$ are invariant under an infinite-order translation? Show that the only such rational functions are constants, so $E \cap F = \mathbf{Q}$.

## Solution

> [!success]- Complete Derivation
> Let $k = \mathbf{Q}$ (or any field of characteristic 0), and let $K = k(t)$ be the rational function field in one variable $t$ over $k$.
>
> ### 1. Construction of the Subfields $E$ and $F$
> Define two $k$-automorphisms $\sigma, \tau \in \operatorname{Aut}_k(K)$ by their actions on the generator $t$:
> $$
> \sigma(t) = -t, \quad \tau(t) = 2 - t.
> $$
> Both $\sigma$ and $\tau$ are involutions:
> $$
> \sigma^2(t) = \sigma(-t) = -(-t) = t \implies \sigma^2 = \operatorname{id}_K,
> $$
> $$
> \tau^2(t) = \tau(2 - t) = 2 - (2 - t) = t \implies \tau^2 = \operatorname{id}_K.
> $$
> Let $E = K^{\langle \sigma \rangle}$ and $F = K^{\langle \tau \rangle}$ be the respective fixed fields.
> - By Artin's theorem, $[K : E] = |\langle \sigma \rangle| = 2$, and $E = k(t^2)$ because $t^2$ is $\sigma$-invariant and satisfies $X^2 - t^2 \in k(t^2)[X]$ of degree 2.
> - Similarly, $[K : F] = |\langle \tau \rangle| = 2$, and $F = k((t - 1)^2)$ because
>   $$
>   \tau((t - 1)^2) = ((2 - t) - 1)^2 = (1 - t)^2 = (t - 1)^2.
>   $$
> - The subfields $E$ and $F$ are distinct: for instance, $t^2 \in E$, but $t^2 \notin F$ since $\tau(t^2) = (2 - t)^2 = t^2 - 4t + 4 \ne t^2$.
>
> ### 2. Determination of the Intersection $E \cap F$
> Any element $g(t) \in E \cap F$ is fixed by both $\sigma$ and $\tau$, and therefore by the subgroup $G = \langle \sigma, \tau \rangle \le \operatorname{Aut}_k(K)$ generated by them.
>
> Consider the composition $\rho = \tau \circ \sigma$:
> $$
> \rho(t) = \tau(\sigma(t)) = \tau(-t) = 2 - (-t) = t + 2.
> $$
> Thus $\rho$ is the translation by 2:
> $$
> \rho^m(t) = t + 2m \quad \text{for each } m \in \mathbf{Z}.
> $$
> Since $k$ has characteristic 0, $2m \ne 0$ for all $m \ne 0$, so $\rho$ has infinite order in $\operatorname{Aut}_k(K)$.
>
> Now let $g(t) = \frac{P(t)}{Q(t)} \in K = k(t)$ be any element in $E \cap F$, where $P, Q \in k[t]$ are relatively prime polynomials and $Q \ne 0$ is monic.
> Since $g$ is fixed by $\rho$, we have
> $$
> g(t + 2) = g(t) \implies \frac{P(t + 2)}{Q(t + 2)} = \frac{P(t)}{Q(t)}.
> $$
> Thus $P(t + 2) Q(t) = P(t) Q(t + 2)$.
> If $\deg Q \ge 1$, then $Q(t)$ has at least one root $\alpha \in \bar{k}$. The relation implies that if $\alpha$ is a root of $Q$, then $\alpha + 2$ must also be a root of $Q(t)$ (since $P$ and $Q$ have no common roots). By induction, the infinite set
> $$
> \{\alpha + 2m \mid m \in \mathbf{Z}_{\ge 0}\}
> $$
> would consist entirely of roots of $Q(t)$. But a non-zero polynomial in $k[t]$ can have only finitely many roots.
> Therefore, $\deg Q = 0$, so $Q(t)$ is a non-zero constant.
> Similarly, applying the same argument to $g(t) - c$ shows that $P(t)$ must also be constant.
>
> Consequently:
> $$
> g(t) \in k.
> $$
> Since $k \subseteq E \cap F$ is obvious, we conclude:
> $$
> E \cap F = k.
> $$
>
> ### 3. Non-Algebraicity of $K$ over $E \cap F$
> The field $K = k(t)$ is purely transcendental over $k = E \cap F$, with transcendence degree
> $$
> \operatorname{tr.deg}_{E \cap F}(K) = \operatorname{tr.deg}_k(k(t)) = 1 > 0.
> $$
> In particular, the generator $t \in K$ does not satisfy any non-trivial polynomial relation with coefficients in $k = E \cap F$.
> Therefore, $K$ is **not algebraic** over $E \cap F$.

## Notes

- In the finite-dimensional Galois setting, if $K/k$ is a finite Galois extension and $E = K^H, F = K^N$, then $E \cap F = K^{\langle H, N \rangle}$, so $[K : E \cap F] \le [K : k] < \infty$, which is always algebraic.
- The failure of algebraicity in this exercise illustrates that the Galois correspondence behaves very differently for infinite groups of automorphisms of transcendental extensions.
