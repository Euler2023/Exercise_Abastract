# Exercise Gal111: Fixed Fields of an Automorphism and Cyclic Finite Extensions

## Problem Statement

> [!question] Exercise VI.27
> Let $k$ be a field, $k^a$ an algebraic closure, and $\sigma$ an automorphism of $k^a$ leaving $k$ fixed. Let $F$ be the fixed field of $\sigma$. Show that every finite extension of $F$ is cyclic.
>
> *(The above two problems are examples of Artin, showing how to dig holes in an algebraically closed field.)*

## Hints

> [!hint]- Hint 1: Finite Orbits under $\sigma$
> For any $\alpha \in k^a$, the minimal polynomial of $\alpha$ over $k$ has only finitely many roots in $k^a$. Show that the orbit $\{\sigma^j(\alpha) : j \in \mathbb Z\}$ is finite.

> [!hint]- Hint 2: Polynomial Invariance and Separability
> Form the polynomial $P(X) = \prod_{j=1}^m (X - \sigma^j(\alpha))$ over the orbit. Show that $P(X) \in F[X]$, and deduce that $\alpha$ is separable over $F$ with cyclic Galois group generated by $\sigma$.

> [!hint]- Hint 3: Primitive Element Theorem
> Since every element in a finite extension $E/F$ is separable over $F$, apply the primitive element theorem to write $E = F(\gamma)$.

## Solution

> [!success]- Solution
>
> Let $k^a$ be an algebraic closure of $k$, let $\sigma \in \operatorname{Aut}_k(k^a)$, and let
> $$
> F = (k^a)^{\langle \sigma \rangle} = \{x \in k^a \mid \sigma(x) = x\}
> $$
> be the fixed field of $\sigma$.
>
> ### 1. Finiteness of $\sigma$-Orbits and Invariant Polynomials
>
> Let $\alpha \in k^a$ be any element. Because $k^a$ is algebraic over $k$, $\alpha$ has a minimal polynomial $m_\alpha(X) \in k[X]$.
> The polynomial $m_\alpha(X)$ has only finitely many roots in $k^a$, say $R \subset k^a$.
>
> Because $\sigma$ fixes $k$ pointwise, $\sigma$ permutes the roots in $R$:
> $$
> m_\alpha(\sigma(x)) = \sigma(m_\alpha(x)) = 0 \quad \text{for all } x \in R.
> $$
> Thus the orbit of $\alpha$ under the cyclic subgroup $\langle \sigma \rangle$,
> $$
> \mathcal O_\alpha = \{\sigma^j(\alpha) : j \in \mathbb Z\},
> $$
> is a subset of $R$, and hence is a non-empty **finite** set.
>
> Let $m = |\mathcal O_\alpha|$. Then $\sigma^m(\alpha) = \alpha$, and the orbit consists of $m$ distinct elements:
> $$
> \mathcal O_\alpha = \{\alpha_1, \alpha_2, \dots, \alpha_m\}, \qquad \text{where } \alpha_{j+1} = \sigma(\alpha_j).
> $$
> Consider the monic polynomial
> $$
> P_\alpha(X) = \prod_{j=1}^m (X - \alpha_j) = \prod_{j=1}^m (X - \sigma^j(\alpha)) \in k^a[X].
> $$
> Applying $\sigma$ to the coefficients of $P_\alpha(X)$:
> $$
> \sigma(P_\alpha)(X) = \prod_{j=1}^m (X - \sigma(\alpha_j)) = \prod_{j=1}^m (X - \alpha_{j+1}) = P_\alpha(X).
> $$
> Because all coefficients of $P_\alpha(X)$ are fixed by $\sigma$, they lie in the fixed field $F$:
> $$
> P_\alpha(X) \in F[X].
> $$
>
> ---
>
> ### 2. Separability and Simple Extensions
>
> Since the elements $\alpha_1, \dots, \alpha_m$ are pairwise distinct by construction, $P_\alpha(X)$ has no multiple roots.
> Since $P_\alpha(\alpha) = 0$, the minimal polynomial of $\alpha$ over $F$, denoted $\operatorname{Irr}(\alpha, F)$, divides $P_\alpha(X)$ in $F[X]$.
> In particular, $\operatorname{Irr}(\alpha, F)$ has simple roots, so $\alpha$ is **separable** over $F$.
>
> Furthermore, all roots of $P_\alpha(X)$ are powers of $\sigma$ applied to $\alpha$, and hence lie in $F(\alpha)$:
> $$
> \alpha_j = \sigma^{j-1}(\alpha) \in F(\alpha).
> $$
> Thus $F(\alpha)$ is the splitting field of the separable polynomial $\operatorname{Irr}(\alpha, F)$ over $F$.
> Therefore, $F(\alpha)/F$ is a finite Galois extension.
> The restriction $\sigma|_{F(\alpha)}$ is an $F$-automorphism of $F(\alpha)$ that cyclically permutes the roots of $\operatorname{Irr}(\alpha, F)$, and its fixed field in $F(\alpha)$ is $F(\alpha) \cap F = F$.
> By the Fundamental Theorem of Galois Theory,
> $$
> \operatorname{Gal}(F(\alpha)/F) = \langle \sigma|_{F(\alpha)} \rangle \cong \mathbb Z/m\mathbb Z.
> $$
> Thus every simple extension $F(\alpha)/F$ is cyclic.
>
> ---
>
> ### 3. General Finite Extensions of $F$
>
> Let $E/F$ be an arbitrary finite extension of $F$ inside $k^a$.
> By Section 2, every element of $E$ is separable over $F$.
> Thus $E/F$ is a finite separable field extension.
>
> By the **Primitive Element Theorem**, any finite separable extension is simple:
> $$
> E = F(\gamma) \quad \text{for some } \gamma \in E.
> $$
> Applying the result of Section 2 to $\alpha = \gamma$, the extension $E = F(\gamma)$ is Galois over $F$ with Galois group
> $$
> \operatorname{Gal}(E/F) = \langle \sigma|_E \rangle \cong \mathbb Z/[E:F]\mathbb Z.
> $$
>
> Therefore, every finite extension of $F$ is cyclic.
