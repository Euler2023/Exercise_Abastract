# Exercise Gal136: Cyclic Galois Group from an Irreducible Reduction Modulo p

## Problem Statement

> [!question] Lang, Chapter VII, Exercise 1
> Let $K$ be a Galois extension of the rationals $\mathbb Q$, with group $G$. Let $B$ be the integral closure of $\mathbb Z$ in $K$, and let $\alpha\in B$ be such that $K=\mathbb Q(\alpha)$. Let $f(X)=\operatorname{Irr}(\alpha,\mathbb Q,X)$. Let $p$ be a prime number, and assume that $f$ remains irreducible mod $p$ over $\mathbb Z/p\mathbb Z$. What can you say about the Galois group $G$? (Artin asked this question to Tate on his qualifying exam.)

## Hints

> [!hint]- Hint 1: Relate the residue field to the roots modulo $p$
> The minimal polynomial $f(X)\in\mathbb Z[X]$ is monic of degree $n=[K:\mathbb Q]=|G|$. If $\mathfrak P$ is a prime ideal of $B$ lying above $p$, consider the subring $\mathbb F_p[\bar{\alpha}]\subseteq B/\mathfrak P$, where $\bar{\alpha}=\alpha\bmod\mathfrak P$.

> [!hint]- Hint 2: Compute the residue class degree and ramification index
> Because $\bar{f}(X)\in\mathbb F_p[X]$ is irreducible of degree $n$, the subfield generated by $\bar{\alpha}$ has degree $n$ over $\mathbb F_p$. Compare this with the fundamental degree identity $[K:\mathbb Q]=e\cdot f\cdot g$.

> [!hint]- Hint 3: Identify the decomposition group with the residue Galois group
> Since $f=n$, what are $e$ and $g$? What does this tell you about the decomposition group $G_{\mathfrak P}$ and the inertia group $I_{\mathfrak P}$? Use the canonical reduction homomorphism to the Galois group of a finite field.

## Solution

> [!success]- Complete Derivation
> Let $n=[K:\mathbb Q]=|G|$. Since $\alpha\in B$, its minimal polynomial $f(X)=\operatorname{Irr}(\alpha,\mathbb Q,X)$ has integer coefficients and is monic of degree $n$.
> 
> Let $\bar{f}(X)=f(X)\bmod p\in\mathbb F_p[X]$. By hypothesis, $\bar{f}(X)$ is irreducible of degree $n$ over $\mathbb F_p=\mathbb Z/p\mathbb Z$.
> 
> Let $\mathfrak P$ be any prime ideal of $B$ lying above $p$, so $\mathfrak P\cap\mathbb Z=p\mathbb Z$. In the residue class field $k_{\mathfrak P}=B/\mathfrak P$, the image $\bar{\alpha}=\alpha\bmod\mathfrak P$ satisfies $\bar{f}(\bar{\alpha})=0$. Since $\bar{f}$ is irreducible over $\mathbb F_p$ of degree $n$, the subfield $\mathbb F_p[\bar{\alpha}]$ is isomorphic to $\mathbb F_{p^n}$, and hence:
> $$
> f(\mathfrak P|p) = [B/\mathfrak P : \mathbb F_p] \ge [\mathbb F_p[\bar{\alpha}] : \mathbb F_p] = n.
> $$
> 
> Because $K/\mathbb Q$ is a Galois extension of degree $n$, all prime ideals of $B$ lying above $p$ are conjugate under $G$. By the fundamental degree identity:
> $$
> [K:\mathbb Q] = e\cdot f\cdot g = n,
> $$
> where $g$ is the number of primes lying above $p$, $e$ is the ramification index, and $f=f(\mathfrak P|p)$ is the residue class degree. Since $e\ge1$ and $g\ge1$, the inequality $f\ge n$ forces:
> $$
> f = n,\qquad e = 1,\qquad g = 1.
> $$
> 
> This implies:
> 1. There is a unique prime ideal $\mathfrak P$ in $B$ lying above $p$, which means $p$ is unramified and inert in $K$.
> 2. The decomposition group of $\mathfrak P$ over $\mathbb Q$ is the stabilizer of $\mathfrak P$ in $G$:
>    $$
>    G_{\mathfrak P} = \{\sigma\in G : \sigma(\mathfrak P)=\mathfrak P\} = G,
>    $$
>    since $\mathfrak P$ is the only prime above $p$.
> 3. Since $e=1$, the inertia group is trivial:
>    $$
>    I_{\mathfrak P} = \{\sigma\in G_{\mathfrak P} : \sigma(x)\equiv x\pmod{\mathfrak P}\text{ for all }x\in B\} = \{1\}.
>    $$
> 4. By Proposition 2.5 of Chapter VII, the canonical reduction homomorphism:
>    $$
>    \mu: G_{\mathfrak P} \longrightarrow \operatorname{Gal}(k_{\mathfrak P}/\mathbb F_p) = \operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb F_{p^n}/\mathbb F_p)
>    $$
>    is an isomorphism.
> 
> The Galois group of a finite field extension $\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb F_{p^n}/\mathbb F_p)$ is cyclic of order $n$, generated by the arithmetic Frobenius automorphism:
> $$
> \operatorname{Frob}_p: \xi \longmapsto \xi^p.
> $$
> 
> Pulling back via $\mu^{-1}$, the Galois group $G$ possesses a generator $\sigma\in G$ (the Frobenius element $\left(\frac{K/\mathbb Q}{\mathfrak P}\right)$) satisfying:
> $$
> \sigma(x) \equiv x^p \pmod{\mathfrak P}\qquad\text{for all }x\in B.
> $$
> 
> In conclusion:
> The Galois group $G$ is a **cyclic group of order $n$** (where $n=\deg f=[K:\mathbb Q]$), generated by the Frobenius automorphism at $p$.

## Notes

- **Historical Context:** Lang explicitly notes that Michael Artin asked this question to John Tate on his Ph.D. qualifying examination at Princeton University.
- **Dedekind–Kummer Criterion:** The argument does not require the full ring of integers $B$ to equal $\mathbb Z[\alpha]$; it only uses the fact that the subfield $\mathbb F_p[\bar{\alpha}]$ has degree $n$, which bounds the residue class degree from below.
- **Independent Derivation:** The solution above is an independent derivation using the Galois decomposition sequence from Lang Chapter VII §2.
