# Exercise Gal194: Inert Primes in a Noncyclic Galois Extension

## Problem Statement

> [!question] Neukirch VI.3.1
> If the Galois extension $L|K$ is not cyclic, then there are at most finitely many primes of $K$ which do not split in $L$.

## Hints

> [!hint]- Hint
> Here "do not split" means there is only one prime above the prime of $K$. At an unramified such prime, the whole Galois group would be a cyclic decomposition group.

> [!hint]- Stronger hint
> One prime above an unramified p would make the whole Galois group equal to its cyclic decomposition group.

## Solution

> [!success]- Solution
> **Proof scope:** Independent derivation
>
> In this section, "nonsplit" means having only one prime above it in $L$, not merely failing to split completely. For an unramified finite prime $\mathfrak p$ with a unique prime $\mathfrak P$ above it, its decomposition group is $D_\mathfrak P=G(L/K)$. But the Galois group of an unramified local extension is the Galois group of a finite residue-field extension and is cyclic, generated by Frobenius. This is a contradiction.
> Every nonsplit prime therefore belongs to the set of ramified or infinite primes. A finite extension of number fields ramifies at only finitely many places, so the set is finite.

## Notes

The statement was checked against the original page images. [Jürgen Neukirch, Algebraic Number Theory, English ed., 1999, Ch. VI, §3, Exercise 1, printed p.379, PDF p.398]. The solution is independently derived. Interpreting "nonsplit" as "not completely split" would make the statement false: a biquadratic extension, for example, has infinitely many primes that do not split completely.
