# Exercise Gal119: Degree of Rational Functions and Automorphisms of Rational Function Fields

## Problem Statement

> [!question]
> Let $k$ be a field and $X$ a variable over $k$. Let
> $$
> \varphi(X) = \frac{f(X)}{g(X)}
> $$
> be a rational function in $k(X)$, expressed as a quotient of two polynomials $f, g$ which are relatively prime. Define the degree of $\varphi$ to be $\max(\deg f, \deg g)$. Let $Y = \varphi(X)$.
> 
> (a) Show that the degree of $\varphi$ is equal to the degree of the field extension $k(X)$ over $k(Y)$ (assuming $Y \notin k$).
> 
> (b) Show that every automorphism of $k(X)$ over $k$ can be represented by a rational function $\varphi$ of degree 1, and is therefore induced by a map
> $$
> X \mapsto \frac{aX + b}{cX + d}
> $$
> with $a, b, c, d \in k$ and $ad - bc \ne 0$.
> 
> (c) Let $G$ be the group of automorphisms of $k(X)$ over $k$. Show that $G$ is generated by the following automorphisms:
> $$
> \tau_b: X \mapsto X + b, \quad \sigma_a: X \mapsto aX \ (a \ne 0), \quad \operatorname{inv}: X \mapsto X^{-1}
> $$
> with $a, b \in k$.

## Hints

> [!hint]- Weak Hint
> - For (a), construct a polynomial in $k(Y)[T]$ that has $X$ as a root and show that it is irreducible using Gauss's Lemma.
> - For (b), an automorphism must satisfy $[k(X) : k(Y)] = 1$.
> - For (c), use elementary row operations or matrix factorizations in $\operatorname{GL}_2(k)$.

> [!hint]- Moderate Hint
> - In (a), consider $P(T) = f(T) - Y g(T) \in k[Y][T] \cong k[T][Y]$. Since $P$ is linear in $Y$ and $\gcd(f, g) = 1$, $P$ is primitive in $k[T][Y]$, hence irreducible.
> - In (c), if $c = 0$, the map is linear $X \mapsto \frac{a}{d} X + \frac{b}{d}$. If $c \ne 0$, decompose $\frac{aX + b}{cX + d} = \frac{a}{c} + \frac{bc - ad}{c(cX + d)}$.

> [!hint]- Strong Hint
> When $c \ne 0$, write the fractional linear transformation as the composition:
> $$
> X \xmapsto{\tau_{d/c}} X + \frac{d}{c} \xmapsto{\operatorname{inv}} \left(X + \frac{d}{c}\right)^{-1} \xmapsto{\sigma_{(bc - ad)/c^2}} \frac{bc - ad}{c^2} \left(X + \frac{d}{c}\right)^{-1} \xmapsto{\tau_{a/c}} \frac{aX + b}{cX + d}.
> $$

## Solution

> [!success]- Complete Derivation
> Let $k$ be a field and $k(X)$ the rational function field in one variable over $k$.
>
> ---
>
> ### Part (a): Degree of $k(X)$ over $k(Y)$
> Let $\varphi(X) = \frac{f(X)}{g(X)}$ with $f, g \in k[X]$, $\gcd(f, g) = 1$, and $Y = \varphi(X) \notin k$.
> Let $d = \deg \varphi = \max(\deg f, \deg g) \ge 1$.
>
> In the extension $k(X)/k(Y)$, the element $X$ satisfies the relation
> $$
> Y = \frac{f(X)}{g(X)} \iff f(X) - Y g(X) = 0.
> $$
> Consider the auxiliary polynomial
> $$
> P(T) = f(T) - Y g(T) \in k(Y)[T].
> $$
> We determine the minimal polynomial of $X$ over $k(Y)$:
>
> 1. **Degree of $P(T)$:**
>    Since $Y$ is an indeterminate over $k$ (as $Y \notin k$ and $X$ is transcendental), the coefficients of $T^j$ in $f(T) - Y g(T)$ are of the form $a_j - Y b_j$. Since $\gcd(f, g) = 1$, not both leading coefficients vanish, so
>    $$
>    \deg_T P(T) = \max(\deg f, \deg g) = d.
>    $$
>
> 2. **Irreducibility of $P(T)$ over $k(Y)$:**
>    We view $P(T)$ as an element of the bivariate polynomial ring $k[Y, T] \cong k[T][Y]$:
>    $$
>    P(T) = -g(T) Y + f(T).
>    $$
>    As a polynomial in $Y$ with coefficients in the UFD $R = k[T]$:
>    - Its degree in $Y$ is 1 (since $g(T) \ne 0$).
>    - Its content in $k[T]$ is $\gcd(-g(T), f(T)) = \gcd(f(T), g(T)) = 1$.
>    A polynomial of degree 1 in $R[Y]$ with content 1 is irreducible in $R[Y] = k[T, Y]$.
>    By Gauss's Lemma, since $P(T)$ is irreducible in $k[T][Y]$, it is also irreducible in the polynomial ring over the fraction field $\operatorname{Frac}(k[Y])[T] = k(Y)[T]$.
>
> 3. **Degree of the Extension:**
>    Since $P(T) \in k(Y)[T]$ is irreducible and $P(X) = 0$, the minimal polynomial of $X$ over $k(Y)$ is $\frac{1}{\operatorname{lc}(P)} P(T)$, whose degree is $d$.
>    Since $k(X) = k(Y)(X)$, we obtain:
>    $$
>    [k(X) : k(Y)] = \deg_T P(T) = \max(\deg f, \deg g) = \deg \varphi.
>    $$
>
> ---
>
> ### Part (b): Classification of Automorphisms
> Let $\Phi \in \operatorname{Aut}_k(k(X))$ be a $k$-automorphism of $k(X)$.
> Since $\Phi$ fixes $k$ pointwise, it is completely determined by the image of $X$:
> $$
> Y = \Phi(X) = \varphi(X) \in k(X).
> $$
> The map $\Phi$ is an automorphism if and only if its image is the whole field $k(X)$, which means:
> $$
> k(Y) = k(X) \iff [k(X) : k(Y)] = 1.
> $$
> By part (a), $[k(X) : k(Y)] = \deg \varphi$. Therefore:
> $$
> \deg \varphi = 1.
> $$
> Writing $\varphi(X) = \frac{f(X)}{g(X)}$ with $\gcd(f, g) = 1$, the condition $\max(\deg f, \deg g) = 1$ implies:
> $$
> f(X) = a X + b, \quad g(X) = c X + d,
> $$
> with $a, b, c, d \in k$, where not both $a$ and $c$ are 0.
> The condition that $f$ and $g$ have no common factor of degree 1 means they are not proportional, which is equivalent to:
> $$
> ad - bc \ne 0.
> $$
> Conversely, for any $a, b, c, d \in k$ with $ad - bc \ne 0$, the rational function $\varphi(X) = \frac{aX + b}{cX + d}$ has degree 1, so $[k(X) : k(\varphi(X))] = 1$, and $X \mapsto \varphi(X)$ defines an automorphism of $k(X)$ over $k$.
>
> ---
>
> ### Part (c): Generators of the Automorphism Group
> Let $G = \operatorname{Aut}_k(k(X)) \cong \operatorname{PGL}_2(k)$. Every element of $G$ is given by a Möbius transformation
> $$
> \varphi(X) = \frac{aX + b}{cX + d}, \quad ad - bc \ne 0.
> $$
> We show that every such transformation is a composition of automorphisms from the specified sets:
>
> - **Case 1: $c = 0$.**
>   Since $ad - bc = ad \ne 0$, we have $d \ne 0$ and $a \ne 0$. Then:
>   $$
>   \varphi(X) = \frac{a}{d} X + \frac{b}{d}.
>   $$
>   This is precisely the composition:
>   $$
>   \varphi = \tau_{b/d} \circ \sigma_{a/d},
>   $$
>   where $\sigma_{a/d}: X \mapsto \frac{a}{d} X$ (since $\frac{a}{d} \ne 0$) and $\tau_{b/d}: X \mapsto X + \frac{b}{d}$.
>
> - **Case 2: $c \ne 0$.**
>   Perform polynomial division:
>   $$
>   \frac{aX + b}{cX + d} = \frac{\frac{a}{c}(cX + d) + \left(b - \frac{ad}{c}\right)}{cX + d} = \frac{a}{c} + \frac{bc - ad}{c(cX + d)} = \frac{a}{c} + \frac{bc - ad}{c^2} \cdot \frac{1}{X + \frac{d}{c}}.
>   $$
>   Let $\Delta = \frac{bc - ad}{c^2} \ne 0$. Then $\varphi(X)$ can be factored into a sequence of elementary transformations:
>   1. Translation: $X_1 = \tau_{d/c}(X) = X + \frac{d}{c}$;
>   2. Inversion: $X_2 = \operatorname{inv}(X_1) = \frac{1}{X + \frac{d}{c}}$;
>   3. Scaling: $X_3 = \sigma_\Delta(X_2) = \Delta \cdot \frac{1}{X + \frac{d}{c}}$;
>   4. Translation: $X_4 = \tau_{a/c}(X_3) = \frac{a}{c} + \Delta \cdot \frac{1}{X + \frac{d}{c}} = \varphi(X)$.
>   In other words:
>   $$
>   \varphi = \tau_{a/c} \circ \sigma_\Delta \circ \operatorname{inv} \circ \tau_{d/c}.
>   $$
>
> In both cases, $\varphi$ is expressed as a finite product of elements from $\{\tau_b \mid b \in k\}$, $\{\sigma_a \mid a \in k^\times\}$, and the inversion $\operatorname{inv}: X \mapsto X^{-1}$.
> Thus these elements generate $G$.

## Notes

- This result establishes the canonical isomorphism $\operatorname{Aut}_k(k(X)) \cong \operatorname{PGL}_2(k) = \operatorname{GL}_2(k) / k^\times$.
- Lüroth's theorem asserts that every intermediate field $k \subsetneq L \subseteq k(X)$ is of the form $L = k(Y)$ for some rational function $Y = \varphi(X)$, and by part (a), $[k(X) : L] = \deg \varphi$.
