# Exercise Gal215: Galois Descent of a Quotient Isogeny

## Problem Statement

> [!question] Original Exercise 7.8.2
> Let $E/k$ be an elliptic curve and $C\subset E[N]$ a finite subgroup. Set $l=k(E[N])$, $G=\operatorname{Gal}(l/k)$,
> $$
> H=\{\sigma\in G:\sigma(C)=C\},\qquad k'=l^H.
> $$
> The quotient curve $E/C$ and quotient isogeny $\varphi$ have been constructed over $l$. Show that they can be defined over $k'$.
>
> (a) The group $G$ acts on $l(E)$ from the left through its action on $l$, written $f\mapsto{}^\sigma f$. The group $E[N]$ acts by translations $f\mapsto\tau_P^*f$. Check that the semidirect product $H\ltimes C$ acts by
> $$
> (\sigma,P):f\longmapsto\tau_P^*{}^\sigma f.
> $$
> (Hints are at the end of the book.)
>
> (b) Let $K=l(E)^{H\ltimes C}$. Show that $K\cap l=k'$, and explain why $K$ is the function field of a curve $E'$ over $k'$. We do not yet know $E'$ is elliptic.
>
> (c) Use Theorem 7.7.1 to show that $l(E')$ is the subfield generated by $l$ and $K$. Show that this is also $l(E)^C=l(E/C)$, so $E'$ is isomorphic over $l$ to $E/C$.
>
> (d) Show that the injection $i:l(E')\to l(E)$ restricts to a $k'$-injection $i:k'(E')\to k'(E)$, giving a morphism $\varphi:E\to E'$ over $k'$. In particular $\varphi(0_E)\in E'(k')$; together with (c), this shows $E'$ is elliptic over $k'$.
>
> (e) Over what subfield of $\mathbb Q(j,E_j[N])$ does this show that $E_j\to E_j/\langle Q_\tau\rangle$ is defined?

## Hints

> [!hint]- Hint 1
> First compute conjugation of a translation by a coefficient automorphism. Then use finite-group Galois correspondence inside $l(E)$.

> [!hint]- Hint 2
> The subgroup fixing the compositum $lK$ is the intersection of the subgroups fixing $l$ and $K$, namely the translations by $C$.

## Solution

> [!success]- Solution
> **Proof provenance:** Independent derivation. Imported results are named at their point of use.
>
> Work in characteristic zero as in the chapter, so all finite torsion and fixed-field extensions below are separable. Put $A=l(E)$.
>
> (a) Fix ordinary left composition of coefficient automorphisms. Coefficient conjugation and translation satisfy
> $$
> {}^\sigma(\tau_{P'}^*f)=\tau_{\sigma P'}^*({}^\sigma f).
> $$
> Indeed both sides, evaluated at $R$, apply $\sigma$ to the coefficients of $f$ and replace its argument by $R+\sigma P'$. Thus, for $T_{\sigma,P}=\tau_P^*{}^\sigma$, direct composition gives
> $$
> T_{\sigma,P}T_{\sigma',P'}=T_{\sigma\sigma',\,P+\sigma P'}.
> $$
> The law $(\sigma,P)(\sigma',P')=(\sigma\sigma',P+\sigma P')$ is precisely the left-composition semidirect product. It is defined because $H$ preserves $C$. The action is faithful: restriction to constants detects $\sigma$, and a translation acting trivially on a curve's function field is the identity, hence $P=0$.
>
> (b) Translations fix every constant in $l$, while $H$ acts there as its original Galois action. Consequently $K\cap l=l^H=k'$. The field $A$ is finite over $K$ by the theorem on fixed fields of finite groups; thus $K/k'$ has transcendence degree one and is finitely generated. Moreover $k'$ is algebraically closed in $K$: any element of $K$ algebraic over $k'$ is algebraic over $l$, and the geometric integrality of $E/l$ implies it belongs to $l$, hence to $K\cap l$. The function-field/curve correspondence gives a nonsingular projective geometrically integral curve $E'/k'$ with function field $K$.
>
> (c) In characteristic zero this regular function field remains a field on extending constants, and Theorem 7.7.1 identifies $l(E')$ with the compositum $lK\subset A$. Now $A/K$ is Galois with group $H\ltimes C$. Its subgroup fixing $l$ is exactly $C$. Galois correspondence therefore gives
> $$
> lK=A^C=l(E/C).
> $$
> This proves the required isomorphism after extension of constants, including genus one.
>
> (d) The elements of $K$ are fixed by the subgroup $H\times\{0\}$ and therefore lie in $A^H=k'(E)$. This proves the restricted field injection. The contravariant correspondence gives a nonconstant morphism of nonsingular projective curves $\varphi:E\to E'$ over $k'$. Its base change is the quotient isogeny. Since $0_E$ is $k'$-rational, so is $\varphi(0_E)$; take it as the origin on the genus-one curve $E'$. Then $\varphi$ preserves origins and is an isogeny over $k'$.
>
> (e) In the universal torsion basis $(P_\tau,Q_\tau)$, the stabilizer of $\langle Q_\tau\rangle$ in $\mathrm{GL}_2(\mathbb Z/N\mathbb Z)$ consists of the matrices $\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\0&d\end{pmatrix}$. Its fixed field is $\mathbb Q(j,f_0)=\mathbb Q(X_0(N)_{\mathrm{alg}})$: this is the cyclic-subgroup fixed-field computation of Section 7.7. Thus the quotient isogeny descends to this field.
>
> The searchable field diagram of Figure 7.7 is
> $$
> \begin{array}{ccccc}
> &&A=l(E)&&\\
> &&\big\vert\ C&&\\
> l=k(E[N])&&A^C=l(E')=l(E/C)&&\\
> \big\vert\ H&&\big\vert\ H&&\\
> k'=l^H&\subset&K=k'(E')&&\\
> \big\vert&&&&\\
> k&&&&
> \end{array}
> $$
> Here $A/K$ has group $H\ltimes C$, and $l/k$ has group $G$.
