# Exercise Gal208: A Nonabelian Unramified Extension over a Quadratic Field

## Problem Statement

> [!question] Neukirch VI.7.6
> Show that $K|\mathbb Q(\sqrt{19\cdot151})$ is a (non-abelian!) unramified extension.

## Hints

> [!hint]- Hint
> The discriminant square root cuts out $A_5$. At each ramified rational prime, squarefree discriminant forces inertia to be a transposition.

> [!hint]- Stronger hint
> Tame discriminant exponent one forces transposition inertia; its intersection with A_5 is trivial.

## Solution

> [!success]- Solution
> **Proof scope:** Independent derivation
>
> Write $D=2869$ and $F=\mathbb Q(\sqrt D)$. The Vandermonde product $\Delta=\prod_{i<j}(\alpha_i-\alpha_j)$ satisfies $\Delta^2=D$, and permutations act on $\Delta$ by their signs. Since $G(K/\mathbb Q)=S_5$ and $F=K^{A_5}$, we obtain $G(K/F)=A_5$, which is indeed nonabelian.
> We have $\mathcal O_k=\mathbb Z[\alpha]$ and $d_k=19\cdot151$, so the only possible finite ramification in $K/\mathbb Q$ is at $19,151$. Neither prime divides $|S_5|=120$, so the ramification is tame. For the permutation representation on five roots, the tame discriminant exponent is $5-\#(\text{inertia orbits})$. Here that exponent is $1$, so an inertia generator has just one two-cycle and fixes the other three points. Thus the inertia group is $C_2$ generated by a transposition.
> Consequently the relative inertia group at every finite prime is $I\cap A_5=1$. Primes outside $19,151$ were already unramified. Hence $K/F$ is unramified at every finite place.
> **Convention at infinite places.** The field $F$ is real quadratic, whereas $K$ is not totally real, so some real place becomes complex. Under the book's convention that $\mathbb C/\mathbb R$ is unramified (VI, printed p.398/PDF417), the exercise's term "unramified" is valid. Under the small-Hilbert-class-field convention requiring real places to split completely, one should instead say "unramified at every finite place."
