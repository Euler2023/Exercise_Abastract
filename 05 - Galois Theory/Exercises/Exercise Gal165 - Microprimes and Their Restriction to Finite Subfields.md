# Exercise Gal165: Microprimes and Their Restriction to Finite Subfields

## Problem Statement

> [!question] Neukirch IV.4.1
> Assume that every closed abelian subgroup of $G$ is procyclic. Let $K|k$ be a finite extension. A microprime $\mathfrak p$ of $K$ is by definition a conjugacy class $\{\sigma\}\subseteq G_K$ of some Frobenius element $\sigma\in\operatorname{Frob}(\bar k|K)$ which is not a proper power $\sigma'^n$, $n>1$, of some other Frobenius element $\sigma'\in\operatorname{Frob}(\bar k|K)$. Let $\operatorname{spec}(K)$ be the set of all microprimes of $K$. Show that if $L|K$ is a finite extension, then there is a canonical mapping
> $$
> \pi:\operatorname{spec}(L)\longrightarrow\operatorname{spec}(K).
> $$
> Above any microprime $\mathfrak p$ there are only finitely many microprimes $\mathfrak P$ of $L$, i.e., the set $\pi^{-1}(\mathfrak p)$ is finite. We write $\mathfrak P\mid\mathfrak p$ to mean $\mathfrak P\in\pi^{-1}(\mathfrak p)$.

## Hints

> [!hint]- Hint 1
> If $s$ is a Frobenius element, then $d$ is injective on every procyclic group containing $s$. First use this to show that the entire centralizer $C_{G_K}(s)$ is procyclic and has a unique generator of positive integral degree.

> [!hint]- Hint 2
> Replace a primitive Frobenius element of $G_L$ by its unique primitive root in $G_K$, then take its $G_K$ conjugacy class.

> [!hint]- Hint 3
> Fix a primitive element $s$ downstairs. The microprimes above it are parametrized by $\overline{\langle s\rangle}$ orbits on a finite coset space, equivalently by double cosets.

## Solution

> [!success]- Solution
> **Proof scope:** Independent derivation
>
> Write $D=d_K:G_K\twoheadrightarrow\widehat{\mathbb Z}$. Being a Frobenius element means that $D(s)$ is a positive integer. Let $B$ be a closed procyclic subgroup containing $s$. The closed cyclic subgroup generated by $s$ maps isomorphically under $D$ onto $D(s)\widehat{\mathbb Z}$. Thus every Sylow component of $B$ contains a copy of $\mathbb Z_p$, so $B\simeq\widehat{\mathbb Z}$, and $D|_B$ is injective, since every $p$ component is nonzero.
>
> If $t$ centralizes $s$ and $D(t)=0$, then $\overline{\langle s,t\rangle}$ is closed abelian and hence procyclic by hypothesis. The preceding paragraph gives $t=1$. Thus $D$ is injective on the entire centralizer $C=C_{G_K}(s)$. Consequently $C$ is abelian and hence also procyclic by hypothesis. Its image contains the open subgroup $D(s)\widehat{\mathbb Z}$ and is therefore $m\widehat{\mathbb Z}$ for a positive integer $m\mid D(s)$. There is a unique element $s_0\in C$ with $D(s_0)=m$, and $s=s_0^{D(s)/m}$. Every Frobenius root lies in $C$, so the primitive root $s_0$ is unique, and $C=\overline{\langle s_0\rangle}$.
>
> For a primitive Frobenius element $t$ in $G_L$, use its unique primitive root $s_0$ in $G_K$ to define $\pi([t]_{G_L})=[s_0]_{G_K}$. Conjugation preserves $D$ and the unique primitive root, so this is independent of representatives.
>
> Fix a primitive $s$ downstairs and set $C=\overline{\langle s\rangle}$ and $H=G_L$. Each double coset $H gC$ corresponds to a finite orbit. Let $f_g$ be the smallest positive integer such that $g s^{f_g}g^{-1}\in H$. It exists because $G_K/H$ is finite. This element is primitive in $H$: if it were a proper positive integral power of another Frobenius element, that root would lie in the centralizer $gCg^{-1}$. Injectivity of $D$ and positivity and integrality of the degree force the root to be $g s^r g^{-1}$, but $0<r<f_g$ contradicts minimality. Conversely, every microprime mapping to $[s]$ arises this way. Changing the double-coset representative changes only the $H$ conjugacy class.
>
> Thus $\pi^{-1}([s])$ is in bijection with the finite set $H\backslash G_K/C$, proving finiteness of the fiber. For a tower of two finite extensions, uniqueness of primitive roots also makes these maps compatible with composition.

- **Source and proof status:** [Jürgen Neukirch, Algebraic Number Theory, English ed., 1999, Ch. IV, §4, Exercise 1, printed p. 290, PDF p. 309]. Independent proof using centralizers, primitive roots, and double cosets, explicitly invoking the additional hypothesis that every closed abelian subgroup is procyclic.
