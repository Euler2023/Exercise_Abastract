# Exercise Gal73: Intermediate Fields of an S3 Times C2 Extension

## Problem Statement

> [!question] 2023 Peking University problem
> Let
> $$
> K=\mathbb Q(\sqrt2,\sqrt{-3},\sqrt[3]5).
> $$
> Prove that:
>
> 1. $K/\mathbb Q$ is Galois;
> 2. $\operatorname{Gal}(K/\mathbb Q)\cong S_3\times C_2$;
> 3. determine all intermediate fields of $K/\mathbb Q$.

## Hints

> [!hint]- Hint 1
> Separate the splitting field of $x^3-5$ from the quadratic field $\mathbb Q(\sqrt2)$.

> [!hint]- Hint 2
> The $S_3$ splitting field has a unique quadratic subfield. Use it to compute the intersection of the two factors.

> [!hint]- Hint 3
> Write the three reflections of $S_3$ as $u_0,u_1,u_2$, where $u_j$ fixes $\sqrt[3]5\,\omega^j$. Classify the subgroups of $S_3\times C_2$ by order.

## Solution

> [!success]- Solution
> Put
> $$
> \alpha=\sqrt[3]5,\qquad
> \omega=\frac{-1+\sqrt{-3}}2,\qquad
> \beta=\sqrt2.
> $$
> The field $K$ is the splitting field over $\mathbb Q$ of
> $$
> (x^3-5)(x^2-2),
> $$
> because the roots are $\alpha,\alpha\omega,\alpha\omega^2$ and $\pm\beta$. In characteristic zero the polynomial is separable, so $K/\mathbb Q$ is Galois.
>
> Let
> $$
> L_1=\mathbb Q(\alpha,\omega),\qquad L_2=\mathbb Q(\beta).
> $$
> The polynomial $x^3-5$ is Eisenstein at $5$, and its discriminant is
> $$
> -27\cdot5^2=-3\cdot15^2,
> $$
> which is not a rational square. Hence $[L_1:\mathbb Q]=6$ and
> $$
> \operatorname{Gal}(L_1/\mathbb Q)\cong S_3.
> $$
> Its unique quadratic subfield is $\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-3})$. Since this is not $\mathbb Q(\sqrt2)$, the intersection $L_1\cap L_2$ cannot be quadratic; hence it is $\mathbb Q$. Both factors are Galois, so restriction gives
> $$
> \operatorname{Gal}(K/\mathbb Q)
> \cong S_3\times C_2,
> \qquad [K:\mathbb Q]=12.
> $$
>
> We now list the complete Galois correspondence. Let $r$ generate the order-three subgroup of $S_3$, let $u_j$ be the reflection fixing $\alpha\omega^j$ for $j=0,1,2$, and let $t$ be the sign change of $\beta$. Thus $t$ commutes with $S_3$.
>
> The trivial endpoints are
> $$
> \begin{array}{c|c}
> \text{subgroup}&\text{fixed field}\\ \hline
> S_3\times C_2&\mathbb Q\\
> \{1\}&K.
> \end{array}
> $$
>
> The three subgroups of order $6$ and their quadratic fixed fields are
> $$
> \begin{array}{c|c}
> S_3\times\{1\}&\mathbb Q(\beta)\\
> \langle r,t\rangle&\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-3})\\
> \langle r,u_0t\rangle&\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-6}).
> \end{array}
> $$
> Here the last subgroup is independent of the chosen reflection: replacing $u_0$ by another $u_j$ does not change the subgroup generated together with $r$ and $t$.
>
> The three subgroups of order $4$ are the Sylow $2$-subgroups
> $$
> \langle u_j,t\rangle\cong C_2^2,
> $$
> and their fixed fields are
> $$
> \mathbb Q(\alpha\omega^j),\qquad j=0,1,2.
> $$
>
> The unique subgroup of order $3$ is $\langle r\rangle$, with fixed field
> $$
> \mathbb Q(\sqrt{-3},\sqrt2).
> $$
>
> Finally, the seven subgroups of order $2$ are generated by the seven involutions. Their fixed fields are
> $$
> \begin{array}{c|c}
> \langle t\rangle&\mathbb Q(\alpha,\sqrt{-3})\\
> \langle u_j\rangle&\mathbb Q(\alpha\omega^j,\sqrt2),\quad j=0,1,2\\
> \langle u_jt\rangle&\mathbb Q(\alpha\omega^j,\sqrt{-6}),\quad j=0,1,2.
> \end{array}
> $$
> Each displayed field has the degree predicted by the subgroup index, so it is the full fixed field. The counts
> $$
> 2+3+3+1+7=16
> $$
> exhaust all subgroups of $S_3\times C_2$, hence all intermediate fields.
