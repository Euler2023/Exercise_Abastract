# Exercise Gal122: Compositum of Splitting Fields of X to the p Minus a

## Problem Statement

> [!question]
> Let $a \ne 0, \ne \pm 1$ be a square-free integer. For each prime number $p$, let $K_p$ be the splitting field of the polynomial $X^p - a$ over $\mathbf{Q}$. Show that $[K_p : \mathbf{Q}] = p(p - 1)$. For each square-free integer $m > 0$, let
> $$
> K_m = \prod_{p \mid m} K_p
> $$
> be the compositum of all fields $K_p$ for $p \mid m$. Let $d_m = [K_m : \mathbf{Q}]$ be the degree of $K_m$ over $\mathbf{Q}$. Show that if $m$ is odd then $d_m = \prod_{p \mid m} d_p$, and if $m$ is even, $m = 2n$ then $d_{2n} = d_n$ or $2d_n$ according as $\sqrt{a}$ is or is not in the field of $m$-th roots of unity $\mathbf{Q}(\zeta_m)$.

## Hints

> [!hint]- Weak Hint
> Express the splitting field $K_p$ as $\mathbf{Q}(\zeta_p, a^{1/p})$. Analyze the cyclotomic part $\mathbf{Q}(\zeta_m)$ and the radical part generated by $a^{1/p}$ separately.

> [!hint]- Moderate Hint
> - For a single prime $p$, Eisenstein's criterion at a prime dividing $a$ shows that $X^p - a$ is irreducible over $\mathbf{Q}$. Then check irreducibility over $\mathbf{Q}(\zeta_p)$.
> - Over the cyclotomic field $F_m = \mathbf{Q}(\zeta_m)$, the extension $K_m / F_m$ is generated by $\{a^{1/p} \mid p \mid m\}$. Use Kummer theory to determine the degree $[K_m : F_m]$.

> [!hint]- Strong Hint
> When $m$ is odd, the primes $p \mid m$ are all odd, and the radical extensions $F_m(a^{1/p})$ are linearly disjoint of degree $p$ because their degrees are distinct primes and $\mathbf{Q}(\zeta_m)$ contains no nontrivial $p$-th roots of $a$. When $p = 2 \mid m$, $K_2 = \mathbf{Q}(\sqrt{a})$, which is quadratic over $\mathbf{Q}$. Determine whether $\sqrt{a}$ already lies in the cyclotomic base field $\mathbf{Q}(\zeta_m)$.

## Solution

> [!success]- Complete Derivation
> Let $a \in \mathbf{Z}$ be square-free, $a \ne 0, \pm 1$.
>
> ---
>
> ### 1. Degree of $K_p$ over $\mathbf{Q}$
> The roots of $X^p - a$ are $\zeta_p^j a^{1/p}$ for $j = 0, 1, \dots, p - 1$, where $\zeta_p$ is a primitive $p$-th root of unity and $a^{1/p}$ is a real $p$-th root (or any fixed root).
> Thus the splitting field is:
> $$
> K_p = \mathbf{Q}(\zeta_p, a^{1/p}).
> $$
>
> - **Case $p = 2$:**
>   The polynomial is $X^2 - a$. Its roots are $\pm \sqrt{a}$. Since $a$ is square-free and $a \ne 0, \pm 1$, $a$ is not a square in $\mathbf{Q}$, so $X^2 - a$ is irreducible over $\mathbf{Q}$.
>   The splitting field is $K_2 = \mathbf{Q}(\sqrt{a})$, and
>   $$
>   [K_2 : \mathbf{Q}] = 2 = 2(2 - 1).
>   $$
>
> - **Case $p$ is an odd prime:**
>   Since $a$ is square-free and $a \ne \pm 1$, there exists a prime $\ell \mid a$ with $\ell^2 \nmid a$.
>   By Eisenstein's criterion at $\ell$, $X^p - a$ is irreducible in $\mathbf{Z}[X]$ and hence over $\mathbf{Q}$.
>   Therefore, $[\mathbf{Q}(a^{1/p}) : \mathbf{Q}] = p$.
>
>   Now consider the cyclotomic field $\mathbf{Q}(\zeta_p)$. Its degree is $[\mathbf{Q}(\zeta_p) : \mathbf{Q}] = p - 1$.
>   In the tower $\mathbf{Q} \subseteq \mathbf{Q}(\zeta_p) \subseteq K_p = \mathbf{Q}(\zeta_p, a^{1/p})$, the degree $[K_p : \mathbf{Q}(\zeta_p)]$ must divide $[\mathbf{Q}(a^{1/p}) : \mathbf{Q}] = p$, so it is either 1 or $p$.
>   If it were 1, then $a^{1/p} \in \mathbf{Q}(\zeta_p)$, which would imply that $p = [\mathbf{Q}(a^{1/p}) : \mathbf{Q}]$ divides $[\mathbf{Q}(\zeta_p) : \mathbf{Q}] = p - 1$, impossible since $\gcd(p, p - 1) = 1$.
>   Thus $[K_p : \mathbf{Q}(\zeta_p)] = p$.
>   By the tower property:
>   $$
>   d_p = [K_p : \mathbf{Q}] = [K_p : \mathbf{Q}(\zeta_p)] [\mathbf{Q}(\zeta_p) : \mathbf{Q}] = p(p - 1).
>   $$
>
> ---
>
> ### 2. Compositum for Square-Free $m$
> For any square-free integer $m > 0$, let
> $$
> F_m = \prod_{p \mid m} \mathbf{Q}(\zeta_p) = \mathbf{Q}(\zeta_m).
> $$
> Since the primes $p \mid m$ are distinct, the cyclotomic fields $\mathbf{Q}(\zeta_p)$ are linearly disjoint over $\mathbf{Q}$, so:
> $$
> [F_m : \mathbf{Q}] = \varphi(m) = \prod_{p \mid m} (p - 1).
> $$
> Over $F_m$, the field $K_m$ is generated by adjoining the $p$-th roots $a^{1/p}$ for all primes $p \mid m$:
> $$
> K_m = F_m\left(a^{1/p} : p \mid m\right).
> $$
>
> ---
>
> ### 3. The Case Where $m$ is Odd
> Suppose $m$ is odd. Then every prime $p \mid m$ is an odd prime.
>
> We claim that over $F_m$, the extensions $F_m(a^{1/p})$ are linearly disjoint of degree $p$, so:
> $$
> [K_m : F_m] = \prod_{p \mid m} p = m.
> $$
>
> - **Proof of Claim:**
>   Let $p_1, \dots, p_k$ be the distinct odd prime factors of $m$.
>   Suppose by induction that for some $j < k$, $L = F_m(a^{1/p_1}, \dots, a^{1/p_j})$ has degree $p_1 \cdots p_j$ over $F_m$.
>   We must show that $[L(a^{1/p_{j+1}}) : L] = p_{j+1}$.
>   Let $p = p_{j+1}$. If $a^{1/p} \in L$, then by Kummer theory over $F_m$ (since $\zeta_p \in F_m$), $a \in (L^*)^p \cap F_m^*$.
>   Since $[L : F_m] = p_1 \cdots p_j$ is coprime to $p$, any element in $F_m^*$ that becomes a $p$-th power in $L$ must already be a $p$-th power in $F_m^*$.
>   Thus $a = u^p$ for some $u \in F_m = \mathbf{Q}(\zeta_m)$.
>   Taking the norm $N_{\mathbf{Q}}^{F_m}$:
>   $$
>   N_{\mathbf{Q}}^{F_m}(a) = a^{[F_m : \mathbf{Q}]} = a^{\varphi(m)}.
>   $$
>   On the other hand, $N_{\mathbf{Q}}^{F_m}(u^p) = (N_{\mathbf{Q}}^{F_m}(u))^p \in \mathbf{Q}^{*p}$.
>   Thus $a^{\varphi(m)}$ must be a $p$-th power in $\mathbf{Q}$.
>   Since $a$ is square-free, the prime factorization of $a$ in $\mathbf{Z}$ has all exponents equal to 1.
>   Therefore, $a^{\varphi(m)}$ is a $p$-th power in $\mathbf{Q}$ if and only if $p \mid \varphi(m)$.
>   However, if $a^{1/p} \in F_m$, then $\mathbf{Q}(a^{1/p}) \subseteq F_m$. Since $a^{1/p}$ is real, its Galois closure over $\mathbf{Q}$ contains $\mathbf{Q}(a^{1/p})$, whose degree is $p$. But $F_m/\mathbf{Q}$ is an abelian extension, so all its subfields are Galois over $\mathbf{Q}$. But $\mathbf{Q}(a^{1/p})$ is NOT Galois over $\mathbf{Q}$ because $p$ is an odd prime and $a^{1/p}$ has non-real conjugates.
>   This contradiction shows that $a^{1/p} \notin F_m$.
>   By Kummer theory, $X^p - a$ is irreducible over $L$, so $[L(a^{1/p}) : L] = p$.
>
> By induction, $[K_m : F_m] = \prod_{p \mid m} p$.
> Therefore:
> $$
> d_m = [K_m : \mathbf{Q}] = [K_m : F_m] [F_m : \mathbf{Q}] = \left(\prod_{p \mid m} p\right) \left(\prod_{p \mid m} (p - 1)\right) = \prod_{p \mid m} p(p - 1) = \prod_{p \mid m} d_p.
> $$
>
> ---
>
> ### 4. The Case Where $m$ is Even: $m = 2n$
> Since $m$ is square-free, $n$ must be an odd square-free integer, and $\gcd(2, n) = 1$.
> The prime factors of $m$ are 2 together with the prime factors of $n$.
>
> By definition:
> $$
> K_m = K_{2n} = K_2 \cdot K_n = \mathbf{Q}(\sqrt{a}) \cdot K_n = K_n(\sqrt{a}).
> $$
> Since $[K_n(\sqrt{a}) : K_n] \in \{1, 2\}$, we have:
> $$
> d_{2n} = [K_{2n} : \mathbf{Q}] = [K_n(\sqrt{a}) : K_n] [K_n : \mathbf{Q}] = [K_n(\sqrt{a}) : K_n] d_n.
> $$
> Thus $d_{2n} = d_n$ if $\sqrt{a} \in K_n$, and $d_{2n} = 2d_n$ if $\sqrt{a} \notin K_n$.
>
> We now show that $\sqrt{a} \in K_n \iff \sqrt{a} \in \mathbf{Q}(\zeta_m)$:
> - **The Cyclotomic Field:** Since $m = 2n$ with $n$ odd, $\mathbf{Q}(\zeta_m) = \mathbf{Q}(\zeta_{2n}) = \mathbf{Q}(\zeta_n)$.
> - **Galois Group of $K_n / \mathbf{Q}(\zeta_n)$:** By part 3, the extension $K_n / \mathbf{Q}(\zeta_n)$ is a Kummer extension with Galois group
>   $$
>   \operatorname{Gal}(K_n / \mathbf{Q}(\zeta_n)) \cong \prod_{p \mid n} \mathbf{Z}/p\mathbf{Z}.
>   $$
>   Since $n$ is odd, every prime divisor $p \mid n$ is an odd prime, so $[K_n : \mathbf{Q}(\zeta_n)] = n$ is an **odd integer**.
> - **Quadratic Subfields:** If $\sqrt{a} \in K_n$, then $\mathbf{Q}(\zeta_n, \sqrt{a})$ is an intermediate field:
>   $$
>   \mathbf{Q}(\zeta_n) \subseteq \mathbf{Q}(\zeta_n, \sqrt{a}) \subseteq K_n.
>   $$
>   The degree $[\mathbf{Q}(\zeta_n, \sqrt{a}) : \mathbf{Q}(\zeta_n)]$ divides 2.
>   On the other hand, it must also divide $[K_n : \mathbf{Q}(\zeta_n)] = n$, which is odd!
>   Since $\gcd(2, n) = 1$, the only divisor of both 2 and $n$ is 1:
>   $$
>   [\mathbf{Q}(\zeta_n, \sqrt{a}) : \mathbf{Q}(\zeta_n)] = 1 \iff \sqrt{a} \in \mathbf{Q}(\zeta_n) = \mathbf{Q}(\zeta_m).
>   $$
>
> Therefore, $\sqrt{a} \in K_n$ if and only if $\sqrt{a} \in \mathbf{Q}(\zeta_m)$.
> Consequently:
> $$
> d_{2n} = \begin{cases} d_n & \text{if } \sqrt{a} \in \mathbf{Q}(\zeta_m), \\ 2 d_n & \text{if } \sqrt{a} \notin \mathbf{Q}(\zeta_m). \end{cases}
> $$

## Notes

- By the Kronecker-Weber theorem and the theory of quadratic Gauss sums, $\sqrt{a} \in \mathbf{Q}(\zeta_m)$ occurs if and only if the discriminant of the quadratic field $\mathbf{Q}(\sqrt{a})$ divides $m$.
  Specifically, if $a$ is an odd square-free integer, $\sqrt{a^*} \in \mathbf{Q}(\zeta_{|a|})$ where $a^* = (-1)^{(a-1)/2} a$.
