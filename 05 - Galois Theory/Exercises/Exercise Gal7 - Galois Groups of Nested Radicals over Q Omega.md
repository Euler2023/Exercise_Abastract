# Exercise Gal7: Galois Groups of Nested Radicals over Q(omega)

## Problem Statement

> [!question] Exercise 9.14
> Let $F=\mathbb Q(\omega)$, where $\omega=e^{2\pi i/3}$. Determine the Galois group over $F$ of the splitting field of
> $$
> \text{(a) }\sqrt[3]{2+\sqrt2},
> \qquad
> \text{(b) }\sqrt{2+\sqrt[3]2}.
> $$

## Hints

> [!hint]- Hint 1
> In part (a), put $t=x^3$. The equation becomes
> $$
> t^2-4t+2=0,
> $$
> so its two roots are $2\pm\sqrt2$. Regard the required field as the compositum of the corresponding cubic radical extensions.

> [!hint]- Hint 2
> In part (a), set $c=uv=\sqrt[3]2$ and $q=u/v$. Compare the splitting fields of
> $$
> x^3-2
> \quad\text{and}\quad
> x^3-3x-6
> $$
> through their unique quadratic subfields.

> [!hint]- Hint 3
> In part (b), put $t=x^2$. Then $(t-2)^3=2$, so one must take square roots of
> $$
> 2+\sqrt[3]2,
> \qquad
> 2+\omega\sqrt[3]2,
> \qquad
> 2+\omega^2\sqrt[3]2.
> $$
> First study the field generated by their pairwise products.

> [!hint]- Hint 4
> The pairwise-product field in part (b) is the splitting field of
> $$
> h(x)=x^4-24x^2-80x-96.
> $$
> Use Artin's quartic resolvent method, and then adjoin the remaining element $\sqrt{10}$.

## Solution

> [!success]- Solution
> **(a) Two cubic splitting fields.**
>
> The element in the problem is a root of
> $$
> P(x)=x^6-4x^3+2.
> $$
> Substituting $t=x^3$ gives
> $$
> t^2-4t+2=0,
> $$
> whose roots are $2+\sqrt2$ and $2-\sqrt2$. Put
> $$
> s=\sqrt2,
> \qquad
> u^3=2+s,
> \qquad
> v^3=2-s,
> $$
> choosing the positive real cube roots. Since $F=\mathbb Q(\omega)$ contains all cube roots of unity, the required splitting field is
> $$
> K=F(s,u,v).
> $$
>
> Set
> $$
> c=uv=\sqrt[3]2,
> \qquad
> q=\frac uv.
> $$
> Let $A$ be the splitting field over $\mathbb Q$ of $x^3-2$. Thus
> $$
> A=\mathbb Q(c,\omega).
> $$
> The polynomial $x^3-2$ is Eisenstein at $2$, and
> $$
> \Delta(x^3-2)=-108=-3\cdot6^2.
> $$
> Therefore
> $$
> \operatorname{Gal}(A/\mathbb Q)\cong S_3,
> $$
> and its unique quadratic subfield is $\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-3})=F$.
>
> Furthermore,
> $$
> q^3=\frac{2+s}{2-s}=3+2s,
> \qquad
> q^{-3}=3-2s.
> $$
> Hence, if
> $$
> \alpha=q+q^{-1},
> $$
> then
> $$
> \alpha^3-3\alpha=q^3+q^{-3}=6.
> $$
> Thus $\alpha$ is a root of
> $$
> p(x)=x^3-3x-6.
> $$
> The rational-root test shows that $p$ is irreducible over $\mathbb Q$, and
> $$
> \Delta(p)=-864=-6\cdot12^2.
> $$
> Let $B$ be its splitting field. By Artin's cubic discriminant criterion,
> $$
> \operatorname{Gal}(B/\mathbb Q)\cong S_3,
> $$
> and the unique quadratic subfield of $B$ is $\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-6})$.
>
> The three roots of $p$ are
> $$
> \alpha_0=q+q^{-1},
> \quad
> \alpha_1=\omega q+\omega^2q^{-1},
> \quad
> \alpha_2=\omega^2q+\omega q^{-1}.
> $$
> Therefore $B\subset K$. Conversely, the inverse Fourier relation
> $$
> q=\frac{\alpha_0+\omega^2\alpha_1+\omega\alpha_2}{3}
> $$
> shows that $q\in AB$. Since $c\in A$, we have
> $$
> s=\frac{q^3-3}{2},
> \qquad
> u=\frac{2+s}{cq},
> \qquad
> v=\frac cu.
> $$
> Hence $K=AB$.
>
> The intersection $A\cap B$ is Galois over $\mathbb Q$. In an $S_3$-extension, the only nontrivial proper Galois intermediate field is its unique quadratic subfield. The quadratic subfields of $A$ and $B$ are respectively
> $$
> \mathbb Q(\sqrt{-3})
> \quad\text{and}\quad
> \mathbb Q(\sqrt{-6}),
> $$
> which are distinct. Also $A\ne B$, so
> $$
> A\cap B=\mathbb Q.
> $$
> Consequently,
> $$
> \operatorname{Gal}(K/\mathbb Q)
> \cong S_3\times S_3.
> $$
> Since $F$ is the quadratic subfield of the first $S_3$ factor, fixing $F$ replaces that factor by $A_3$. Therefore
> $$
> \operatorname{Gal}(K/F)
> \cong A_3\times S_3
> \cong C_3\times S_3.
> $$
>
> **(b) Three quadratic extensions and their pairwise-product field.**
>
> Here the element in the problem is a root of
> $$
> P(x)=x^6-6x^4+12x^2-10.
> $$
> Substituting $t=x^2$ gives
> $$
> t^3-6t^2+12t-10=0,
> \qquad\text{equivalently}\qquad
> (t-2)^3=2.
> $$
> Put
> $$
> a=\sqrt[3]2,
> \qquad
> E=F(a),
> \qquad
> U_j=2+\omega^j a
> \quad(j=0,1,2),
> $$
> and choose $r_j$ with $r_j^2=U_j$. The required splitting field is
> $$
> K=E(r_0,r_1,r_2)
>   =E(r_0)E(r_1)E(r_2).
> $$
>
> Define the pairwise products
> $$
> x_0=r_1r_2,
> \qquad
> x_1=r_0r_2,
> \qquad
> x_2=r_0r_1.
> $$
> The four elements
> $$
> \begin{aligned}
> \beta_0&=x_0+x_1+x_2,\\
> \beta_1&=x_0-x_1-x_2,\\
> \beta_2&=-x_0+x_1-x_2,\\
> \beta_3&=-x_0-x_1+x_2
> \end{aligned}
> $$
> are the roots of
> $$
> h(x)=x^4-24x^2-80x-96.
> $$
> Indeed,
> $$
> x_0^2+x_1^2+x_2^2=12,
> $$
> $$
> x_0^2x_1^2+x_0^2x_2^2+x_1^2x_2^2
> =10(U_0+U_1+U_2)=60,
> $$
> and
> $$
> x_0x_1x_2=U_0U_1U_2=10;
> $$
> expanding the product of $x-\beta_i$ gives the displayed quartic.
>
> Let $H$ be the splitting field of $h$ over $\mathbb Q$. We first prove that $h$ is irreducible. After the invertible change of variable $x=2y$ and division by $16$, we obtain
> $$
> \frac1{16}h(2y)=y^4-6y^2-10y-6.
> $$
> This polynomial is Eisenstein at $2$: every nonleading coefficient is divisible by $2$, while its constant term $-6$ is not divisible by $4$. It is therefore irreducible over $\mathbb Q$. Since
> $$
> h(x)=16\left(\frac{x^4}{16}-6\frac{x^2}{4}-10\frac{x}{2}-6\right)
>      =16\widetilde h\left(\frac x2\right),
> $$
> where $\widetilde h(y)=y^4-6y^2-10y-6$. A nontrivial factorization of $h$ would give one of $\widetilde h$ after substituting $x=2y$ and dividing by $16$. Hence $h$ is irreducible over $\mathbb Q$.
>
> Since all four roots $\beta_i$ lie in $K$, we have $H\subset K$. Also,
> $$
> x_0=\frac{\beta_0+\beta_1}{2},
> \qquad
> x_1=\frac{\beta_0+\beta_2}{2},
> \qquad
> x_2=\frac{\beta_0+\beta_3}{2},
> $$
> so $x_0,x_1,x_2\in H$. We now give two ways to identify the Galois group of $H/\mathbb Q$.
>
> **First method: the resolvent cubic.** With Artin's convention, the resolvent cubic of $h$ is
> $$
> g(y)=y^3+24y^2+384y+2816.
> $$
> Translating the variable by $y=z-8$ gives
> $$
> g(z-8)=z^3+192z+768.
> $$
> This polynomial is Eisenstein at $3$: both nonleading coefficients are divisible by $3$, while
> $$
> 9\nmid768.
> $$
> Hence $g(z-8)$, and therefore $g(y)$, is irreducible over $\mathbb Q$. Finally,
> $$
> \Delta(h)=-44\,236\,800=-3\cdot3840^2.
> $$
> By Artin's resolvent criterion, the irreducibility of $g$ implies that $3$ divides the order of $\operatorname{Gal}(H/\mathbb Q)$. Since $h$ is irreducible and its discriminant is not a square, Artin's quartic classification now gives
> $$
> \operatorname{Gal}(H/\mathbb Q)\cong S_4.
> $$
>
> **Second method: the degree-$6$ subfield.** This route uses the compositum structure directly and avoids the resolvent cubic. From the pairwise products,
> $$
> \frac{x_1x_2}{x_0}=r_0^2=U_0=2+a,
> $$
> and hence $a=\sqrt[3]2\in H$. Moreover, the square root of the discriminant always belongs to the splitting field, so
> $$
> \sqrt{\Delta(h)}=3840\sqrt{-3}\in H.
> $$
> Thus
> $$
> E=\mathbb Q(\omega,a)=F(a)\subset H.
> $$
> As established in part (a), $E$ is the splitting field of $x^3-2$ and $[E:\mathbb Q]=6$. Therefore
> $$
> 6\mid[H:\mathbb Q]
>   =\left|\operatorname{Gal}(H/\mathbb Q)\right|.
> $$
> The transitive subgroups of $S_4$ are
> $$
> S_4,\quad A_4,\quad D_4,\quad C_4,\quad V_4.
> $$
> Only $S_4$ and $A_4$ have order divisible by $3$. Since $\Delta(h)$ is not a square in $\mathbb Q$, the Galois group is not contained in $A_4$. Hence this method again gives
> $$
> \operatorname{Gal}(H/\mathbb Q)\cong S_4.
> $$
>
> The square root of the discriminant generates its quadratic subfield. Since $A_4$ is the unique index-$2$ subgroup of $S_4$, this quadratic subfield is unique and equals
> $$
> \mathbb Q(\sqrt{-3})=F.
> $$
> Hence
> $$
> \operatorname{Gal}(H/F)\cong A_4.
> $$
>
> It remains to recover the original square roots. We have
> $$
> (r_0r_1r_2)^2=U_0U_1U_2=10.
> $$
> Choose the sign so that $r_0r_1r_2=\sqrt{10}$. Then
> $$
> r_0=\frac{\sqrt{10}}{x_0},
> \qquad
> r_1=\frac{\sqrt{10}}{x_1},
> \qquad
> r_2=\frac{\sqrt{10}}{x_2}.
> $$
> The second method above already shows that $E=F(a)\subset H$. Therefore
> $$
> K=H(\sqrt{10}).
> $$
>
> The $S_4$-extension $H/\mathbb Q$ has only one quadratic subfield, namely $F$. Since $\mathbb Q(\sqrt{10})\ne F$,
> $$
> H\cap F(\sqrt{10})=F.
> $$
> Both extensions are Galois over $F$, so restriction gives
> $$
> \operatorname{Gal}(K/F)
> \cong
> \operatorname{Gal}(H/F)
> \times
> \operatorname{Gal}(F(\sqrt{10})/F)
> \cong A_4\times C_2.
> $$

## Notes

The exercise statement is source material from [S1, Ch. 16, Section 9, Ex. 9.14, printed p. 509, PDF p. 521]. The solution is an independent derivation modeled on the compositum method used in [[05 - Galois Theory/Exercises/Exercise Gal74 - Two S3 Splitting Fields and Their Compositum|Exercise Gal74]]. Part (a) uses Artin's cubic discriminant criterion [S1, Ch. 16, Section 8, Thm. 16.8.5, printed p. 493, PDF p. 505]. Part (b) proves the quartic irreducible by a scaled Eisenstein argument and retains two proofs that its Galois group is $S_4$: Artin's resolvent-cubic criterion, and a compositum argument using the degree-$6$ subfield $F(\sqrt[3]2)$. The quartic resolvent and discriminant criteria are from [S1, Ch. 16, Section 9, Props. 16.9.5 and 16.9.8, printed pp. 495-496, PDF pp. 507-508]. No Pell-unit calculation, prime-ideal factorization, or general Kummer class-independence theorem is imported.
