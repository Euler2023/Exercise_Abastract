# Exercise Gal144: Frobenius Automorphisms and the Correct Residue Cardinality

## Problem Statement

> [!question] Neukirch I.9.2
> If $L/K$ is a Galois extension of algebraic number fields, and $\mathfrak P$ a prime ideal which is unramified over $K$ (i.e., $\mathfrak p=\mathfrak P\cap K$ is unramified in $L$), then there is one and only one automorphism $\varphi_{\mathfrak P}\in G(L/K)$ such that
> $$
> \varphi_{\mathfrak P}a\equiv a^q\pmod{\mathfrak P}
> \quad\text{for all }a\in\mathcal O,
> $$
> where $q=[\kappa(\mathfrak P):\kappa(\mathfrak p)]$. It is called the Frobenius automorphism. The decomposition group $G_{\mathfrak P}$ is cyclic and $\varphi_{\mathfrak P}$ is a generator of $G_{\mathfrak P}$.

Here $\mathcal O=\mathcal O_L$ and $G_{\mathfrak P}$ is the decomposition group. Prime ideals of number fields are understood to be nonzero.

> [!warning] Source issue
> The printed exponent is incorrect. It must be $q=\#\kappa(\mathfrak p)$, the cardinality of the base residue field, not the degree $[\kappa(\mathfrak P):\kappa(\mathfrak p)]$. For $L=\mathbb Q(i)$, $K=\mathbb Q$, and $\mathfrak P=(3)$, the printed degree is $2$; the claimed congruence at $a=2$ would require $2\equiv4\pmod{(3)}$, which is false. We retain the printed claim and prove the corrected theorem below.

## Hints

> [!hint]- Hint 1
> Check the exponent on the unramified inert prime $3$ in $\mathbb Z[i]$. Its residue extension is $\mathbb F_9/\mathbb F_3$.

> [!hint]- Hint 2
> For the corrected statement, the finite-field automorphism is $x\mapsto x^{\#\kappa(\mathfrak p)}$. Lift it through the decomposition-inertia exact sequence.

> [!hint]- Hint 3
> Unramifiedness makes the inertia kernel trivial. For uniqueness among all field automorphisms, first show that any automorphism satisfying the congruence must stabilize $\mathfrak P$.

## Solution

> [!success]- Independent derivation
> **1. Refute the printed exponent.** In $L=\mathbb Q(i)$ one has $\mathcal O=\mathbb Z[i]$. The polynomial $X^2+1$ is irreducible modulo $3$, so $\mathcal O/(3)\cong\mathbb F_9$. The ideal $(3)$ is prime and occurs to exponent one in $3\mathcal O$, hence is unramified, with residue degree $2$. Every $\mathbb Q$-automorphism fixes the integer $2$. The printed congruence with $q=2$ would force $2\equiv2^2=1\bmod(3)$, impossible. Thus the literal printed claim is false.
>
> **2. Prove the finite-field statement with the correct exponent.** Put
> $$
> q=\#\kappa(\mathfrak p),\qquad
> f=[\kappa(\mathfrak P):\kappa(\mathfrak p)].
> $$
> These fields have cardinalities $q$ and $q^f$. The map $F(x)=x^q$ preserves addition and multiplication in positive characteristic, fixes the base field, and is injective, hence bijective on the finite field. Its order is exactly $f$: $F^f$ is the identity, and for $0<j<f$ the polynomial $X^{q^j}-X$ cannot have all $q^f$ elements as roots. Since the number of automorphisms of a degree-$f$ extension is at most $f$, the entire residue Galois group is generated by $F$.
>
> **3. Lift uniquely and check the quantifier over the whole Galois group.** The reduction map gives the exact sequence
> $$
> 1\longrightarrow I_{\mathfrak P}\longrightarrow G_{\mathfrak P}
> \longrightarrow\operatorname{Gal}(\kappa(\mathfrak P)/\kappa(\mathfrak p))
> \longrightarrow1.
> $$
> Finite fields are perfect, and $\mathfrak p$ is unramified, so $I_{\mathfrak P}=1$. The reduction map is therefore an isomorphism. Let $\varphi_{\mathfrak P}$ be the unique lift of $F$. By its definition it satisfies
> $$
> \varphi_{\mathfrak P}a\equiv a^q\pmod{\mathfrak P}
> \qquad(a\in\mathcal O).
> $$
> It generates $G_{\mathfrak P}$, since $F$ generates the residue group.
>
> If some $\sigma\in G(L/K)$ satisfies this congruence, then $a\in\mathfrak P$ implies $\sigma(a)\in\mathfrak P$. Hence $\sigma\mathfrak P\subseteq\mathfrak P$. Both are maximal ideals, so they are equal. Thus $\sigma\in G_{\mathfrak P}$ and its reduction is $F$; injectivity of the reduction map forces $\sigma=\varphi_{\mathfrak P}$. This proves uniqueness among all elements of $G(L/K)$.
>
> In the counterexample the corrected exponent is $q=3$, and the Frobenius is complex conjugation, since $i^3=-i$ modulo $(3)$.
