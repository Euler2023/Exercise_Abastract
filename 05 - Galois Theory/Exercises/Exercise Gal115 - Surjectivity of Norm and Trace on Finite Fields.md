# Exercise Gal115: Surjectivity of Norm and Trace on Finite Fields

## Problem Statement

> [!question]
> Let $F$ be a finite field and $K$ a finite extension of $F$. Show that the norm $N_F^K$ and the trace $\operatorname{Tr}_F^K$ are surjective (as maps from $K$ into $F$).

## Hints

> [!hint]- Weak Hint
> Recall that every finite extension of a finite field is a cyclic Galois extension generated by the Frobenius automorphism. Express the trace and norm explicitly in terms of powers of the Frobenius automorphism.

> [!hint]- Moderate Hint
> - For the trace, consider it as an $F$-linear map from the $F$-vector space $K$ to $F$. What are the possible dimensions of the image? Could the trace map be identically zero on $K$?
> - For the norm, consider the restriction to the multiplicative group $K^\times \to F^\times$ as a group homomorphism between finite cyclic groups.

> [!hint]- Strong Hint
> - The trace polynomial $\sum_{j=0}^{n-1} X^{q^j}$ has degree $q^{n-1}$, whereas $|K| = q^n > q^{n-1}$. Since a non-zero polynomial cannot have more roots than its degree, the trace cannot vanish identically on $K$.
> - For the norm, $N(x) = x^{(q^n-1)/(q-1)}$. The kernel of $N|_{K^\times}$ is the subgroup of roots of $X^{(q^n-1)/(q-1)} - 1$, which has cardinality at most (and in fact exactly) $(q^n-1)/(q-1)$. Apply the first isomorphism theorem for finite groups.

## Solution

> [!success]- Complete Derivation
> Let $F = \mathbb{F}_q$ where $q = p^m$ is a prime power, and let $[K : F] = n$, so that $K = \mathbb{F}_{q^n}$.
> The field extension $K/F$ is Galois with cyclic Galois group $G = \operatorname{Gal}(K/F) = \langle \sigma \rangle \cong \mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}$, generated by the Frobenius automorphism $\sigma(x) = x^q$.
>
> ---
>
> ### 1. Surjectivity of the Trace $\operatorname{Tr}_F^K$
> The trace of an element $x \in K$ is given by
> $$
> \operatorname{Tr}_F^K(x) = \sum_{j=0}^{n-1} \sigma^j(x) = \sum_{j=0}^{n-1} x^{q^j} = x + x^q + x^{q^2} + \dots + x^{q^{n-1}}.
> $$
> The trace map is $F$-linear:
> $$
> \operatorname{Tr}_F^K(a x + b y) = a \operatorname{Tr}_F^K(x) + b \operatorname{Tr}_F^K(y) \quad \text{for all } a, b \in F, \, x, y \in K.
> $$
> Therefore, the image $\operatorname{im}(\operatorname{Tr}_F^K)$ is an $F$-subspace of the 1-dimensional $F$-vector space $F$. Consequently, there are only two possibilities:
> $$
> \operatorname{im}(\operatorname{Tr}_F^K) = \{0\} \quad \text{or} \quad \operatorname{im}(\operatorname{Tr}_F^K) = F.
> $$
> Suppose for contradiction that $\operatorname{im}(\operatorname{Tr}_F^K) = \{0\}$. Then every element $x \in K$ satisfies
> $$
> P(x) = x^{q^{n-1}} + x^{q^{n-2}} + \dots + x^q + x = 0.
> $$
> But $P(X) \in F[X]$ is a non-zero polynomial of degree $q^{n-1}$. Since a field can contain at most $\deg P$ roots of $P$, the number of roots of $P$ in $K$ satisfies
> $$
> \#\{x \in K \mid P(x) = 0\} \le q^{n-1}.
> $$
> However, $|K| = q^n > q^{n-1}$ because $n \ge 1$ and $q \ge 2$. This is a contradiction.
>
> (Alternatively, by Dedekind's theorem on the linear independence of distinct characters/automorphisms, the automorphisms $\operatorname{id}, \sigma, \dots, \sigma^{n-1}$ are linearly independent over $K$, so their sum $\operatorname{Tr}_F^K$ cannot be the zero map.)
>
> Thus $\operatorname{Tr}_F^K$ is not the zero map, which forces
> $$
> \operatorname{im}(\operatorname{Tr}_F^K) = F.
> $$
> That is, the trace map is surjective.
>
> ---
>
> ### 2. Surjectivity of the Norm $N_F^K$
> The norm of an element $x \in K$ is given by
> $$
> N_F^K(x) = \prod_{j=0}^{n-1} \sigma^j(x) = \prod_{j=0}^{n-1} x^{q^j} = x^{1 + q + q^2 + \dots + q^{n-1}} = x^{\frac{q^n - 1}{q - 1}}.
> $$
> Clearly $N_F^K(0) = 0 \in F$. For nonzero elements, the norm restricts to a group homomorphism
> $$
> N: K^\times \to F^\times.
> $$
> The multiplicative group $K^\times$ is cyclic of order $q^n - 1$, and $F^\times$ is cyclic of order $q - 1$.
>
> The kernel of $N|_{K^\times}$ consists of all elements $x \in K^\times$ such that
> $$
> x^{\frac{q^n - 1}{q - 1}} = 1.
> $$
> In any field, the polynomial $X^{\frac{q^n - 1}{q - 1}} - 1$ has at most $\frac{q^n - 1}{q - 1}$ roots. On the other hand, since $K^\times$ is a cyclic group of order $q^n - 1$ and $\frac{q^n - 1}{q - 1}$ divides $q^n - 1$, the cyclic group $K^\times$ has a unique subgroup of order $\frac{q^n - 1}{q - 1}$, all of whose elements satisfy this equation.
>
> Therefore, the kernel has size exactly
> $$
> |\ker(N|_{K^\times})| = \frac{q^n - 1}{q - 1}.
> $$
> By the first isomorphism theorem for groups, the image of $N|_{K^\times}$ has cardinality
> $$
> |\operatorname{im}(N|_{K^\times})| = \frac{|K^\times|}{|\ker(N|_{K^\times})|} = \frac{q^n - 1}{\frac{q^n - 1}{q - 1}} = q - 1.
> $$
> Since $\operatorname{im}(N|_{K^\times}) \subseteq F^\times$ and $|F^\times| = q - 1$, the inclusion must be an equality:
> $$
> \operatorname{im}(N|_{K^\times}) = F^\times.
> $$
> Combining this with $N(0) = 0$, we conclude that $N_F^K: K \to F$ is surjective.
>
> Both the norm and the trace are therefore surjective maps from $K$ into $F$.

## Notes

- The surjectivity of the norm and trace for finite fields combined with Hilbert's Theorem 90 provides an exact description of the kernels:
  - $\ker(\operatorname{Tr}_F^K) = \{y^q - y \mid y \in K\} = (\sigma - 1)K$;
  - $\ker(N_F^K|_{K^\times}) = \{y^{q-1} \mid y \in K^\times\} = \{y / \sigma(y) \mid y \in K^\times\}$.
- In characteristic $p$, if $E/k$ is an inseparable extension, the trace is identically zero. For separable extensions, the trace is always a non-zero linear functional (by Dedekind independence), but surjectivity onto $k$ requires $k$ to be 1-dimensional over itself (which holds here) and the image to be non-zero.
