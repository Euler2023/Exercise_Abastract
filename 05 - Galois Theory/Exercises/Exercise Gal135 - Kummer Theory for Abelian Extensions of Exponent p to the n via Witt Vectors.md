# Exercise Gal135: Kummer Theory for Abelian Extensions of Exponent p to the n via Witt Vectors

## Problem Statement

> [!question]
> Develop the Kummer theory for abelian extensions of $k$ of exponent $p^n$ by using $W_n(k)$. In other words, show that there is a bijection between subgroups $B$ of $W_n(k)$ containing $\wp W_n(k)$ and abelian extensions as above, given by
> $$
> B \mapsto K_B
> $$
> where $K_B = k(\wp^{-1} B)$. All of this is due to Witt, cf. the references at the end of §8, especially [Wi 37]. The proofs are the same, *mutatis mutandis*, as those given for the Kummer theory in the text.

## Hints

> [!hint]- Weak Hint
> Recall the classical Kummer pairing $\langle \sigma, a \rangle = \sigma(\alpha)/\alpha \in \boldsymbol{\mu}_m$ where $\alpha^m = a$. For Witt vectors, replace multiplication with Witt addition and $m$-th roots with $\wp^{-1}$.

> [!hint]- Moderate Hint
> Define the pairing:
> $$
> \langle \cdot, \cdot \rangle: \operatorname{Gal}(K_B/k) \times B \longrightarrow W_n(\mathbf{F}_p) \cong \mathbf{Z}/p^n\mathbf{Z}, \quad \langle \sigma, x \rangle = \sigma(\xi) - \xi
> $$
> where $\wp(\xi) = x$. Show this pairing is bilinear, well-defined modulo $\wp W_n(k)$, and non-degenerate on the left by definition of $K_B$.

> [!hint]- Strong Hint
> To prove non-degeneracy on the right, suppose $\langle \sigma, x \rangle = 0$ for all $\sigma \in \operatorname{Gal}(K_B/k)$. Then $\xi$ is fixed by all of $\operatorname{Gal}(K_B/k)$, which implies $\xi \in W_n(k)$, so $x = \wp(\xi) \in \wp W_n(k)$. To prove surjectivity onto all abelian extensions of exponent $p^n$, use the vanishing of cohomology $H^1(G, W_n(K)) = 0$ from Exercise 49.

## Solution

> [!success]- Complete Derivation
> Let $k$ be a field of characteristic $p > 0$, and let $W_n(k)$ be the additive group of truncated Witt vectors of length $n$ with entries in $k$.
> The Artin-Schreier-Witt operator $\wp: W_n(\bar{k}) \to W_n(\bar{k})$ is $\wp(\xi) = F\xi - \xi$, which is an additive homomorphism with kernel $W_n(\mathbf{F}_p) \cong \mathbf{Z}/p^n\mathbf{Z}$.
>
> ---
>
> ### 1. Construction of the Extension $K_B$ from a Subgroup $B$
> Let $B$ be a subgroup of $W_n(k)$ containing $\wp W_n(k)$.
> Define:
> $$
> \wp^{-1}(B) = \{\xi \in W_n(\bar{k}) \mid \wp(\xi) \in B\},
> $$
> and let $K_B$ be the subfield of $\bar{k}$ generated over $k$ by all coordinates of all vectors in $\wp^{-1}(B)$:
> $$
> K_B = k\left(\wp^{-1} B\right) = k\left(\{\xi_i \mid \xi \in \wp^{-1}(B), \, 0 \le i < n\}\right).
> $$
>
> - **Galois and Abelian Extension:**
>   For each $\xi \in \wp^{-1}(B)$ and $\sigma \in \operatorname{Aut}_k(\bar{k})$:
>   $$
>   \wp(\sigma \xi) = \sigma(\wp \xi) = \sigma(b) = b \in B \quad (\text{since } b \in W_n(k)).
>   $$
>   Thus $\sigma\xi \in \wp^{-1}(B)$.
>   By Exercise 50, $\sigma\xi - \xi \in W_n(\mathbf{F}_p) \subset W_n(k) \subseteq W_n(K_B)$.
>   Hence $\sigma(K_B) \subseteq K_B$, so $K_B/k$ is normal and separable, hence **Galois**.
>
> - **The Artin-Schreier-Witt Pairing:**
>   Define the pairing:
>   $$
>   \langle \cdot, \cdot \rangle: \operatorname{Gal}(K_B/k) \times B \longrightarrow W_n(\mathbf{F}_p), \quad \langle \sigma, x \rangle = \sigma(\xi) - \xi,
>   $$
>   where $\xi \in W_n(\bar{k})$ is any solution to $\wp(\xi) = x \in B$.
>   1. **Well-Defined:** If $\xi'$ is another solution, $\xi' - \xi \in W_n(\mathbf{F}_p) \subseteq W_n(k)$, so $\sigma(\xi' - \xi) = \xi' - \xi$.
>      Thus $\sigma\xi' - \xi' = \sigma\xi - \xi$.
>   2. **Additive in $\sigma$:**
>      $$
>      \langle \sigma \tau, x \rangle = \sigma\tau(\xi) - \xi = \sigma(\tau\xi - \xi) + (\sigma\xi - \xi) = (\tau\xi - \xi) + (\sigma\xi - \xi) = \langle \sigma, x \rangle + \langle \tau, x \rangle,
>      $$
>      since $\tau\xi - \xi \in W_n(\mathbf{F}_p)$ is fixed by $\sigma$.
>   3. **Additive in $x$:**
>      If $\wp(\xi) = x$ and $\wp(\eta) = y$, then $\wp(\xi + \eta) = x + y$, so:
>      $$
>      \langle \sigma, x + y \rangle = \sigma(\xi + \eta) - (\xi + \eta) = (\sigma\xi - \xi) + (\sigma\eta - \eta) = \langle \sigma, x \rangle + \langle \sigma, y \rangle.
>      $$
>   4. **Left Kernel:**
>      If $\langle \sigma, x \rangle = 0$ for all $x \in B$, then $\sigma(\xi) = \xi$ for all $\xi \in \wp^{-1}(B)$.
>      Since $K_B$ is generated by the components of all such $\xi$, $\sigma = \operatorname{id}_{K_B}$.
>      Thus the left kernel of the pairing is $\{1\}$.
>      This gives an injection:
>      $$
>      \operatorname{Gal}(K_B/k) \hookrightarrow \operatorname{Hom}\left(B / \wp W_n(k), W_n(\mathbf{F}_p)\right).
>      $$
>      Since $W_n(\mathbf{F}_p) \cong \mathbf{Z}/p^n\mathbf{Z}$, $\operatorname{Gal}(K_B/k)$ is an **abelian group of exponent dividing $p^n$**.
>
>   5. **Right Kernel:**
>      Suppose $x \in B$ satisfies $\langle \sigma, x \rangle = 0$ for all $\sigma \in \operatorname{Gal}(K_B/k)$.
>      Then $\sigma\xi = \xi$ for all $\sigma \in \operatorname{Gal}(K_B/k)$, where $\wp(\xi) = x$.
>      By the fundamental theorem of Galois theory, the fixed field of $\operatorname{Gal}(K_B/k)$ is $k$.
>      Therefore, all components of $\xi$ lie in $k$, meaning:
>      $$
>      \xi \in W_n(k).
>      $$
>      Consequently, $x = \wp(\xi) \in \wp W_n(k)$.
>      Thus the right kernel of the pairing is precisely $\wp W_n(k)$.
>
> Passing to the quotient, the pairing induces a non-degenerate bilinear pairing:
> $$
> \operatorname{Gal}(K_B/k) \times \left( B / \wp W_n(k) \right) \longrightarrow W_n(\mathbf{F}_p) \cong \mathbf{Z}/p^n\mathbf{Z}.
> $$
> When $[K_B : k] < \infty$, Pontryagin duality for finite abelian groups yields:
> $$
> \operatorname{Gal}(K_B/k) \cong \operatorname{Hom}\left(B / \wp W_n(k), \mathbf{Z}/p^n\mathbf{Z}\right),
> $$
> and in particular:
> $$
> [K_B : k] = (B : \wp W_n(k)).
> $$
>
> ---
>
> ### 2. Recovering the Subgroup from the Extension
> Given any abelian extension $K/k$ of exponent dividing $p^n$, define:
> $$
> B_K = \wp(W_n(K)) \cap W_n(k).
> $$
> - Clearly $\wp W_n(k) \subseteq B_K \subseteq W_n(k)$, so $B_K$ is a subgroup of $W_n(k)$ containing $\wp W_n(k)$.
> - For $K = K_B$, an element $x \in W_n(k)$ belongs to $B_{K_B}$ if and only if there exists $\xi \in W_n(K_B)$ with $\wp(\xi) = x$.
>   Then $\xi \in \wp^{-1}(B_K)$.
>   By the non-degeneracy of the pairing proven above, $\sigma\xi - \xi = 0$ for all $\sigma \in \operatorname{Gal}(K_B/k) \iff x \in B$.
>   Therefore:
>   $$
>   B_{K_B} = B.
>   $$
>
> ---
>
> ### 3. Surjectivity: Every Abelian Extension of Exponent $p^n$ is of Form $K_B$
> Let $K/k$ be an arbitrary finite abelian extension of exponent dividing $p^n$, with Galois group $G = \operatorname{Gal}(K/k)$.
> Consider the exact sequence of $G$-modules:
> $$
> 0 \longrightarrow W_n(\mathbf{F}_p) \longrightarrow W_n(K) \xrightarrow{\wp} W_n(K) \longrightarrow 0.
> $$
> Taking Galois cohomology yields the long exact sequence:
> $$
> 0 \to W_n(\mathbf{F}_p) \to W_n(k) \xrightarrow{\wp} W_n(k) \cap \wp W_n(K) \to H^1(G, W_n(\mathbf{F}_p)) \to H^1(G, W_n(K)).
> $$
> By Exercise 49 (Hilbert's Theorem 90 for Witt vectors):
> $$
> H^1(G, W_n(K)) = 0.
> $$
> Furthermore, since $G$ acts trivially on $W_n(\mathbf{F}_p)$, 1-cocycles are simply homomorphisms:
> $$
> H^1(G, W_n(\mathbf{F}_p)) = \operatorname{Hom}(G, W_n(\mathbf{F}_p)).
> $$
> Therefore, the connecting homomorphism induces an isomorphism:
> $$
> B_K / \wp W_n(k) = (W_n(k) \cap \wp W_n(K)) / \wp W_n(k) \xrightarrow{\sim} \operatorname{Hom}(G, W_n(\mathbf{F}_p)).
> $$
> In particular:
> $$
> (B_K : \wp W_n(k)) = |\operatorname{Hom}(G, \mathbf{Z}/p^n\mathbf{Z})| = |G| = [K : k].
> $$
> Since $K_{B_K} \subseteq K$ and $[K_{B_K} : k] = (B_K : \wp W_n(k)) = [K : k]$, we conclude:
> $$
> K_{B_K} = K.
> $$
>
> ---
>
> ### 4. Conclusion: The Bijection
> The mappings
> $$
> B \longmapsto K_B = k(\wp^{-1} B) \quad \text{and} \quad K \longmapsto B_K = \wp(W_n(K)) \cap W_n(k)
> $$
> establish an inclusion-preserving **bijection** between:
> - subgroups $B \le W_n(k)$ containing $\wp W_n(k)$;
> - abelian Galois extensions $K/k$ of exponent dividing $p^n$ (inside a fixed algebraic closure).
>
> Under this bijection:
> 1. $B_1 \subseteq B_2 \iff K_{B_1} \subseteq K_{B_2}$;
> 2. $K_{B_1 \cap B_2} = K_{B_1} \cap K_{B_2}$ and $K_{B_1 + B_2} = K_{B_1} K_{B_2}$;
> 3. $[K_B : k] = (B : \wp W_n(k))$, with $K_B/k$ finite if and only if $(B : \wp W_n(k))$ is finite.

## Notes

- This theorem, formulated and proved by Ernst Witt in 1936–1937, completely solves the problem of abelian extensions of exponent $p^n$ in characteristic $p$, serving as the exact characteristic-$p$ analogue of Kummer theory.
- The duality $\operatorname{Gal}(K_B/k) \cong \operatorname{Hom}(B/\wp W_n(k), \mathbf{Z}/p^n\mathbf{Z})$ is foundational in modern $p$-adic Hodge theory, crystalline cohomology, and the study of finite flat group schemes.
