# Exercise Gal154: Algebraic Unramified and Tame Extensions of Fp Laurent Series

## Problem Statement

> [!question] Neukirch II.9.3
> Show that the maximal unramified extension of $K=\mathbb F_p((t))$ is $T=\overline{\mathbb F}_p((t))$, where $\overline{\mathbb F}_p$ is the algebraic closure, and that the maximal tamely ramified extension is $T(\sqrt[m]{t}:m\in\mathbb N,(m,p)=1)$.

> [!warning] Source issue
> The printed field $\overline{\mathbb F}_p((t))$ allows unbounded coefficient fields and is too large. The algebraic maximal unramified extension is the union of the finite constant extensions. The second formula is correct after this replacement.

## Hints

> [!hint]- Hint 1
> A finite unramified extension has a finite residue field, hence is $\mathbb F_{p^r}((t))$. Take their union, carefully distinguishing it from all Laurent series over the infinite constant field.

> [!hint]- Hint 2
> Over that union, every unit has all prime-to-p roots by Hensel and algebraic closedness of the residue field. Only roots of t are needed.

## Solution

> [!success]- Solution
> **Proof scope:** Independent derivation and stated source corrections
>
> The algebraic maximal unramified extension is
> $$
> T_{\rm alg}=\bigcup_{r\ge1}\mathbb F_{p^r}((t)),
> $$
> where the union is directed under divisibility of $r$. Each field in it is finite unramified with the indicated residue field, and the unramified/residue-field correspondence of II.7.2 shows that all finite unramified extensions occur this way. Equivalently its elements are exactly the Laurent series whose coefficients lie in one common finite extension of $\mathbb F_p$.
>
> This union is strictly smaller than the printed $\overline{\mathbb F}_p((t))$. Choose coefficients $a_j$ of distinct unbounded prime degrees over $\mathbb F_p$. The series $\sum_{j\ge1}a_jt^j$ cannot belong to a single finite constant extension, so is not in the union. It is, however, a limit of its truncations, each of which belongs to the union. Indeed every series over the algebraic closure is so approximated. Thus $\overline{\mathbb F}_p((t))$ is the completion of $T_{\rm alg}$, not the algebraic maximal unramified field itself.
>
> Use $T_{\rm alg}$ in the second assertion. It is henselian with value group $\mathbb Z$ and algebraically closed residue field. If $(m,p)=1$, every unit $u$ has an $m$-th root: choose an $m$-th root of its residue, lift it, and apply Hensel to the resulting principal unit. The radical description (7.7) now shows that every finite tame extension is generated by roots of elements $t^ru$; the unit roots already lie in the base, so only $m$-th roots of $t$ are needed. Conversely each such extension is tame, since the powers $1,t^{1/m},\ldots,t^{(m-1)/m}$ have distinct value classes, giving degree and ramification index $m$, and the composita remain tame by (7.9). Therefore
> $$
> K^t=T_{\rm alg}(t^{1/m}:(m,p)=1).
> $$
> The roots of unity required to make these radical extensions Galois are already present as constants. All unions here are algebraic unions; taking a completion would change the requested extension.
