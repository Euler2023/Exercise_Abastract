# Exercise Gal188: The Abelianized Weil Group

## Problem Statement

> [!question] Neukirch V.1.11
> The Weil group of a local field $K$ is the preimage $W_K$ of $\mathbb Z$ under $d_K:G_K\to\widehat{\mathbb Z}$. Show:
>
> The norm residue symbol of the maximal abelian extension $K^{\rm ab}/K$ yields an isomorphism
> $$
> (\ ,K^{\rm ab}/K):K^*\xrightarrow{\sim}W_K^{\rm ab},
> $$
> which maps the unit group $U_K$ onto the inertia group $I(K^{\rm ab}/K)$, and the group of principal units $U_K^{(1)}$ onto the ramification group $R(K^{\rm ab}/K)$.

## Hints

> [!hint]- Hint 1
> Choose a lift $\Phi$ of arithmetic Frobenius and write $W_K=I_K\rtimes\langle\Phi\rangle$.

> [!hint]- Hint 2
> In abelianization the inertia factor is the quotient by the closed subgroup generated by commutators in $I_K$ and by $\Phi i\Phi^{-1}i^{-1}$.

## Solution

> [!success]- Independent derivation
> We use the usual Weil topology: $I_K$ is open with its profinite topology and $W_K/I_K=\mathbb Z$ is discrete. Abelianization means quotient by the **closed** commutator subgroup. Choose a Frobenius lift $\Phi\in G_K$. Its integer powers split the Weil quotient, so
> $$
> W_K=I_K\rtimes\langle\Phi\rangle,\qquad W_K^{\rm ab}=C\times\mathbb Z,
> $$
> where $C=I_K/\overline{[I_K,I_K]\langle\Phi i\Phi^{-1}i^{-1}:i\in I_K\rangle}$.
>
> The continuous homomorphism $\widehat{\mathbb Z}\to G_K$ sending $1$ to $\Phi$ is injective, since composition with $d_K$ is the identity. Thus $G_K=I_K\rtimes\Phi^{\widehat{\mathbb Z}}$. The same quotient $C$ occurs in $G_K^{\rm ab}$: integer powers are dense in $\Phi^{\widehat{\mathbb Z}}$, and compactness/continuity show that commutators with arbitrary profinite powers are limits of commutators with integer powers. Hence
> $$
> G_K^{\rm ab}=C\times\widehat{\mathbb Z},\qquad W_K^{\rm ab}\hookrightarrow G_K^{\rm ab}
> $$
> identifies its image with $d_K^{-1}(\mathbb Z)$.
>
> Local reciprocity is an isomorphism from the profinite completion of $K^*=\pi^{\mathbb Z}\times U_K$ onto $G_K^{\rm ab}$, compatible with valuation and degree. Because $U_K$ is already profinite, this completion is $\pi^{\widehat{\mathbb Z}}\times U_K$. The subgroup $\pi^{\mathbb Z}\times U_K$ therefore corresponds exactly to $d_K^{-1}(\mathbb Z)=W_K^{\rm ab}$, with the stated topologies. This gives the required isomorphism.
>
> The degree-zero subgroup is $U_K$, so its image is inertia. For principal units use the independently proved ramification reciprocity theorem V (6.2): for each finite abelian extension it maps $U_K^{(n)}$ onto the upper ramification group $G^n$, $n\ge0$. At $n=1$ this is the wild ramification group; inverse limits give $U_K^{(1)}\cong R(K^{\rm ab}/K)$. Surjectivity on the inverse limit follows also from compactness of $U_K^{(1)}$ and its surjectivity on every finite quotient.
