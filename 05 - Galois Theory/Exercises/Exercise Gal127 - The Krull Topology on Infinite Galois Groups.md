# Exercise Gal127: The Krull Topology on Infinite Galois Groups

## Problem Statement

> [!question]
> Let $K/k$ be a Galois extension. We define the **Krull topology** on the group $G(K/k) = G$ by defining a base for open sets to consist of all sets $\sigma H$ where $\sigma \in G$ and $H = G(K/F)$ for some finite extension $F$ of $k$ contained in $K$.
> 
> (a) Show that if one takes only those sets $\sigma H$ for which $F$ is finite Galois over $k$ then one obtains another base for the same topology.
> 
> (b) The projective limit $\varprojlim_{H} G/H$ is embedded in the direct product
> $$
> \varprojlim_{H} G/H \to \prod_H G/H.
> $$
> Give the direct product the product topology. By Tychonoff's theorem in elementary point set topology, the direct product is compact because it is a direct product of finite groups, which are compact (and of course also discrete). Show that the inverse limit $\varprojlim G/H$ is closed in the product, and is therefore compact.
> 
> (c) Conclude that $G(K/k)$ is compact.
> 
> (d) Show that every closed subgroup of finite index in $G(K/k)$ is open.
> 
> (e) Show that the closed subgroups of $G(K/k)$ are precisely those subgroups which are of the form $G(K/F)$ for some extension $F$ of $k$ contained in $K$.
> 
> (f) Let $H$ be an arbitrary subgroup of $G$ and let $F$ be the fixed field of $H$. Show that $G(K/F)$ is the closure of $H$ in $G$.

## Hints

> [!hint]- Weak Hint
> Recall that every finite extension $F/k$ inside a Galois extension $K/k$ is contained in a finite Galois extension $F'/k$ (the normal closure of $F$ over $k$).

> [!hint]- Moderate Hint
> - For (a), show that any basic open neighborhood of $\sigma$ defined by a finite extension contains a basic open neighborhood defined by its normal closure.
> - For (b), write the condition defining the inverse limit as the intersection of closed sets defined by compatibility with transition maps.
> - For (d), a subgroup of finite index has only finitely many cosets. If each coset is closed, what does that say about their union and complements?

> [!hint]- Strong Hint
> In (f), show that an element $\sigma \in G$ lies in the closure $\bar{H}$ if and only if for every finite Galois extension $F_0/k$, the restriction $\left.\sigma\right|_{F_0}$ matches $\left.\tau\right|_{F_0}$ for some $\tau \in H$. Relate this to $\sigma$ fixing the fixed field $K^H$.

## Solution

> [!success]- Complete Derivation
> Let $K/k$ be an arbitrary Galois extension (normal and separable, possibly infinite), and let $G = \operatorname{Gal}(K/k)$.
>
> ---
>
> ### Part (a): Alternative Base from Finite Galois Subfields
> Let $\mathcal{B}_1 = \{\sigma \operatorname{Gal}(K/F) \mid \sigma \in G, \, [F : k] < \infty, \, F \subseteq K\}$.
> Let $\mathcal{B}_2 = \{\sigma \operatorname{Gal}(K/E) \mid \sigma \in G, \, E/k \text{ finite Galois}, \, E \subseteq K\}$.
> Clearly $\mathcal{B}_2 \subseteq \mathcal{B}_1$, so every open set generated by $\mathcal{B}_2$ is open in the topology generated by $\mathcal{B}_1$.
>
> Conversely, let $U = \sigma \operatorname{Gal}(K/F) \in \mathcal{B}_1$.
> Since $F/k$ is a finite extension contained in the normal extension $K/k$, the normal closure $E$ of $F$ over $k$ is obtained by adjoining all conjugates of a finite generating set of $F$ over $k$.
> Thus $E/k$ is a **finite Galois extension** contained in $K$, and $F \subseteq E$.
> The inclusion of fields $F \subseteq E$ reverses under the Galois correspondence:
> $$
> \operatorname{Gal}(K/E) \subseteq \operatorname{Gal}(K/F).
> $$
> Multiplying by $\sigma$ yields:
> $$
> \sigma \operatorname{Gal}(K/E) \subseteq \sigma \operatorname{Gal}(K/F) = U.
> $$
> Since $\sigma \operatorname{Gal}(K/E) \in \mathcal{B}_2$ contains $\sigma$ and is contained in $U$, every open set in the $\mathcal{B}_1$-topology is a union of sets in $\mathcal{B}_2$.
> Hence $\mathcal{B}_2$ is a base for the same topology.
>
> ---
>
> ### Part (b): Closedness of the Inverse Limit in the Product
> Let $\mathcal{N}$ be the family of open normal subgroups $H = \operatorname{Gal}(K/E)$ where $E/k$ ranges over all finite Galois extensions in $K$.
> For $H_1, H_2 \in \mathcal{N}$ with $H_2 \subseteq H_1$ (corresponding to $E_1 \subseteq E_2$), there is a canonical projection map:
> $$
> \pi_{H_1, H_2}: G/H_2 \to G/H_1, \quad \sigma H_2 \mapsto \sigma H_1.
> $$
> The inverse limit is defined as:
> $$
> \varprojlim_{H \in \mathcal{N}} G/H = \left\{ (x_H)_{H \in \mathcal{N}} \in \prod_{H \in \mathcal{N}} G/H \;\middle|\; \pi_{H_1, H_2}(x_{H_2}) = x_{H_1} \text{ for all } H_2 \subseteq H_1 \right\}.
> $$
> For each pair $H_2 \subseteq H_1$, define the map
> $$
> f_{H_1, H_2}: \prod_{H \in \mathcal{N}} G/H \longrightarrow G/H_1 \times G/H_1, \quad (x_H) \longmapsto (\pi_{H_1, H_2}(x_{H_2}), x_{H_1}).
> $$
> Since each factor $G/H$ is finite and discrete, the projection maps and $f_{H_1, H_2}$ are continuous.
> The diagonal $\Delta = \{(y, y) \mid y \in G/H_1\}$ is closed in the discrete finite product $G/H_1 \times G/H_1$.
> The preimage
> $$
> C_{H_1, H_2} = f_{H_1, H_2}^{-1}(\Delta) = \left\{ (x_H) \in \prod G/H \;\middle|\; \pi_{H_1, H_2}(x_{H_2}) = x_{H_1} \right\}
> $$
> is the continuous inverse image of a closed set, hence **closed** in $\prod_{H \in \mathcal{N}} G/H$.
> Therefore, the inverse limit is the intersection of closed sets:
> $$
> \varprojlim_{H \in \mathcal{N}} G/H = \bigcap_{H_2 \subseteq H_1} C_{H_1, H_2},
> $$
> and is consequently a **closed subset** of the product space $\prod_{H \in \mathcal{N}} G/H$.
>
> By Tychonoff's theorem, the product of compact spaces is compact. Since each finite group $G/H$ is compact, $\prod G/H$ is compact.
> Being a closed subset of a compact space, $\varprojlim G/H$ is **compact**.
>
> ---
>
> ### Part (c): Compactness of $G(K/k)$
> Consider the canonical map
> $$
> \Phi: G \longrightarrow \varprojlim_{H \in \mathcal{N}} G/H, \quad \sigma \longmapsto (\sigma H)_{H \in \mathcal{N}}.
> $$
> - **Injective:** If $\Phi(\sigma) = 1$, then $\sigma \in H = \operatorname{Gal}(K/E)$ for all finite Galois extensions $E/k$.
>   Since $K = \bigcup E$ is the directed union of all finite Galois subextensions, $\sigma$ fixes every element of $K$, so $\sigma = \operatorname{id}_G$.
> - **Surjective:** Let $(x_H) \in \varprojlim G/H$. For each $H = \operatorname{Gal}(K/E)$, $x_H$ is a coset $\sigma_E H$.
>   For any finite subcollection, the intersection of these closed cosets in $G$ is non-empty by compatibility. By the finite intersection property, there exists $\sigma \in \bigcap_H \sigma_E H$. Then $\Phi(\sigma) = (x_H)$.
> - **Topological Isomorphism:** The basic open sets in $\varprojlim G/H$ are cylinder sets, which pullback under $\Phi$ precisely to the basic open sets $\sigma H$ in $G$.
>
> Thus $\Phi$ is an isomorphism of topological groups:
> $$
> G \cong \varprojlim_{H \in \mathcal{N}} G/H.
> $$
> Since the inverse limit is compact by part (b), $G = G(K/k)$ is **compact**.
>
> ---
>
> ### Part (d): Closed Subgroups of Finite Index are Open
> Let $J \le G$ be a closed subgroup of finite index $m = (G : J) < \infty$.
> Let $\{\sigma_1 J, \sigma_2 J, \dots, \sigma_m J\}$ be the distinct left cosets of $J$ in $G$, with $\sigma_1 = 1$.
> Since $J$ is closed, every coset $\sigma_i J$ is homeomorphic to $J$ and is therefore closed.
>
> The complement of $J$ in $G$ is the finite union of the remaining cosets:
> $$
> G \setminus J = \bigcup_{i=2}^m \sigma_i J.
> $$
> Since a finite union of closed sets is closed, $G \setminus J$ is closed in $G$.
> Consequently, its complement $J$ is **open** in $G$.
>
> ---
>
> ### Part (e): Characterization of Closed Subgroups
> 1. **Subgroups of the form $G(K/F)$ are closed:**
>    For any intermediate field $F$ with $k \subseteq F \subseteq K$, $F = \bigcup_{\alpha \in F} k(\alpha)$.
>    For each $\alpha \in F$, let $E_\alpha$ be the Galois closure of $k(\alpha)$ over $k$.
>    Then $\sigma$ fixes $\alpha$ if and only if $\left.\sigma\right|_{E_\alpha} \in \operatorname{Gal}(E_\alpha / k(\alpha))$.
>    This is a union of cosets of $\operatorname{Gal}(K/E_\alpha)$, so it is closed.
>    Thus $G(K/F) = \bigcap_{\alpha \in F} G(K/k(\alpha))$ is an intersection of closed sets, hence **closed**.
>
> 2. **Every closed subgroup has this form:**
>    Let $H \le G$ be a closed subgroup, and let $F = K^H$ be its fixed field.
>    Clearly $H \subseteq G(K/F)$.
>    By part (f) below, the closure of $H$ is $\bar{H} = G(K/F)$.
>    Since $H$ is closed, $H = \bar{H} = G(K/F)$.
>
> Thus the closed subgroups are precisely those of the form $G(K/F)$.
>
> ---
>
> ### Part (f): Closure of an Arbitrary Subgroup
> Let $H \le G$ be an arbitrary subgroup, and let $F = K^H$ be its fixed field.
> We must show that
> $$
> \bar{H} = G(K/F).
> $$
> - **Inclusion $\bar{H} \subseteq G(K/F)$:**
>   By part (e), $G(K/F)$ is a closed subgroup of $G$.
>   Since $H \subseteq G(K/F)$ by definition of the fixed field $F = K^H$, the topological closure satisfies $\bar{H} \subseteq G(K/F)$.
>
> - **Inclusion $G(K/F) \subseteq \bar{H}$:**
>   Let $\sigma \in G(K/F)$. To show that $\sigma \in \bar{H}$, we must show that every open neighborhood of $\sigma$ intersects $H$.
>   By part (a), a neighborhood base at $\sigma$ consists of cosets $\sigma \operatorname{Gal}(K/E)$ where $E/k$ is a finite Galois extension contained in $K$.
>   We must show:
>   $$
>   \sigma \operatorname{Gal}(K/E) \cap H \ne \varnothing.
>   $$
>   Consider the restriction of $H$ to $E$:
>   $$
>   H|_E = \{\left.\tau\right|_E \mid \tau \in H\} \le \operatorname{Gal}(E/k).
>   $$
>   The fixed field of $H|_E$ in $E$ is $E \cap K^H = E \cap F$.
>   By the finite Galois correspondence applied to the finite Galois extension $E/k$, the subgroup fixing $E \cap F$ is:
>   $$
>   \operatorname{Gal}(E / E \cap F) = H|_E.
>   $$
>   Since $\sigma \in G(K/F)$, $\sigma$ fixes $F$ pointwise, and in particular fixes $E \cap F$.
>   Therefore:
>   $$
>   \left.\sigma\right|_E \in \operatorname{Gal}(E / E \cap F) = H|_E.
>   $$
>   Thus there exists some $\tau \in H$ such that $\left.\tau\right|_E = \left.\sigma\right|_E$.
>   This means $\left.\sigma^{-1} \tau\right|_E = \operatorname{id}_E$, which implies:
>   $$
>   \sigma^{-1} \tau \in \operatorname{Gal}(K/E) \iff \tau \in \sigma \operatorname{Gal}(K/E).
>   $$
>   Since $\tau \in H$, we have $\tau \in \sigma \operatorname{Gal}(K/E) \cap H$.
>   Thus every basic neighborhood of $\sigma$ contains an element of $H$, proving $\sigma \in \bar{H}$.
>
> Therefore, $\bar{H} = G(K/F)$.

## Notes

- Wolfgang Krull established this topological correspondence in 1928, resolving the problem that for an infinite Galois extension $K/k$, the mapping $H \mapsto K^H$ has non-closed subgroups as fibers (all subgroups with the same closure produce the same fixed field).
- Open subgroups correspond bijectively to finite subextensions, while general closed subgroups correspond bijectively to arbitrary subextensions.
