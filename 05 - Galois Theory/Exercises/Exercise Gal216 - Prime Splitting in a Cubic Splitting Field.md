# Exercise Gal216: Prime Splitting in a Cubic Splitting Field

## Problem Statement

> [!question] Original Exercise 9.1.1
> Let $F=\mathbb Q(d^{1/3},\mu_3)$ be the non-Abelian number field from the section. Granting that the factorization of $p\nmid3d$ in $\mathbb Q(d^{1/3})$ comes from reducing $x^3-d$ modulo $p$, obtain the factorization of $p$ in $F$. (A hint for this exercise is at the end of the book.)

## Hints

> [!hint]- Hint 1
> Frobenius permutes the three roots with cycle lengths given by the irreducible factor degrees.

> [!hint]- Hint 2
> In the Galois closure its order is the common residue degree, and the number of primes is six divided by that degree.

## Solution

> [!success]- Solution
> **Proof scope:** Independent derivation.
>
> The hypotheses say $[F:\mathbb Q]=6$ and $\operatorname{Gal}(F/\mathbb Q)\cong S_3$. For $p\nmid3d$ the polynomial has three distinct roots in its splitting field over $\mathbb F_p$ and $p$ is unramified. If $p\equiv2\pmod3$, cubing is bijective on $\mathbb F_p^*$, so there is exactly one root and the other two form an irreducible quadratic factor. Frobenius is a transposition, of order two. If $p\equiv1\pmod3$, the cube roots of unity lie in $\mathbb F_p$. When $d$ is a cube all three roots lie there, giving identity Frobenius; otherwise a cubic with no root is irreducible, giving a three-cycle.
>
> For an unramified prime of a Galois number field, its decomposition group is cyclic generated by Frobenius and its order is the residue degree $f$. Thus $g=6/f$ and every ramification index is one:
> $$
> p\mathcal O_F=\begin{cases}
> \mathfrak p_1\cdots\mathfrak p_6,&p\equiv1\pmod3,\ d\text{ a cube mod }p,\quad f=1,\\
> \mathfrak p_1\mathfrak p_2,&p\equiv1\pmod3,\ d\text{ not a cube mod }p,\quad f=3,\\
> \mathfrak p_1\mathfrak p_2\mathfrak p_3,&p\equiv2\pmod3,\quad f=2.
> \end{cases}
> $$
> All displayed prime ideals are distinct. Their norms are $p^f$, so each line also verifies the norm identity $N(p\mathcal O_F)=p^6$.
