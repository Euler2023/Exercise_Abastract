# Exercise Gal159: Galois Groups after a Disjoint Base Extension

## Problem Statement

> [!question] Neukirch IV.1.1
> Let $L|k$ be a Galois extension and $K|k$ an arbitrary extension, both contained in a common extension $\Omega|k$. If $L\cap K=k$, then the mapping
> $$
> G(LK|K)\longrightarrow G(L|k),\qquad\sigma\longmapsto\sigma|_L,
> $$
> is a topological isomorphism, that is, an isomorphism of groups and a homeomorphism of topological spaces.

## Hints

> [!hint]- Hint 1
> The extension $LK/K$ is still Galois, and restriction is continuous and injective. Use compactness to show that its image is closed.

> [!hint]- Hint 2
> The fixed field in $L$ of the restriction image is exactly $L\cap K$. Apply the infinite Galois correspondence to the closed image subgroup.

## Solution

> [!success]- Solution
> **Proof scope:** Independent derivation
>
> Every element of $L/k$ is separable over $k$, and its minimal polynomial splits in $L$. Its minimal polynomial over $K$ consequently splits in $LK$, so $LK/K$ is Galois. Restriction gives a group homomorphism $r$. If $\sigma$ is the identity on $L$ and fixes $K$, it fixes the field $LK$ generated by them. Hence $r$ is injective.
>
> Basic open neighborhoods in the Krull topology are subgroups fixing finite subextensions. The inverse image under restriction of the pointwise stabilizer of finitely many elements of $L$ is again an open stabilizer, so $r$ is continuous. Galois groups are compact Hausdorff groups. Thus $H=r(G(LK/K))$ is closed.
>
> An element $a\in L$ is fixed by $H$ exactly when, viewed in $LK$, it is fixed by $G(LK/K)$, equivalently when $a\in K$. Therefore
> $$
> L^H=L\cap K=k.
> $$
> The infinite Galois correspondence applies to closed subgroups, giving $H=G(L/k)$. This uses the topological Galois correspondence proved in the text [Jürgen Neukirch, Algebraic Number Theory, English ed., 1999, Ch. IV, §1, (1.2), printed pp. 263–264, PDF pp. 282–283], not the conclusion of this exercise. Thus $r$ is a continuous bijection. A continuous bijection from a compact space to a Hausdorff space is a homeomorphism, giving the required topological isomorphism.

- **Source and proof status:** [Jürgen Neukirch, Algebraic Number Theory, English ed., 1999, Ch. IV, §1, Exercise 1, printed p. 264, PDF p. 283]. This independent restriction-map and closed-subgroup argument allows infinite Galois $L/k$ and does not require $K/k$ to be Galois or finite.
- **Topology:** An abstract group bijection alone would not complete the proof. The compact-to-Hausdorff argument above supplies the topological conclusion.
