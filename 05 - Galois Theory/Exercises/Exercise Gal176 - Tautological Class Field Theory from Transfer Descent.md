# Exercise Gal176: Tautological Class Field Theory from Transfer Descent

## Problem Statement

> [!question] Neukirch IV.§6, Exercise 6
> (Tautological class field theory.) Assume that the profinite group $G$ satisfies the condition: for every finite Galois extension,
> $$
> \operatorname{Ver}:G_K^{ab}\longrightarrow(G_L^{ab})^{G(L/K)}
> $$
> is an isomorphism. (These are the profinite groups of "strict cohomological dimension 2" (see [145], chap. III, th. (3.6.4)).) Put $A_K=G_K^{ab}$ and form the direct limit $A=\varinjlim_K A_K$ via the transfer. Then $A_K$ is identified with $A^{G_K}$.
> Show that for every cyclic extension $L/K$ one has
> $$
> \#H^i(G(L/K),A_L)=\begin{cases}[L:K]&\text{for }i=0,\\1&\text{for }i=-1,\end{cases}
> $$
> and that for every surjective homomorphism $d:G\to\widehat{\mathbb Z}$, the induced map $v:A_k=G^{ab}\to\widehat{\mathbb Z}$ is a henselian valuation with respect to $d$. The corresponding reciprocity map $r_{L/K}:G(L/K)\to A_K/N_{L/K}A_L$ is essentially the identity.

## Hints

> [!hint]- Hint 1 — first step
> For a cyclic quotient $\Gamma/H$, first identify the image of $H^{ab}\to\Gamma^{ab}$ and its kernel.

> [!hint]- Hint 2 — proof route
> For a cyclic quotient $\Gamma/H$, its commutator subgroup is $[\Gamma,H]$. Use $\operatorname{Ver}\circ j=N$ and the assumed transfer descent.

## Solution

> [!success]- Solution
> **Proof scope:** Independent derivation
>
> Transitivity of transfer defines the directed limit. Every finite $L/K$ embeds into a further finite Galois $M/K$. The hypothesis makes $\operatorname{Ver}_{M/K}$ injective, so all transfers are injective and each $A_K$ may be regarded as a subgroup of $A$. Conjugation defines the action of $G$ on $A$. Every element of $A_K$ is fixed by the open subgroup $G_K$, making the action discrete and continuous.
> If $a\in A^{G_K}$, choose a finite Galois level $L/K$ containing a representative. Injectivity of transfer implies that $a$ is already fixed by $G(L/K)$ in $A_L$. The hypothesis gives $a\in\operatorname{Ver}A_K$. Hence $A^{G_K}=A_K$.
> Now suppose $\Gamma=G_K,H=G_L,Q=\Gamma/H$ is cyclic. Write $B=H^{\mathrm{ab}}$ and $C=\Gamma^{\mathrm{ab}}$, and let $j:B\to C$ be the map induced by inclusion. Choose $\tau\in\Gamma$ lifting a generator of $Q$. Since $\Gamma$ is topologically generated by $H,\tau$, its closed commutator subgroup satisfies $\Gamma'=[\Gamma,H]$, and therefore
> $$
> \ker j=[\Gamma,H]/H'=(\tau-1)B.
> $$
> On $B$ the module norm satisfies $N_Q=\operatorname{Ver}\circ j$. The hypothesis $\operatorname{Ver}:C\xrightarrow{\sim}B^Q$ then gives $\ker N_Q=\ker j=(\tau-1)B$, so $\widehat H^{-1}(Q,B)=0$. Likewise,
> $$
> \widehat H^0(Q,B)=B^Q/N_QB
> \cong C/jB\cong\Gamma/H=Q,
> $$
> gives the orders of the two groups.
> For $d$, the induced map $v:G^{\mathrm{ab}}\to\widehat{\mathbb Z}$ is surjective. For finite $K/k$, the norm is the inclusion $G_K^{\mathrm{ab}}\to G^{\mathrm{ab}}$, so the value group is $d(G_K)=f_K\widehat{\mathbb Z}$. After normalization, every finite-level value group is $\widehat{\mathbb Z}$, contains the standard integers, and has quotient $\mathbb Z/n$ modulo each $n$. This gives precisely the henselian index conditions.
> Finally, $A_K/N_{L/K}A_L=C/jB\cong G(L/K)^{\mathrm{ab}}$. The two quotients are represented by the class of the same element of $\Gamma$, so the reciprocity isomorphism is this identity identification.
