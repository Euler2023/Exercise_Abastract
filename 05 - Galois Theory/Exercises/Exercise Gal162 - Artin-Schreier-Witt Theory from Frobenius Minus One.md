# Exercise Gal162: Artin-Schreier-Witt Theory from Frobenius Minus One

## Problem Statement

> [!question] Neukirch IV.3.3
> Show that the operator
> $$
> \wp:W_n(\bar k)\longrightarrow W_n(\bar k),\qquad\wp a=Fa-a,
> $$
> is a homomorphism with cyclic kernel $\mu_\wp$ of order $p^n$. Discuss the corresponding Kummer theory for the abelian extensions of exponent $p^n$.

## Hints

> [!hint]- Hint 1
> In characteristic $p$, Witt Frobenius takes the $p$-th power of each coordinate and is an additive homomorphism. Its fixed coordinates are precisely those in $\mathbb F_p$.

> [!hint]- Hint 2
> The element $1$ of $W_n(\mathbb F_p)$ has additive order $p^n$. Solve $Fa-a=b$ coordinate by coordinate using equations of the form $X^p-X=c$.

> [!hint]- Hint 3
> The preceding exercise verifies axiom (3.1). Check each hypothesis of the general Kummer theorem (3.3), then write the parameter quotient and the pairing.

## Solution

> [!success]- Solution
> **Proof scope:** Independent derivation
>
> Let $\operatorname{char}k=p$ and $\bar k=k^{sep}$. Over a coefficient ring of characteristic $p$, Witt Frobenius is $F(a_0,\ldots,a_{n-1})=(a_0^p,\ldots,a_{n-1}^p)$ and is an additive homomorphism. Thus $\wp=F-1$ is an additive homomorphism commuting with the Galois action.
>
> **Kernel and surjectivity.** The equality $Fa=a$ holds if and only if every coordinate lies in $\mathbb F_p$, so the kernel is $W_n(\mathbb F_p)$. In this ring, $p(a_0,a_1,\ldots)=(0,a_0,\ldots)$, so the unit vector $1=(1,0,\ldots)$ has additive order $p^n$. The kernel has $p^n$ elements and is therefore generated by $1$, giving $\mu_\wp\simeq\mathbb Z/p^n\mathbb Z$.
>
> To solve $Fa-a=b$, the first coordinate must satisfy $a_0^p-a_0=b_0$. Each subsequent coordinate $i$ has an equation of the form
> $$
> a_i^p-a_i=b_i+P_i(a_0,\ldots,a_{i-1},b_0,\ldots,b_{i-1}),
> $$
> where $P_i$ comes from the triangular polynomials with integer coefficients defining Witt addition and subtraction. At each step the right side is known, and $X^p-X-c$ has derivative $-1$, so it has a root in the separably closed field $\bar k$. Thus the equations can be solved coordinate by coordinate and $\wp$ is surjective. This does not require $k$ to be perfect.
>
> **Checking the hypotheses of the general Kummer theorem.** The module $A=W_n(\bar k)$ is a continuous additive Galois module. The preceding exercise proves that it satisfies (3.1) for all finite cyclic extensions. We have just shown that $\wp$ is surjective with kernel the cyclic group $W_n(\mathbb F_p)$ fixed over the base field. Hence the general Kummer theorem (3.3) applies. Its statement and proof are in [Jürgen Neukirch, Algebraic Number Theory, English ed., 1999, Ch. IV, §3, printed pp. 279–280, PDF pp. 298–299].
>
> For $K\subseteq\bar k$, abelian extensions of exponent dividing $p^n$ correspond bijectively to additive subgroups
> $$
> \wp W_n(K)\subseteq B\subseteq W_n(K)
> $$
> by the rule $L=K(\wp^{-1}B)$. This means adjoining all coordinates of every Witt vector satisfying $F\alpha-\alpha\in B$. The inverse rule is $B=\wp W_n(L)\cap W_n(K)$. The pairing is explicitly
> $$
> G(L/K)\times B/\wp W_n(K)\longrightarrow\mathbb Z/p^n\mathbb Z,
> \qquad(\sigma,\bar b)\longmapsto\sigma(\alpha)-\alpha,\quad\wp\alpha=b.
> $$
> Changing the solution adds only a fixed kernel element, and changing the representative of $b$ adds only a vector from $W_n(K)$. Thus the pairing is well-defined. The general theorem gives
> $$
> B/\wp W_n(K)\simeq\operatorname{Hom}_{cont}(G(L/K),\mathbb Z/p^n\mathbb Z).
> $$
> For finite extensions, the paired groups are finite dual groups. For infinite extensions, the characters on the right must be continuous. The case $n=1$ recovers Artin–Schreier theory for $X^p-X=b$.

## Notes

- **Source and proof status:** [Jürgen Neukirch, Algebraic Number Theory, English ed., 1999, Ch. IV, §3, Exercise 3, printed p. 283, PDF p. 302]. Independent proof of the kernel description and surjectivity. The classification applies, hypothesis by hypothesis, the general operator Kummer theorem fully proved in the book.
- **Notation:** The group $\mu_\wp$ is the additive cyclic group $W_n(\mathbb F_p)$, rather than the degenerate multiplicative group of $p^n$-th roots of unity in a field of characteristic $p$. Following the convention in the main text, exponent $p^n$ here means exponent dividing $p^n$.
